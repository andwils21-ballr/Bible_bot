# Philippians 2

## The Mind of Christ

**1** So if there is any comfort in Christ, any consolation from love, any fellowship of the Spirit, any affection and compassion,

**2** make my joy complete by being of the same mind, having the same love, being one in soul and of one mind.

**3** Do nothing from rivalry or conceit, but in humility count others more important than yourselves,

**4** each of you looking not only to his own interests, but also to the interests of others.

**5** Have this mind among yourselves, which was also in Christ Jesus,

**6** who, though He was in the form of God, did not count equality with God a thing to be seized,

**7** but emptied Himself, taking the form of a slave, being born in the likeness of men; and found in appearance as a man,

**8** He humbled Himself by becoming obedient to the point of death, even death on a cross.

**9** Therefore God has highly exalted Him and given Him the name that is above every name,

**10** so that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of those in heaven and on earth and under the earth,

**11** and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father.

## Lights in the World

**12** So, my beloved, as you have always obeyed, not only as in my presence but much more now in my absence, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling,

**13** for it is God who works in you, both to will and to work, for His good pleasure.

**14** Do all things without grumbling or arguing,

**15** so that you may be blameless and innocent, children of God without defect in the middle of a crooked and twisted generation, among whom you shine as lights in the world,

**16** holding fast the word of life, so that in the day of Christ I may have reason to boast that I did not run in vain or labor in vain.

**17** Even if I am poured out as a drink offering on the sacrifice and service of your faith, I am glad and rejoice with you all.

**18** In the same way you also should be glad and rejoice with me.

## Timothy and Epaphroditus

**19** I hope in the Lord Jesus to send Timothy to you soon, so that I too may be cheered by news of you.

**20** For I have no one like him, who will be genuinely concerned for your welfare.

**21** They all seek their own interests, not those of Jesus Christ.

**22** But you know his proven worth, how as a son with a father he has served with me in the gospel.

**23** So I hope to send him as soon as I see how things go with me,

**24** and I am confident in the Lord that I myself will also come soon.

**25** I have thought it necessary to send to you Epaphroditus, my brother and fellow worker and fellow soldier, and your messenger and minister to my need,

**26** for he has been longing for you all and has been distressed because you heard that he was ill.

**27** Indeed he was ill, near to death. But God had mercy on him, and not only on him but on me also, so that I would not have sorrow upon sorrow.

**28** I am the more eager to send him, therefore, so that you may rejoice at seeing him again, and that I may be less sorrowful.

**29** So receive him in the Lord with all joy, and honor such men,

**30** for he came near to death for the work of Christ, risking his life to complete what was lacking in your service to me.

## Notes

- **v6 "did not count equality with God a thing to be seized"** — *harpagmos*, from *harpazō*, **seize**, **snatch**, the verb of Paul *caught up* to the third heaven (2 Corinthians 12:2). Either equality with God was not something to grasp at, or it was His already and not something to hold onto for His own gain. In both readings, what He did with it was to empty Himself.
- **vv7–9 "taking the form of a slave… He humbled Himself… God has highly exalted Him"** — *morphē*, **form**, at both ends: *the form of God* (v6), *the form of a slave* (v7). The rising after the lowering is the pattern of Isaiah's servant: *My servant… will rise high and be lifted up, and be very exalted* (Isaiah 52:13), *hypsōthēsetai* in the Greek; Paul's *hyperypsōsen* is the same verb made higher still.
- **vv10–11 "at the name of Jesus every knee should bow… and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord"** — the LORD's oath in Isaiah, in the chapter where He says *There is no other god besides Me, a righteous God and a Savior* (45:21): *By Myself I have sworn… To Me every knee shall bow, every tongue shall swear* (Isaiah 45:23). What the LORD swore would be given to Him alone is given at the name of Jesus, and *Lord*, *Kyrios*, is the word the Greek Bible uses for the LORD's own name.
