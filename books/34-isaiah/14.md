# Isaiah 14

**1** For the LORD will have compassion on Jacob, and will again choose Israel, and will settle them in their own land; and the guest will join them and attach himself to the house of Jacob.

**2** Peoples will take them and bring them to their place, and the house of Israel will possess them in the LORD's land as male and female servants. They will take captive those who took them captive, and rule over their slave drivers.

**3** And it shall come to pass, in the day the LORD gives you rest from your pain and your turmoil and the hard service you were made to serve,

**4** that you will take up this taunt against the king of Babylon, and say:

"How the slave driver has ceased,  
the taskmaster has ceased!

**5** The LORD has broken the staff of the wicked,  
the rod of rulers,

**6** that struck the peoples in fury  
with blows that never let up,  
that ruled the nations in anger  
with persecution no one held back.

**7** The whole earth is at rest and quiet;  
they break out into singing.

**8** Even the cypresses rejoice over you,  
the cedars of Lebanon:  
'Since you were laid low,  
no woodcutter comes up against us.'

**9** Sheol beneath is stirred up for you,  
to meet you when you come;  
it rouses the shades for you,  
all the leaders of the earth;  
it raises from their thrones  
all the kings of the nations.

**10** All of them will answer  
and say to you:  
'You too have become as weak as we are;  
you have become like us.'

**11** Your pomp is brought down to Sheol,  
with the sound of your harps;  
maggots are the bed spread beneath you,  
and worms are your covering.

**12** How you have fallen from heaven,  
Day Star, son of the dawn!  
How you are cut down to the ground,  
you who laid the nations low!

**13** You said in your heart:  
'I will ascend to heaven;  
above the stars of God  
I will set my throne on high;  
I will sit on the mount of assembly  
in the far reaches of the north;

**14** I will ascend above the heights of the clouds;  
I will make myself like the Most High.'

**15** But you are brought down to Sheol,  
to the far reaches of the pit.

**16** Those who see you will stare at you  
and look you over:  
'Is this the man who made the earth tremble,  
who shook kingdoms,

**17** who made the world like a wilderness  
and tore down its cities,  
who never let his prisoners go home?'

**18** All the kings of the nations, every one of them,  
lie in honor, each in his own tomb;

**19** but you are cast out, away from your grave,  
like a detestable branch,  
clothed with the slain, those pierced by the sword,  
who go down to the stones of the pit,  
like a corpse trampled underfoot.

**20** You will not be joined with them in burial,  
because you have destroyed your land;  
you have slain your people.  
The seed of evildoers  
will never be named again.

**21** Prepare a slaughtering place for his sons  
for the iniquity of their fathers,  
so that they do not rise and possess the earth  
and fill the world with cities."

**22** "I will rise up against them,"  
declares the LORD of hosts,  
"and cut off from Babylon name and remnant,  
offspring and posterity,"  
declares the LORD.

**23** "I will make it a possession for the hedgehog,  
and pools of water,  
and I will sweep it with the broom of destruction,"  
declares the LORD of hosts.

## The LORD's Plan for Assyria

**24** The LORD of hosts has sworn:  
"As I have planned,  
so it will be;  
as I have purposed,  
so it will stand:

**25** to break Assyria in My land,  
and trample him on My mountains;  
his yoke will depart from them,  
and his burden will depart from their shoulder."

**26** This is the plan made for the whole earth,  
and this is the hand stretched out  
over all the nations.

**27** For the LORD of hosts has planned,  
and who will annul it?  
His hand is stretched out,  
and who will turn it back?

## The Oracle Against Philistia

**28** In the year that King Ahaz died this oracle came:

**29** Do not rejoice, Philistia, all of you,  
that the rod that struck you is broken;  
for from the serpent's root will come an adder,  
and its fruit will be a flying fiery serpent.

**30** The poorest of the poor will graze,  
and the needy will lie down in safety;  
but I will kill your root with famine,  
and it will slay your remnant.

**31** Wail, gate! Cry out, city!  
Melt in fear, Philistia, all of you!  
For smoke comes out of the north,  
and there is no straggler in its ranks.

**32** What will one answer the messengers of the nation?  
"The LORD has founded Zion,  
and in her the afflicted of His people will find refuge."

## Notes

- **vv2–4 "slave drivers… the hard service you were made to serve… How the slave driver has ceased"** — *nogesim*, the word for Pharaoh's slave drivers (Exodus 3:7; 5:6), and *ha-avodah ha-qashah*, the *hard service* of Exodus 1:14. The fall of Babylon is told in the words of the release from Egypt, as the return was in 11:15–16 and the song in 12:2.
- **v4 "the taskmaster"** — *madhevah*, a word found only here, whose meaning is not known. The Greek has *epispoudastēs*, "one who hurries others on," from the verb the Greek of Exodus uses for the slave drivers who *pressed* Israel (*katespeudon*, Exodus 5:13); the rendering follows it. Taken from the Aramaic *dehav*, "gold" (Daniel 2:32), it would be "the golden city." "This taunt" — *mashal*, a proverb, a saying that likens one thing to another; the Greek calls it a *thrēnos*, a lament.
- **vv4, 5, 10 "taunt… rulers… you have become like us"** — the same three letters, *m-sh-l*, three times: the *mashal*, "taunt," is taken up against the *moshlim*, "rulers," and the dead greet the king with *nimshalta*, "you have been made like" us.
- **vv8, 13–15 "the cedars of Lebanon… the far reaches of the north… the far reaches of the pit"** — felling Lebanon's cedars is a conqueror's boast: *I have gone up… to the far reaches of Lebanon; I cut down its tallest cedars, its choicest cypresses*, says the king of Assyria (37:24). Here the trees are glad the king is gone. *Yarkhetei*, "far reaches," twice: he aims at *the far reaches of the north*, which Psalm 48:2 gives to Mount Zion (*Mount Zion, the heights of the north, the city of the great King*), and is sent to *the far reaches of the pit*.
- **v11 "maggots are the bed spread beneath you"** — *yutsa*, the verb for spreading a bed, as in *if I make my bed in Sheol* (Psalm 139:8).
- **v12 "Day Star, son of the dawn"** — *helel ben shachar*. *Helel* is from *halal*, "to shine" (*when His lamp shone on my head*, Job 29:3); the Greek has *heōsphoros*, "dawn-bringer," the morning star. The taunt is spoken *against the king of Babylon* (v4), and when he lands, the dead ask, *Is this the man?* (v16). "Cut down to the ground" — *nigda'ta*, the verb used of the tall trees *hewn down* in 10:33. Jesus uses the same picture for Satan: *I saw Satan fall like lightning from heaven* (Luke 10:18).
- **v14 "I will make myself like the Most High"** — *eddammeh*, from *damah*, "to be like." The LORD answers it in the book's second half: *To whom then will you liken Me, that I should be equal?* (40:25). In v24 He swears by the same verb, *ka'asher dimmiti*, "as I have planned."
- **v19 "like a detestable branch"** — *ke-netser nit'av*. *Netser* is the *branch* from the roots of Jesse (11:1); *nit'av* is the verb of *to'evah*, "detestable." One branch bears fruit; the other is thrown out.
- **vv25–27 "his yoke will depart from them… the hand stretched out… who will turn it back?"** — the words of 10:27 again, *his burden will depart from your shoulder, and his yoke from your neck*. *Ha-yad ha-netuyah*, "the hand stretched out," and *shuv*, "turn back," are the words of the refrain, *His anger has not turned away, and His hand is still stretched out* (5:25 to 10:4). The hand that was stretched out against Israel is now stretched out over all the nations.
- **vv28–29 "In the year that King Ahaz died… a flying fiery serpent"** — dated like the vision of 6:1, *In the year that King Uzziah died*. *Saraf me'ofef*: the *seraphim* who flew above the throne (6:2, *ye'ofef*, the same verb) and the *fiery serpents* of the wilderness (Numbers 21:6) share the name. "From the serpent's root" — *mi-shoresh nachash*, a root as in 11:1 and 11:10, the root of Jesse.
