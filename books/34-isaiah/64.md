# Isaiah 64

**1** Oh, that You would tear open the heavens and come down,  
that the mountains would quake at Your presence,

**2** as when fire kindles brushwood,  
and fire makes water boil,  
to make Your name known to Your adversaries,  
so that the nations might tremble at Your presence!

**3** When You did awesome things that we did not look for,  
You came down; the mountains quaked at Your presence.

**4** From of old no one has heard,  
no ear has perceived,  
no eye has seen a God besides You,  
who acts for the one who waits for Him.

**5** You meet the one who gladly does righteousness,  
those who remember You in Your ways.  
You were angry, and we sinned;  
we have been in them a long time,  
and will we be saved?

**6** We have all become like one who is unclean,  
and all our righteous deeds are like a menstrual cloth.  
We all wither like a leaf,  
and our iniquities, like the wind, carry us away.

**7** There is no one who calls on Your name,  
who rouses himself to take hold of You;  
for You have hidden Your face from us  
and made us melt away in the power of our iniquities.

**8** But now, LORD, You are our Father;  
we are the clay, and You are our potter;  
we are all the work of Your hand.

**9** Do not be angry beyond measure, LORD,  
and do not remember iniquity forever.  
Look on us, please: we are all Your people.

**10** Your holy cities have become a wilderness;  
Zion has become a wilderness,  
Jerusalem a desolation.

**11** Our holy and beautiful house,  
where our fathers praised You,  
has been burned with fire,  
and all our precious things have become ruins.

**12** Will You restrain Yourself at these things, LORD?  
Will You keep silent and afflict us beyond measure?

## Notes

- **v1 "Oh, that You would tear open the heavens and come down"** — in the Hebrew these words end 63:19, and the Hebrew numbers the rest of this chapter one lower (64:2 is Hebrew 64:1); the English numbering is followed. *Qara*, "tear," is the word for tearing a garment. At Jesus' baptism Mark says He saw *the heavens being torn open* (Mark 1:10), where the Greek of Isaiah has *if You open the heaven*.
- **v4 "no eye has seen a God besides You, who acts for the one who waits for Him"** — Paul writes, *as it is written: What no eye has seen and no ear has heard, and what has not entered the heart of man, what God has prepared for those who love Him* (1 Corinthians 2:9). His words are close to this verse, though not those of the Greek of Isaiah, which has *we have not heard, nor have our eyes seen a God besides You, and Your works which You will do for those who wait for mercy*.
- **v5 "we have been in them a long time, and will we be saved?"** — *bahem olam ve-nivvashea*, "in them, long; and we will be saved." Read as a question, *them* are the sins of the line before, and the people ask whether there is any hope left. Read as a statement, *them* are *Your ways* two lines above: *in them we will be forever, and we will be saved*. The Hebrew has no mark of a question. The Greek reads the line another way: *You were angry, and we sinned; therefore we went astray*.
- **v6 "like one who is unclean… like a menstrual cloth"** — *tame*, "unclean," the cry the blighted man must call out, *Unclean! Unclean!* (Leviticus 13:45); *beged iddim*, "a garment of monthly flow," the uncleanness of a woman in her monthly separation, which passed to anyone who touched her and to everything she lay or sat on (Leviticus 15:19–20). The Greek has *the rag of a woman sitting apart*. The people say their righteous deeds themselves carry the uncleanness.
- **v8 "we are the clay, and You are our potter"** — *yotsrenu*, "the one who forms us," the potter of 29:16 and *the One who formed him* of 45:9. There the clay talks back to the potter; here it asks Him to remember that it is His work.
- **v11 "Our holy and beautiful house… has been burned with fire"** — the burning is told in 2 Kings 25:9, *He burned the house of the LORD*. *Beautiful* is *tif'arteinu*, from *pa'ar*, the word of the LORD's promise in 60:7, *I will beautify My beautiful house*.
- **v12 "Will You restrain Yourself… Will You keep silent"** — *tit'appaq… techesheh*, the two verbs of 42:14, *For a long time I have held My peace; I have kept still and restrained Myself*, where the LORD said the silence would end; *restrained* is also the word of 63:15. The answer comes in the next chapter: *I will not keep silent, but I will repay* (65:6).
