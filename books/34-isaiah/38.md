# Isaiah 38

**1** In those days Hezekiah became sick and was near death. Isaiah the prophet son of Amoz came to him and said to him, "This is what the LORD says: 'Set your house in order, for you shall die; you shall not live.'"

**2** Then Hezekiah turned his face to the wall and prayed to the LORD,

**3** and said, "Please, O LORD, remember how I have walked before You in faithfulness and with a whole heart, and have done what is good in Your eyes." Hezekiah wept bitterly.

**4** Then the word of the LORD came to Isaiah, saying,

**5** "Go and say to Hezekiah, 'This is what the LORD, the God of David your father, says: I have heard your prayer; I have seen your tears. I will add fifteen years to your days.

**6** I will deliver you and this city out of the hand of the king of Assyria, and I will defend this city.

**7** This is the sign to you from the LORD, that the LORD will do this thing that He has spoken:

**8** I am about to make the shadow on the steps, which has gone down on the steps of Ahaz with the sun, go back ten steps.'" So the sun went back ten steps on the steps by which it had gone down.

## Hezekiah's Song

**9** A writing of Hezekiah king of Judah, when he had been sick and had recovered from his sickness:

**10** I said: In the quiet of my days  
I must go to the gates of Sheol;  
I am deprived of the rest of my years.

**11** I said: I will not see Yah,  
Yah in the land of the living;  
I will look on man no more  
among those who dwell where all has ceased.

**12** My dwelling is pulled up and taken from me  
like a shepherd's tent;  
I have rolled up my life like a weaver;  
He cuts me off from the loom;  
from day to night You bring me to an end.

**13** I quieted myself until morning;  
like a lion He breaks all my bones;  
from day to night You bring me to an end.

**14** Like a swift, like a crane, I chirp;  
I moan like a dove;  
my eyes are worn out with looking upward.  
Lord, I am oppressed; be my pledge!

**15** What shall I say?  
He has spoken to me, and He Himself has done it.  
I will walk slowly all my years  
because of the bitterness of my soul.

**16** Lord, by these things men live,  
and in all of them is the life of my spirit;  
You restore me to health and make me live.

**17** It was for my peace  
that I had bitter, bitter grief;  
but You have set Your heart on my soul  
and kept it from the pit of destruction,  
for You have cast all my sins  
behind Your back.

**18** For Sheol does not thank You;  
death does not praise You;  
those who go down to the pit  
do not hope for Your faithfulness.

**19** The living, the living, he thanks You,  
as I do this day;  
a father makes known to his children  
Your faithfulness.

**20** The LORD is ready to save me,  
and we will play my songs on strings  
all the days of our life,  
at the house of the LORD.

**21** Isaiah said, "Let them take a cake of figs and rub it on the boil, and he will recover."

**22** Hezekiah said, "What is the sign that I shall go up to the house of the LORD?"

## Notes

- **vv1–8, 21–22 and 2 Kings 20** — the account of 2 Kings 20:1–11, shortened, with Hezekiah's song in the middle. Kings has the fig cake and Hezekiah's question before the sign (20:7–8); Isaiah sets them at the end, after the song. Kings also has the question whether the shadow should go forward or back (20:9–10), and the promise *I will heal you; on the third day you shall go up to the house of the LORD* (20:5).
- **v8 "the steps of Ahaz"** — *ma'alot*, "steps, ascents," the word of the Psalms' heading *A song of ascents* (Psalm 120). The king whose father would not ask for a sign (7:11–12) is given one on his father's steps.
- **v9 "A writing of Hezekiah"** — *mikhtav*, "a writing"; the Psalms' heading *mikhtam* (Psalm 16) differs by one letter.
- **vv10–11 "the gates of Sheol… among those who dwell where all has ceased"** — *chadel*, "ceasing," with the letters of *cheled*, "the world, a lifetime," in another order.
- **v14 "I chirp… be my pledge"** — *atsaftsef*, the verb of the ghosts that *chirp* (8:19; 29:4): the sick man already sounds like the dead. *Orveni*, "stand surety for me," the word of Judah's promise for Benjamin, *I myself will stand as the pledge for him* (Genesis 43:9).
- **v17 "You have set Your heart on my soul… cast all my sins behind Your back"** — *chashaqta*, the verb of the LORD's choice of Israel, *did the LORD set His heart on you* (Deuteronomy 7:7). Sins thrown behind God's back are put where He no longer looks at them.
- **vv18–19 "Sheol does not thank You… The living, the living, he thanks You"** — the plea of Psalm 6:5, *in death there is no remembrance of You; in Sheol who will praise You?*
