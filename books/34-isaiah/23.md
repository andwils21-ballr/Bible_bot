# Isaiah 23

**1** The oracle concerning Tyre.

Wail, ships of Tarshish,  
for it is laid waste, without house or harbor!  
From the land of Kittim it is revealed to them.

**2** Be silent, inhabitants of the coast,  
merchants of Sidon,  
whose envoys crossed the sea  
and filled you;

**3** on many waters  
the grain of Shihor,  
the harvest of the Nile, was her revenue,  
and she was the marketplace of the nations.

**4** Be ashamed, Sidon,  
for the sea has spoken, the stronghold of the sea:  
"I have not been in labor or given birth;  
I have not raised young men  
or brought up young women."

**5** When the report comes to Egypt,  
they will be in anguish at the report about Tyre.

**6** Cross over to Tarshish;  
wail, inhabitants of the coast!

**7** Is this your exultant city,  
whose origin is from days of old,  
whose feet carried her  
to live as a guest far away?

**8** Who has planned this against Tyre,  
the giver of crowns,  
whose merchants were princes,  
whose traders were the honored of the earth?

**9** The LORD of hosts has planned it,  
to defile the pride of all glory,  
to bring into contempt all the honored of the earth.

**10** Overflow your land like the Nile,  
daughter of Tarshish;  
there is no restraint anymore.

**11** He has stretched out His hand over the sea;  
He has shaken kingdoms;  
the LORD has given command concerning Canaan  
to destroy its strongholds.

**12** He said: "You will exult no more,  
you oppressed virgin daughter of Sidon.  
Arise, cross over to Kittim;  
even there you will have no rest."

**13** Look at the land of the Chaldeans!  
This is the people that was not;  
Assyria founded it for desert creatures.  
They set up their siege towers;  
they stripped her palaces bare;  
they made her a ruin.

**14** Wail, ships of Tarshish,  
for your stronghold is laid waste!

**15** And it shall come to pass in that day, that Tyre will be forgotten seventy years, like the days of one king. At the end of seventy years it will be with Tyre as in the song of the prostitute:

**16** "Take a harp,  
go around the city,  
forgotten prostitute!  
Play well, sing many songs,  
that you may be remembered."

**17** And it shall come to pass at the end of seventy years, that the LORD will visit Tyre, and she will return to her wages and play the prostitute with all the kingdoms of the world on the face of the earth.

**18** Her merchandise and her wages will be holy to the LORD; they will not be stored up or hoarded, for her merchandise will go to those who dwell before the LORD, for food to eat their fill and for fine clothing.

## Notes

- **v1 "ships of Tarshish… the land of Kittim"** — the far western sea route. Tarshish and Kittim are sons of Javan in the table of nations (Genesis 10:4); the *ships of Tarshish* were among the proud things brought low on the day of the LORD (2:16).
- **v7 "to live as a guest far away"** — *la-gur*, the verb of the guest: Tyre's colonists went out to live in other men's lands.
- **v8 "whose traders were the honored of the earth"** — *kin'aneha*, literally "her Canaanites," set beside *her merchants*: the name of the people had become a word for a trader. In v11 the LORD gives command *concerning Canaan*, the merchant coast itself.
- **v11 "He has stretched out His hand over the sea"** — *yado natah al ha-yam*, the act of Moses at the Red Sea (*Moses stretched out his hand over the sea*, Exodus 14:21), and the words of the refrain, *His hand is still stretched out* (5:25 to 10:4). Here the hand is over the sea Tyre ruled.
- **v13 "Look at the land of the Chaldeans! This is the people that was not"** — the verse is hard. As it stands, Babylon's land is held up to Tyre as a warning: a people that was nothing, given by Assyria to the desert creatures, its towers and palaces ruined. The Greek reads a shorter line: *and to the land of the Chaldeans, and this has been laid waste by the Assyrians; its wall has fallen*. The read form *bachunav*, "its siege towers," is followed.
- **vv15–18 "the song of the prostitute… her wages will be holy to the LORD"** — *etnan*, the prostitute's wage, which the law forbade to be brought to the LORD: *You shall not bring the wages of a prostitute… into the house of the LORD your God* (Deuteronomy 23:18). Tyre's trade is pictured as selling herself to every kingdom, and at the end her wages are turned over to the LORD's people. *Seventy years* is the length of Babylon's rule over the nations in Jeremiah 25:11.
