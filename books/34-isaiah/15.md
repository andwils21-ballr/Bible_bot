# Isaiah 15

**1** The oracle concerning Moab.

Ar of Moab is laid waste in a night;  
it is ruined.  
Kir of Moab is laid waste in a night;  
it is ruined.

**2** They have gone up to the temple, and Dibon to the high places, to weep;  
over Nebo and over Medeba Moab wails.  
Every head is shaved bald;  
every beard is cut off.

**3** In its streets they put on sackcloth;  
on its roofs and in its squares  
everyone wails, running with tears.

**4** Heshbon and Elealeh cry out;  
their voice is heard as far as Jahaz.  
So the armed men of Moab cry aloud;  
his soul trembles within him.

**5** My heart cries out for Moab;  
her fugitives flee as far as Zoar,  
to Eglath-shelishiyah.  
Up the ascent of Luhith  
they go weeping;  
on the road to Horonaim  
they raise a cry of ruin.

**6** The waters of Nimrim are desolate;  
the grass is withered, the new growth is gone;  
there is nothing green.

**7** So the wealth they have gained  
and what they have stored up  
they carry away over the Brook of the Willows.

**8** The cry has gone around the borders of Moab;  
her wailing reaches Eglaim;  
her wailing reaches Beer-elim.

**9** The waters of Dimon are full of blood;  
yet I will bring still more on Dimon:  
a lion for those of Moab who escape,  
and for the remnant of the land.

## Notes

- **v1 "The oracle concerning Moab"** — Moab was Lot's son by his elder daughter (Genesis 19:37). Jeremiah's oracle on Moab takes up much of this one and the next, often word for word (Jeremiah 48:5, 29–38). "Ruined" — *nidmah*, the verb of Isaiah's own cry in the temple, *I am ruined* (6:5).
- **v2 "Every head is shaved bald; every beard is cut off"** — the signs of mourning, with the sackcloth of v3. Isaiah calls Jerusalem to the same in 22:12, *to weeping and mourning, to baldness and to putting on sackcloth*.
- **v5 "My heart cries out for Moab"** — the prophet grieves for the enemy he condemns, and he says it again: *I weep… I drench you with my tears* (16:9); *my inner parts moan for Moab like a harp* (16:11). Jeremiah takes up the line: *my heart moans for Moab like flutes* (Jeremiah 48:36).
- **v5 "Eglath-shelishiyah"** — literally "the third Eglath," or "a heifer three years old," as the Greek reads it (*damalis trietēs*). Jeremiah 48:34 has it in a list of towns, from Zoar to Horonaim, and it is rendered here as a place.
- **vv2, 9 "Dibon… the waters of Dimon are full of blood"** — the town is Dibon in v2; in v9 it is spelled Dimon, twice, beside *dam*, "blood." The spelling makes the town's name sound like what fills its waters.
- **v9 "a lion for those of Moab who escape, and for the remnant"** — *peletah*, "those who escape," and *she'erit*, "remnant": the words of hope for Israel in 10:20 (*peletah*, and *she'ar* from the same root as *she'erit*). Moab's survivors meet a lion.
