# Isaiah 47

**1** Come down and sit in the dust,  
virgin daughter of Babylon;  
sit on the ground without a throne,  
daughter of the Chaldeans!  
For you will no longer be called  
tender and delicate.

**2** Take the millstones and grind flour;  
remove your veil;  
strip off your skirt, uncover your legs,  
wade through the rivers.

**3** Your nakedness will be uncovered,  
and your shame will be seen.  
I will take vengeance,  
and I will spare no one.

**4** Our Redeemer — the LORD of hosts is His name —  
is the Holy One of Israel.

**5** Sit in silence, and go into darkness,  
daughter of the Chaldeans;  
for you will no longer be called  
the mistress of kingdoms.

**6** I was angry with My people;  
I profaned My inheritance,  
and I gave them into your hand.  
You showed them no mercy;  
on the aged you made your yoke very heavy.

**7** You said, "I will be mistress forever,"  
so that you did not lay these things to heart  
or remember how it would end.

**8** So now hear this, you lover of pleasures,  
who sits secure,  
who says in her heart,  
"I am, and there is none besides me;  
I will not sit as a widow  
or know the loss of children."

**9** These two things will come to you  
in a moment, in one day:  
the loss of children and widowhood.  
They will come on you in full measure,  
in spite of your many sorceries  
and the great power of your spells.

**10** You felt secure in your wickedness;  
you said, "No one sees me."  
Your wisdom and your knowledge led you astray,  
and you said in your heart,  
"I am, and there is none besides me."

**11** But disaster will come on you,  
which you will not know how to charm away;  
calamity will fall on you,  
which you will not be able to ward off;  
ruin will come on you suddenly,  
which you do not know.

**12** Stand fast in your spells  
and your many sorceries,  
with which you have labored from your youth;  
perhaps you will be able to profit,  
perhaps you will strike terror.

**13** You are wearied with your many counsels;  
let them stand up and save you,  
the astrologers of the heavens,  
who gaze at the stars,  
who make known at the new moons  
what will come on you.

**14** They have become like stubble;  
the fire burns them;  
they cannot deliver themselves  
from the power of the flame.  
It is no coal to warm oneself by,  
no fire to sit before!

**15** So they have become to you,  
those with whom you have labored,  
who have traded with you from your youth;  
they wander off, each his own way;  
there is no one to save you.

## Notes

- **v3 "I will spare no one"** — *lo efga adam*, "I will not meet a man." *Paga* is to meet someone, with a plea or with a blow; in 53:12 and 59:16 the same verb is to intercede. The line can mean that the LORD lets no one plead for Babylon, or that He meets no one with mercy. The Greek has *I will no longer hand you over to men*.
- **vv8, 10 "I am, and there is none besides me"** — *ani ve-afsi od*: Babylon speaks the LORD's own words. *Efes*, "none besides," and *od*, "no other," are the words of 45:6, *there is none besides Me; I am the LORD, and there is no other*, and of 46:9.
- **v13 "the astrologers of the heavens"** — *hoverei shamayim*, the read form; the written form spells it as a verb. The verb *havar* is found nowhere else, and what it does is not known; the Greek has *the astrologers of heaven*, which is what the next two lines describe: they gaze at the stars and announce at each new moon (*mod'im le-chodashim*) what is coming.
