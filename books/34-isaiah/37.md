# Isaiah 37

## Hezekiah Sends to Isaiah

**1** And it came to pass, when King Hezekiah heard it, that he tore his clothes, covered himself with sackcloth and went into the house of the LORD.

**2** He sent Eliakim, who was over the household, Shebna the secretary and the elders of the priests, covered with sackcloth, to Isaiah the prophet son of Amoz.

**3** They said to him, "This is what Hezekiah says: 'This day is a day of distress, of rebuke and of disgrace. The children have come to the mouth of the womb, and there is no strength to give birth.

**4** It may be that the LORD your God will hear the words of the Rabshakeh, whom his master the king of Assyria has sent to defy the living God, and will rebuke the words that the LORD your God has heard. So lift up a prayer for the remnant that is left.'"

**5** So the servants of King Hezekiah came to Isaiah,

**6** and Isaiah said to them, "Say this to your master: 'This is what the LORD says: Do not be afraid of the words you have heard, with which the servants of the king of Assyria have reviled Me.

**7** I will put a spirit in him, so that he will hear a report and return to his own land, and I will make him fall by the sword in his own land.'"

## Sennacherib's Letter

**8** The Rabshakeh returned and found the king of Assyria fighting against Libnah, for he had heard that the king had left Lachish.

**9** Then the king heard it said of Tirhakah king of Cush, "He has come out to fight against you." When he heard it, he sent messengers to Hezekiah, saying,

**10** "Say this to Hezekiah king of Judah: 'Do not let your God in whom you trust deceive you by saying, "Jerusalem will not be given into the hand of the king of Assyria."

**11** You have heard what the kings of Assyria have done to all the lands, devoting them to destruction. Will you be delivered?

**12** Did the gods of the nations deliver them, the nations my fathers destroyed, Gozan, Haran, Rezeph and the people of Eden who were in Telassar?

**13** Where is the king of Hamath, the king of Arpad, the king of the city of Sepharvaim, of Hena and Ivvah?'"

## Hezekiah's Prayer

**14** Hezekiah took the letter from the hand of the messengers and read it. Then Hezekiah went up to the house of the LORD and spread it out before the LORD.

**15** Hezekiah prayed to the LORD, saying,

**16** "O LORD of hosts, the God of Israel, who is enthroned on the cherubim, You are God, You alone, of all the kingdoms of the earth. You have made the heavens and the earth.

**17** Incline Your ear, O LORD, and hear; open Your eyes, O LORD, and see. Hear all the words of Sennacherib, which he has sent to defy the living God.

**18** Truly, O LORD, the kings of Assyria have laid waste all the lands and their land,

**19** and have cast their gods into the fire, for they were no gods, but the work of human hands, wood and stone; so they destroyed them.

**20** So now, O LORD our God, save us from his hand, so that all the kingdoms of the earth may know that You alone are the LORD."

## The Word of the LORD against Sennacherib

**21** Then Isaiah son of Amoz sent to Hezekiah, saying, "This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: Because you have prayed to Me concerning Sennacherib king of Assyria,

**22** this is the word that the LORD has spoken concerning him:  
She despises you, she scorns you,  
the virgin daughter of Zion;  
behind you she shakes her head,  
the daughter of Jerusalem.

**23** Whom have you defied and reviled?  
Against whom have you raised your voice  
and lifted your eyes on high?  
Against the Holy One of Israel!

**24** By your servants you have defied the Lord,  
and you have said, 'With the multitude of my chariots  
I have gone up to the heights of the mountains,  
to the far reaches of Lebanon.  
I cut down its tallest cedars,  
its choicest cypresses;  
I entered its farthest height,  
its densest forest.

**25** I dug wells and drank waters,  
and with the sole of my feet I dried up  
all the streams of Egypt.'

**26** Have you not heard?  
Long ago I made it;  
from days of old I formed it;  
now I have brought it to pass,  
that you should turn fortified cities  
into heaps of ruins,

**27** while their inhabitants, short of strength,  
were dismayed and put to shame.  
They were like the grass of the field  
and the green herb,  
like grass on the housetops,  
like a field before the grain has grown.

**28** I know your sitting down,  
your going out and your coming in,  
and your raging against Me.

**29** Because you have raged against Me  
and your arrogance has come up to My ears,  
I will put My hook in your nose  
and My bit in your lips,  
and I will turn you back on the way  
by which you came.

**30** "This shall be the sign for you: this year you shall eat what grows of itself, and in the second year what springs from that; then in the third year sow and reap, plant vineyards and eat their fruit.

**31** The survivors of the house of Judah who remain shall again take root downward and bear fruit upward.

**32** For out of Jerusalem shall go a remnant, and survivors out of Mount Zion. The zeal of the LORD of hosts will do this.

**33** "Therefore this is what the LORD says concerning the king of Assyria: He shall not come into this city or shoot an arrow there, nor come before it with a shield, nor cast up a siege mound against it.

**34** By the way that he came, by the same he shall return, and he shall not come into this city, declares the LORD.

**35** For I will defend this city to save it, for My own sake and for the sake of David My servant."

## The Night of the Messenger

**36** The messenger of the LORD went out and struck down a hundred and eighty-five thousand in the camp of the Assyrians. When people rose early in the morning, they were all dead bodies.

**37** So Sennacherib king of Assyria set out and went home, and lived in Nineveh.

**38** As he was bowing down in the house of Nisroch his god, Adrammelech and Sharezer his sons struck him down with the sword and escaped to the land of Ararat. Esarhaddon his son reigned in his place.

## Notes

- **vv1–38 and 2 Kings 19** — the same account, nearly word for word. Isaiah numbers it one verse longer, dividing Hezekiah's prayer after *saying* (vv15–16). The differences: *the LORD of hosts* in the prayer (v16); *that You alone are the LORD* where Kings has *that You, O LORD, are God alone* (v20); no *I have heard* after the prayer, so that the answer runs straight on, *Because you have prayed to Me… this is the word* (vv21–22); *by your servants* for Kings' *by your messengers* (v24); *waters* where Kings has *foreign waters* (v25); *a field* (*shedemah*) where Kings has *scorched* (*shedefah*) (v27); and no *And it came to pass that night* (v36).
- **v3 "The children have come to the mouth of the womb, and there is no strength to give birth"** — the picture of 26:18, *we were with child; we writhed; we gave birth, as it were, to wind*.
- **vv4, 31–32 "a prayer for the remnant… a remnant, and survivors out of Mount Zion"** — *she'erit* and *peletah*, the words of 10:20, *the remnant of Israel and the survivors of the house of Jacob*.
- **vv26–29 "Long ago I made it… I will put My hook in your nose"** — the answer of 10:5–15: Assyria was the rod in the LORD's hand, and the rod boasted. "Your raging against Me" — *hitraggezkha*, from *ragaz*, "to tremble, rage."
- **v31 "take root downward and bear fruit upward"** — as in 27:6, *Jacob will take root; Israel will blossom and put out shoots*.
- **v32 "The zeal of the LORD of hosts will do this"** — the words that close the promise of the child on David's throne (9:7).
- **v36 "The messenger of the LORD went out"** — what 10:33–34 and 31:8 had announced: *Assyria will fall by a sword, not of man*. *Mal'akh*, "messenger," the word for the LORD's sent one.
- **vv37–38 "in the house of Nisroch his god… his sons struck him down"** — the king who asked *Where are the gods of Hamath and Arpad?* (36:19) dies in the house of his own god.
