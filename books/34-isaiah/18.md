# Isaiah 18

**1** Woe to the land of whirring wings  
beyond the rivers of Cush,

**2** which sends envoys by the sea,  
in vessels of papyrus on the waters!  
Go, swift messengers,  
to a nation tall and smooth,  
to a people feared near and far,  
a nation of line upon line, that tramples down,  
whose land the rivers divide.

**3** All you who live in the world,  
you who dwell on the earth,  
when a signal is raised on the mountains, you will see it;  
when the ram's horn is blown, you will hear it.

**4** For thus the LORD said to me:  
"I will be still and look on from My dwelling place,  
like shimmering heat in the sunlight,  
like a cloud of dew in the heat of harvest."

**5** For before the harvest, when the blossom is over  
and the flower becomes a ripening grape,  
He will cut off the shoots with pruning hooks,  
and the spreading branches He will lop off and clear away.

**6** They will all be left to the birds of prey of the mountains  
and to the beasts of the earth;  
the birds of prey will summer on them,  
and all the beasts of the earth will winter on them.

**7** At that time a gift will be brought to the LORD of hosts — a people tall and smooth — and from a people feared near and far, a nation of line upon line, that tramples down, whose land the rivers divide, to the place of the name of the LORD of hosts, Mount Zion.

## Notes

- **v1 "beyond the rivers of Cush"** — Cush is the land the Greek calls *Aithiopia*, Ethiopia, here and in every verse of Isaiah that names it (*Tirhakah king of Cush*, 37:9). Its river is the Gihon of Eden, *the one winding through all the land of Cush* (Genesis 2:13). "Whirring wings" — *tsiltsal*, a word for whirring or buzzing, the *cricket* of Deuteronomy 28:42; the Greek reads *the wings of ships*, their sails.
- **v2 "vessels of papyrus"** — *kelei gome*, the reed of the *basket of papyrus* in which Moses was set on the Nile (Exodus 2:3).
- **vv2, 7 "a nation of line upon line"** — *goy qav-qav*, the doubled word for a measuring line. It comes again in 28:10, 13, *qav la-qav*, "line upon line," of a speech Israel will hear *with stammering lips and another tongue* (28:11). What it says of this nation is not known: one that measures out lands, one strong beyond measure, or one whose speech sounds to Israel like a babble. "Whose land the rivers divide" — *bazu*, a word found only here and in v7.
- **vv3–6 "I will be still and look on"** — the LORD waits like the summer heat and the dew that ripen the crop, and then, before the harvest, cuts the shoots. *Mazmerot*, "pruning hooks," are what the spears are beaten into in 2:4.
- **v7 "a gift will be brought to the LORD of hosts — a people tall and smooth"** — the Hebrew has no *from* before the first *people*, only before the second (*and from a people feared*); as written, the people themselves are the gift. The Greek has *from a people* both times, and most English supplies it. Isaiah ends with the nations bringing Israel's scattered brothers *as an offering to the LORD… to My holy mountain Jerusalem* (66:20). *Kings will bring You gifts*, *shai*, the word here (Psalm 68:29); *Cush will run to stretch out its hands to God* (68:31). In Acts an Ethiopian, an official of the queen of the Ethiopians, *had come to Jerusalem to worship* (Acts 8:27).
