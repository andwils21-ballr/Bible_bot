# Isaiah 3

**1** For the Lord, the LORD of hosts,  
is about to take away from Jerusalem and from Judah  
every prop and support:  
all support of bread,  
and all support of water;

**2** the mighty man and the man of war,  
the judge and the prophet,  
the diviner and the elder,

**3** the captain of fifty and the man of rank,  
the counselor and the skilled craftsman,  
and the expert in charms.

**4** I will make boys their princes,  
and whims shall rule over them.

**5** The people will oppress one another,  
each man his fellow and each his neighbor;  
the youth will be insolent to the elder,  
and the despised to the honored.

**6** When a man takes hold of his brother  
in his father's house:  
"You have a cloak; you shall be our ruler,  
and this heap of ruins shall be in your charge,"

**7** in that day he will cry out:  
"I will not be a healer;  
in my house there is neither bread nor cloak;  
you shall not make me ruler of the people."

**8** For Jerusalem has stumbled,  
and Judah has fallen,  
because their speech and their deeds are against the LORD,  
defying His glorious presence.

**9** The look on their faces testifies against them;  
they declare their sin like Sodom;  
they do not hide it.  
Woe to them!  
For they have brought evil on themselves.

**10** Tell the righteous that it shall be well with them,  
for they shall eat the fruit of their deeds.

**11** Woe to the wicked! It shall go badly with him,  
for what his hands have done shall be done to him.

**12** My people — their oppressors are children,  
and women rule over them.  
My people, those who guide you lead you astray,  
and have swallowed up the way of your paths.

**13** The LORD has taken His place to contend;  
He stands to judge the peoples.

**14** The LORD will enter into judgment  
with the elders of His people and their princes:  
"It is you who have devoured the vineyard;  
the plunder of the poor is in your houses.

**15** What do you mean by crushing My people,  
and grinding the faces of the poor?"  
declares the Lord GOD of hosts.

**16** The LORD said:  
Because the daughters of Zion are haughty,  
and walk with outstretched necks,  
glancing with their eyes,  
walking with mincing steps,  
and jingling with their feet,

**17** the Lord will strike with scabs  
the heads of the daughters of Zion,  
and the LORD will lay bare their foreheads.

**18** In that day the Lord will take away the finery of the anklets, the headbands and the crescents,

**19** the pendants, the bracelets and the veils,

**20** the headdresses, the armlets, the sashes, the perfume boxes and the amulets,

**21** the signet rings and the nose rings,

**22** the festal robes, the mantles, the cloaks and the purses,

**23** the mirrors, the linen garments, the turbans and the shawls.

**24** Instead of perfume there will be rot;  
instead of a sash, a rope;  
instead of well-set hair, baldness;  
instead of a rich robe, a girding of sackcloth;  
branding instead of beauty.

**25** Your men shall fall by the sword,  
and your mighty ones in battle.

**26** Her gates shall lament and mourn;  
and emptied, she shall sit on the ground.

## Notes

- **v1 "every prop and support"** — *mash'en u-mash'enah*, the same word in its masculine and feminine forms: every kind of support. The list that follows (vv2–3) names what Judah leaned on, and puts *the diviner* and *the expert in charms* beside the judge and the prophet.
- **v4 "whims shall rule over them"** — *ta'alulim*, "caprices" or "childishness": the rulers are boys, and what rules is their whim.
- **vv6–7 "I will not be a healer"** — *chovesh*, "one who binds up," the verb of 1:6, *not bound up*: no one will take on the wounds of the city. He has a cloak, and that is enough to be offered the throne.
- **v9 "they declare their sin like Sodom"** — the third time Sodom is named (1:9, 10).
- **v12 "their oppressors are children, and women rule over them"** — *me'olel… nashim*, as the Hebrew is pointed. The same letters can be read *me'olel*, "gleaning," and *noshim*, "creditors," and that is how the Greek takes them: *your exactors glean you, and the creditors rule over you* (*hoi apaitountes*). The pointing makes the line continue v4, the rule of boys.
- **vv13–14 "The LORD has taken His place to contend… It is you who have devoured the vineyard"** — *la-riv*, the lawsuit of chapter 1 again. *Bi'artem*, "you have grazed off" or "burned," is the word the vineyard song uses for what will happen to the vineyard (5:5, *it shall be grazed bare*); the song itself follows two chapters on.
- **v17 "lay bare their foreheads"** — *pothen ye'areh*. *Pot* is rare and uncertain: "their foreheads" (where the hair is shaved), or "their private parts" (shame by stripping). Both fit the verse; the text here reads the first, after *the heads* in the line before.
- **vv18–23 "the anklets, the headbands and the crescents…"** — twenty-one items of finery. Several words are found only here, and their exact meaning is guessed from the setting and from related words. The *crescents* are the moon-shaped ornaments Gideon took from the camels of Midian (Judges 8:21).
- **v24 "branding instead of beauty"** — *ki tachat yofi*, where *ki* is read as a noun, "a burn": the brand of a captive. Every line of the verse is a reversal of vv18–23.
