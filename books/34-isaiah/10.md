# Isaiah 10

**1** Woe to those who make unjust laws,  
and those who keep putting oppression in writing,

**2** to turn the poor away from justice  
and to rob the afflicted of My people of their right,  
so that widows become their spoil,  
and they plunder the orphans.

**3** What will you do on the day of punishment,  
in the ruin that will come from far away?  
To whom will you flee for help,  
and where will you leave your wealth?

**4** Nothing is left but to crouch among the prisoners  
or to fall among the slain.  
For all this His anger has not turned away,  
and His hand is still stretched out.

## Assyria, the Rod of My Anger

**5** Woe to Assyria, the rod of My anger;  
the staff in their hand is My fury!

**6** Against a godless nation I send him,  
and against the people of My wrath I command him,  
to take spoil and seize plunder,  
and to trample them down like the mud of the streets.

**7** But this is not what he intends,  
and this is not what his heart plans;  
it is in his heart to destroy,  
and to cut off nations, not a few.

**8** For he says:  
"Are not my commanders all kings?

**9** Is not Calno like Carchemish?  
Is not Hamath like Arpad?  
Is not Samaria like Damascus?

**10** As my hand has reached the kingdoms of the idols,  
whose carved images outnumbered those of Jerusalem and Samaria,

**11** shall I not do to Jerusalem and her images  
as I have done to Samaria and her idols?"

**12** And it shall come to pass, when the Lord has finished all His work on Mount Zion and in Jerusalem, that I will punish the fruit of the king of Assyria's arrogant heart and the pride of his haughty eyes.

**13** For he says:  
"By the strength of my hand I have done it,  
and by my wisdom, for I have understanding.  
I have removed the borders of peoples  
and plundered their treasures;  
like a mighty one I have brought down the inhabitants.

**14** My hand has found the wealth of the peoples like a nest,  
and as one gathers eggs that have been left,  
so I have gathered all the earth;  
and there was none that fluttered a wing,  
or opened its beak, or chirped."

**15** Shall the axe boast over him who hews with it,  
or the saw make itself great over him who wields it?  
As if a rod should wield those who lift it,  
as if a staff should lift him who is not wood!

**16** Therefore the Lord, the LORD of hosts,  
will send leanness among his fat ones,  
and under his glory a blaze will be kindled,  
burning like fire.

**17** The light of Israel will become a fire,  
and his Holy One a flame;  
it will burn and devour his thorns and his briers in one day.

**18** The glory of his forest and of his fruitful land  
He will consume, both soul and body,  
and it will be as when a sick man wastes away.

**19** The remnant of the trees of his forest will be so few  
that a child could write them down.

## A Remnant Will Return

**20** And it shall come to pass in that day, that the remnant of Israel and the survivors of the house of Jacob will no longer lean on him who struck them, but will lean on the LORD, the Holy One of Israel, in truth.

**21** A remnant will return, the remnant of Jacob, to the Mighty God.

**22** For though your people Israel be like the sand of the sea, only a remnant of them will return. Destruction is decreed, overflowing with righteousness.

**23** For the Lord GOD of hosts will carry out the full end that is decreed, throughout the whole earth.

**24** Therefore thus says the Lord GOD of hosts: "My people who live in Zion, do not be afraid of Assyria, when he strikes you with the rod and lifts his staff against you, as in Egypt.

**25** For in a very little while the fury will be spent, and My anger will turn to their destruction."

**26** The LORD of hosts will wield a whip against him, as in the striking of Midian at the rock of Oreb; His staff will be over the sea, and He will lift it, as in Egypt.

**27** And it shall come to pass in that day, that his burden will depart from your shoulder, and his yoke from your neck, and the yoke will be broken because of fatness.

## The March on Jerusalem

**28** He has come to Aiath;  
he has passed through Migron;  
at Michmash he leaves his baggage.

**29** They have crossed the pass;  
"At Geba we will spend the night."  
Ramah trembles;  
Gibeah of Saul has fled.

**30** Cry aloud, daughter of Gallim!  
Listen, Laishah!  
Poor Anathoth!

**31** Madmenah is in flight;  
the inhabitants of Gebim take cover.

**32** This very day he will halt at Nob;  
he shakes his fist  
at the mount of the daughter of Zion,  
the hill of Jerusalem.

**33** The Lord, the LORD of hosts,  
is about to lop the boughs with a terrifying crash;  
the tall in stature will be hewn down,  
and the lofty will be brought low.

**34** The thickets of the forest will be cut down with iron,  
and Lebanon will fall by the Majestic One.

## Notes

- **vv2, 6 "widows become their spoil, and they plunder the orphans… to take spoil and seize plunder"** — *shalal* and *bazaz*, the two words of the child's name in 8:1, Maher-shalal-hash-baz, "swift is the spoil, speedy the plunder." The lawmakers make spoil of the widow and plunder of the orphan; in v6 the LORD sends Assyria *lishlol shalal ve-lavoz baz*, to do the same to them.
- **v4 "For all this His anger has not turned away"** — the refrain's last appearance (5:25; 9:12, 17, 21). It is answered in 12:1 with the same two words, *shuv*, "turn," and *af*, "anger": *Your anger has turned away, and You have comforted me*.
- **vv5, 15, 24, 26 "the rod… the staff"** — *shevet* and *matteh*, a pair that runs through the chapter. Assyria is the LORD's rod (v5); the rod boasts as if it lifted the hand that holds it (v15); Assyria lifts his staff against Zion *as in Egypt* (v24), and the LORD lifts His staff over the sea *as in Egypt* (v26), as Moses was told to lift his over the Red Sea (Exodus 14:16). *Be-derekh Mitsrayim*, "as in Egypt," is the same phrase both times: the oppressor's staff and the deliverer's.
- **vv9–11 "Is not Samaria like Damascus?… shall I not do to Jerusalem"** — the king counts Jerusalem among *the kingdoms of the idols*, and the LORD among the idols. His officer makes the same speech outside Jerusalem's walls: *Where are the gods of Hamath and Arpad?… Have they delivered Samaria from my hand?* (36:19).
- **v13 "like a mighty one"** — *ka-abbir*. The consonants are those of God's title in 1:24, *the Mighty One of Israel* (*avir*); the reading tradition voices them one way for God and another for a strong man or a bull. Spelled out, the king's boast and God's title are the same word.
- **vv16, 27 "leanness among his fat ones… because of fatness"** — *mishmannav* and *shamen*, one root. Assyria's fat ones waste away, and the yoke on Israel's neck breaks *mipnei shamen*, literally "from before fat." *Shemen* is oil, and also the fat of a body (*my flesh has grown lean, with no fat*, *mi-shamen*, Psalm 109:24). The phrase is uncertain: the picture may be a neck grown too thick for the yoke, or the oil of anointing. The Greek has neither: *the yoke will be destroyed from your shoulders*. "His burden… from your shoulder" — *subbolo… shikhmekha*, the words of 9:4.
- **vv19–22 "remnant"** — *she'ar*, five times. Assyria's remnant is a handful of trees a child can count (v19); Israel's is the *she'ar* that *will return*, the name of Isaiah's son Shear-jashub (7:3), and it returns *to the Mighty God*, *el gibbor*, the name given the child in 9:6.
- **vv22–23 "though your people Israel be like the sand of the sea"** — the promise to Abraham, *as the sand that is on the lip of the sea* (Genesis 22:17), is turned: as many as the sand, and still only a remnant comes back. The Greek has *will be saved* for *will return*, and Paul quotes it: *the remnant will be saved, for the Lord will carry out His word on the earth, completing it and cutting it short* (Romans 9:27–28).
- **vv28–32 "Aiath… Nob"** — the enemy comes town by town, each nearer, until Jerusalem is in sight. Michmash, Geba and Gibeah of Saul are the ground of Saul's war with the Philistines (*the pass of Michmash*, 1 Samuel 13:23); Anathoth is a town of priests, Jeremiah's home (Jeremiah 1:1); Nob is *the city of the priests*, put to the sword at Saul's order (1 Samuel 22:17–19). "Poor Anathoth" — *aniyyah Anatot*, two words that chime.
- **v34 "by the Majestic One"** — *be-addir*. *Addir* is used of the LORD in this book, *there the LORD in majesty will be for us* (33:21), and of great trees elsewhere, *the majestic ones are ruined* (Zechariah 11:2). Read the first way, the line matches *with iron* above it, the hand that fells the forest. The Greek reads it the second way: *Lebanon will fall with its lofty ones*.
