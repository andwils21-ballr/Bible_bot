# Isaiah 20

**1** In the year that the commander in chief came to Ashdod, when Sargon king of Assyria sent him, and he fought against Ashdod and took it,

**2** at that time the LORD spoke through Isaiah son of Amoz, saying, "Go, loosen the sackcloth from your waist and take your sandals off your feet." He did so, walking naked and barefoot.

**3** The LORD said, "As My servant Isaiah has walked naked and barefoot three years as a sign and a portent against Egypt and Cush,

**4** so the king of Assyria will lead away the captives of Egypt and the exiles of Cush, young and old, naked and barefoot, with buttocks bared, the nakedness of Egypt.

**5** They will be dismayed and ashamed because of Cush, whom they looked to, and Egypt, their boast.

**6** In that day the inhabitants of this coastland will say, 'This is what has become of the one we looked to, to whom we fled for help, to be rescued from the king of Assyria! How then will we escape?'"

## Notes

- **v1 "the commander in chief… Sargon king of Assyria"** — *tartan*, the title of an Assyrian officer, the one sent with others to Jerusalem in 2 Kings 18:17. Sargon is named nowhere else in the Bible. The Greek has neither name right (*Tanathan… Arna*).
- **v2 "spoke through Isaiah"** — *be-yad*, literally "by the hand of": the message is the act. Isaiah says nothing in this chapter; for three years he walks, and the walking is the word.
- **v3 "a sign and a portent"** — *ot u-mofet*, the words of 8:18, *Here am I and the children the LORD has given me, as signs and portents in Israel*. Isaiah himself is now one.
- **vv5–6 "Cush, whom they looked to… the one we looked to"** — *mabbat*, "what one looks to," twice: the people of the coast had looked to Egypt and Cush, the powers of the Nile, against Assyria, and they watch them led away naked. Isaiah 18 and 19 have just spoken of those two lands; here the hope placed in them is ended.
