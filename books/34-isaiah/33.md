# Isaiah 33

**1** Woe to you, destroyer,  
who yourself have not been destroyed,  
you traitor,  
whom no one has betrayed!  
When you have finished destroying,  
you will be destroyed;  
when you have done betraying,  
they will betray you.

**2** LORD, be gracious to us;  
we wait for You.  
Be their arm every morning,  
our deliverance in the time of trouble.

**3** At the sound of the tumult peoples flee;  
when You rise up, nations scatter;

**4** and your spoil is gathered as the caterpillar gathers;  
as locusts leap, men leap on it.

**5** The LORD is exalted, for He dwells on high;  
He has filled Zion with justice and righteousness;

**6** There will be stability in your times,  
a wealth of deliverance, wisdom and knowledge;  
the fear of the LORD is his treasure.

**7** Their valiant ones cry out in the streets;  
the envoys of peace weep bitterly.

**8** The highways lie waste;  
the traveler has ceased.  
He has broken the covenant;  
he has despised the cities;  
he has no regard for man.

**9** The land mourns and languishes;  
Lebanon is ashamed and withers;  
Sharon is like a desert;  
Bashan and Carmel shake off their leaves.

**10** "Now I will arise," says the LORD,  
"now I will rise high;  
now I will be lifted up.

**11** You conceive chaff;  
you give birth to stubble;  
your breath is a fire that will consume you.

**12** The peoples will be as if burned to lime,  
like thorns cut down that are burned in the fire.

**13** Hear, you who are far off, what I have done;  
and you who are near, acknowledge My might."

**14** The sinners in Zion are afraid;  
trembling has seized the godless:  
"Who among us can live as a guest with the devouring fire?  
Who among us can live as a guest with everlasting burnings?"

**15** He who walks righteously and speaks uprightly,  
who rejects the gain of oppression,  
who shakes his hands free of a bribe,  
who stops his ears from hearing of bloodshed,  
and shuts his eyes from looking on evil,

**16** he will dwell on the heights;  
his refuge will be the fortresses of rocks;  
his bread will be given him;  
his water will be sure.

**17** Your eyes will see the King in His beauty;  
they will see a land that stretches afar.

**18** Your heart will muse on the terror:  
"Where is he who counted?  
Where is he who weighed the tribute?  
Where is he who counted the towers?"

**19** You will no longer see the insolent people,  
the people of a speech too deep to understand,  
of a stammering tongue that no one comprehends.

**20** Look on Zion, the city of our appointed feasts!  
Your eyes will see Jerusalem,  
a home at ease, a tent that will not be moved,  
whose stakes will never be pulled up,  
nor any of its cords be broken.

**21** But there the LORD in majesty will be for us  
a place of broad rivers and streams,  
where no galley with oars can go,  
nor majestic ship pass.

**22** For the LORD is our judge;  
the LORD is our lawgiver;  
the LORD is our king;  
He will save us.

**23** Your ropes hang loose;  
they cannot hold the mast firm in its place  
or keep the sail spread.  
Then prey and spoil in abundance will be divided;  
even the lame will take the plunder.

**24** No inhabitant will say, "I am sick";  
the people who dwell there will be forgiven their iniquity.

## Notes

- **v2 "Be their arm every morning, our deliverance"** — *zero'am*, "their arm," the prayer turning from *us* to *them* and back in one line. The Greek has another line entirely.
- **v7 "Their valiant ones"** — *er'ellam*, a word found only here, and close to *Ariel* (29:1); "valiant ones" is the sense the parallel *envoys of peace* allows.
- **v18 "Where is he who counted?"** — the Assyrian officials who counted the tribute and the towers are gone. The Greek has *Where are the scribes?*, and Paul's questions follow it: *Where is the wise? Where is the scribe?* (1 Corinthians 1:20).
