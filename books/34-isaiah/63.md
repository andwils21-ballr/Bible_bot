# Isaiah 63

**1** Who is this coming from Edom,  
in crimsoned garments from Bozrah,  
this one splendid in His clothing,  
striding in the greatness of His strength?  
"It is I, speaking in righteousness,  
mighty to save."

**2** Why is Your clothing red,  
and Your garments like one who treads in the winepress?

**3** "I have trodden the winepress alone,  
and from the peoples no one was with Me.  
I trod them in My anger  
and trampled them in My wrath;  
their lifeblood spattered on My garments,  
and I have stained all My clothing.

**4** For the day of vengeance was in My heart,  
and the year of My redemption had come.

**5** I looked, and there was no one to help;  
I was appalled that there was no one to uphold;  
My own arm brought Me deliverance,  
and My wrath upheld Me.

**6** I trampled down the peoples in My anger,  
made them drunk in My wrath,  
and poured out their lifeblood on the earth."

## Remembering the Days of Old

**7** I will recount the LORD's acts of loyal love,  
the praises of the LORD,  
according to all that the LORD has done for us,  
and His great goodness to the house of Israel,  
which He has shown them according to His compassion,  
according to the abundance of His loyal love.

**8** For He said, "Surely they are My people,  
sons who will not deal falsely";  
and He became their Savior.

**9** In all their distress He was distressed,  
and the messenger of His presence saved them;  
in His love and in His pity He redeemed them;  
He lifted them up and carried them  
all the days of old.

**10** But they rebelled  
and grieved His Holy Spirit;  
so He turned to be their enemy,  
and He Himself fought against them.

**11** Then His people remembered the days of old, the days of Moses:  
Where is He who brought them up out of the sea  
with the shepherds of His flock?  
Where is He who put His Holy Spirit among them,

**12** who made His glorious arm go at the right hand of Moses,  
who split the waters before them  
to make for Himself an everlasting name,

**13** who led them through the depths?  
Like a horse in the wilderness,  
they did not stumble.

**14** Like livestock going down into the valley,  
the Spirit of the LORD gave them rest.  
So You led Your people,  
to make for Yourself a glorious name.

**15** Look down from heaven and see,  
from Your holy and glorious dwelling.  
Where are Your zeal and Your might?  
The yearning of Your heart and Your compassion  
are restrained toward me.

**16** For You are our Father,  
though Abraham does not know us  
and Israel does not acknowledge us;  
You, LORD, are our Father;  
our Redeemer from of old is Your name.

**17** Why, LORD, do You make us wander from Your ways,  
and harden our heart so that we do not fear You?  
Return for the sake of Your servants,  
the tribes of Your inheritance.

**18** Your holy people possessed it for a little while;  
our adversaries have trampled down Your sanctuary.

**19** We have long been like those You never ruled,  
like those not called by Your name.

## Notes

- **v1 "Who is this coming from Edom… from Bozrah"** — *Edom* sounds like *adom*, "red," the word of v2, *Why is Your clothing red?*; Esau was named Edom for *that red, that red there* (Genesis 25:30). Bozrah, Edom's city, has the root letters of *batsir*, the grape harvest, *b-ts-r*. Both names point to the winepress before it is named. Edom has already been the place of the LORD's judgment: *the LORD has a sacrifice in Bozrah, a great slaughter in the land of Edom* (34:6).
- **vv3–4 "I have stained all My clothing… the year of My redemption"** — *eg'alti*, "I have stained," and *ge'ulai*, "My redemption," are spelled with the same three letters, *g-'-l*: one root means "to defile," the other "to buy back," the root of the *Redeemer* of 59:20. The garments are stained in the work of redeeming. "The day of vengeance… the year" — the pair of 61:2, *the year of the LORD's favor, and the day of vengeance of our God*, in the reverse order.
- **v3 "I have trodden the winepress alone"** — John sees the rider called the Word of God *clothed in a robe dipped in blood*, and *He treads the winepress of the wine of the fury of the wrath of God the Almighty* (Revelation 19:13, 15).
- **v5 "I was appalled that there was no one to uphold; My own arm brought Me deliverance"** — the words of 59:16, told there of the LORD, *He saw that there was no man, and was appalled that there was no one to intercede; then His own arm brought Him deliverance*, and here spoken by Him. There He put on *garments of vengeance* (59:17); here they are stained.
- **v9 "In all their distress He was distressed"** — the read form, *lo tsar*, "to Him there was distress." The written form is *lo tsar*, "not an adversary," or with other vowels *lo tsir*, "no envoy." The Greek reads it so and joins *in all their distress* to v8, *He became their salvation out of all their distress*, then goes on: *Not an envoy, nor a messenger, but He Himself saved them*. The Hebrew *mal'akh panav*, "the messenger of His face," then becomes "His face," that is, He Himself, as in Deuteronomy 4:37, where *be-fanav*, "by His face," is *He Himself brought you out of Egypt*.
- **vv9, 14 "the messenger of His presence… gave them rest"** — both halves of the LORD's promise to Moses, *My face will go, and I will give you rest* (Exodus 33:14): *panav*, "His face," His presence, and *tenichennu*, "gave them rest," the same verb. After the golden calf the LORD said He would send *a messenger* (Exodus 33:2), and Moses answered, *If Your face is not going, do not take us up from here* (33:15).
- **vv10–11 "His Holy Spirit"** — *ruach qodsho*, found only here and in David's prayer, *do not take Your Holy Spirit from me* (Psalm 51:11). Paul's *do not grieve the Holy Spirit of God* (Ephesians 4:30) turns v10 into a warning.
- **v11 "Then His people remembered… the shepherds of His flock"** — the Hebrew is *he remembered the days of old, Moses, His people*: the one who remembers is not named, and the question that follows, *Where is He…?*, is the people's. The shepherds are Moses and Aaron: *You led Your people like a flock by the hand of Moses and Aaron* (Psalm 77:20). The Greek has the singular, *the shepherd of the sheep*, and Hebrews takes those words for Jesus: *the God of peace, who brought up from the dead the great shepherd of the sheep* (Hebrews 13:20).
- **v16 "You are our Father, though Abraham does not know us"** — the people were told, *Look to Abraham your father* (51:2). In their prayer they say Abraham would not know them, and they call the LORD their Father, twice in this verse and again in 64:8.
