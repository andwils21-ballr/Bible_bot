# Isaiah 7

**1** And it came to pass in the days of Ahaz son of Jotham, son of Uzziah, king of Judah, that Rezin king of Aram and Pekah son of Remaliah, king of Israel, went up to Jerusalem to wage war against it, but could not prevail against it.

**2** When it was told to the house of David, "Aram has settled in Ephraim," his heart and the heart of his people shook as the trees of the forest shake before the wind.

**3** The LORD said to Isaiah, "Go out now to meet Ahaz, you and Shear-jashub your son, at the end of the conduit of the upper pool, on the highway to the Launderer's Field,

**4** and say to him: Take care and be calm; do not fear, and do not let your heart lose courage because of these two smoldering stubs of firebrands, because of the fierce anger of Rezin and Aram and the son of Remaliah.

**5** Because Aram has plotted evil against you, with Ephraim and the son of Remaliah, saying,

**6** 'Let us go up against Judah and terrify it, and break it open for ourselves, and set up the son of Tabeel as king in it,'

**7** thus says the Lord GOD:

It shall not stand,  
and it shall not come to pass.

**8** For the head of Aram is Damascus,  
and the head of Damascus is Rezin;  
within sixty-five years  
Ephraim will be shattered and no longer be a people.

**9** The head of Ephraim is Samaria,  
and the head of Samaria is the son of Remaliah.  
If you will not stand firm in faith,  
you will not stand firm at all."

**10** Again the LORD spoke to Ahaz, saying,

**11** "Ask a sign for yourself from the LORD your God; make it as deep as Sheol or as high as the heights above."

**12** Ahaz said, "I will not ask, and I will not test the LORD."

**13** Then Isaiah said, "Hear now, house of David! Is it too little for you to weary men, that you weary my God also?

**14** Therefore the Lord Himself will give you a sign:  
the virgin will conceive and bear a son,  
and she will call his name Immanuel.

**15** He will eat curds and honey  
by the time he knows how to refuse the evil and choose the good.

**16** For before the boy knows how to refuse the evil and choose the good, the land whose two kings you dread will be deserted.

**17** The LORD will bring upon you, upon your people and upon your father's house days such as have not come since the day Ephraim broke away from Judah: the king of Assyria."

**18** And it shall come to pass in that day, that the LORD will whistle for the fly that is at the far end of the rivers of Egypt, and for the bee that is in the land of Assyria.

**19** They will all come and settle in the steep ravines and in the clefts of the rocks, on all the thornbushes and on all the watering places.

**20** In that day the Lord will shave with a razor hired beyond the River — with the king of Assyria — the head and the hair of the feet, and it will sweep away the beard too.

**21** And it shall come to pass in that day, that a man will keep alive a young cow and two sheep;

**22** and because of the abundance of milk they give, he will eat curds; for everyone who is left in the land will eat curds and honey.

**23** And it shall come to pass in that day, that every place where there used to be a thousand vines, worth a thousand pieces of silver, will become briers and thorns.

**24** With arrows and bow one will go there, for all the land will be briers and thorns.

**25** As for all the hills that used to be hoed with a hoe, you will not go there for fear of briers and thorns; they will become a place where cattle are let loose and sheep tread.

## Notes

- **v3 "Shear-jashub your son"** — *She'ar yashuv*, "a remnant will return." The boy is a living message at his father's side, and the name returns as a promise in 10:21, *A remnant will return, the remnant of Jacob, to the Mighty God*.
- **v9 "If you will not stand firm in faith, you will not stand firm at all"** — *im lo ta'aminu ki lo te'amenu*, the same verb twice, *aman*, "to be firm," the root of *amen*: to believe is to stand on what holds.
- **v11 "as deep as Sheol"** — *ha'meq she'alah*, which can be read *she'alah*, "ask it deep," or *she'olah*, "deep to Sheol"; the line plays on both.
- **v12 "I will not test the LORD"** — the words of the law, *You shall not test the LORD your God* (Deuteronomy 6:16), used by a king who will not ask because he has already chosen Assyria (2 Kings 16:7).
- **v13 "that you weary my God also"** — in v11 the prophet said *the LORD your God*; now, after Ahaz's refusal, *my God*.
- **v14 "the virgin will conceive and bear a son"** — the Hebrew has *hinneh ha-almah harah ve-yoledet ben*, "the young woman is with child and is about to bear a son." *Almah* is a young woman of marriageable age; the word does not by itself say "virgin," for which the Hebrew has *betulah*. Both words are used of Rebekah before her marriage: *a virgin* (*betulah*, Genesis 24:16) and *the young woman* (*ha-almah*, 24:43). The Greek of Isaiah has *idou hē parthenos en gastri lēmpsetai*, "the virgin will conceive," and Matthew quotes the Greek of the birth of Jesus (Matthew 1:23). This line follows the Greek, which Matthew confirms; the Hebrew's *she will call his name* is kept (the Greek has *you will call*).
- **v14 "Immanuel"** — *immanu el*, "with us is God." The name returns as the owner of the land (8:8) and as a battle cry (8:10, *for God is with us*).
- **vv15–16 "before the boy knows how to refuse the evil and choose the good"** — the sign has a clock: before the child is old enough to choose, Damascus and Samaria will be deserted. The same clock runs for the prophet's next son (8:4).
- **v20 "the hair of the feet"** — a polite phrase for the body's hair below the waist; the shaving is the shame of captives, as Hanun shamed David's men by shaving *half of each one's beard* (2 Samuel 10:4).
- **v23 "a thousand vines, worth a thousand pieces of silver"** — the price of Solomon's vineyard (Song of Songs 8:11–12): the best land of Judah gone to briers and thorns, the pair of 5:6.
