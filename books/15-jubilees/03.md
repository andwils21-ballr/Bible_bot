# Jubilees 3

**1** On the six days of the second week, by the word of the Lord, we brought to Adam all the wild animals, and all the cattle, and all the birds, and everything that moves on the earth[, and everything that moves in the water, each by its kind and its type: the wild animals on the first day, the cattle on the second day, the birds on the third day, all that moves on the earth on the fourth day, and what moves in the water on the fifth day].

**2** Adam named them all, each by its name, and as he called them, so was their name.

**3** On these five days Adam saw all this, male and female of every kind that was on the earth, but he was alone, and he found no one to help him, like himself.

**4** The Lord said to us, "It is not good for the man to be alone. Let us make him a help like himself."

**5** The Lord our God cast a deep sleep upon him, and he slept[, and He took for the woman one rib from among his ribs, and this rib was the beginning of the woman from among his ribs, and He built up the flesh in its place, and He built the woman].

**6** He woke Adam from his sleep[, and when he woke he rose on the sixth day, and He brought her to him, and he knew her, and said to her, "This at last is bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh. She shall be called my wife, because she was taken from her husband."]

**7** For this reason a man and his wife shall be one, and for this reason a man shall leave his father and his mother and cling to his wife, and they shall become one flesh.

**8** In the first week Adam was made, and the rib, his wife; in the second week He showed her to him[; and for this reason the commandment was given to keep women in their uncleanness seven days for a male, and for a female twice seven days].

**9** After Adam had completed forty days in the land where he had been created, we brought him into the garden of Eden to serve it and to keep it[, but his wife they brought in on the eightieth day, and after this she entered the garden of Eden].

**10** For this reason the commandment is written on the tablets of heaven for a woman who gives birth: "If she bears a male, she shall remain in her uncleanness seven days, like the first week of days, and thirty-three days she shall remain in the blood of her purifying. She shall not touch anything holy, and she shall not enter the sanctuary, until she completes these days for a male.

**11** For a female, she shall remain in her uncleanness two weeks of days, like the first two weeks, and sixty-six days in the blood of her purifying; and in all they will be eighty days."

**12** When she had completed these eighty days, we brought her into the garden of Eden, for it is holier than all the earth, and every tree that is planted in it is holy.

**13** For this reason the law of these days was set for a woman who bears a male or a female: she shall touch nothing holy, and she shall not enter the sanctuary, until these days are completed for the male or the female.

**14** This is the law and testimony written down for Israel, for them to keep all the days.

**15** In the first week of the first jubilee, Adam and his wife were in the garden of Eden seven years, serving it and keeping it. We gave him work, and we taught him to do everything that is fitting for tilling.

**16** He served it, and he was naked but did not know it and was not ashamed. He guarded the garden from the birds and the wild animals and the cattle, and gathered its fruit and ate, and set aside what was left for himself and for his wife, and set aside what was to be kept.

**17** When the seven years were finished, which he had completed there, seven years exactly[, in the second month, on the seventeenth day of the month, the serpent came and approached the woman, and the serpent said to the woman, "Has God commanded you, saying, 'You shall not eat from any tree of the garden'?"]

**18** She said to it, "From all the fruit of the trees in the garden the Lord has told us, 'Eat[.' But from the fruit of the tree that is in the middle of the garden God has told us, 'You shall not eat from it, and you shall not touch it, or you will die]."

**19** The serpent said to the woman, "You will surely not die. For the Lord knows that on the day you eat from it your eyes will be opened, and you will be like gods, and you will know good and evil."

**20** The woman saw the tree, that it was pleasant and delightful to the eye, and that its fruit was good to eat[, and she took from it and ate].

**21** When she had first covered her shame with fig leaves, [she gave some to Adam, and he ate, and his eyes were opened, and he saw that he was naked].

**22** He took fig leaves and sewed them together, and made himself an apron, and covered his shame.

**23** The Lord cursed the serpent, and was angry with it forever.

**24** [He was angry with the woman, because she had listened to the voice of the serpent and eaten, and He said to her,] "I will greatly increase your sorrow and your pain. In sorrow you shall bear children. Your return shall be to your husband, and he will rule over you."

**25** To Adam also He said, "Because you listened to your wife and ate from the tree that I commanded you not to eat from, [cursed is the ground because of you. Thorns and thistles it will bring forth for you, and you will eat your bread by the sweat of your face, until you return to the earth from which you were taken. For earth you are, and to earth you will return]."

**26** He made them garments of skin, and clothed them, and sent them out of the garden of Eden.

**27** On that day, as Adam went out of the garden of Eden, he burned incense as a soothing aroma: frankincense and galbanum and stacte and spices, in the morning with the rising of the sun, from the day he covered his shame.

**28** On that day the mouths of all the wild animals and the cattle and the birds, of everything that walks and everything that moves, were closed from speaking. For they had all spoken with one another with one lip and one tongue.

**29** He sent out of the garden of Eden all flesh that was in the garden of Eden, and all flesh was scattered, each by its kind and its type, to the place that had been created for it.

**30** To Adam alone, of all the wild animals and cattle, He gave the means to cover his shame.

**31** For this reason it is commanded on the tablets, for all who know the judgment of the law, that they cover their shame and do not uncover themselves as the nations uncover themselves.

**32** On the new moon of the fourth month, Adam and his wife went out of the garden of Eden, and they lived in the land of Elda, in the land where they had been created.

**33** Adam named his wife Eve.

**34** They had no son until the first jubilee[, and after this he knew her].

**35** He worked the land as he had been taught in the garden of Eden.

## Notes

- **Words in [brackets]** — not in either Ge'ez text here, which is cut short in these verses. They follow Charles's English, made from other Ge'ez manuscripts: vv1, 5, 6, 8, 9, 17, 18, 20, 21, 24 and 25. The Greek, which gives only summaries of this chapter, agrees in substance in four of them: the order of the naming by days (v1), the rib (v5), Eve brought in on *the eightieth day of her formation* (v9), and *Adam knew Eve his wife* (v34, where the bracket follows the Greek).
- **v4 "The Lord said to us… Let us make him a help like himself"** — in Genesis the Lord says *I will make for him a help as his counterpart* (Genesis 2:18). In Jubilees He speaks to the angels, and says *let us*, as He does over the making of humankind: *Let us make humankind in our image* (Genesis 1:26).
- **vv5, 8 "one rib from among his ribs… the rib, his wife"** — *gabo*, which Charles renders *rib*. The Hebrew of Genesis 2:21, *tsela*, is rendered *side* in this project's Genesis; see the note there.
- **vv8–13 "forty days… the eightieth day"** — the law for a mother after childbirth: seven days unclean and thirty-three days in the blood of her purifying for a boy, two weeks and sixty-six days for a girl (Leviticus 12:2–5). That makes forty and eighty, and Jubilees finds the reason in Eden: Adam was brought into the garden on his fortieth day, Eve on her eightieth. The garden is holy (v12), and the rule of the sanctuary is the rule of the garden.
- **v17 "in the second month, on the seventeenth day of the month"** — the same date on which the flood will begin: *in the second month, on the seventeenth day of the month — on that day all the fountains of the great deep were split open* (Genesis 7:11).
- **v19 "You will surely not die"** — Genesis 3:4–5, with one change: the serpent says *the Lord knows*, where Genesis has *God knows*.
- **v27 "he burned incense as a soothing aroma: frankincense and galbanum and stacte and spices"** — the incense of the tabernacle: *stacte and onycha and galbanum… and pure frankincense* (Exodus 30:34). Adam's first act outside the garden is a priest's.
- **v28 "one lip and one tongue"** — the words of Babel: *All the earth was one lip and one set of words* (Genesis 11:1). Before the animals lost their speech, the serpent's talk with the woman was nothing strange.
- **v31 "that they cover their shame and do not uncover themselves as the nations uncover themselves"** — God clothed Adam and Eve (Genesis 3:21), and Jubilees makes that clothing a law.
- **v32 "the land of Elda"** — named only here in the book. The Ge'ez says *the land of their creation*: the place Adam was made, outside the garden (v9).
