# Jubilees 11

**1** In the thirty-fifth jubilee, in the third week, in its first year, Reu took a wife named Ara, the daughter of Ur, the son of Kesed, and she bore him a son, and he called his name Serug, in the seventh year of this week.

**2** In this jubilee the sons of Noah began to fight one another, to take captives and to kill each his brother, and to shed the blood of men on the earth, and to eat blood, and to build strong cities and walls and towers, and one man to raise himself over the people, and to found the beginnings of kingdoms[, and to go to war, people against people and nation against nation and city against city; and all began to do evil, and to get weapons, and to teach their sons war, and they began to capture cities and to sell male and female slaves].

**3** Ur the son of Kesed built the city of Ara of the Chaldeans, and named it after his own name and his father's name.

**4** They made themselves molten images, and each worshiped the idol, the molten image he had made for himself[; and they began to make carved images and unclean likenesses, and cruel spirits helped them and led them astray into transgression and uncleanness].

**5** The prince Mastema worked hard to do all this, and he sent out by the hand of spirits, those who had been put under his hand, to do all error and sin and all transgression, to corrupt and destroy and shed blood on the earth.

**6** For this reason Serug was called Serug, for everyone turned to do every sin.

**7** He grew up and lived in Ur of the Chaldeans, near the father of his wife's mother, and he worshiped idols[; and he took a wife in the thirty-sixth jubilee, in the fifth week, in its first year, named Melka, the daughter of Kaber, the daughter of his father's brother].

**8** She bore him Nahor in the first year of this week; and he grew up and lived in Ur, among the people of Ur of the Chaldeans, and his father taught him the researches of the Chaldeans, to practice divination and to read omens by the signs of heaven.

**9** In the thirty-seventh jubilee, in the sixth week, in its first year, he took a wife named Iyaska, the daughter of Nestag of the Chaldeans.

**10** She bore him Terah in the seventh year of this week.

**11** The prince Mastema sent ravens and birds to eat the seed sown in the land, and to ruin the land[, and to rob the sons of men of their labors. Before they could plow in the seed, the ravens picked it from the surface of the ground].

**12** For this reason he called his name Terah[, because the ravens and the birds brought them to poverty and ate their seed].

**13** The years began to be barren because of the birds, and they ate all the fruit of the trees from the trees; only with great effort could they save a little of all the fruit of the earth in their days.

**14** In this thirty-ninth jubilee, in the second week, in the first year[, Terah took a wife named Edna, the daughter of Abram, the daughter of his father's sister].

**15** In the seventh year of this week she bore him a son[, and he called his name Abram, after the name of his mother's father, for he had died before his daughter conceived a son].

**16** The child began to understand the errors of the earth, that all were going astray after carved images and after uncleanness[; and his father taught him writing, and when he was two weeks of years old he separated himself from his father, so as not to worship idols with him].

**17** He began to pray to the Creator of all, that He would save him from the errors of the sons of men[, and that his portion would not fall into error after uncleanness and shame].

**18** The time came to sow seed in the land[; and they all went out together to protect their seed from the ravens, and Abram went out with those who went, and the child was a boy of fourteen years].

**19** A cloud of ravens came to eat the seed, and Abram ran to meet them before they settled on the ground, and cried out to them before they settled on the ground to eat the seed, and said, "Do not come down. Go back to the place you came from." They turned back.

**20** That day he turned back the clouds of ravens seventy times, and of all the ravens, in all the fields where Abram was, not one settled there.

**21** All who were with him in all the fields saw him cry out, and all the ravens turn back[; and his name became great in all the land of the Chaldeans].

**22** That year all who were sowing came to him, and he went with them until the time of sowing was over, and they sowed their land[; and that year they brought home enough grain, and ate and were satisfied].

**23** In the first year of the fifth week Abram taught those who made tools for oxen, the workers in wood, and they made a vessel above the ground, facing the frame of the plow, to put the seed on it; the seed fell down from it onto the share of the plow and was hidden in the earth, and they no longer feared the ravens.

**24** They did this on every plow frame above the ground, and they sowed and worked all the land as Abram commanded them, and they no longer feared the birds.

## Notes

- **Verse numbers** — the EOTC divides this chapter into 24 verses; other editions have 23. From v4 on its numbers run one ahead of Charles's.
- **Words in [brackets]** — not in either Ge'ez text here, which is cut short in these verses. They follow Charles's English, made from other Ge'ez manuscripts: vv2, 4, 7, 11, 12, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 21 and 22. The Greek agrees where it overlaps: the wars and kingdoms (v2), *Melcha, daughter of Chaber* (v7), and Abram's name, *whom his mother named after her own father; for he had already died before his birth* (v15). Without vv14–15's brackets, the Ge'ez here never names Abram's mother, and never gives the child's name at all.
- **vv14–15 "Edna, the daughter of Abram, the daughter of his father's sister"** — so Charles; the Greek has *daughter of Abraham, his father's brother*. Abram is named after his mother's father.
- **vv10, 15 the dates** — Terah is born in the thirty-seventh jubilee, sixth week, seventh year: 36 × 49 + 5 × 7 + 7 = 1806. Abram is born in the thirty-ninth jubilee, second week, seventh year: 38 × 49 + 7 + 7 = 1876. Terah is seventy, as in Genesis: *Terah lived seventy years, and he fathered Abram* (Genesis 11:26).
- **vv2–5 "one man to raise himself over the people… molten images… the prince Mastema"** — Genesis 10–11 says nothing of idols before Abram. Jubilees sets them in Ur, among Abram's own fathers, with Mastema's spirits behind them. Joshua says the same of Terah: *Your fathers lived beyond the River from of old, Terah the father of Abraham and the father of Nahor, and they served other gods* (Joshua 24:2).
- **vv11–24 the ravens** — Mastema's birds eat the seed; the fourteen-year-old Abram turns them back seventy times in a day, and then teaches the carpenters to fit a seed box to the plow, so that the seed goes straight into the furrow. Before God calls him, Abram is already the one who prays to the Creator (v17) and feeds his neighbors.
