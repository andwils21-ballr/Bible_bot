# Jubilees 17

**1** In the first year of the fifth week of this jubilee Isaac was weaned, and Abraham made a great feast in the third month, on the day his son Isaac was weaned.

**2** Ishmael, the son of Hagar the Egyptian, was before the face of Abraham his father, in his place; and Abraham rejoiced and blessed the Lord, because he had seen his sons and had not died without sons.

**3** He remembered the word He had spoken to him on the day Lot parted from him; and he rejoiced because God had given him seed on the earth to inherit it, and with all his mouth he blessed the One who created all things.

**4** Sarah saw Ishmael playing and dancing, and Abraham rejoicing with great joy, and she was jealous of Ishmael; and she said to Abraham, "Drive out this slave woman and her son, for the son of this slave woman shall not inherit with my son Isaac."

**5** The word was grievous in Abraham's eyes, because of his maidservant and because of his son, that he should drive them away from him.

**6** The Lord said to Abraham, "Let it not be grievous in your eyes over the boy and over the slave woman. All that Sarah says to you — listen to her words and do it, for through Isaac shall name and seed be called for you.

**7** As for the son of this slave woman, I will make him into a great nation, for he is of your seed."

**8** Abraham rose early in the morning and took bread and a skin of water, and set them on the shoulders of Hagar and the child, and sent her away.

**9** She went and wandered in the wilderness of Beersheba, and the water was finished from the skin, and the child was thirsty and could not go on, and fell down.

**10** His mother took him and cast him under an olive tree, and went and sat opposite him, about a bowshot away, for she said, "Let me not see the death of my child"; and as she sat she wept.

**11** An angel of God, one of the holy ones, said to her, "Why are you weeping, Hagar? Rise, lift up the child and hold him in your hand, for the Lord has heard your voice and has seen the child."

**12** She opened her eyes and saw a well of water, and she went and filled her skin with water and gave her child a drink; and she rose and went toward the wilderness of Paran.

**13** The child grew and became an archer, and the Lord was with him; and his mother took a wife for him from the daughters of Egypt.

**14** She bore him a son, and he called his name Nebaioth, for she said, "The Lord was near to me when I called on Him."

**15** And it came to pass in the seventh week, in its first year, in the first month of this jubilee, on the twelfth of this month, that there were words in heaven about Abraham, that he was faithful in all that He told him, and that he loved the Lord, and that in every affliction he was faithful.

**16** The prince Mastema came and said before God, "Here — Abraham loves Isaac his son and delights in him above all. Tell him to offer him as a burnt offering on the altar, and You will see whether he does this command, and You will know whether he is faithful in everything in which You test him."

**17** The Lord knew that Abraham was faithful in every affliction He told him of; for He had tested him with his land and with famine, and had tested him with the wealth of kings, and had tested him again with his wife, when she was taken from him, and with circumcision; and had tested him with Ishmael and Hagar his maidservant, when he sent them away.

**18** In everything in which He had tested him he was found faithful, and his soul was not impatient, and he was not slow to act, for he was faithful and a lover of the Lord.

## Notes

- **vv3, 4, 12, 15, 16, 17** — the words the EOTC text as held here lacks are rendered from the Asmara printing of the Ge'ez (1962/63); the Latin, which covers vv1–5 of this chapter, has vv3 and 4 too. Without the end of v4 Sarah never asks for Hagar to be sent away, and without the end of v16 Mastema never says what the test should be.
- **vv1–14 Hagar and Ishmael sent away** — Genesis 21:8–21, retold in order, in this project's wording where the Ge'ez allows: *Drive out this slave woman and her son, for the son of this slave woman shall not inherit with my son, with Isaac… through Isaac shall seed be called for you* (21:10, 12). **v4 "playing and dancing"** — so the Ge'ez; the Latin has *playing with Isaac*; Genesis 21:9 has only *laughing*, the verb of Isaac's name. In Jubilees it is Abraham's joy in Ishmael that makes Sarah jealous. **v10 "under an olive tree"** — Genesis 21:15 has *under one of the bushes*. **v11 "an angel of God, one of the holy ones"** — Genesis 21:17 has *the messenger of God called to Hagar from the heavens*.
- **v1 the date** — the first year of the fifth week of the forty-first jubilee is 40 × 49 + 4 × 7 + 1 = 1989, two years after Isaac's birth (16:15). The weaning feast falls in the third month, the month of his birth.
- **v13 "his mother took a wife for him"** — the EOTC writes *for them*, *lomu*; the second Ge'ez text has the same. **v14 "Nebaioth"** — Ishmael's firstborn in Genesis 25:13. The name is explained from *The Lord was near*; Genesis gives no reason for it.
- **vv15–18 Mastema's challenge** — the scene of Job 1:9–11, where *the Satan* answers the LORD: *Is it for nothing that Job fears God?… stretch out Your hand and touch all that he has*. Genesis 22:1 says only *God tested Abraham*; Jubilees names the accuser. The Greek has it too: *Mastipham, the ruler of the demons… approached God and said, "If Abraham loves you, let him sacrifice his son to you."* **v15 the date** — the first year of the seventh week is 40 × 49 + 6 × 7 + 1 = 2003, when Isaac, born in 1987, is sixteen; the Greek puts him at twenty-five.
- **vv17–18 the tests** — the land he left (12:28), the famine that drove him to Egypt (13:10–11), Sarai taken by Pharaoh (13:13), circumcision (chapter 15), and Hagar and Ishmael sent away (vv8–12). Charles's English gives these in the same order. **"a lover of the Lord"** — James: *Abraham believed God, and it was counted to him as righteousness, and he was called friend of God* (James 2:23). Hebrews: *By faith Abraham, when he was tested, offered up Isaac* (Hebrews 11:17).
