# Jubilees 28

**1** He lifted his feet and went to the land of the east, to Laban, Rebekah's brother; and he stayed with him and served him for Rachel his daughter one week.

**2** In the first year of the third week he said to him, "Give me my wife, for whom I have served you seven years." Laban said to Jacob, "I will give you your wife."

**3** Laban made a feast, and took Leah his elder daughter and gave her to Jacob as a wife, and gave her Zilpah his maidservant as a maid; and Jacob did not know, for he thought she was Rachel.

**4** He went in to her, and here — she was Leah. Jacob was angry with Laban and said to him, "Why have you done this to me? Did I not serve you for Rachel and not for Leah? Why have you wronged me?

**5** Take your daughter, and I will go, for you have done evil to me." For Jacob loved Rachel more than Leah; for Leah's eyes were weak, though her form was very lovely, and Rachel's eyes were beautiful, and her form beautiful and very lovely.

**6** Laban said to Jacob, "It is not done so in our land, to give the younger before the elder." It is not right to do this; for so it is ordained and written on the tablets of heaven, that no one should give his younger daughter before the elder, but one gives the elder first and after her the younger; and against the man who does so they write down guilt in heaven, and no one who does this thing is righteous, for this deed is evil before the Lord.

**7** You too, command the sons of Israel not to do this thing: let them neither take nor give the younger before they have given the elder, for it is very wicked.

**8** Laban said to Jacob, "Let the seven days of the feast for this one pass, and I will give you Rachel, so that you serve me another seven years, pasturing my sheep as you did in the first week."

**9** On the day when the seven days of Leah's feast had passed, Laban gave Rachel to Jacob, that he might serve him another seven years; and he gave Rachel Bilhah, Zilpah's sister, as a maid.

**10** He served another seven years for Rachel, for Leah had been given to him for nothing.

**11** The Lord opened Leah's womb, and she conceived and bore Jacob a son, and he called his name Reuben, on the fourteenth of the ninth month, in the first year of the third week.

**12** But Rachel's womb was closed, for the Lord saw that Leah was hated and Rachel loved.

**13** Jacob went in to Leah again, and she conceived and bore Jacob a second son, and he called his name Simeon, on the twenty-first of the tenth month, in the third year of this week.

**14** Jacob went in to Leah again, and she conceived and bore him a third son, and he called his name Levi[, on the new moon of the first month, in the sixth year of this week].

**15** Once more he went in to her, and she bore a fourth son; and he called his name Judah, on the fifteenth of the third month, in the first year of the fourth week.

**16** In all this Rachel was jealous of Leah, for she did not bear; and she said to Jacob, "Give me children." Jacob said, "Have I withheld from you the fruit of your womb? Have I forsaken you?"

**17** When Rachel saw that Leah had borne Jacob four sons, Reuben and Simeon and Levi and Judah, Rachel said to Jacob, "Go in to Bilhah my maid, and she will conceive and bear a son for me."

**18** [She gave Bilhah her maid as a wife.] He went in, and she conceived and bore him a son; and she called his name Dan, on the ninth of the sixth month, in the sixth year of the third week.

**19** Jacob went in again to Bilhah, and she conceived and bore Jacob a second son, and Rachel called his name Naphtali, on the fifth of the seventh month, in the second year of the fourth week.

**20** When Leah saw that she had become barren and was not bearing, she too was jealous of Rachel, and she too gave Zilpah her maid to Jacob as a wife; and she conceived and bore a son, and Leah called his name Gad, on the twelfth of the eighth month, in the third year of the fourth week.

**21** He went in to her again, and she conceived and bore him a second son, and Leah called his name Asher, on the second of the eleventh month, in the fifth year of the fourth week.

**22** Jacob went in to Leah, and she conceived and bore Jacob a son, and he called his name Issachar, on the fourth of the fifth month, in the fourth year of the fourth week; and she gave him to a wet nurse.

**23** Jacob went in to her again, and she conceived and bore two, a son and a daughter, and she called the son's name Zebulun and the daughter's name Dinah[, on the seventh of the seventh month, in the sixth year of the fourth week].

**24** The Lord had mercy on Rachel and opened her womb; and she conceived and bore a son, and she called his name Joseph, on the first day of the fourth month, in the sixth year of this fourth week.

**25** In the days when Joseph was born, Jacob said to Laban, "Give me my wives and my sons, and let me go to my father Isaac and make a house for myself; for I have completed the years in which I served you for your two daughters, and I will go to my father's house."

**26** Laban said to Jacob, "Stay with me for your wages, and pasture my flock for me again, and take your wages."

**27** They agreed with each other that he would give him as his wages every one of the lambs and kids born dark, speckled, and marked with white; that would be his wages.

**28** All the flocks bore speckled young, all of them marked, spotted and dark and speckled; and again the flocks bore young like themselves; and all that were marked were Jacob's, and those without a mark were Laban's.

**29** Jacob's possessions grew very great, and he acquired cattle and sheep and donkeys and camels, and menservants and maidservants.

**30** Laban and his sons were jealous of Jacob, and Laban took back his sheep from him and watched him with evil intent.

## Notes

- **vv4–7, 11, 13, 15, 16, 17, 18, 22, 24** — the words the EOTC text as held here lacks are rendered from the Asmara printing of the Ge'ez (1962/63); the Latin, which covers this chapter from v16 to v27, has vv17–24 too. **Words in [brackets]** are in no Ge'ez text here and follow Charles's English: the opening of v18; the date of Levi's birth in v14, which the Asmara file skips; and the date of Zebulun and Dinah (v23). There the Asmara printing reads, like the Latin, *in the seventh month of the seventh year, in the sixth week of the forty-fourth jubilee* — 2149, after Joseph's birth and after Jacob leaves Laban (29:5) — so the date follows Charles and the book's own sequence. Without these verses the EOTC never names Judah or Dan, never tells of Joseph's birth, and gives only four of the birth dates.
- **vv1–10 Leah and Rachel** — Genesis 29:1–30: *Jacob lifted his feet and went to the land of the sons of the east… And it came to pass in the morning — and here, it was Leah… It is not done so in our place, to give the younger before the firstborn* (29:1, 25–26). **v5 "Leah's eyes were weak"** — Genesis 29:17 has *Leah's eyes were soft*. **vv6–7 the law of the elder daughter** — Laban's excuse becomes a law of the tablets of heaven, given to Israel through Moses.
- **vv11–24 the children** — Genesis 29:31–30:24. Jubilees dates every birth. In this book's reckoning the third week of the forty-fourth jubilee begins in 2122 and the fourth in 2129, so the twelve children are born in thirteen years: Reuben in 2122, Simeon in 2124, Levi and Dan in 2127, Judah in 2129, Naphtali in 2130, Gad in 2131, Issachar in 2132, Asher in 2133, and Zebulun, Dinah and Joseph in 2134. **v16 "Give me children"** — Genesis 30:1–2: *Give me sons — and if not, I am dead… Am I in the place of God, who has withheld from you the fruit of the womb?* **v23 "Zebulun… Dinah"** — born together here, as twins; Genesis 30:20–21 tells them one after the other.
- **vv25–30 the wages** — Genesis 30:25–43: *every speckled and spotted sheep, and every dark one among the lambs, and the spotted and speckled among the goats. That shall be my wages* (30:32). Jubilees leaves out Jacob's peeled rods (30:37–42): the flocks simply bear marked young. **v30 "watched him with evil intent"** — Genesis 31:2: *Jacob saw Laban's face, and here — it was not toward him as yesterday and the day before.*
