# Jubilees 37

**1** On the day Isaac, the father of Jacob and Esau, died, the sons of Esau heard that Isaac had given the elder's portion to Jacob his younger son[, and they were very indignant].

**2** They quarreled with their father, saying, "Why, when you are the elder and Jacob the younger, has your father given Jacob the elder's portion and passed you over?"

**3** He said to them, "Because I sold my birthright to Jacob for a little dish of lentils; and on the day my father sent me to hunt game for him, so that after eating he might bless me, he came with deceit and brought food and drink to my father, and my father blessed him and put me under his hand.

**4** Now our father has made us swear, me and him, that we will not seek evil, one against his brother, and that we will live in love and in peace, each with his brother, and not corrupt our ways."

**5** They said to him, "We will not listen to you and make peace with him, for our strength is greater than his strength, and we are stronger than he. We will go against him and kill him and destroy his sons; and if you will not go with us, we will do you harm too.

**6** Now listen to us: let us send to Aram and to Philistia and to Moab and to Ammon, and choose for ourselves chosen men who are eager for battle, and go against him and fight with him, and root him out of the earth before he grows strong."

**7** Their father said to them, "Do not go, and do not make war with him, or you will fall before him."

**8** They said to him, "This is just your way, from your youth until this day: you put your neck under his yoke. We will not listen to this word."

**9** They sent to Aram, and to Aduram, to their father's friend, and hired with them a thousand fighting men, chosen men of war.

**10** There came to them from Moab and from the sons of Ammon, hired, a thousand chosen men; and from Philistia a thousand chosen men of war; and from Edom and from the Horites a thousand chosen fighting men; and from the Kittim mighty men of war.

**11** They said to their father, "Go out and lead them, or else we will kill you."

**12** He was filled with wrath and anger when he saw that his sons were forcing him to go before them and lead them against Jacob his brother.

**13** But then he remembered all the evil that lay hidden in his heart against Jacob his brother, and he did not remember the oath he had sworn to his father and his mother, that he would seek no evil, all his days, against Jacob his brother.

**14** Through all this Jacob did not know that they were coming against him to battle; and he was mourning for Leah his wife, until they came very near to the tower with [four thousand] warriors, chosen men of war.

**15** The men of Hebron sent to him, saying, "Your brother has come against you to fight you with four thousand men girded with the sword, and they carry shields and weapons of war"; for they loved Jacob more than Esau. They told him, for Jacob was a more generous and merciful man than Esau.

**16** Jacob did not believe it until they came near the tower.

**17** He shut the gates of the tower; and he stood on the battlements and spoke to his brother Esau and said, "Noble is the comfort with which you have come to comfort me for my wife who has died! Is this the oath that you swore to your father, and again to your mother, before they died? You have broken the oath, and in the moment you swore it to your father you were condemned."

**18** Then Esau answered and said to him, "Neither the sons of men nor the beasts of the earth have an oath of righteousness that, once sworn, they keep forever; but every day they plan evil against one another, how each may kill his enemy and foe.

**19** You hate me and my sons forever, and there is no keeping brotherhood with you.

**20** Hear this word that I tell you: if the boar changes its skin and makes its bristles soft as wool, and if horns come out on its head like the horns of a stag and of sheep, then I will keep brotherhood with you. If the breasts have been parted from their mother, you have not been a brother to me.

**21** If the wolves make peace with the lambs, so as not to devour them or do them violence, and if their hearts are toward them for good, then there will be peace in my heart toward you.

**22** If the lion becomes the ox's yokefellow, and makes peace with him, and is bound under one yoke with him and plows with him, then I will make peace with you.

**23** If the ravens become white like the *raza*, then know that I have loved you, and I will make peace with you. You will be rooted out, and your sons will be rooted out, and there will be no peace for you."

**24** When Jacob saw that he meant evil against him from his heart and with all his soul, to kill him, and that he had come springing like the wild boar that comes on the spear that pierces and kills it and does not draw back from it,

**25** then he spoke to his own men and to his servants, to attack him and all his companions.

## Notes

- **vv3, 10, 14, 17, 18, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24** — the words the EOTC text as held here lacks are rendered from the Asmara printing of the Ge'ez (1962/63); the Latin, which covers this chapter as far as v5, has v3 too. **v20** — the last sentence is obscure in the Asmara printing and in Charles, an *if* whose sense is not clear. **Words in [brackets]** are in no Ge'ez text here: in v1 they follow the Latin; in v14 *four thousand* follows Charles and the sum of v10, four companies of a thousand, where the Asmara printing reads *four hundred*.
- **vv1–13 Esau's sons** — not in Genesis. The Greek tells it in brief: *after Isaac died, Esau, incited by his sons… having gathered nations, came against Jacob and his sons for war*. **v3 "for a little dish of lentils"** — Genesis 25:34; Hebrews 12:16–17: *that no one be… profane like Esau, who for a single meal sold his birthright. For you know that afterward, when he wanted to inherit the blessing, he was rejected.* **v13 "he did not remember the oath"** — the oath of 35:24 and 36:7–8.
- **vv17–23 at the tower** — the Greek: *Jacob, having shut the gates of the fortress, implored Esau to remember their parents' commandments. But since he would not yield, but was insolent and reproachful…* **vv20–23 the impossible things** — Esau's answer turns the prophets' pictures around. Jeremiah 13:23: *Can the Cushite change his skin, or the leopard his spots?* Isaiah 11:6: *The wolf shall live with the lamb*; Isaiah 65:25: *the lion shall eat straw like the ox*. What the prophets promise for the last days, Esau names as the day he will make peace. **v23 "the *raza*"** — so the Ge'ez; something white, but what is not known. Charles leaves the word untranslated.
