# Jubilees 48

**1** In the sixth year of the third week of the forty-ninth jubilee you went and lived there [in the land of Midian] five weeks and one year; and you returned to Egypt in the second week, in the second year, in the fiftieth jubilee.

**2** You know what He spoke to you on Mount Sinai, and what the prince Mastema wanted to do with you when you were returning to Egypt, on the way, at the lodging place.

**3** Did he not seek with all his strength to kill you, and to save the Egyptians from your hand, because he saw that you were sent to do judgment and vengeance on the Egyptians?

**4** I delivered you from his hand; and you performed the signs and wonders that you were sent to perform in Egypt, against Pharaoh, and against all his house, and against all his people.

**5** The Lord did great vengeance on them for Israel's sake, and struck them and killed them with blood and with frogs, with gnats and with dog-flies, and with evil sores breaking out in blisters; and their cattle with death; and with hailstones; with the hail He destroyed everything that grew for them, and the locust devoured what the hail had left; and with darkness; and with the death of their firstborn, of men and of animals; and on all the gods of the Egyptians the Lord took vengeance.

**6** Everything was sent through your hand, that you might declare it before it was done; and you spoke with the king of Egypt before all his servants and before his people.

**7** Everything happened by your word: ten great and terrible judgments came on the land of Egypt, so that you might take vengeance on it for Israel.

**8** The Lord did everything for Israel's sake, and according to His covenant which He had ordained with Abraham, that He would take vengeance on them as they had brought them into bondage by force.

**9** The prince Mastema stood up against you, and wanted to make you fall into Pharaoh's hands; and he helped the Egyptian sorcerers, and they stood up and worked before you.

**10** The evils we let them do; but the healings we did not allow to be done by their hands.

**11** The Lord struck them with evil sores, and they could not stand, for we destroyed them, so that they could not do a single sign.

**12** Through all the signs and wonders the prince Mastema was not put to shame, until he gathered strength and cried to the Egyptians to pursue you with all the power of the Egyptians, with their chariots and their horses, and with all the multitude of the peoples of Egypt.

**13** I stood between you and the Egyptians, between the Egyptians and Israel, and we delivered Israel from his hand and from the hand of the people; and the Lord brought them through the middle of the sea as if it were dry land.

**14** All the people he had brought out to pursue Israel, the Lord our God threw into the middle of the sea, into the depths of the abyss, beneath the sons of Israel. As the people of Egypt had thrown their children into the river, He took vengeance on a million of them; and a thousand strong and energetic men were destroyed for one nursing child of the children of your people whom they had thrown into the river.

**15** On the fourteenth day, and on the fifteenth, and on the sixteenth, and on the seventeenth, and on the eighteenth, the prince Mastema was bound and shut up behind the sons of Israel, so that he might not accuse them.

**16** On the nineteenth day we let them loose, that they might help the Egyptians and pursue the sons of Israel.

**17** He hardened their hearts and made them strong, and they were made strong by the Lord our God, so that He might strike the Egyptians and throw them into the middle of the sea.

**18** On the fourteenth day we bound him, so that he might not accuse the sons of Israel on the day when they asked the Egyptians for vessels and garments — vessels of silver and vessels of gold and vessels of bronze — to plunder the Egyptians in return for the servitude in which they had forced them to serve.

**19** We did not bring the sons of Israel out of Egypt empty-handed.

## Notes

- **vv4, 5, 8, 13, 14** — the words the EOTC text as held here lacks are rendered from the Asmara printing of the Ge'ez (1962/63); the Latin, which covers this chapter as far as v5, has vv4 and 5 too. Without the end of v5 the EOTC lists only six of the ten plagues. **Words in [brackets]** (v1) are in no Ge'ez text here; they follow the Latin.
- **v1 the dates** — Moses goes to Midian in the sixth year of the third week of the forty-ninth jubilee, 48 × 49 + 2 × 7 + 6 = 2372, the year he leaves the court (47:10). He returns in the second year of the second week of the fiftieth jubilee, 49 × 49 + 7 + 2 = 2410, at eighty, as Exodus 7:7 has him. *Five weeks and one year* is thirty-six years; the dates give thirty-eight; the Asmara printing reads *six weeks and one year*, forty-three. The text is left as it stands.
- **vv2–4 the lodging place** — Exodus 4:24: *on the road, at the lodging place, the LORD met him and sought to put him to death.* In Jubilees it is Mastema who seeks to kill Moses, and the angel who delivers him.
- **vv5–11 the ten plagues** — Exodus 7–12. **"on all the gods of the Egyptians"** — Exodus 12:12: *Against all the gods of Egypt I will carry out judgments.* The Greek sets the plagues month by month, from the Nile turned to blood in June to the firstborn in March. **v8** — the promise of Genesis 15:14 (14:13–14 in this book). **vv9–11 the sorcerers** — Exodus 7:11–8:19; 9:11: *The magicians could not stand before Moses because of the boils.* **v10 "The evils we let them do; but the healings we did not allow"** — the Egyptian sorcerers can bring plagues but cannot take them away.
- **vv12–14 the sea** — Exodus 14. **v13 "I stood between the Egyptians and Israel"** — Exodus 14:19–20: *The messenger of God who was going before the camp of Israel moved and went behind them… It came between the camp of Egypt and the camp of Israel.* **v14 "a thousand… for one nursing child"** — the Greek has the same: *a thousand mighty Egyptian men being drowned for every single Israelite infant*.
- **vv15–19 Mastema bound** — from the fourteenth of the first month, the day of the Passover, to the eighteenth, Mastema is bound so that he cannot accuse Israel; on the nineteenth he is let loose to drive the Egyptians into the sea. **v17 "their hearts"** — the EOTC has *ʾalbāsihomu*, "their garments"; the English follows Charles and the sense. **vv18–19 "vessels of silver and vessels of gold"** — Exodus 12:35–36; 3:21: *when you go, you will not go empty-handed.*
