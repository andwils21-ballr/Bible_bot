# Galatians 4

## Sons and Heirs

**1** What I am saying is this: as long as the heir is a child, he is no different from a slave, though he is the owner of everything,

**2** but he is under trustees and managers until the date set by his father.

**3** So with us: when we were children, we were enslaved under the elements of the world.

**4** But when the fullness of time had come, God sent forth His Son, born of a woman, born under the Law,

**5** to redeem those who were under the Law, so that we might receive adoption as sons.

**6** Because you are sons, God sent forth the Spirit of His Son into our hearts, crying, "Abba! Father!"

**7** So you are no longer a slave, but a son; and if a son, then an heir through God.

**8** Formerly, when you did not know God, you were slaves to those that by nature are not gods.

**9** But now that you have come to know God — or rather to be known by God — how can you turn back again to the weak and poor elements, and want to be slaves to them all over again?

**10** You observe days and months and seasons and years!

**11** I am afraid for you, that I may have labored over you in vain.

## Paul's Appeal

**12** Become as I am, for I also have become as you are, brothers, I beg you. You did me no wrong.

**13** You know that it was because of a weakness of the flesh that I first preached the gospel to you,

**14** and though my condition was a trial to you, you did not despise or spurn me, but received me as an angel of God, as Christ Jesus.

**15** Where then is the blessing you felt? For I testify to you that, if it had been possible, you would have torn out your eyes and given them to me.

**16** Have I then become your enemy by telling you the truth?

**17** They court you eagerly, but not for good; they want to shut you out, so that you will court them.

**18** It is good to be courted for a good purpose always, and not only when I am present with you,

**19** my children, for whom I am again in the pains of childbirth until Christ is formed in you!

**20** I wish I could be with you now and change my tone, for I am perplexed about you.

## Hagar and Sarah

**21** Tell me, you who want to be under the Law, do you not listen to the Law?

**22** For it is written that Abraham had two sons, one by the slave woman and one by the free woman.

**23** But the son of the slave woman was born according to the flesh, and the son of the free woman through the promise.

**24** These things are said as an allegory: these women are two covenants. One is from Mount Sinai, bearing children for slavery; she is Hagar.

**25** Now Hagar is Mount Sinai in Arabia; she corresponds to the present Jerusalem, for she is in slavery with her children.

**26** But the Jerusalem above is free, and she is our mother.

**27** For it is written:  
"Rejoice, barren one, who did not bear;  
break out and shout, you who were not in labor!  
For the children of the desolate one are more  
than the children of the one who has a husband."

**28** Now you, brothers, like Isaac, are children of the promise.

**29** But just as at that time the one born according to the flesh persecuted the one born according to the Spirit, so it is now also.

**30** But what does the Scripture say? "Drive out the slave woman and her son, for the son of the slave woman shall not inherit with the son of the free woman."

**31** So, brothers, we are not children of the slave woman, but of the free woman.

## Notes

- **vv3, 9 "the elements of the world… the weak and poor elements"** — *stoicheia*, things set in a row. The word is used of the first lessons, *the first principles of God's words* (Hebrews 5:12), and of what the world is made of, *the working of the elements* (Wisdom of Solomon 7:17; 2 Peter 3:10). Here they are things one is *enslaved under* (v3) and *slaves to* (v9), set beside *those that by nature are not gods* (v8). So the word can be read as the **first lessons** of a child under guardians (vv1–2), the **elements** of the world, or **powers** served as gods. *Elements* keeps all three, as the Greek does.
- **vv24–27 "these women are two covenants… the Jerusalem above… Rejoice, barren one"** — Isaiah 54:1 is spoken to the barren, desolate one the LORD calls back as a wife, *For your Maker is your husband* (54:5–6). Isaiah has already joined Zion to Sarah, *Look to Abraham your father, and to Sarah who bore you… For the LORD comforts Zion* (51:2–3), and in the Greek one verb runs through both, *ōdinō*, **to be in labor**: Sarah *who was in labor with you* (51:2), the barren one *who was not in labor* (54:1). Paul uses it of himself in v19, *I am again in the pains of childbirth*.
- **vv29–30 "persecuted… Drive out the slave woman"** — Genesis says that Sarah saw Hagar's son *laughing* (21:9), *metsacheq*, from the root of Isaac's name; the Greek has *playing with Isaac her son*. The words Paul calls *the Scripture* are Sarah's (21:10), with *the son of the free woman* where she said *my son, with Isaac*, and God told Abraham, *All that Sarah says to you — listen to her voice* (21:12).
