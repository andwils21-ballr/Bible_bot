# Numbers 19

## The Ashes of the Heifer

**1** The LORD spoke to Moses and to Aaron, saying,

**2** "This is the statute of the instruction that the LORD has commanded: Tell the sons of Israel to bring you a red heifer without defect, with no blemish on her, on which a yoke has never come.

**3** You shall give her to Eleazar the priest, and she shall be taken outside the camp and slaughtered in front of him.

**4** Eleazar the priest shall take some of her blood with his finger and sprinkle some of her blood seven times toward the front of the tent of meeting.

**5** The heifer shall be burned in his sight. Her skin, her meat and her blood, with her dung, shall be burned.

**6** The priest shall take cedar wood and hyssop and scarlet yarn and throw them into the fire where the heifer is burning.

**7** Then the priest shall wash his clothes and bathe his body in water, and afterward he may come into the camp, but the priest shall be unclean until evening.

**8** The one who burns her shall wash his clothes in water and bathe his body in water, and he shall be unclean until evening.

**9** A man who is clean shall gather up the ashes of the heifer and put them outside the camp in a clean place. They shall be kept for the congregation of the sons of Israel for the water for impurity. It is a sin offering.

**10** The one who gathers the ashes of the heifer shall wash his clothes, and he shall be unclean until evening. It shall be a lasting statute for the sons of Israel and for the guest who lives among them.

## Touching the Dead

**11** Whoever touches the dead body of any person shall be unclean seven days.

**12** He shall purify himself with the water on the third day and on the seventh day, and then he will be clean. But if he does not purify himself on the third day and on the seventh day, he will not be clean.

**13** Whoever touches a dead person, the body of anyone who has died, and does not purify himself, makes the dwelling of the LORD unclean, and that person shall be cut off from Israel. Because the water for impurity was not thrown on him, he shall be unclean. His uncleanness is still on him.

**14** This is the instruction when a man dies in a tent: everyone who comes into the tent and everyone who is in the tent shall be unclean seven days.

**15** Every open vessel with no lid fastened on it is unclean.

**16** Whoever in the open field touches someone killed by the sword, or someone who has died, or a human bone, or a grave, shall be unclean seven days.

**17** For the unclean person they shall take some of the ashes of the burned sin offering, and fresh running water shall be put on them in a vessel.

**18** A man who is clean shall take hyssop and dip it in the water and sprinkle it on the tent and on all the vessels and on the people who were there, and on the one who touched the bone, or the one killed, or the one who died, or the grave.

**19** The clean man shall sprinkle the unclean on the third day and on the seventh day. On the seventh day he shall purify him, and he shall wash his clothes and bathe in water, and at evening he will be clean.

**20** But the man who becomes unclean and does not purify himself, that person shall be cut off from among the assembly, because he has made the sanctuary of the LORD unclean. The water for impurity has not been thrown on him. He is unclean.

**21** It shall be a lasting statute for them. The one who sprinkles the water for impurity shall wash his clothes, and the one who touches the water for impurity shall be unclean until evening.

**22** Anything the unclean person touches shall be unclean, and the person who touches it shall be unclean until evening."

## Notes

- **v5 "her skin, her meat and her blood, with her dung"** — everything. For the sin offering of the priest and of the congregation, the skin, meat and dung were burned outside the camp, but the blood was put on the altar (Leviticus 4:7, 4:11–12). Here even the blood goes into the fire, except the seven sprinklings of v4. The ashes carry what the blood carried.
- **v6 "cedar wood and hyssop and scarlet yarn"** — the three things used to cleanse a person healed of blight (Leviticus 14:4). There they are dipped in the blood of a bird. Here they are burned with the heifer.
- **v7 "the priest shall be unclean until evening"** — the ashes make the unclean clean, and everyone who handles them becomes unclean: the priest (v7), the man who burns the heifer (v8), the man who gathers the ashes (v10), and the one who sprinkles the water (v21).
- **v9 "the water for impurity"** — *mei niddah*. *Niddah* is the word for a woman's *monthly separation* in Leviticus 15:19, and for *an impurity* in Leviticus 20:21: the water is for removing that state. The Greek calls it *water of sprinkling*, *hydōr rhantismou*, and that phrase is behind the letter to the Hebrews: *the ashes of a heifer sprinkling those who are unclean* make them holy *for the cleansing of the flesh* (Hebrews 9:13).
- **v9 "it is a sin offering"** — *chattat hi*, though it is never put on the altar and is burned outside the camp. **v12 "he shall purify himself"** is the verb from the same root, *yitchatta*, literally **he shall un-sin himself**. Touching the dead is not a sin, and the text uses the vocabulary of sin for removing it anyway.
- **v13 "makes the dwelling of the LORD unclean"** — the unclean person does not have to go near the dwelling. Death's uncleanness left on anyone in the camp reaches the place where the LORD lives among them, the reason the unclean were sent outside the camp in 5:3.
