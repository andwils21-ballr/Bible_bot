# Numbers 22

## Balak Sends for Balaam

**1** The sons of Israel set out and camped in the plains of Moab, across the Jordan from Jericho.

**2** Balak son of Zippor saw all that Israel had done to the Amorites.

**3** Moab was very afraid of the people, because they were many. Moab dreaded the sons of Israel.

**4** Moab said to the elders of Midian, "Now this company will lick up everything around us, as the ox licks up the green of the field." Balak son of Zippor was king of Moab at that time.

**5** He sent messengers to Balaam son of Beor at Pethor, which is by the River, in the land of the sons of his people, to call him, saying, "Here — a people has come out of Egypt. Here — they have covered the eye of the land, and they are living opposite me.

**6** And now please come and curse this people for me, for they are too mighty for me. Perhaps I will be able to strike them and drive them out of the land. For I know that whoever you bless is blessed, and whoever you curse is cursed."

**7** The elders of Moab and the elders of Midian went with the fees for soothsaying in their hand. They came to Balaam and spoke Balak's words to him.

**8** He said to them, "Spend the night here, and I will bring you back word, as the LORD speaks to me." So the chiefs of Moab stayed with Balaam.

**9** God came to Balaam and said, "Who are these men with you?"

**10** Balaam said to God, "Balak son of Zippor, king of Moab, has sent to me:

**11** 'Here — the people that has come out of Egypt has covered the eye of the land. Now come, put a curse on them for me. Perhaps I will be able to fight against them and drive them out.'"

**12** God said to Balaam, "You shall not go with them. You shall not curse the people, for they are blessed."

**13** Balaam rose in the morning and said to Balak's chiefs, "Go to your own land, for the LORD has refused to let me go with you."

**14** So the chiefs of Moab rose and went to Balak and said, "Balaam refused to come with us."

## The Second Embassy

**15** Balak sent chiefs again, more of them and more honored than these.

**16** They came to Balaam and said to him, "Thus says Balak son of Zippor: Please let nothing keep you from coming to me,

**17** for I will honor you greatly, and whatever you tell me I will do. Please come, put a curse on this people for me."

**18** Balaam answered and said to Balak's servants, "If Balak were to give me his house full of silver and gold, I could not go beyond the command of the LORD my God, to do anything small or great.

**19** And now please stay here tonight, you also, so that I may know what more the LORD will say to me."

**20** God came to Balaam at night and said to him, "If the men have come to call you, rise, go with them. But you shall do only what I tell you."

**21** So Balaam rose in the morning and saddled his donkey and went with the chiefs of Moab.

## The Donkey

**22** But God's anger burned because he was going, and the angel of the LORD took his stand in the road as an adversary against him. He was riding on his donkey, and his two young men were with him.

**23** The donkey saw the angel of the LORD standing in the road with his sword drawn in his hand, and the donkey turned off the road and went into the field. Balaam struck the donkey to turn her back onto the road.

**24** Then the angel of the LORD stood in a narrow path between the vineyards, with a wall on this side and a wall on that side.

**25** The donkey saw the angel of the LORD and pressed herself against the wall, and pressed Balaam's foot against the wall, and he struck her again.

**26** The angel of the LORD went on ahead and stood in a narrow place where there was no way to turn to the right or to the left.

**27** The donkey saw the angel of the LORD and lay down under Balaam. Balaam's anger burned, and he struck the donkey with his stick.

**28** The LORD opened the donkey's mouth, and she said to Balaam, "What have I done to you, that you have struck me these three times?"

**29** Balaam said to the donkey, "Because you have made a fool of me! If only there were a sword in my hand, I would kill you now."

**30** The donkey said to Balaam, "Am I not your donkey, whom you have ridden all your life to this day? Have I ever been in the habit of doing this to you?" He said, "No."

**31** Then the LORD uncovered Balaam's eyes, and he saw the angel of the LORD standing in the road with his sword drawn in his hand. He bowed and fell on his face.

**32** The angel of the LORD said to him, "Why have you struck your donkey these three times? Here — I have come out as an adversary, because your way is headlong before me.

**33** The donkey saw me and turned aside before me these three times. If she had not turned aside from me, I would surely have killed you by now, and let her live."

**34** Balaam said to the angel of the LORD, "I have sinned, for I did not know that you were standing in the road against me. And now, if it is displeasing to you, I will go back."

**35** The angel of the LORD said to Balaam, "Go with the men, but you shall speak only the word that I speak to you." So Balaam went with the chiefs of Balak.

## Balak Meets Balaam

**36** When Balak heard that Balaam had come, he went out to meet him at the town of Moab on the border of the Arnon, at the edge of the border.

**37** Balak said to Balaam, "Did I not send to you to call you? Why did you not come to me? Am I really not able to honor you?"

**38** Balaam said to Balak, "Here — I have come to you. Now, am I able to speak anything at all? I shall speak the word that God puts in my mouth."

**39** Balaam went with Balak, and they came to Kiriath-huzoth.

**40** Balak sacrificed cattle and sheep, and sent some to Balaam and to the chiefs who were with him.

**41** And it came to pass in the morning that Balak took Balaam and brought him up to Bamoth-baal, and from there he saw the edge of the people.

## Notes

- **v3 "Moab dreaded the sons of Israel"** — *va-yaqots… mipnei benei Yisra'el*, the words of Exodus 1:12, where Egypt *dreaded the sons of Israel* because they kept multiplying. The king of Moab opens this story where Pharaoh opened Exodus.
- **v5 "they have covered the eye of the land"** — *kissah et ein ha-arets*, the phrase of the locusts in Exodus 10:5. Balak sees Israel as a swarm, and v4 says it plainly: they will *lick up everything around us*.
- **v5 "the land of the sons of his people"** — that is, Balaam's own country. The Latin reads *the land of the sons of Ammon*, a difference of one letter (*ammo*, his people; *Ammon*). *The River*, with no name, is the Euphrates, as in Genesis 31:21.
- **vv6, 11 two words for curse** — Balak's message says *arah*, from *arar*, the word of Genesis 12:3, *the one who treats you lightly I will curse*. When Balaam repeats the message to God he uses *qavah*, from *qavav*, a rarer word for cursing, and Balak uses it himself in v17. Balak's boast, *whoever you bless is blessed, and whoever you curse is cursed*, turns around the blessing Isaac gave Jacob: *Those who curse you, cursed; and those who bless you, blessed* (Genesis 27:29).
- **v22 "as an adversary against him"** — *le-satan lo*. *Satan* is the common noun for an opponent or accuser (see Genesis 26:21), and here it is the angel of the LORD who is Balaam's *satan*. The Greek has *diaballein*, **to oppose**, from which comes *diabolos*, the devil.
- **v28 "these three times"** — *shalosh regalim*, literally **three feet**, the unusual phrase used for Israel's three pilgrim festivals (Exodus 23:14). It comes three times in the chapter (vv28, 32, 33). Balak will say *these three times* to Balaam in 24:10, after Balaam has blessed Israel three times instead of cursing it.
- **v29 "you have made a fool of me"** — *hit'allalt bi*, the verb the LORD uses of what He did to Egypt (Exodus 10:2). The seer who sees nothing is outseen by his donkey, three times.
- **v32 "your way is headlong"** — *yarat*, a rare verb. In its one other clear use, Job says God *throws me into the hands of the wicked* (Job 16:11). The meaning here is uncertain; *reckless*, *perverse* and *headlong* all fit the context. The Greek has *your way is not seemly before me*.
- **v35 "only the word that I speak to you"** — the command of v20, repeated with *speak* where v20 had *do*. The difference is what Balaam now knows: the road is open to him only on those terms. The New Testament remembers him for the fees of v7, *Balaam son of Beor, who loved the wages of wrongdoing*, rebuked by *a speechless beast speaking with a man's voice* (2 Peter 2:15–16).
