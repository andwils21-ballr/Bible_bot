# Numbers 23

## The First Saying

**1** Balaam said to Balak, "Build me seven altars here, and prepare me seven bulls and seven rams here."

**2** Balak did as Balaam had said, and Balak and Balaam offered a bull and a ram on each altar.

**3** Balaam said to Balak, "Stand beside your burnt offering, and I will go. Perhaps the LORD will come to meet me, and whatever He shows me I will tell you." Then he went to a bare height.

**4** God met Balaam, and Balaam said to Him, "I have set up the seven altars, and I have offered a bull and a ram on each altar."

**5** The LORD put a word in Balaam's mouth and said, "Go back to Balak, and this is what you shall say."

**6** So he went back to him, and here — he was standing beside his burnt offering, he and all the chiefs of Moab.

**7** He took up his saying and said:  
"From Aram Balak has brought me,  
the king of Moab from the mountains of the east:  
'Come, curse Jacob for me,  
come, denounce Israel!'

**8** How can I put a curse on one God has not cursed?  
How can I denounce one the LORD has not denounced?

**9** For from the top of the rocks I see him,  
from the hills I watch him.  
Here is a people that lives alone,  
and does not count itself among the nations.

**10** Who can count the dust of Jacob,  
or number the fourth part of Israel?  
Let me die the death of the upright,  
and let my end be like his!"

**11** Balak said to Balaam, "What have you done to me? I took you to curse my enemies, and here — you have done nothing but bless them!"

**12** He answered and said, "Must I not take care to speak what the LORD puts in my mouth?"

## The Second Saying

**13** Balak said to him, "Please come with me to another place, where you will see them. You will see only the edge of them; you will not see all of them. Curse them for me from there."

**14** So he took him to the field of Zophim, to the top of Pisgah, and built seven altars and offered a bull and a ram on each altar.

**15** Balaam said to Balak, "Stand here beside your burnt offering, while I meet the LORD over there."

**16** The LORD met Balaam and put a word in his mouth, and said, "Go back to Balak, and this is what you shall say."

**17** He came to him, and here — he was standing beside his burnt offering, with the chiefs of Moab. Balak said to him, "What has the LORD spoken?"

**18** He took up his saying and said:  
"Rise, Balak, and hear;  
listen to me, son of Zippor.

**19** God is not a man, that He should lie,  
or a son of man, that He should change His mind.  
Has He said, and will He not do it?  
Has He spoken, and will He not make it stand?

**20** Here — I have received a command to bless.  
He has blessed, and I cannot turn it back.

**21** He has not looked on wrongdoing in Jacob,  
or seen trouble in Israel.  
The LORD his God is with him,  
and the shout of a king is among them.

**22** God brings them out of Egypt;  
he has the horns of the wild ox.

**23** For there is no divining in Jacob,  
no soothsaying in Israel.  
Now it shall be said of Jacob and of Israel,  
'What God has done!'

**24** Here is a people that rises like a lioness,  
and lifts itself up like a lion.  
It does not lie down until it eats the prey  
and drinks the blood of the slain."

**25** Balak said to Balaam, "Do not curse them at all, and do not bless them at all!"

**26** Balaam answered and said to Balak, "Did I not tell you, 'Everything the LORD speaks, that I must do'?"

## To Peor

**27** Balak said to Balaam, "Please come, I will take you to another place. Perhaps it will be right in the eyes of God that you curse them for me from there."

**28** So Balak took Balaam to the top of Peor, which looks down on the wasteland.

**29** Balaam said to Balak, "Build me seven altars here, and prepare me seven bulls and seven rams here."

**30** Balak did as Balaam had said, and offered a bull and a ram on each altar.

## Notes

- **v3 "a bare height"** — *shefi*, a word of uncertain meaning, used of the bare hills in Isaiah 41:18 and Jeremiah 3:2. The Greek reads it differently: *Balaam went to inquire of God, and went straight*.
- **vv4, 16 "God met Balaam"** — *va-yiqqar*, from *qarah*, **to happen upon, to meet by chance**. Balaam expected to go out and find the LORD (v3). Each time, the text says God came to him.
- **v7 "his saying"** — *meshalo*, his *mashal*, the word for the song of Heshbon's singers (21:27): a saying, a proverb, a poem with a point. The Greek has *parabolē*, **parable**. The saying is poetry, and the story around it is prose.
- **vv7–8 "curse… denounce"** — three words for cursing in two verses. Balak asked for *arah* and *za'amah*, **curse** and **denounce**, and the answer uses *qavav*, the word of 22:11, with *za'am*, the word for God's indignation: *How can I… whom the LORD has not denounced?*
- **v9 "a people that lives alone"** — *am le-vadad yishkon*. Moses' blessing ends on the same two words, *shakhan*, **live**, and *badad*, **alone**: *Israel lives in safety, alone* (Deuteronomy 33:28). *Does not count itself among the nations* is *lo yitchashav*, it does not reckon itself one of them.
- **v10 "the dust of Jacob… the fourth part of Israel"** — the dust is the promise of Genesis 13:16 and 28:14, *your seed shall be as the dust of the earth*, which *if a man could count*, could be counted. *Rova* is usually taken as **a fourth part**, but the word is uncertain here. The Greek has *the clans of Israel*.
- **v10 "let me die the death of the upright"** — *yesharim*. Balaam wishes for Israel's end, and the text records his: he is killed with the sword among the kings of Midian (31:8).
- **v19 "God is not a man, that He should lie"** — Samuel says the same of God when he tells Saul the kingdom has been torn from him: *He is not a man, that He should change His mind* (1 Samuel 15:29).
- **v22 "the horns of the wild ox"** — *ke-to'afot re'em*. *Re'em* is the wild ox, and *to'afot* is uncertain: **horns**, **peaks**, or **strength**. The Greek renders *re'em* with *monokerōs*, **one-horn**, and from there the older English versions have *unicorn*.
- **v23 "no divining in Jacob"** — *lo nachash be-Ya'aqov*. The preposition *be-* means both *in* and *against*: there is no divining **in** Jacob, or no divining will work **against** Jacob. Both fit the story, where a diviner has been hired against Israel. *Nachash* is the practice forbidden in Leviticus 19:26, and *qesem* is what the elders of Moab brought fees for (22:7).
