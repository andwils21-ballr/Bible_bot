# Numbers 11

## Taberah

**1** The people became complainers, and it was evil in the hearing of the LORD. The LORD heard, and His anger burned, and the fire of the LORD burned among them and consumed some at the edge of the camp.

**2** The people cried out to Moses, and Moses prayed to the LORD, and the fire died down.

**3** The name of that place was called Taberah, because the fire of the LORD had burned among them.

## Who Will Give Us Meat?

**4** The rabble among them felt a craving, and the sons of Israel also wept again and said, "Who will give us meat to eat?

**5** We remember the fish we ate in Egypt for nothing, the cucumbers, the melons, the leeks, the onions and the garlic.

**6** But now our throat is dried up. There is nothing at all — nothing but this manna to look at."

**7** Now the manna was like coriander seed, and it looked like bdellium.

**8** The people went about and gathered it, and ground it with millstones or pounded it in a mortar, and boiled it in pots and made cakes of it. It tasted like cakes made with rich oil.

**9** When the dew fell on the camp at night, the manna fell with it.

## Moses Cannot Carry Them

**10** Moses heard the people weeping, family by family, each man at the entrance of his tent. The anger of the LORD burned hot, and in Moses' eyes it was evil.

**11** Moses said to the LORD, "Why have You brought trouble on Your servant? Why have I not found favor in Your eyes, that You lay the burden of all this people on me?

**12** Did I conceive all this people? Did I give birth to them, that You should say to me, 'Carry them in your arms, as a nurse carries a nursing child,' to the land You swore to their fathers?

**13** Where am I to get meat to give all this people? For they weep before me, saying, 'Give us meat, and let us eat.'

**14** I cannot carry all this people by myself. It is too heavy for me.

**15** If this is how You will treat me, kill me now, if I have found favor in Your eyes, and let me not see my misery."

## Seventy Elders

**16** The LORD said to Moses, "Gather for Me seventy men of the elders of Israel, whom you know to be elders of the people and its officers, and take them to the tent of meeting, and let them stand there with you.

**17** I will come down and speak with you there, and I will take some of the spirit that is on you and put it on them. They shall carry the burden of the people with you, so that you do not carry it by yourself.

**18** Say to the people: Set yourselves apart for tomorrow, and you shall eat meat. For you have wept in the hearing of the LORD, saying, 'Who will give us meat to eat? For it was good for us in Egypt.' So the LORD will give you meat, and you shall eat.

**19** You shall eat it not one day, or two days, or five days, or ten days, or twenty days,

**20** but a whole month, until it comes out of your nostrils and becomes disgusting to you — because you have rejected the LORD who is among you, and have wept before Him, saying, 'Why did we ever come out of Egypt?'"

**21** Moses said, "The people I am among number six hundred thousand on foot, and You have said, 'I will give them meat, and they shall eat for a whole month.'

**22** If flocks and herds were slaughtered for them, would it be enough for them? If all the fish of the sea were gathered for them, would it be enough for them?"

**23** The LORD said to Moses, "Is the LORD's hand too short? Now you will see whether My word comes true for you or not."

**24** So Moses went out and told the people the words of the LORD. He gathered seventy men of the elders of the people and stood them around the tent.

**25** The LORD came down in the cloud and spoke to him, and took some of the spirit that was on him and put it on the seventy elders. When the spirit rested on them, they prophesied; but they did not do so again.

## Eldad and Medad

**26** But two men had stayed in the camp, one named Eldad and the other named Medad, and the spirit rested on them. They were among those written down, but they had not gone out to the tent, and they prophesied in the camp.

**27** A young man ran and told Moses, "Eldad and Medad are prophesying in the camp!"

**28** Joshua son of Nun, Moses' assistant from his youth, answered and said, "My lord Moses, stop them!"

**29** But Moses said to him, "Are you jealous for my sake? If only all the LORD's people were prophets, and the LORD would put His spirit on them!"

**30** Moses went back to the camp, he and the elders of Israel.

## The Quail

**31** A wind went out from the LORD and swept quail in from the sea and let them fall beside the camp, about a day's journey on this side and a day's journey on that side, all around the camp, about two cubits above the ground.

**32** The people rose up all that day and all night and all the next day and gathered the quail. The one who gathered least gathered ten homers. They spread them out all around the camp.

**33** The meat was still between their teeth, before it was chewed, when the anger of the LORD burned against the people, and the LORD struck the people with a very great plague.

**34** The name of that place was called Kibroth-hattaavah, because there they buried the people who had the craving.

**35** From Kibroth-hattaavah the people set out for Hazeroth, and they stayed at Hazeroth.

## Notes

- **v1 "became complainers"** — *ke-mit'onenim*, a form that occurs here and nowhere else in the Bible, and it comes with the little word *ke*, **like**: the people were *like* complainers, or acted as complainers. The text does not say what they complained about. It says only that it was *evil in the hearing of the LORD*.
- **v4 "the rabble"** — *ha-asafsuf*, another word found only here. It is built from *asaf*, to gather, doubled: the gathered-up crowd, the ones who were swept along. Exodus 12:38 used a different phrase, *erev rav*, for the *great mixed crowd* that came out with Israel. The rabble start the craving, and *the sons of Israel also* take it up.
- **vv4, 34 "felt a craving… the people who had the craving"** — *hit'avu ta'avah*, they craved a craving, and the place is named for it: *Kibroth-hattaavah*, **the Graves of Craving**. The word that began the chapter's trouble becomes the name on its graves.
- **v6 "our throat is dried up"** — *nafshenu yeveshah*. *Nefesh* is the throat and the appetite before it is the soul, and here the plain sense is right on the surface: the throat is dry, and nothing to swallow but manna.
- **v7 "like bdellium"** — *bedolach*, named with gold and onyx in the land of Havilah, by the river of Eden (Genesis 2:12). Exodus 16:31 said the manna was white and tasted like wafers with honey. Here it tastes like cakes made with oil.
- **v12 "as a nurse carries a nursing child"** — Moses pictures himself as the mother, *did I conceive… did I give birth*, and then as the one who carries the baby. *Omen*, **nurse**, is a masculine noun, a man who carries and raises a child: *kings shall be your nurses* (Isaiah 49:23).
- **v15 "if this is how You will treat me"** — the Hebrew pronoun is *at*, **you**, in its feminine form, with a masculine verb beside it. The text does not explain it.
- **vv17, 25–29, 31 "spirit… wind"** — *ruach*, one word for both. God takes some of the *ruach* on Moses and puts it on the seventy (v25); it rests on Eldad and Medad (v26); Moses wishes God would put His *ruach* on all the people (v29). Then a *ruach* goes out from the LORD and brings the quail (v31). The same word carries the gift and the judgment.
- **v23 "Is the LORD's hand too short?"** — the question Isaiah answers twice: *the LORD's hand is not too short to save* (59:1, and 50:2).
- **v28 "Moses' assistant from his youth"** — *mi-bechurav*, which can mean **from his youth** or **one of his chosen men**. The Greek takes the second: *the chosen one*.
- **v31 "about two cubits above the ground"** — about three feet. The Hebrew does not say whether the quail flew that low, or lay that deep. The Greek has the same words, *about two cubits from the ground*.
