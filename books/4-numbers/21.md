# Numbers 21

## Hormah

**1** The Canaanite, the king of Arad, who lived in the Negev, heard that Israel was coming by the road to Atharim. He fought against Israel and took some of them captive.

**2** Israel made a vow to the LORD and said, "If You will indeed give this people into my hand, I will devote their towns to destruction."

**3** The LORD listened to the voice of Israel and gave up the Canaanites, and they devoted them and their towns to destruction. So the name of the place was called Hormah.

## The Bronze Serpent

**4** They set out from Mount Hor by the way of the Sea of Reeds, to go around the land of Edom, and the people grew impatient on the way.

**5** The people spoke against God and against Moses: "Why have you brought us up out of Egypt to die in the wilderness? There is no bread and no water, and we are sick of this miserable bread."

**6** So the LORD sent the fiery serpents among the people, and they bit the people, and many people of Israel died.

**7** The people came to Moses and said, "We have sinned, because we have spoken against the LORD and against you. Pray to the LORD, so that He takes the serpents away from us." So Moses prayed for the people.

**8** The LORD said to Moses, "Make a fiery serpent and set it on a pole. Everyone who is bitten, when he sees it, shall live."

**9** So Moses made a bronze serpent and set it on the pole. Whenever a serpent bit a man, he would look to the bronze serpent and live.

## The Journey to Moab

**10** The sons of Israel set out and camped at Oboth.

**11** They set out from Oboth and camped at Iye-abarim, in the wilderness facing Moab, toward the sunrise.

**12** From there they set out and camped in the valley of Zered.

**13** From there they set out and camped on the other side of the Arnon, which is in the wilderness that extends from the border of the Amorites. The Arnon is the border of Moab, between Moab and the Amorites.

**14** That is why it is said in the Book of the Wars of the LORD:  
"Waheb in Suphah,  
and the valleys of the Arnon,

**15** and the slope of the valleys  
that turns toward the dwelling of Ar  
and leans on the border of Moab."

**16** From there they went on to Beer. That is the well where the LORD said to Moses, "Gather the people, and I will give them water."

**17** Then Israel sang this song:  
"Spring up, O well! Sing to it —

**18** the well the princes dug,  
the nobles of the people carved it out,  
with the scepter, with their staffs."  
From the wilderness they went on to Mattanah,

**19** from Mattanah to Nahaliel, from Nahaliel to Bamoth,

**20** and from Bamoth to the valley in the country of Moab, at the top of Pisgah, which looks down on the wasteland.

## Sihon and Og

**21** Israel sent messengers to Sihon king of the Amorites, saying,

**22** "Let me pass through your land. We will not turn aside into field or vineyard. We will not drink water from a well. We will go by the King's Highway until we have passed through your border."

**23** But Sihon would not let Israel pass through his border. Sihon gathered all his people and went out against Israel into the wilderness, and came to Jahaz and fought against Israel.

**24** Israel struck him with the sword and took possession of his land from the Arnon to the Jabbok, as far as the Ammonites, for the border of the Ammonites was strong.

**25** Israel took all these towns, and Israel lived in all the towns of the Amorites, in Heshbon and all its villages.

**26** For Heshbon was the town of Sihon king of the Amorites. He had fought against the former king of Moab and taken all his land out of his hand, as far as the Arnon.

**27** That is why the singers of sayings say:  
"Come to Heshbon, let it be built!  
Let the town of Sihon be set up!

**28** For fire went out from Heshbon,  
a flame from the town of Sihon.  
It consumed Ar of Moab,  
the lords of the heights of the Arnon.

**29** Woe to you, Moab!  
You are lost, people of Chemosh!  
He has given up his sons as fugitives,  
and his daughters into captivity,  
to Sihon king of the Amorites.

**30** We shot at them; Heshbon is lost, as far as Dibon.  
We laid waste as far as Nophah,  
which reaches to Medeba."

**31** So Israel lived in the land of the Amorites.

**32** Moses sent men to scout out Jazer, and they took its villages and drove out the Amorites who were there.

**33** Then they turned and went up by the road to Bashan, and Og king of Bashan came out against them, he and all his people, to battle at Edrei.

**34** The LORD said to Moses, "Do not be afraid of him, for I have given him into your hand, with all his people and his land. You shall do to him as you did to Sihon king of the Amorites, who lived in Heshbon."

**35** So they struck him and his sons and all his people, until no survivor was left to him, and they took possession of his land.

## Notes

- **v1 "the road to Atharim"** — *derekh ha-atarim*, which the Greek takes as a place name, *Atharin*. Read with one letter's difference it would be *the road of the scouts*, *ha-tarim*, the word for the men who *scouted* the land (13:2, 13:25), and that is how some English versions render it.
- **v3 "Hormah"** — from *charam*, **devote to destruction**, the verb of vv2 and 3: to hand over to the LORD by destroying, with nothing kept as plunder. It is the place where Israel, going up against the LORD's word, was beaten *as far as Hormah* (14:45). Now, going up with a vow to the LORD, they win there.
- **v6 "the fiery serpents"** — *ha-nechashim ha-serafim*, from *saraf*, **to burn**, most likely for the burning of the bite. The same word, *serafim*, names the six-winged beings above the LORD's throne in Isaiah 6:2. The Greek has *the deadly serpents*.
- **vv8–9 "a fiery serpent… a bronze serpent… on the pole"** — *nechash nechoshet*, **a serpent of bronze**, two words from the same three letters. The pole is *nes*, the rallying signal of Exodus 17:15, *The LORD Is My Banner*. The people are healed by looking at an image of what bit them. Hezekiah later broke it in pieces, *for until those days the sons of Israel had been burning incense to it*, and it was called *Nehushtan* (2 Kings 18:4). Jesus says, *as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of Man be lifted up* (John 3:14).
- **vv14–15 the Book of the Wars of the LORD** — a book quoted here and nowhere else, and lost. The quotation begins in the middle of a sentence, *et Vahev be-Sufah*, with an object marker and no verb, and *Waheb* and *Suphah* appear nowhere else. They are probably places. The Greek reads a verb into it: *he set Zoob on fire*.
- **vv17–18 the song of the well** — *Beer* means **well**, and *Mattanah*, the next stop, means **gift**, from *natan*, the verb of the LORD's promise at the well: *I will give them water* (v16).
- **v27 "the singers of sayings"** — *ha-moshlim*, those who speak a *mashal*, a saying, proverb or taunt song. The same word names Balaam's oracles in the next chapters (23:7). The song of Heshbon is used again in Jeremiah, against Moab: *fire went out from Heshbon… Woe to you, Moab! The people of Chemosh are lost* (Jeremiah 48:45–46).
- **v30 "We laid waste as far as Nophah, which reaches to Medeba"** — the Hebrew scribes put a dot over one letter of *asher*, **which**, marking it as doubtful. Without that letter the word is *esh*, **fire**, and the Greek reads it so, with *nashim*, **women**, for *we laid waste* (the same consonants): *and the women kindled fire on Moab*. The text here follows the Hebrew as written.
- **v32 "drove out"** — the written text has *vayyirash*, **took possession**, and the reading tradition has *vayyoresh*, **drove out**. The text follows the reading.
