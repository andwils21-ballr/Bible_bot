# Leviticus 21

## A Priest and the Dead

**1** The LORD said to Moses, "Speak to the priests, the sons of Aaron, and say to them: None of them shall make himself unclean for a dead person among his people,

**2** except for his own flesh closest to him — for his mother and for his father, and for his son and for his daughter and for his brother,

**3** and for his unmarried sister, who is close to him because she has had no husband. For her he may make himself unclean.

**4** He shall not make himself unclean as a husband among his people, profaning himself.

**5** They shall not make a bald patch on their heads, and they shall not shave off the edge of their beards, and they shall not cut a gash in their flesh.

**6** They shall be holy to their God, and they shall not profane the name of their God, because they present the LORD's offerings by fire, the bread of their God. They shall be holy.

**7** They shall not take a woman who is a prostitute or who has been disgraced, and they shall not take a woman divorced from her husband, because he is holy to his God.

**8** You shall treat him as holy, because he presents the bread of your God. He shall be holy to you, because I the LORD, who makes you holy, am holy.

**9** A priest's daughter, if she profanes herself by whoring, profanes her father. She shall be burned in the fire.

## The High Priest

**10** The priest who is greatest among his brothers, on whose head the anointing oil was poured and whose hand was filled to wear the garments, shall not let his hair hang loose and shall not tear his clothes.

**11** He shall not go near any dead body. He shall not make himself unclean even for his father or for his mother.

**12** He shall not go out of the sanctuary and profane the sanctuary of his God, because the crown of the anointing oil of his God is on him. I am the LORD.

**13** He shall take as his wife a woman who is a virgin.

**14** A widow, or a divorced woman, or a woman disgraced by prostitution — these he shall not take. He shall take a virgin from his own people as a wife,

**15** and he shall not profane his offspring among his people, because I am the LORD who makes him holy."

## A Blemish

**16** The LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**17** "Speak to Aaron, saying: None of your offspring through their generations who has a blemish shall come near to present the bread of his God.

**18** No man at all who has a blemish shall come near: a man who is blind or lame, or disfigured in the face, or with a limb too long,

**19** or a man who has a broken foot or a broken hand,

**20** or a hunchback, or a withered man, or one with a spot in his eye, or a scab, or a skin eruption, or crushed testicles.

**21** No man of the offspring of Aaron the priest who has a blemish shall come forward to present the LORD's offerings by fire. He has a blemish; he shall not come forward to present the bread of his God.

**22** He may eat the bread of his God, both the most holy and the holy.

**23** But he shall not come to the veil and shall not come near the altar, because he has a blemish, and he shall not profane My holy places, because I am the LORD who makes them holy."

**24** Moses spoke this to Aaron and to his sons and to all the sons of Israel.

## Notes

- **v4 the broken verse** — *lo yittamma ba'al be-ammav le-hechallo*, and it is one of the least recoverable sentences in Leviticus. *Ba'al* means a husband, an owner, a master; *be-ammav* is *among his people*; *le-hechallo* is *to profane himself*. The words are all ordinary and the sentence they make is not. It has been read as a rule about a priest's wife, about a chief among his kin, and as the conclusion of vv1–3.
- **vv18–20 the list nobody can fully translate** — twelve conditions, and several of the words are effectively unknown. *Tevallul* occurs **once** in the Hebrew Bible, in v20. *Sarua* occurs twice, both in Leviticus, and is usually taken as a limb of the wrong length. *Charum* is guessed from a root for cutting, so a damaged face. The English in every Bible is a reconstruction from roots and from the company the words keep, and the confident-sounding lists in modern translations are covering a good deal of uncertainty.
