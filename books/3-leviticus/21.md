# Leviticus 21

## A Priest and the Dead

**1** And the LORD said to Moses, "Say to the priests, the sons of Aaron, and say to them: None of them shall make himself unclean for a dead person among his people,

**2** except for his close flesh — for his mother and for his father, and for his son and for his daughter and for his brother,

**3** and for his unmarried sister, who is close to him because she has had no husband. For her he may make himself unclean.

**4** He shall not make himself unclean as a husband among his people, profaning himself.

**5** They shall not make a bald patch on their heads, and they shall not shave off the edge of their beards, and they shall not cut a gash in their flesh.

**6** They shall be holy to their God, and they shall not profane the name of their God, because they present the offerings by fire of the LORD, the bread of their God. And they shall be holy.

**7** They shall not take a woman who is a prostitute or who has been disgraced, and they shall not take a woman divorced from her husband, because he is holy to his God.

**8** And you shall treat him as holy, because he presents the bread of your God. He shall be holy to you, because I the LORD, who makes you holy, am holy.

**9** And a priest's daughter, if she profanes herself by prostituting herself, profanes her father. She shall be burned in the fire.

## The High Priest

**10** And the priest who is greatest among his brothers, on whose head the anointing oil was poured and whose hand was filled to wear the garments, shall not let his hair go loose and shall not tear his clothes.

**11** And he shall not go in to any dead body. He shall not make himself unclean even for his father or for his mother.

**12** And he shall not go out of the sanctuary and profane the sanctuary of his God, because the crown of the anointing oil of his God is on him. I am the LORD.

**13** And he shall take a wife in her virginity.

**14** A widow, or a divorced woman, or a woman disgraced by prostitution — these he shall not take. He shall take a virgin from his own people as a wife,

**15** and he shall not profane his offspring among his people, because I am the LORD who makes him holy."

## A Blemish

**16** And the LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**17** "Speak to Aaron, saying: None of your offspring through their generations who has a blemish shall come near to present the bread of his God.

**18** No man at all who has a blemish shall come near: a man who is blind or lame, or disfigured in the face, or with a limb too long,

**19** or a man who has a broken foot or a broken hand,

**20** or a hunchback, or a withered man, or one with a spot in his eye, or a scab, or a skin eruption, or crushed testicles.

**21** No man of the offspring of Aaron the priest who has a blemish shall come forward to present the offerings by fire of the LORD. He has a blemish; he shall not come forward to present the bread of his God.

**22** He may eat the bread of his God, from the most holy and from the holy.

**23** But he shall not come to the veil and shall not come near the altar, because he has a blemish, and he shall not profane My holy places, because I am the LORD who makes them holy."

**24** And Moses spoke this to Aaron and to his sons and to all the sons of Israel.

## Notes

- **vv1–3 the list of who a priest may bury** — mother, father, son, daughter, brother, and an unmarried sister. A wife is not on the list. Whether she is covered by the obscure v4, or assumed under *his close flesh* in v2, or genuinely left out, has been argued for as long as the verse has existed, and the text does not resolve it.
- **v4 the broken verse** — *lo yittamma ba'al be-ammav le-hechallo*, and it is one of the least recoverable sentences in Leviticus. *Ba'al* means a husband, an owner, a master; *be-ammav* is *among his people*; *le-hechallo* is *to profane himself*. The words are all ordinary and the sentence they make is not. It has been read as a rule about a priest's wife, about a chief among his kin, and as the conclusion of vv1–3. This rendering follows the words and leaves the difficulty where the Hebrew leaves it.
- **v5 the written and the spoken text** — the consonants say *yiqrechah*, singular, and the reading tradition says *yiqrechu*, plural. It is one of only five places in Leviticus where the two part company; two of the others are Aaron's hand at 9:22 and at 16:21. Nothing turns on it here, which is itself worth seeing: these variants are usually this small.
- **v5 the same three mourning customs, again** — a bald patch, a shaved beard-edge, a gash in the flesh. Two chapters ago the identical practices were forbidden to **everyone** (19:27–28). Here they are forbidden to priests specifically, in almost the same words. The Torah often legislates twice at two levels, and comparing the two tells you which audience each law was aimed at.
- **vv6, 8, 17, 21, 22 "the bread of their God"** — *lechem eloheihem*, the same startling phrase as 3:11, where what goes up from the altar is called God's food. It is used five times in this chapter, always at the moment of saying what a priest is **for**: he is the one who brings God his bread. Nothing in these verses softens it.
- **v9 the priest's daughter** — the harshest sentence in the chapter, and it falls on someone who is not a priest and cannot become one. The stated reason is entirely about her father: *she profanes her father*. Whatever holiness he carries is treated here as something her conduct can damage, and the penalty is the one given at 20:14 for depravity.
- **v10 "the priest who is greatest among his brothers"** — *ha-kohen ha-gadol*, **the great priest**, which is where English gets **high priest**. Chapter 4 called the same office *ha-kohen ha-mashiach*, **the anointed priest**. Two titles for one man, and this one is about rank among his brothers rather than about oil.
- **vv10–11 forbidden to mourn at all** — an ordinary priest may bury his own mother and father (v2). The high priest may not, and may not let his hair down or tear his clothes. That is exactly what Aaron and his surviving sons were forbidden at 10:6 on the day Nadab and Abihu died — the same two verbs. What was an emergency order in chapter 10 is written here as the permanent condition of the office.
- **vv18–20 the list nobody can fully translate** — twelve conditions, and several of the words are effectively unknown. *Tevallul* occurs **once** in the Hebrew Bible, in v20. *Sarua* occurs twice, both in Leviticus, and is usually taken as a limb of the wrong length. *Charum* is guessed from a root for cutting, so a damaged face. The English in every Bible is a reconstruction from roots and from the company the words keep, and the confident-sounding lists in modern translations are covering a good deal of uncertainty.
- **vv22–23 what he keeps** — a priest with a blemish is not put out of the priesthood and not cut off from his family's living. He eats the holy food, including the most holy portions, which is the priests' sustenance and their inheritance in place of land. What he cannot do is approach the altar or the veil. The distinction the chapter draws is between being a priest and performing at the altar, and it draws it in two verses without explanation.
