# Leviticus 9

## The Eighth Day

**1** And it came to pass on the eighth day that Moses called Aaron and his sons and the elders of Israel.

**2** He said to Aaron, "Take for yourself a bull calf for a sin offering and a ram for a burnt offering, both without blemish, and bring them before the LORD.

**3** Speak to the sons of Israel, saying: Take a male goat for a sin offering, and a calf and a lamb, both a year old and without blemish, for a burnt offering,

**4** and an ox and a ram for peace offerings, to sacrifice before the LORD, and a grain offering mixed with oil — because today the LORD is appearing to you."

**5** They brought what Moses had commanded to the front of the tent of meeting, and the whole congregation came near and stood before the LORD.

**6** Moses said, "This is what the LORD commanded you to do, so that the glory of the LORD may appear to you."

**7** Moses said to Aaron, "Come near to the altar and offer your sin offering and your burnt offering, and make atonement for yourself and for the people; then offer the people's offering and make atonement for them, as the LORD has commanded."

## Aaron's Own Offering

**8** Aaron came near to the altar and slaughtered the calf of his own sin offering.

**9** Aaron's sons brought the blood to him, and he dipped his finger in the blood and put it on the horns of the altar, and poured out the rest at the base of the altar.

**10** He turned into smoke on the altar the fat, the kidneys and the lobe of the liver from the sin offering, as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**11** He burned the flesh and the hide in the fire outside the camp.

**12** He slaughtered the burnt offering, and Aaron's sons handed him the blood, and he dashed it against the altar all around.

**13** They handed him the burnt offering piece by piece, with the head, and he turned it into smoke on the altar.

**14** He washed the entrails and the legs, and turned them into smoke on top of the burnt offering on the altar.

## The People's Offering

**15** Then he presented the people's offering. He took the people's goat for the sin offering, slaughtered it, and offered it as a sin offering like the first.

**16** He brought the burnt offering and offered it according to the rule.

**17** He brought the grain offering, and filled his palm from it, and turned it into smoke on the altar, besides the morning burnt offering.

**18** He slaughtered the ox and the ram, the people's peace offering, and Aaron's sons handed him the blood, and he dashed it against the altar all around,

**19** and the fat pieces from the ox and from the ram — the fat tail and the covering fat and the kidneys and the lobe of the liver —

**20** and they put the fat pieces on the breasts, and he turned them into smoke on the altar.

**21** Aaron waved the breasts and the right thigh before the LORD, as Moses had commanded.

## Fire

**22** Aaron lifted his hands toward the people and blessed them, and came down from making the sin offering and the burnt offering and the peace offering.

**23** Moses and Aaron went into the tent of meeting, and came out and blessed the people; and the glory of the LORD appeared to all the people.

**24** Fire came out from before the LORD and consumed the burnt offering and the fat pieces on the altar. All the people saw, and they shouted, and they fell on their faces.

## Notes

- **v1 "on the eighth day"** — seven days shut in at the doorway (8:33), and this is the morning after. The number is doing what it does everywhere in this book: seven is the full count, and the eighth is when the thing the count was for actually happens. Nothing in chapter 8 was the priesthood working. It was the priesthood being assembled.
- **vv7–8 "his own sin offering"** — Aaron's first act as high priest is to kill an animal for his own sin, in front of the whole congregation, before he touches anything belonging to anybody else. The order was set out in v7 and it is not negotiable: *make atonement for yourself and for the people*, in that sequence. A man who has been a priest for eight days is already assumed to need it.
- **v2 "a bull calf"** — *egel ben baqar*, and *egel* is **calf**. The law of chapter 4 requires a bull, *par*, for the anointed priest's sin offering, and what Aaron is told to bring is a calf. The Torah does not remark on the substitution, and Aaron's last recorded involvement with a calf was Exodus 32, where he made one out of the people's gold and built an altar in front of it. The text puts a calf in his hands on his first day and says nothing.
- **vv12, 13, 18 "handed him"** — *va-yamtsi'u*, from *matsa*, **to find**: in this form, to cause something to reach somebody. It is used three times in this chapter and almost nowhere else for passing an object. The picture is of sons working a line — the blood reaching Aaron's hand, then the carcass reaching it piece by piece — with the father never leaving the altar.
- **v17 "filled his palm from it"** — *va-yemalle khappo*, and *filling the hand* is the idiom for ordaining a priest (8:33). Two verses into his working life, the phrase turns literal and ordinary: he is measuring flour. Whatever was installed in him in chapter 8 now looks like a handful of grain going into a fire.
- **v22 "lifted his hands"** — the written consonants of the Hebrew say **his hand**, singular; the reading tradition preserved alongside them says **his hands**. Leviticus has only five words where the written and the spoken forms part company, and two of them are this one — here, and at 16:21, where Aaron lays his hands on the head of the goat that carries the people's sins into the wilderness. Both are Aaron's hands, and both are the gesture that moves something between him and the people.
- **v22 the first blessing, with no words** — a priest blesses Israel for the first time here and the text does not record what he said. The words are not given until Numbers 6:24–26, two books later — *the LORD bless you and keep you* — and there they are given as a command to Aaron. Here the blessing simply happens, is not quoted, and is followed by him walking down the ramp.
- **v23 what the blessing does not accomplish** — Aaron blesses, and nothing. Then Moses and Aaron go inside together, come out, and bless again, and *the glory of the LORD appeared to all the people*. The text sets the two attempts side by side without explaining the difference, and does not say what the two men did in the tent.
- **v24 "fire came out from before the LORD and consumed"** — *va-tetse esh mi-lifnei YHWH va-tokhal*. The same four words open 10:2, five verses later, where the fire comes out and consumes Nadab and Abihu. Nothing marks the second occurrence as a different kind of event. The same fire that answers the offering here, on the best day of Aaron's life, kills two of his sons on the next page, and Hebrew does not give the reader any warning in the vocabulary.
- **v24 "they shouted"** — *va-yaronnu*, from *ranan*, a verb for a loud ringing cry, the word behind the shouting in the Psalms. It is not awe and it is not silence. The people yell, and then go face down on the ground.
