# Leviticus 10

## Strange Fire

**1** Aaron's sons Nadab and Abihu each took his fire pan, put fire in it, laid incense on it, and offered before the LORD strange fire, which He had not commanded them.

**2** Fire came out from before the LORD and consumed them, and they died before the LORD.

**3** Moses said to Aaron, "This is what the LORD spoke of when He said: Through those near Me I will be shown holy, and before all the people I will be honored." Aaron was silent.

**4** Moses called Mishael and Elzaphan, the sons of Uzziel, Aaron's uncle, and said to them, "Come near, carry your brothers away from the front of the holy place, outside the camp."

**5** They came near and carried them in their tunics outside the camp, as Moses had said.

**6** Moses said to Aaron and to Eleazar and Ithamar his sons, "Do not let your hair hang loose and do not tear your clothes, or you will die and anger will come on the whole congregation. But your brothers, the whole house of Israel, may weep for those the LORD has burned.

**7** Do not go out from the entrance of the tent of meeting, or you will die, because the LORD's anointing oil is on you." They did as Moses said.

## What the Priests May Not Do

**8** The LORD spoke to Aaron, saying,

**9** "Do not drink wine or strong drink, you or your sons with you, when you enter the tent of meeting, or you will die. It is a lasting statute through your generations,

**10** and so that you can separate the holy from the ordinary, and the unclean from the clean,

**11** and teach the sons of Israel all the statutes the LORD has spoken to them through Moses."

## The Meal That Was Not Eaten

**12** Moses spoke to Aaron and to Eleazar and Ithamar, his remaining sons: "Take the grain offering that is left from the LORD's offerings by fire and eat it unleavened beside the altar, because it is most holy.

**13** You shall eat it in a holy place, because it is your due and your sons' due from the LORD's offerings by fire; for so I was commanded.

**14** You shall eat the breast that was waved and the thigh that was lifted off in a clean place — you and your sons and your daughters with you — because they are given as your due and your sons' due from the peace offerings of the sons of Israel.

**15** They shall bring the thigh that is lifted off and the breast that is waved, together with the fat pieces for the offerings by fire, to wave them before the LORD; and it shall be yours and your sons' with you as a lasting due, as the LORD has commanded."

**16** Moses searched and searched for the goat of the sin offering, and it had been burned. He was angry with Eleazar and Ithamar, Aaron's remaining sons, and said,

**17** "Why did you not eat the sin offering in the holy place? It is most holy, and He gave it to you to carry the guilt of the congregation, to make atonement for them before the LORD.

**18** Its blood was not brought inside the holy place. You should certainly have eaten it in the holy place, as I commanded."

**19** Aaron said to Moses, "Today they brought their sin offering and their burnt offering before the LORD, and things like this have happened to me. If I had eaten a sin offering today, would that have been good in the eyes of the LORD?"

**20** Moses heard, and it was good in his eyes.

## Notes

- **v1 "strange fire"** — *esh zarah*, and the adjective is already on the books. Exodus 30:9 forbids *qetoret zarah*, **strange incense**, on the incense altar, in a verse about equipment written before either man was ordained. What the text never supplies is what made it strange. The only reason given is negative: *which He had not commanded them*. Not that it was forbidden — that it was not ordered. In a book where every action so far has been prescribed down to which finger, doing something unprescribed turns out to be the offense.
- **v3 the quotation with no source** — Moses says *this is what the LORD spoke*, and then quotes a sentence that is recorded nowhere in the Torah. The nearest thing to it is Exodus 29:43, *and it shall be made holy by My glory* — said of the **tent**, not of people. The verb here, *eqqadesh*, **I will be shown holy**, occurs in this form once in the Hebrew Bible, in this verse. Aaron has just lost two sons, and the explanation he is given is a quotation the reader has never been shown.
- **v3 "Aaron was silent"** — *va-yiddom Aharon*, two words, and it ends the scene. This exact form occurs three times in the Bible, and one of the others is Joshua 10:13, *and the sun stood still*. The verb is not only about sound; it is about a thing stopping. What Aaron does is not described as grief, acceptance, shock, or protest. He simply comes to a halt, and the text moves on to the disposal of the bodies.
- **v10 "separate the holy from the ordinary"** — *le-havdil*, the verb of Genesis 1: God *separated* light from darkness, the waters from the waters, day from night. It is the work of the first, second and fourth days, and it is handed here to the priesthood as its job description — holy from ordinary, unclean from clean. What God did to the world, the priest is to keep doing inside the camp.
