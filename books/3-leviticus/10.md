# Leviticus 10

## Strange Fire

**1** And Aaron's sons Nadab and Abihu each took his fire pan, and put fire in them, and laid incense on it, and brought near before the LORD strange fire, which He had not commanded them.

**2** And fire came out from before the LORD and consumed them, and they died before the LORD.

**3** And Moses said to Aaron, "This is what the LORD spoke, saying: Through those near Me I will be shown holy, and before all the people I will be honored." And Aaron was silent.

**4** And Moses called Mishael and Elzaphan, the sons of Uzziel, Aaron's uncle, and said to them, "Come near, carry your brothers away from the front of the holy place, outside the camp."

**5** And they came near and carried them in their tunics outside the camp, as Moses had said.

**6** And Moses said to Aaron and to Eleazar and Ithamar his sons, "Do not let your hair go loose and do not tear your clothes, or you will die and anger will come on the whole congregation. But your brothers, the whole house of Israel, may weep for the burning the LORD has burned.

**7** And do not go out from the entrance of the tent of meeting, or you will die, because the LORD's anointing oil is on you." And they did as Moses said.

## What the Priests May Not Do

**8** And the LORD spoke to Aaron, saying,

**9** "Do not drink wine or strong drink, you or your sons with you, when you come into the tent of meeting, or you will die. It is a lasting statute through your generations,

**10** and so that you can separate between the holy and the ordinary, and between the unclean and the clean,

**11** and teach the sons of Israel all the statutes the LORD has spoken to them through Moses."

## The Meal That Was Not Eaten

**12** And Moses spoke to Aaron and to Eleazar and Ithamar, his remaining sons: "Take the grain offering that is left from the offerings by fire of the LORD and eat it unleavened beside the altar, because it is most holy.

**13** And you shall eat it in a holy place, because it is your due and your sons' due from the offerings by fire of the LORD; for so I was commanded.

**14** And the breast that was waved and the thigh that was lifted off you shall eat in a clean place — you and your sons and your daughters with you — because they are given as your due and your sons' due from the peace offerings of the sons of Israel.

**15** The thigh that was lifted off and the breast that was waved they shall bring with the offerings by fire of the fat pieces, to wave before the LORD; and it shall be yours and your sons' with you as a lasting due, as the LORD has commanded."

**16** And Moses searched and searched for the goat of the sin offering, and here — it had been burned. And he was angry with Eleazar and Ithamar, Aaron's remaining sons, and said,

**17** "Why did you not eat the sin offering in the holy place? It is most holy, and He gave it to you to carry the guilt of the congregation, to make atonement for them before the LORD.

**18** Look — its blood was not brought inside the holy place. You should certainly have eaten it in the holy place, as I commanded."

**19** And Aaron said to Moses, "Look — today they brought their sin offering and their burnt offering before the LORD, and things like this have happened to me. If I had eaten a sin offering today, would that have been good in the eyes of the LORD?"

**20** And Moses heard, and it was good in his eyes.

## Notes

- **v1 "strange fire"** — *esh zarah*, and the adjective is already on the books. Exodus 30:9 forbids *qetoret zarah*, **strange incense**, on the incense altar, in a verse about equipment written before either man was ordained. What the text never supplies is what made it strange. The only reason given is negative: *which He had not commanded them*. Not that it was forbidden — that it was not ordered. In a book where every action so far has been prescribed down to which finger, doing something unprescribed turns out to be the offense.
- **vv1, 3, 4 the word that runs through the chapter** — *qarav*, **to come near**, is the verb behind every offering in chapters 1 through 9: an offering is a *qorban*, a thing brought near. Here Nadab and Abihu *bring near* strange fire (v1); Moses says God will be shown holy *bi-qrovai*, **through those near Me** (v3); and the cousins who carry the bodies out are told *qirvu*, **come near** (v4). The same root covers the offering, the offenders, and the men who clear away what is left.
- **v3 the quotation with no source** — Moses says *this is what the LORD spoke*, and then quotes a sentence that is recorded nowhere in the Torah. The nearest thing to it is Exodus 29:43, *and it shall be made holy by My glory* — said of the **tent**, not of people. The verb here, *eqqadesh*, **I will be shown holy**, occurs in this form once in the Hebrew Bible, in this verse. Aaron has just lost two sons, and the explanation he is given is a quotation the reader has never been shown.
- **v3 "And Aaron was silent"** — *va-yiddom Aharon*, two words, and it ends the scene. This exact form occurs four times in the Bible, and one of the others is Joshua 10:13, *and the sun stood still*. The verb is not only about sound; it is about a thing stopping. What Aaron does is not described as grief, acceptance, shock, or protest. He simply comes to a halt, and the text moves on to the disposal of the bodies.
- **v5 "carried them in their tunics"** — the garments Moses put on them in 8:13. Whether the tunics are what the bodies were carried **in** or carried **by** is not settled by the Hebrew, but either way, the last mention of Nadab and Abihu in the chapter is their priestly clothing, intact. The fire consumed the men and not the uniform.
- **v6 "do not let your hair go loose"** — *al tifra'u*, from *para*, to unbind or let run wild, and with *do not tear your clothes* it names the two standard gestures of mourning. Aaron and his two surviving sons are forbidden both, and forbidden to leave the doorway (v7). Their brothers may be wept for by the entire nation — the text says so in the same breath — by everyone except the three men who actually lost them.
- **v6 "anger will come"** — *yiqtsof*, and the Hebrew gives the verb no subject at all: *and upon the whole congregation it will be angry*. Wrath is spoken of as a thing that arrives rather than as something somebody does. English cannot leave a verb unattached like that, and every translation has to decide whether to supply *He*. Nothing in the verse supplies it.
- **v9 wine, placed here** — the ban on drinking before entering the tent arrives immediately after two priests die doing something unauthorized, and is addressed to Aaron directly — one of very few times in the Torah God speaks to him without Moses. The text does not say Nadab and Abihu had been drinking. It puts the law here, and says nothing.
- **v10 "separate between"** — *le-havdil*, the verb of Genesis 1: God *separated* light from darkness, the waters from the waters, day from night. It is the work of the first three days, and it is handed here to the priesthood as its job description — holy from ordinary, unclean from clean. What God did to the world, the priest is to keep doing inside the camp.
- **v14 "and your daughters with you"** — the breast and the thigh may be eaten by the priest's whole household, daughters included, because they are eaten in a *clean* place rather than a *holy* one. The distinction of v10 does immediate work: the most holy portions are restricted to males in the courtyard, and these are not most holy, so the family eats them at home.
- **v16 "searched and searched"** — *darosh darash*, the verb doubled with itself, which is Hebrew leaning on a word. *Darash* is to seek, inquire, investigate — the verb behind *midrash*. Moses hunts for the goat, finds it burnt, and loses his temper at the two sons who are left, on the day their brothers died.
- **vv19–20 Aaron answers, and wins** — his first words since the silence of v3, and he does not apologize. He points at the day itself: *things like this have happened to me* — the only phrase in the chapter that comes close to saying what he feels, and it does not name it. Then a question rather than a defense: *would that have been good in the eyes of the LORD?* And Moses, who has been right about everything since the first chapter, hears it and drops the charge. The book does not say who was correct about the law. It says the argument ended.
