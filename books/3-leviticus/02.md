# Leviticus 2

## Flour, Oil, Frankincense

**1** And when a person brings a grain offering to the LORD, his offering shall be fine flour, and he shall pour oil on it and put frankincense on it.

**2** And he shall bring it to Aaron's sons, the priests, and one of them shall scoop out a handful of its fine flour and oil, with all its frankincense, and the priest shall turn its memorial portion into smoke on the altar — an offering by fire, a soothing aroma to the LORD.

**3** And what is left of the grain offering shall be Aaron's and his sons' — most holy, from the LORD's offerings by fire.

## Baked, Griddled, Panned

**4** And when you bring a grain offering baked in an oven, it shall be unleavened loaves of fine flour mixed with oil, or unleavened wafers spread with oil.

**5** And if your offering is a grain offering on a griddle, it shall be fine flour mixed with oil, unleavened.

**6** Break it into pieces and pour oil on it. It is a grain offering.

**7** And if your offering is a grain offering in a pan, it shall be made of fine flour with oil.

**8** And you shall bring the grain offering made from these to the LORD, and present it to the priest, and he shall bring it near to the altar.

**9** And the priest shall lift its memorial portion from the grain offering and turn it into smoke on the altar — an offering by fire, a soothing aroma to the LORD.

**10** And what is left of the grain offering shall be Aaron's and his sons' — most holy, from the LORD's offerings by fire.

## What May Not Go Up, and What Must

**11** No grain offering that you bring to the LORD shall be made with leaven, for you shall not turn any sourdough or any honey into smoke as an offering by fire to the LORD.

**12** As an offering of firstfruits you may bring them to the LORD, but they shall not go up on the altar for a soothing aroma.

**13** And you shall season every grain offering with salt. You shall not let the salt of the covenant of your God be missing from your grain offering. With all your offerings you shall bring salt.

**14** And if you bring a grain offering of firstfruits to the LORD, you shall bring fresh ears roasted in fire, crushed fresh grain, as your grain offering of firstfruits.

**15** And you shall put oil on it and lay frankincense on it. It is a grain offering.

**16** And the priest shall turn its memorial portion into smoke — from its crushed grain and from its oil, with all its frankincense — an offering by fire to the LORD.

## Notes

- **v1 "grain offering"** — *minchah*, and the word has nothing to do with grain. It means a **gift**, and specifically the kind of gift that acknowledges a difference in rank: what a subject sends a king, what Jacob sends ahead to Esau. The first two occurrences in the Bible are Genesis 4:3–5, where Cain brings *a minchah* of the fruit of the ground and Abel brings one from his flock — the same noun covers both, and it is the word the whole quarrel turns on. By the time it reaches this chapter it has narrowed to the bloodless offering, but it still means tribute.
- **v1 "a person"** — *nefesh*, where 1:2 had *adam*. *Nefesh* is the breath, the life, the throat — the word rendered *soul* in most English Bibles and never meaning a spirit separable from a body. The animal chapter addresses the human being; the flour chapter addresses the appetite.
- **v2 "its memorial portion"** — *azkarah*, from *zakhar*, **to remember**, and the noun occurs only in the sacrificial laws. It is the handful that goes into the fire while the rest goes to the priests, and the name does not say whose memory it serves. Either God is being reminded of the giver, or the giver is reminding himself. The word is built so that both fit, and nothing in the law decides it.
- **v3 "most holy"** — *qodesh qadashim*, **holiness of holinesses**, the same construction applied to the altar at Exodus 40:10 and the name of the inner room. It is said here of a priest's dinner. The leftovers of a bag of flour carry the highest grade of holiness the system has.
- **v6 "Break it into pieces"** — *patot otah pittim*, literally **crumble it crumbs**: the verb takes its own noun, which is how Hebrew presses a word and how English only stutters. The noun *pat* survives as the ordinary word for a morsel of bread — *a pat of bread* is what Abraham offers the three men at Genesis 18:5.
- **v11 "no leaven… and no honey"** — two things banned from the fire, and neither is explained. Both are agents of fermentation; both make dough rise and go sour on their own. *Devash* covers bee honey and thick fruit syrup alike. What the verse forbids is not sweetness or sourness but what they do — the altar takes nothing that changes by itself.
- **v12 what is brought but not burned** — the same leaven and honey **may** be presented as an offering of firstfruits; they simply do not go up on the altar. Two verses, and the distinction they draw is one an English reader can miss entirely: a thing can be given to the LORD and still be wrong to burn.
- **v13 "the salt of the covenant of your God"** — stated three times in one verse, from three directions: salt your offering, do not let the salt be missing, bring salt with everything. No reason is given and no story is attached. Salt is the preservative that stops exactly what leaven and honey start, which sets the verse directly against v11 — but the text calls it a **covenant** rather than a preservative, and leaves the phrase standing with no explanation anywhere in the Torah.
- **v14 "fresh ears roasted in fire"** — *aviv*, the barley at the green stage, before it hardens. It is also a month: *the month of Aviv*, the one Israel left Egypt in (Exodus 34:18), named for the condition of the grain when the year turns. So the firstfruits offering here and the calendar itself are named by the same word, and the month is measured by a crop rather than by a number.
