# Leviticus 19

## You Shall Be Holy

**1** The LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**2** "Speak to the whole congregation of the sons of Israel and say to them: You shall be holy, because I, the LORD your God, am holy.

**3** Each of you shall fear his mother and his father, and you shall keep My sabbaths. I am the LORD your God.

**4** Do not turn to worthless gods, and do not make cast gods for yourselves. I am the LORD your God.

**5** When you sacrifice a peace offering to the LORD, you shall sacrifice it so that it is accepted for you.

**6** It shall be eaten on the day you sacrifice it and on the next day, and what is left until the third day shall be burned in the fire.

**7** If it is eaten at all on the third day, it is foul. It will not be accepted.

**8** Whoever eats it carries his guilt, because he has profaned what is holy to the LORD, and that person shall be cut off from his people.

## What You Leave in the Field

**9** When you reap the harvest of your land, you shall not finish reaping to the edge of your field, and you shall not gather the gleanings of your harvest.

**10** You shall not strip your vineyard bare, and you shall not gather the fallen grapes of your vineyard. You shall leave them for the poor and for the guest. I am the LORD your God.

## How to Treat Each Other

**11** You shall not steal, and you shall not deal falsely, and you shall not lie to one another.

**12** You shall not swear falsely by My name, and so profane the name of your God. I am the LORD.

**13** You shall not exploit your neighbor and you shall not rob him. The wages of a hired man shall not stay with you overnight until morning.

**14** You shall not curse a deaf man, and you shall not put a stumbling block in front of a blind man; and you shall fear your God. I am the LORD.

**15** You shall not do injustice in judgment. You shall not show favor to the poor or defer to the great. In righteousness you shall judge your neighbor.

**16** You shall not go around spreading slander among your people. You shall not stand on the blood of your neighbor. I am the LORD.

**17** You shall not hate your brother in your heart. You shall be sure to correct your neighbor, and not carry guilt because of him.

**18** You shall not take revenge and you shall not hold a grudge against the sons of your people; and you shall love your neighbor as yourself. I am the LORD.

## Mixtures

**19** You shall keep My statutes. You shall not mate your livestock with a different kind. You shall not sow your field with two kinds of seed. You shall not put on a garment woven of two kinds of material.

**20** When a man lies with a woman who is a slave promised to another man, and she has not been redeemed or given her freedom, there shall be an inquiry. They shall not be put to death, because she was not free.

**21** He shall bring his guilt offering to the LORD, to the entrance of the tent of meeting — a ram for a guilt offering.

**22** The priest shall make atonement for him with the ram of the guilt offering before the LORD, for the sin he has committed; and he shall be forgiven for it.

**23** When you come into the land and plant any tree for food, you shall treat its fruit as forbidden. For three years it shall be forbidden to you. It shall not be eaten.

**24** In the fourth year all its fruit shall be holy, a praise offering to the LORD.

**25** In the fifth year you may eat its fruit, so that it will yield more for you. I am the LORD your God.

## Marks on the Body

**26** You shall not eat anything with the blood in it. You shall not practice divination and you shall not read omens.

**27** You shall not round off the hair at the sides of your head, and you shall not ruin the edge of your beard.

**28** You shall not make a gash in your flesh for the dead, and you shall not put tattoo marks on yourselves. I am the LORD.

**29** Do not profane your daughter by making her a prostitute, or the land will turn to prostitution and be filled with depravity.

**30** You shall keep My sabbaths and fear My sanctuary. I am the LORD.

**31** Do not turn to ghosts, and do not seek out spirits, and so become unclean by them. I am the LORD your God.

## The Old, the Foreigner, the Scales

**32** You shall rise in the presence of the gray-haired and honor the old; and you shall fear your God. I am the LORD.

**33** When a guest lives with you in your land, you shall not wrong him.

**34** The guest living with you shall be to you as a native among you, and you shall love him as yourself, because you were guests in the land of Egypt. I am the LORD your God.

**35** You shall not do injustice in judgment — in measuring length, in weight, or in quantity.

**36** You shall have honest scales, honest weights, an honest ephah and an honest hin. I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt.

**37** You shall keep all My statutes and all My rulings and do them. I am the LORD."

## Notes

- **vv18 and 34 the sentence and its twin** — *ve-ahavta le-re'akha kamokha*, **and you shall love your neighbor as yourself**, is the most quoted line in Leviticus. Sixteen verses later the identical construction is used again, of somebody else: *ve-ahavta lo kamokha*, **and you shall love him as yourself**, said of the *ger*, the guest living in your land. Those two verses are the only places in the entire Hebrew Bible where this phrase occurs. The famous one has a twin, and the twin is almost never quoted.
- **v18 "love your neighbor"** — the Hebrew does not say *love your neighbor* the way English does. It is *ve-ahavta **le**-re'akha* — love **to** your neighbor, with a preposition where a direct object would be expected. The construction pushes the sense toward acting lovingly toward someone rather than feeling something about them, and it sits at the end of a paragraph made entirely of actions: do not steal, do not withhold wages overnight, do not curse the deaf, do not take revenge. The command arrives as the summary of a list of things to do and not do.
- **v2 who is being addressed** — *kol adat benei Yisrael*, **the whole congregation**. Everything up to chapter 16 was spoken to Aaron and his sons; the demand to be holy was a priest's job description. Here the same word is handed to everyone, and the chapter that follows is about wages, scales, gleaning, gossip, and the old man you stand up for.
- **vv9–10 what is left in the field** — four things: the edge of the field is not reaped, the dropped stalks are not picked up, the vineyard is not stripped, the fallen grapes are not gathered. The poor and the guest are not given anything — they are left something, and they come and take it themselves. This is the law Ruth is working under in Boaz's field, and it is written as a restriction on the owner rather than as a charity.
- **v11 "you shall not lie to one another"** — *ish ba-amito*, the rare word from 6:2, where a man who lies to his *amit* about a deposit is said to break faith with the LORD. Both verses use the same verb, *kachash*. Nine of the ten occurrences of *amit* in the Bible are in Leviticus.
- **v14 the deaf man and the blind man** — a curse the deaf man cannot hear, and a stone the blind man cannot see. Both are offenses with no witness and no possibility of retaliation, and the verse ends *and you shall fear your God*. That formula occurs five times in the Bible, all of them in Leviticus, and every one of the five is attached to a command about someone who cannot fight back: the deaf and blind here, the old man at v32, and at 25:17, 36 and 43 a fellow Israelite in debt or in service.
- **v16 "stand on the blood of your neighbor"** — *lo ta'amod al dam re'ekha*, and what it means is not clear. The preposition can be *on*, *against*, *beside* or *over*; the phrase might forbid profiting from a neighbor's death, standing by while his life is at risk, or testifying in a capital case. The first half of the verse is about carrying tales, which suggests speech. The Hebrew supports more than one reading and nothing in the verse settles it.
- **v17 "you shall be sure to correct"** — *hokheach tokhiach*, the verb doubled with itself. *Yakhach* is to argue a case, set someone right, have it out. The verse pairs it with *do not hate your brother in your heart*: the alternative to silent hatred is saying something. And the clause that follows, *and not carry guilt because of him*, makes the silence itself the offense — the one who says nothing ends up holding the guilt.
- **v19 "two kinds"** — *kil'ayim*, and the word for the forbidden cloth is *sha'atnez*, which occurs twice in the Bible, here and at Deuteronomy 22:11 — where it is defined as wool and linen together. The three bans are one idea: no crossbred animals, no mixed seed, no mixed cloth. It is the logic of chapter 11 applied to farming and weaving, and no reason is given here either.
- **v20 the hardest verse in the chapter** — two of its words are effectively unrecoverable. *Necherefet*, describing the woman's status, occurs **once** in the Hebrew Bible, in this verse; it is usually taken as designated or promised to a man. *Biqqoret*, the consequence, also occurs once in this form, and has been read as an inquiry, a distinction in punishment, and compensation. What the verse plainly says is that the two are not put to death, and that the reason is her legal condition: *because she was not free*.
- **v23 "you shall treat its fruit as forbidden"** — the Hebrew is *va-araltem orlato*, **you shall foreskin its foreskin**, the verb taking its own noun, and the noun is the word for the foreskin removed at circumcision. Young fruit is spoken of as uncircumcised, so the tree is under the same language as a child in its first days. English cannot do the doubling and has nothing to do with the image, so the word here is *forbidden* and the picture is in this note.
- **v28 "tattoo marks"** — *ketovet qa'aqa*, and *qa'aqa* occurs **once** in the Hebrew Bible, in this verse. It sits beside a ban on gashing the flesh *la-nefesh*, **for the dead** — both are mourning practices, cut into the body. The two halves of the verse belong together, and the ban is not on marking as such but on marking oneself over a death.
- **v36 "an honest ephah and an honest hin"** — *tsedeq* four times in one verse, the noun usually rendered **righteousness**. Applied to scales it means accurate, and the chapter uses one word for both, so that a correct weight and a right action are the same thing. The verse then attaches the exodus to it: *I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt* — the credential offered for a rule about shopkeeping.
