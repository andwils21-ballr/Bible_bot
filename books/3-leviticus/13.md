# Leviticus 13

## What the Priest Looks For

**1** And the LORD spoke to Moses and to Aaron, saying,

**2** "When a person has on the skin of his body a swelling or a scab or a bright spot, and it becomes on the skin of his body a mark of blight, he shall be brought to Aaron the priest, or to one of his sons the priests.

**3** And the priest shall look at the mark on the skin of the body. If the hair in the mark has turned white, and the mark looks deeper than the skin of his body, it is a mark of blight. And the priest shall look at him and declare him unclean.

**4** But if the bright spot on the skin of his body is white and does not look deeper than the skin, and its hair has not turned white, the priest shall shut the marked man away for seven days.

**5** And the priest shall look at him on the seventh day, and here — if the mark has stayed as it was and has not spread in the skin, the priest shall shut him away a second seven days.

**6** And the priest shall look at him again on the seventh day, and here — if the mark has faded and has not spread in the skin, the priest shall declare him clean. It is a scab. And he shall wash his clothes and be clean.

**7** But if the scab keeps spreading in the skin after he has shown himself to the priest for his cleansing, he shall show himself to the priest a second time.

**8** And the priest shall look, and here — if the scab has spread in the skin, the priest shall declare him unclean. It is blight.

## When It Covers Everything

**9** When a mark of blight is on a person, he shall be brought to the priest.

**10** And the priest shall look, and here — if there is a white swelling in the skin and it has turned the hair white, and there is raw flesh in the swelling,

**11** it is an old blight in the skin of his body, and the priest shall declare him unclean. He shall not shut him away, because he is unclean.

**12** But if the blight breaks out all over the skin, and the blight covers all the skin of the marked man from his head to his feet, everywhere the priest can see,

**13** then the priest shall look, and here — if the blight has covered all his flesh, he shall declare the mark clean. It has all turned white; he is clean.

**14** But on the day raw flesh appears on him he shall be unclean.

**15** And the priest shall look at the raw flesh and declare him unclean. The raw flesh is unclean; it is blight.

**16** Or if the raw flesh turns back and becomes white, he shall come to the priest,

**17** and the priest shall look at him, and here — if the mark has turned white, the priest shall declare the mark clean. He is clean.

## A Boil and a Burn

**18** And when there is a boil on the skin of someone's body and it heals,

**19** and in the place of the boil there is a white swelling or a reddish-white bright spot, it shall be shown to the priest.

**20** And the priest shall look, and here — if it looks lower than the skin and its hair has turned white, the priest shall declare him unclean. It is a mark of blight that has broken out in the boil.

**21** But if the priest looks at it, and here — there is no white hair in it, and it is not lower than the skin, and it is faded, then the priest shall shut him away seven days.

**22** And if it keeps spreading in the skin, the priest shall declare him unclean. It is a mark.

**23** But if the bright spot stays where it is and has not spread, it is the scar of the boil, and the priest shall declare him clean.

**24** Or when there is a burn from fire on the skin of someone's body, and the raw place of the burn becomes a reddish-white or white bright spot,

**25** the priest shall look at it, and here — if the hair in the bright spot has turned white and it looks deeper than the skin, it is blight that has broken out in the burn. And the priest shall declare him unclean. It is a mark of blight.

**26** But if the priest looks at it, and here — there is no white hair in the bright spot, and it is not lower than the skin, and it is faded, then the priest shall shut him away seven days.

**27** And the priest shall look at him on the seventh day. If it keeps spreading in the skin, the priest shall declare him unclean. It is a mark of blight.

**28** But if the bright spot stays where it is and has not spread in the skin, and it is faded, it is the swelling of the burn, and the priest shall declare him clean, because it is the scar of the burn.

## On the Head or in the Beard

**29** And when a man or a woman has a mark on the head or in the beard,

**30** the priest shall look at the mark, and here — if it looks deeper than the skin and there is thin yellow hair in it, the priest shall declare him unclean. It is a scaly patch; it is blight of the head or of the beard.

**31** And when the priest looks at the mark of the scaly patch, and here — it does not look deeper than the skin, and there is no black hair in it, then the priest shall shut the marked person away for seven days.

**32** And the priest shall look at the mark on the seventh day, and here — if the scaly patch has not spread, and there is no yellow hair in it, and the patch does not look deeper than the skin,

**33** then he shall shave himself, but he shall not shave the patch; and the priest shall shut the patch away a second seven days.

**34** And the priest shall look at the scaly patch on the seventh day, and here — if the patch has not spread in the skin and does not look deeper than the skin, the priest shall declare him clean. And he shall wash his clothes and be clean.

**35** But if the scaly patch does keep spreading in the skin after he has been declared clean,

**36** the priest shall look at him, and here — if the patch has spread in the skin, the priest does not need to look for the yellow hair. He is unclean.

**37** But if the patch has stayed as it was and black hair has grown in it, the patch is healed. He is clean, and the priest shall declare him clean.

## What Is Not a Mark

**38** And when a man or a woman has bright spots on the skin of the body, white bright spots,

**39** the priest shall look, and here — if the bright spots on the skin of their body are a faded white, it is a rash that has broken out in the skin. He is clean.

**40** And when a man's head loses its hair, he is bald. He is clean.

**41** And if he loses the hair at the front of his head, he is bald at the forehead. He is clean.

**42** But if there is a reddish-white mark on the bald head or the bald forehead, it is blight breaking out on his bald head or his bald forehead.

**43** And the priest shall look at him, and here — if the swelling of the mark is reddish-white on his bald head or his bald forehead, looking like blight on the skin of the body,

**44** he is a blighted man. He is unclean. The priest shall certainly declare him unclean; his mark is on his head.

## Outside the Camp

**45** And the person who has the blight, who has the mark on him — his clothes shall be torn and his hair shall hang loose, and he shall cover his upper lip and call out, "Unclean! Unclean!"

**46** All the days the mark is on him he shall be unclean. He is unclean. He shall live alone; his home shall be outside the camp.

## On Cloth and on Leather

**47** And when a garment has a mark of blight on it, whether a wool garment or a linen garment,

**48** or on the warp or the weft of the linen or the wool, or on leather or on anything made of leather,

**49** and the mark is greenish or reddish in the garment, or on the leather, or on the warp or the weft, or on any leather object, it is a mark of blight, and it shall be shown to the priest.

**50** And the priest shall look at the mark and shut the marked thing away for seven days.

**51** And he shall look at the mark on the seventh day. If the mark has spread in the garment, or in the warp or the weft, or in the leather, whatever the leather is used for, the mark is a malignant blight. It is unclean.

**52** And he shall burn the garment, or the warp or the weft, in wool or in linen, or any leather object that has the mark on it, because it is a malignant blight. It shall be burned in the fire.

**53** But if the priest looks, and here — the mark has not spread in the garment, or in the warp or the weft, or in any leather object,

**54** then the priest shall give orders, and they shall wash the thing that has the mark on it, and he shall shut it away a second seven days.

**55** And the priest shall look after the mark has been washed, and here — if the mark has not changed its look, and the mark has not spread, it is unclean. You shall burn it in the fire. It is an eaten-through spot, whether on the back of it or on the front.

**56** But if the priest looks, and here — the mark has faded after it was washed, then he shall tear it out of the garment, or out of the leather, or out of the warp or the weft.

**57** And if it appears again in the garment, or in the warp or the weft, or in any leather object, it is breaking out. You shall burn in the fire the thing that has the mark on it.

**58** And the garment, or the warp or the weft, or any leather object that you wash and the mark leaves it, shall be washed a second time, and it is clean.

**59** This is the instruction for a mark of blight in a garment of wool or linen, or in the warp or the weft, or in any leather object, to declare it clean or to declare it unclean.

## Notes

- **v2 "blight"** — *tsara'at*, and it is not leprosy. English Bibles have printed *leprosy* since the Greek translators reached for *lepra*, a general word for scaly skin, and Latin and then English took it from them. Read what the chapter actually legislates for: a condition that turns hair white; that can cover a body **completely**, at which point the person is declared **clean** (v13); that breaks out in wool, in linen, in leather (vv47–59) and, in the next chapter, in the plaster of a house. No single illness does all of that. At Exodus 4:6 it arrives on Moses' hand and leaves again inside two verses. What the word names is not a diagnosis but a **spreading ruin** in a surface — skin, cloth, or wall — and the priest's whole job is to decide whether it is spreading.
- **v2 "a mark"** — *nega*, from *naga*, **to touch**. The noun is a touch, a stroke — the same verb that ran through chapter 11, where anyone who *touches* a carcass is unclean until evening. What is on the skin is described as something that has touched the person, not something they have. The chapter uses it forty-seven times.
- **the priest is not a physician** — he prescribes nothing. In fifty-nine verses there is no treatment, no ointment, no prayer, no cure. He looks, he waits seven days, he looks again, and he says one of two words: *clean* or *unclean*. Nothing here is about making anyone better. It is about deciding what may come near.
- **vv12–13 covered head to foot, and clean** — the verse that rules out any reading of this as a contagion law. A man whose skin is entirely white is sent home; a man with one patch of **raw flesh** is put out (v14). Whatever the priest is looking for, more of it is not worse. The dangerous state is the one in flux — something spreading, something changing — and the moment it stops changing the man is clean, however he looks.
- **v4 "shall shut the marked man away"** — *hisgir*, to close in, and this is the first quarantine in literature that gets a written procedure: seven days, look, seven days again, look. It is not punishment and it is not treatment. It is time, used as an instrument — the law's way of asking a question that only waiting can answer.
- **v30 "a scaly patch"** — *netek*, from a verb meaning to tear or pull away, used of a hair coming out and of a cord snapping. On the head and in the beard the test changes: not white hair but **thin yellow** hair, and v33 requires the man to shave around the patch and leave the patch itself unshaved, so the priest can see whether its edge has moved. The procedure is designed for a second look a week later.
- **v39 the look-alike that is clean** — *bohaq*, some faded white patchiness of the skin, and the law names it in order to let it go. Two verses later baldness gets the same treatment twice over (vv40–41): *he is bald. He is clean.* A chapter this severe about what to exclude takes the trouble to say out loud which ordinary things are not it.
- **v45 "Unclean! Unclean!"** — the man is made to announce himself. And the two things he must do — torn clothes and loose hair — are the two things Aaron was **forbidden** at 10:6, in the same words, *paro'a* and *parom*. What the high priest may not do in the worst grief of his life is imposed on this man as a permanent condition. He is dressed as a mourner, and the person he is mourning is himself.
- **v46 "He shall live alone"** — *badad yeshev*. Lamentations opens with the same two words turned on a city: *How lonely sits the city that was full of people* — *yashvah vadad ha-ir*. The phrase belongs to this law first, and the poet later says of Jerusalem what the law says of one man with a mark on his skin.
- **vv47–59 the same word, on a coat** — the section that makes *leprosy* impossible. A garment gets *tsara'at*; so do the warp and the weft, and a leather bag; and the procedure is identical to the one for a human being, down to the two seven-day waits. What the cloth gets is called *tsara'at mam'eret*, a **malignant** blight, a word used three times in the Bible — twice here and once in chapter 14, of a house.
- **v55 "an eaten-through spot"** — *pechetet*, which occurs once in the Hebrew Bible, in this verse, and is related to a word for a pit or hollow. The sense is that something has gone through the fabric. It is the last technical term the chapter introduces and there is nothing to check it against.
- **what the chapter never says** — no cause is given, anywhere. Not sin, not contact, not punishment, not uncleanness caught from someone else. The narratives elsewhere do link it to a person's conduct — Miriam at Numbers 12:10, Uzziah at 2 Chronicles 26:19 — but the law itself is silent, and reading those stories back into it turns a procedure for looking at skin into a verdict on the person, which is the one thing these fifty-nine verses refuse to do.
