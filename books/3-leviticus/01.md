# Leviticus 1

## From the Herd

**1** And He called to Moses, and the LORD spoke to him from the tent of meeting, saying,

**2** "Speak to the sons of Israel and say to them: When any of you brings an offering to the LORD, you shall bring it from the livestock — from the herd or from the flock.

**3** If his offering is a burnt offering from the herd, he shall bring an unblemished male. He shall bring it to the entrance of the tent of meeting, so that he may be accepted before the LORD.

**4** He shall lean his hand on the head of the burnt offering, and it will be accepted for him, to make atonement for him.

**5** He shall slaughter the young bull before the LORD, and the sons of Aaron, the priests, shall bring the blood and dash it against the altar all around, the altar at the entrance of the tent of meeting.

**6** He shall skin the burnt offering and cut it into pieces.

**7** The sons of Aaron the priest shall put fire on the altar and lay out wood on the fire.

**8** The sons of Aaron, the priests, shall lay out the pieces, the head and the suet, on the wood burning on the altar.

**9** He shall wash its entrails and its legs with water, and the priest shall turn all of it into smoke on the altar — a burnt offering, an offering by fire, a soothing aroma to the LORD.

## From the Flock

**10** If his burnt offering is from the flock, from the sheep or from the goats, he shall bring an unblemished male.

**11** He shall slaughter it on the north flank of the altar before the LORD, and the sons of Aaron, the priests, shall dash its blood against the altar all around.

**12** He shall cut it into pieces, with its head and its suet, and the priest shall lay them out on the wood burning on the altar.

**13** He shall wash the entrails and the legs with water, and the priest shall bring all of it and turn it into smoke on the altar. It is a burnt offering, an offering by fire, a soothing aroma to the LORD.

## From the Birds

**14** If his offering to the LORD is a burnt offering of birds, he shall bring his offering from the turtledoves or from the young pigeons.

**15** The priest shall bring it to the altar and pinch off its head and turn it into smoke on the altar, and its blood shall be drained out against the side of the altar.

**16** He shall remove its crop with its feathers and throw it beside the altar, on the east side, at the place of the ashes.

**17** He shall tear it open by its wings without severing it, and the priest shall turn it into smoke on the altar, on the burning wood. It is a burnt offering, an offering by fire, a soothing aroma to the LORD.

## Notes

- **v1 "And He called"** — *va-yiqra*, a verb with no subject named, and it is the first word of the book. Hebrew tradition titles books by their opening words, so this book is simply called *Vayiqra*, **And He Called**. The English name comes from the Greek by way of the Latin and means *about the Levites*, which is a description of the contents; the Hebrew name is the first thing that happens in it.
- **v1 and it answers the last verse of Exodus** — Exodus closed with *Moses could not come into the tent of meeting, because the cloud had settled on it* (40:35). The man who built the tent was shut out of it. The next sentence in the Bible is this one: he is called, and the voice comes to him *from the tent of meeting*. The problem at the end of one book is solved in the first four words of the next, and neither book says so.
- **v2 "when any of you"** — *adam ki yaqriv mikkem*, and *adam* is the word from the first chapters of Genesis: the human being, the one made from *adamah*, the ground. Hebrew has *ish* for a man and could have used it. The sacrificial law opens by addressing the species.
- **v2 "offering"** — *qorban*, from *qarav*, **to come near**. The causative *hiqriv* — used four times in this chapter — means to bring something near. A sacrifice in Hebrew is not named for what happens to it; it is named for what it is for. It is the thing you bring near with, because you cannot go near yourself.
- **v3 "burnt offering"** — *olah*, from *alah*, **to go up**. Not named for burning but for rising: the whole animal goes up in smoke and nothing comes back down to the table. It is the only offering in the system where the worshipper eats none of it.
- **v3 "so that he may be accepted before the LORD"** — *li-rtsono*, and the pronoun is not fixed. It can mean **for his acceptance** — so that God will receive him — or **of his own will**, voluntarily. Hebrew grammar allows either and the verse does not choose. Both readings are about the same question: whose willingness is at stake.
- **v4 "he shall lean his hand"** — *ve-samakh yado*, and *samakh* is not a touch. It is the verb for putting weight on a thing: leaning on a wall, propping something up. The hand does not rest on the head, it presses.
- **v5 who does the killing** — the verbs in vv3–6 have one subject, the man who brought the animal: *he shall bring*, *he shall lean his hand*, *he shall slaughter*, *he shall skin it*, *he shall cut it into pieces*. The priests take over only at the blood. An Israelite with a bull at the tent door cuts its throat himself, in front of everyone, and then butchers it. Nothing in the chapter is done on his behalf until the blood is in a bowl.
- **v9 "a soothing aroma"** — *reiach nichoach*, from *nuach*, **to rest, to settle**. The first time this phrase occurs in the Bible is Genesis 8:21, when Noah comes out of the ark and burns offerings, and God smells it and resolves never again to curse the ground. The man's name, *Noach*, is built from the same three letters — though Genesis 5:29 explains it from a different root, *nacham*, to comfort. The smell is one that settles: it is not a fragrance, it is a subsiding.
- **v11 "the north flank of the altar"** — *yerekh ha-mizbeach*, the **thigh** of the altar, the same noun that is the hip joint at Genesis 32:25 and the side of the dwelling at Exodus 40:22. Hebrew gives built things bodies as a matter of course.
- **v15 "pinch off its head"** — *malaq*, a verb that occurs twice in the Hebrew Bible, here and at 5:8, and both times of a bird's neck. 5:8 adds the detail that fixes it: *at the back of its neck, and he shall not sever it*. The head is opened from the bird, not taken off it. This whole section is the poor man's version of the same rite — a bird instead of a bull — and it is given the same closing sentence, word for word, as the bull in v9.
