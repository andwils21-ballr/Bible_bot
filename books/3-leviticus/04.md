# Leviticus 4

## If the Anointed Priest Sins

**1** And the LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**2** "Speak to the sons of Israel, saying: When a person sins by mistake against any of the commands of the LORD about things that are not to be done, and does any one of them —

**3** if it is the anointed priest who sins, bringing guilt on the people, then for the sin he has committed he shall bring an unblemished young bull to the LORD as a sin offering.

**4** And he shall bring the bull to the entrance of the tent of meeting before the LORD, and lean his hand on its head and slaughter it before the LORD.

**5** And the anointed priest shall take some of the bull's blood and bring it into the tent of meeting.

**6** And the priest shall dip his finger in the blood and sprinkle some of it seven times before the LORD, in front of the veil of the holy place.

**7** And the priest shall put some of the blood on the horns of the altar of fragrant incense that stands before the LORD in the tent of meeting; and he shall pour out all the rest of the bull's blood at the base of the altar of burnt offering at the entrance of the tent of meeting.

**8** And he shall lift off all the fat of the bull of the sin offering: the fat covering the entrails and all the fat on the entrails,

**9** and the two kidneys with the fat on them at the loins, and the lobe of the liver, which he shall remove with the kidneys —

**10** just as it is lifted off the ox of the peace offering. And the priest shall turn them into smoke on the altar of burnt offering.

**11** But the hide of the bull and all its flesh, with its head and its legs, and its entrails and its dung —

**12** all the rest of the bull he shall carry outside the camp, to a clean place, to the ash dump, and burn it on a wood fire. At the ash dump it shall be burned.

## If the Whole Assembly Sins

**13** And if the whole assembly of Israel strays, and the matter is hidden from the eyes of the congregation, and they do any one of the things the LORD commanded not to be done, and they are guilty —

**14** when the sin they have committed becomes known, the congregation shall offer a young bull as a sin offering and bring it before the tent of meeting.

**15** And the elders of the assembly shall lean their hands on the bull's head before the LORD, and it shall be slaughtered before the LORD.

**16** And the anointed priest shall bring some of the bull's blood into the tent of meeting,

**17** and the priest shall dip his finger in the blood and sprinkle seven times before the LORD, in front of the veil.

**18** And he shall put some of the blood on the horns of the altar that stands before the LORD in the tent of meeting; and he shall pour out all the rest of the blood at the base of the altar of burnt offering at the entrance of the tent of meeting.

**19** And he shall lift off all its fat and turn it into smoke on the altar.

**20** He shall do with this bull just as he did with the bull of the sin offering. And the priest shall make atonement for them, and they shall be forgiven.

**21** And he shall carry the bull outside the camp and burn it as he burned the first bull. It is the sin offering of the congregation.

## When a Chief Sins

**22** When a chief sins and does by mistake any one of the things the LORD his God commanded not to be done, and he is guilty —

**23** or if the sin he has committed is made known to him — he shall bring as his offering an unblemished male goat.

**24** And he shall lean his hand on the head of the goat and slaughter it in the place where the burnt offering is slaughtered before the LORD. It is a sin offering.

**25** And the priest shall take some of the blood of the sin offering with his finger and put it on the horns of the altar of burnt offering, and pour out its blood at the base of the altar of burnt offering.

**26** He shall turn all its fat into smoke on the altar, like the fat of the peace offering. And the priest shall make atonement for him for his sin, and he shall be forgiven.

## If One of the People Sins

**27** And if anyone from the people of the land sins by mistake, doing any one of the things the LORD commanded not to be done, and he is guilty —

**28** or if the sin he has committed is made known to him — he shall bring as his offering an unblemished female goat for his sin.

**29** And he shall lean his hand on the head of the sin offering and slaughter the sin offering in the place of the burnt offering.

**30** And the priest shall take some of its blood with his finger and put it on the horns of the altar of burnt offering, and pour out all the rest of its blood at the base of the altar.

**31** He shall remove all its fat, as the fat is removed from the peace offering, and the priest shall turn it into smoke on the altar for a soothing aroma to the LORD. And the priest shall make atonement for him, and he shall be forgiven.

**32** And if he brings a lamb as his offering for a sin offering, he shall bring an unblemished female.

**33** And he shall lean his hand on the head of the sin offering and slaughter it as a sin offering in the place where the burnt offering is slaughtered.

**34** And the priest shall take some of the blood of the sin offering with his finger and put it on the horns of the altar of burnt offering, and pour out all the rest of its blood at the base of the altar.

**35** He shall remove all its fat, as the fat of the sheep is removed from the peace offering, and the priest shall turn it into smoke on the altar, on top of the LORD's offerings by fire. And the priest shall make atonement for him for the sin he has committed, and he shall be forgiven."

## Notes

- **vv3, 5, 16 "the anointed priest"** — *ha-kohen ha-mashiach*, and the second word is **messiah**. It means simply *the smeared one*, from *mashach*, to rub oil onto something. In the whole Torah — all five books — the noun occurs in exactly four places, and every one of them is in Leviticus: here three times, and again at 6:22, always of this priest. It is not used of Moses, not of Abraham, not of any king, because there are no kings yet. When it does attach to a king it is in 1 Samuel, of Saul and David, eleven times over. The word an English reader arrives at loaded with expectation enters the Bible as a job description for whoever is currently wearing the oil.
- **v2 "by mistake"** — *bi-shgagah*, from *shagag*, to stray or wander off. The chapter is about accidents from the first sentence to the last, and there is no provision anywhere in it for a sin committed on purpose. Someone who knew and did it anyway is not addressed here at all — Numbers 15:30 will call that acting *with a high hand* and give it a different outcome. What this chapter legislates for is the person who finds out afterward.
- **v2 "sins… sin offering"** — *chata* and *chatat*, the same word. Hebrew uses one noun for **the sin** and for **the animal brought because of it**, and the verses switch between the two senses without a marker: *slaughter the sin offering in the place of the burnt offering* (v29) is literally *slaughter the sin*. English has to pick a different word each time and loses the identification the Hebrew makes automatically.
- **the descending scale, and how far the blood travels** — four cases in one chapter, ranked: the anointed priest, the whole assembly, a chief, one ordinary person. The animal shrinks — bull, bull, male goat, female goat or lamb — and so does the distance the blood is carried. For the first two the priest takes it **inside the tent**, sprinkles it seven times at the veil and puts it on the incense altar. For the last two it goes no further than the horns of the altar out in the courtyard. The more central the sinner, the deeper into the sanctuary the contamination is treated.
- **v22 "When a chief sins"** — the other three cases open with *im*, **if**: *if the anointed priest sins*, *if the whole assembly strays*, *if one person sins*. The chief alone gets *asher*, **when**. One word, and it is the difference between a possibility and a scheduled event.
- **vv11–12 "outside the camp, to a clean place, to the ash dump"** — what is left of the bull after the fat is burned on the altar is not eaten by anybody. It is carted out past the boundary of the settlement and burned on an open fire at the place where the altar ashes are tipped. The text is careful to say that this dump is a **clean** place, *maqom tahor* — the carcass is not being disposed of as refuse, and the ground it burns on is not defiled. It is simply not fit to stay inside.
- **vv20, 26, 31, 35 "and they shall be forgiven"** — *ve-nislach lahem*, and the verb is passive with no one named as doing it. The priest is the subject of the sentence before — *the priest shall make atonement for him* — and then the grammar hands off and nobody is credited with the forgiveness. It reads the same way all four times, at the end of each case, like a stamp.
