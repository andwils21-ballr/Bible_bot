# Leviticus 16

## After the Death of the Two Sons

**1** And the LORD spoke to Moses after the death of Aaron's two sons, when they came near before the LORD and died.

**2** And the LORD said to Moses, "Speak to Aaron your brother, that he is not to come at just any time into the holy place inside the veil, in front of the cover that is on the ark, or he will die — because I appear in the cloud over the cover.

**3** With this Aaron shall come into the holy place: with a bull calf for a sin offering and a ram for a burnt offering.

**4** He shall put on a holy linen tunic, and linen undergarments shall be on his body, and he shall tie a linen sash around him and wind a linen turban on his head. These are holy garments. And he shall bathe his body in water and put them on.

**5** And from the congregation of the sons of Israel he shall take two male goats for a sin offering and one ram for a burnt offering.

## The Two Goats

**6** And Aaron shall present the bull of the sin offering that is his own, and make atonement for himself and for his household.

**7** And he shall take the two goats and set them before the LORD at the entrance of the tent of meeting.

**8** And Aaron shall cast lots over the two goats: one lot for the LORD and one lot for Azazel.

**9** And Aaron shall present the goat on which the lot for the LORD fell, and make it a sin offering.

**10** And the goat on which the lot for Azazel fell shall be set alive before the LORD, to make atonement over it, to send it away to Azazel into the wilderness.

## Inside the Veil

**11** And Aaron shall present the bull of the sin offering that is his own, and make atonement for himself and for his household, and he shall slaughter the bull of the sin offering that is his own.

**12** And he shall take a fire pan full of coals of fire from the altar before the LORD, and his two hands full of finely ground fragrant incense, and bring them inside the veil.

**13** And he shall put the incense on the fire before the LORD, and the cloud of the incense shall cover the cover that is over the Testimony, so that he does not die.

**14** And he shall take some of the blood of the bull and sprinkle it with his finger on the front of the cover, on the east side; and in front of the cover he shall sprinkle some of the blood seven times with his finger.

**15** And he shall slaughter the goat of the sin offering that is for the people and bring its blood inside the veil, and do with its blood as he did with the blood of the bull, and sprinkle it on the cover and in front of the cover.

**16** And he shall make atonement for the holy place because of the uncleannesses of the sons of Israel and because of their rebellions, for all their sins. And so he shall do for the tent of meeting, which dwells with them in the middle of their uncleannesses.

**17** And no one at all shall be in the tent of meeting when he goes in to make atonement in the holy place, until he comes out. And he shall make atonement for himself and for his household and for the whole assembly of Israel.

**18** And he shall go out to the altar that is before the LORD and make atonement for it, and take some of the blood of the bull and some of the blood of the goat and put it on the horns of the altar all around.

**19** And he shall sprinkle some of the blood on it with his finger seven times, and cleanse it and set it apart from the uncleannesses of the sons of Israel.

## The Goat That Goes Away

**20** And when he has finished making atonement for the holy place and the tent of meeting and the altar, he shall present the live goat.

**21** And Aaron shall lean both his hands on the head of the live goat and confess over it all the guilt of the sons of Israel and all their rebellions, for all their sins, and put them on the head of the goat, and send it away into the wilderness by the hand of a man appointed for it.

**22** And the goat shall carry on itself all their guilt to a land cut off; and he shall send the goat away in the wilderness.

**23** And Aaron shall come into the tent of meeting and take off the linen garments he put on when he went into the holy place, and leave them there.

**24** And he shall bathe his body in water in a holy place and put on his garments, and come out and make his burnt offering and the burnt offering of the people, and make atonement for himself and for the people.

**25** And the fat of the sin offering he shall turn into smoke on the altar.

**26** And the one who sends the goat away to Azazel shall wash his clothes and bathe his body in water, and after that he may come into the camp.

**27** And the bull of the sin offering and the goat of the sin offering, whose blood was brought in to make atonement in the holy place, shall be carried outside the camp, and their hides and their flesh and their dung shall be burned in the fire.

**28** And the one who burns them shall wash his clothes and bathe his body in water, and after that he may come into the camp.

## Once a Year

**29** And this shall be a lasting statute for you: in the seventh month, on the tenth of the month, you shall afflict yourselves and do no work at all — neither the native nor the guest living among you —

**30** because on this day atonement shall be made for you, to cleanse you. You shall be clean before the LORD from all your sins.

**31** It is a sabbath of complete rest for you, and you shall afflict yourselves. It is a lasting statute.

**32** And the priest who is anointed and whose hand is filled to serve as priest in place of his father shall make the atonement, and he shall put on the linen garments, the holy garments,

**33** and make atonement for the most holy place; and for the tent of meeting and for the altar he shall make atonement; and for the priests and for all the people of the assembly he shall make atonement.

**34** And this shall be a lasting statute for you, to make atonement for the sons of Israel for all their sins once a year." And he did as the LORD commanded Moses.

## Notes

- **v1 where this chapter is anchored** — the greatest day in Israel's calendar is dated, in its first sentence, by a funeral. *After the death of Aaron's two sons, when they came near before the LORD and died.* Chapter 10 left that event without an explanation and Aaron without an answer. What arrives six chapters later is not an explanation but a procedure: this is how a man goes in there and comes out alive.
- **v4 the clothes he does not wear** — Exodus 28 spends a chapter on gold, blue, purple, crimson, the breastpiece with twelve stones, the golden plate reading *Holy to the LORD*. On the one day of the year the high priest enters the innermost room, he takes all of it off and dresses in plain linen — tunic, undergarments, sash, turban, nothing else. The text still calls them *holy garments*. He goes in looking like nobody.
- **v8 "Azazel"** — *la-azazel*, and nobody knows what it means. The word occurs four times in the Hebrew Bible, all of them in this chapter, and nowhere else. Three readings have been argued for centuries: that it is a compound of *ez*, goat, and *azal*, to go away — the source of English **scapegoat**, coined by Tyndale; that it is an abstract noun meaning utter removal; or that it is a name. The grammar of this verse leans to the third, because of the parallel it sets up — *one lot for the LORD and one lot for Azazel* — where the matching term is a person. The text says no more than that, and neither can anyone else.
- **vv8–10 what the lot decides** — two goats, identical, bought by the same congregation for the same purpose. One is killed and its blood carried into the holiest room in the world; the other is stood up alive, loaded, and walked out into the desert. Nothing about either animal chooses which. A lot is cast, and that is the whole of the difference between them.
- **v13 the cloud he hides behind** — v2 says God appears *in the cloud over the cover*, and v13 has the high priest raise a cloud of incense to cover that same cover, *so that he does not die*. The smoke is not decoration and not atmosphere; it is a screen. The one man permitted into the room goes in and immediately obscures his own view of it.
- **v16 "which dwells with them in the middle of their uncleannesses"** — this is what the last six chapters were building toward. The tent is not a clean place set apart from a dirty camp; it is a holy thing sitting in the middle of ordinary human mess, and 15:31 already said the risk that creates. The atonement in this chapter is made **for the building** before it is made for anybody in it. The sanctuary itself needs cleaning, and what has dirtied it is everyone living around it.
- **v17 "no one at all"** — *ve-khol adam lo yihyeh*, and *adam* is the whole human race. Not an assistant, not another priest. For the length of this rite the most populous building in the camp holds one man, and the text pauses to say so.
- **v21 "confess over it"** — *hitvaddah*, from *yadah*, the verb that first appeared at 5:5 where an ordinary Israelite had to say out loud what he had done, and which also gives the **thanksgiving** offering its name at 7:12. Here it reaches its largest form: one man, hands on a goat's head, saying the wrongs of an entire nation aloud. The words are not recorded.
- **v21 "both his hands"** — the written consonants say *his hand*, singular; the reading tradition says *his hands*. Leviticus has five such places, and the other one where Aaron's hand is at issue is 9:22, where he lifts it to bless the people. Both are his hands on or toward Israel, and in both the tradition reads two where the letters say one. Note also that everywhere else in Leviticus one hand is leaned on an animal's head (1:4, 4:4). This is the only sacrifice that takes both.
- **v22 "a land cut off"** — *erets gezerah*, from *gazar*, **to cut**. The noun turns up otherwise only in Ezekiel's temple plan, where it names a separated area. So the goat goes to a cut-off land, which is what the wilderness is, and the phrase occurs once in this sense. The animal is not killed and is not an offering. It is transport.
- **vv7, 10, 21–22 and 14:7** — two chapters ago a man returning from outside the camp had two birds taken: one killed over running water, one dipped in its blood and released into open country. Here it is two goats: one killed, one released into the wilderness. The same verb carries both, *shillach*, **to send off**, and the same logic — something dies, and something else walks away carrying what was wrong, and is never mentioned again.
- **v29 "you shall afflict yourselves"** — *te'annu et nafshoteikhem*, **you shall afflict your souls**, and *nefesh* is the throat and the appetite as much as the inner life. The Torah does not use the word **fast** here or anywhere in this chapter. What is commanded is a day of doing without, undefined, and the tradition that made it a fast had to read the definition out of this phrase. What the verse does say plainly is who it binds: the native and the foreigner living among them, in the same clause.
- **v34 "once a year"** — *achat ba-shanah*, the last three words before the chapter closes. Everything else in Leviticus runs daily, or when someone sins, or when a woman gives birth, or when a mark appears. This happens once, on a fixed date, whether or not anything has gone wrong — the only rite in the book scheduled by the calendar rather than by an event.
