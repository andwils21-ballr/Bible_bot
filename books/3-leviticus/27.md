# Leviticus 27

## Vowing a Person

**1** And the LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**2** "Speak to the sons of Israel and say to them: When a man makes a special vow to the LORD by the valuation of persons,

**3** the valuation of a male from twenty years old up to sixty years old shall be fifty shekels of silver, by the shekel of the sanctuary.

**4** And if it is a female, the valuation shall be thirty shekels.

**5** And if the person is from five years old up to twenty years old, the valuation of a male shall be twenty shekels, and of a female ten shekels.

**6** And if from a month old up to five years old, the valuation of a male shall be five shekels of silver, and of a female three shekels of silver.

**7** And if the person is sixty years old or more, the valuation of a male shall be fifteen shekels, and of a female ten shekels.

**8** But if he is too poor to pay the valuation, he shall be made to stand before the priest, and the priest shall set a value on him. The priest shall value him according to what the one who made the vow can afford.

## Vowing an Animal

**9** And if it is an animal of the kind that is brought as an offering to the LORD, anything of it that he gives to the LORD shall be holy.

**10** He shall not exchange it or substitute it, good for bad or bad for good. And if he does substitute one animal for another, both it and its substitute shall be holy.

**11** And if it is any unclean animal, of the kind that is not brought as an offering to the LORD, he shall make the animal stand before the priest,

**12** and the priest shall value it as good or bad. As the priest values it, so it shall be.

**13** And if he wants to redeem it, he shall add a fifth to its valuation.

## Vowing a House or a Field

**14** And when a man sets apart his house as holy to the LORD, the priest shall value it as good or bad. As the priest values it, so it shall stand.

**15** And if the one who set it apart wants to redeem his house, he shall add a fifth of the valuation in silver to it, and it shall be his.

**16** And if a man sets apart to the LORD part of the field he holds, its valuation shall be by its seed: fifty shekels of silver for a homer of barley seed.

**17** If he sets apart his field starting from the year of jubilee, it shall stand at the full valuation.

**18** But if he sets apart his field after the jubilee, the priest shall calculate the price for him by the years that remain until the year of jubilee, and it shall be deducted from the valuation.

**19** And if the one who set apart the field wants to redeem it, he shall add a fifth of the valuation in silver to it, and it shall stand as his.

**20** But if he does not redeem the field, or if he has sold it to another man, it can no longer be redeemed.

**21** And when the field goes out in the jubilee, it shall be holy to the LORD, like a devoted field. It shall become the priest's holding.

**22** And if he sets apart to the LORD a field he bought, which is not part of the field he holds,

**23** the priest shall calculate for him the amount of the valuation up to the year of jubilee, and he shall pay the valuation that same day as a holy gift to the LORD.

**24** In the year of jubilee the field shall return to the one he bought it from, to the one who holds the land.

**25** And every valuation shall be by the shekel of the sanctuary. Twenty gerahs make a shekel.

## What Cannot Be Vowed

**26** But no one may set apart a firstborn animal, which as a firstborn already belongs to the LORD. Whether ox or sheep, it is the LORD's.

**27** And if it is among the unclean animals, he may ransom it at the valuation and add a fifth to it. And if it is not redeemed, it shall be sold at the valuation.

**28** But nothing that a man devotes to the LORD from anything he has, whether human or animal or field he holds, shall be sold or redeemed. Everything devoted is most holy to the LORD.

**29** No one devoted from among people shall be ransomed. He shall surely be put to death.

## The Tenth

**30** And every tenth of the land, whether grain or the fruit of the trees, is the LORD's. It is holy to the LORD.

**31** And if a man wants to redeem any of his tenth, he shall add a fifth to it.

**32** And every tenth of the herd and the flock, every tenth one that passes under the rod, shall be holy to the LORD.

**33** He shall not pick out good from bad, and he shall not substitute it. And if he does substitute it, both it and its substitute shall be holy. It cannot be redeemed."

**34** These are the commandments that the LORD commanded Moses for the sons of Israel on Mount Sinai.

## Notes

- **v2 "a special vow"** — *yafli neder*, from *pala*, the root of *pele*, **wonder**: the verb of 22:21. The book's last chapter opens with the vow a person makes beyond anything required.
- **v2 "the valuation"** — *be-erkekha*, literally *by your valuation*, but the *your* has frozen into the word itself and no longer means anyone. v23 proves it: *ha-erkekha*, **the** your-valuation, which is impossible grammar unless *erkekha* had become a fixed term.
- **vv3–7 the price of a person** — a vow to give a person to God is paid in silver, and the amounts run by age and sex. **Thirty shekels** is the valuation of a woman here. It is also the price owed when an ox gores a slave to death (Exodus 21:32), the wage Zechariah is paid and throws away (Zechariah 11:12–13), and the sum paid to betray Jesus, which Matthew ties to those words (Matthew 27:9, where he names the prophet as Jeremiah).
- **v8 "according to what the one who made the vow can afford"** — *al pi asher tassig yad*, as far as his hand reaches. The same law that sets fifty shekels for a grown man sets the poor man's price by what he has.
- **v10 "both it and its substitute shall be holy"** — the man who tries to swap a weaker animal for the one he vowed does not get the better one back. He loses both.
- **v28 "devoted"** — *cherem*, what is given over to God so completely that it can never come back into ordinary use: not sold, not redeemed. The same word names the spoil of Jericho that Achan took (Joshua 7:1). With property it goes to the priests (v21). v29 applies it to a person, and there the text says only that the person *shall surely be put to death* and cannot be ransomed; it gives no case and no further rule.
- **v30 "every tenth"** — *ma'aser*, the first law of the tithe. Abraham had given a tenth of everything to Melchizedek (Genesis 14:20), and Jacob had vowed a tenth at Bethel (Genesis 28:22). The book ends by making the patriarchs' voluntary tenth a standing law.
- **v32 "every tenth one that passes under the rod"** — the shepherd counted the flock as it went out one by one under his staff, and every tenth animal was the LORD's, good or bad, without choosing. Ezekiel uses the picture for God counting His people back from exile: *I will make you pass under the rod* (Ezekiel 20:37).
- **v34 "on Mount Sinai"** — Leviticus opens in the tent of meeting (1:1) and closes on the mountain, as chapters 25 and 26 did (25:1, 26:46).
