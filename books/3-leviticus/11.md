# Leviticus 11

## On the Land

**1** And the LORD spoke to Moses and to Aaron, saying to them,

**2** "Speak to the sons of Israel, saying: These are the creatures you may eat out of all the animals on the earth.

**3** Whatever parts the hoof and has the hoof cleft in two and brings up the cud, among animals — that you may eat.

**4** But these you shall not eat, of those that bring up the cud or part the hoof: the camel, because it brings up the cud but does not part the hoof — it is unclean for you;

**5** and the rock badger, because it brings up the cud but does not part the hoof — it is unclean for you;

**6** and the hare, because it brings up the cud but does not part the hoof — it is unclean for you;

**7** and the pig, because it parts the hoof and has the hoof cleft in two, but does not chew the cud — it is unclean for you.

**8** You shall not eat their flesh, and you shall not touch their carcasses. They are unclean for you.

## In the Water

**9** These you may eat of all that is in the water: anything in the water, in the seas and in the streams, that has fins and scales — those you may eat.

**10** But anything in the seas and in the streams that does not have fins and scales, among all that swarms in the water and among all the living creatures in the water — they are loathsome to you.

**11** They shall be loathsome to you. You shall not eat their flesh, and you shall loathe their carcasses.

**12** Anything in the water that does not have fins and scales is loathsome to you.

## The Birds

**13** And these you shall loathe among the birds — they shall not be eaten, they are loathsome: the eagle, the bearded vulture, the black vulture,

**14** the kite and the falcon of every kind,

**15** every raven of every kind,

**16** the ostrich, the nighthawk, the gull, and the hawk of every kind,

**17** the owl, the cormorant, the great owl,

**18** the white owl, the pelican, the carrion vulture,

**19** the stork, the heron of every kind, the hoopoe, and the bat.

## Things That Swarm

**20** Every winged swarming thing that goes on four is loathsome to you.

**21** But these you may eat of every winged swarming thing that goes on four: those that have jointed legs above their feet, for leaping with them on the ground.

**22** These of them you may eat: the locust of every kind, the bald locust of every kind, the cricket of every kind, and the grasshopper of every kind.

**23** But every other winged swarming thing that has four feet is loathsome to you.

## What Touching Them Does

**24** And by these you will become unclean. Anyone who touches their carcasses will be unclean until evening,

**25** and anyone who carries any of their carcasses shall wash his clothes and be unclean until evening —

**26** every animal that parts the hoof but does not have it cleft in two, and does not bring up the cud. They are unclean for you; anyone who touches them will be unclean.

**27** And every creature that goes on its paws, among all the creatures that go on four, is unclean for you. Anyone who touches their carcasses will be unclean until evening,

**28** and the one who carries their carcasses shall wash his clothes and be unclean until evening. They are unclean for you.

**29** And these are unclean for you among the swarming things that swarm on the ground: the mole, the mouse, the great lizard of every kind,

**30** the gecko, the monitor lizard, the wall lizard, the skink, and the chameleon.

**31** These are the unclean for you among all the swarming things. Anyone who touches them when they are dead will be unclean until evening.

**32** And anything they fall on when they are dead becomes unclean — any vessel of wood, or clothing, or hide, or sackcloth, any vessel that work is done with. It shall be put in water and be unclean until evening, and then it is clean.

**33** And every earthen vessel that any of them falls into — everything in it becomes unclean, and you shall break it.

**34** Any food that could be eaten, on which water has come, becomes unclean; and any drink that could be drunk, in any vessel, becomes unclean.

**35** And anything their carcass falls on becomes unclean. An oven or a stove shall be torn down. They are unclean, and unclean they shall be for you.

**36** But a spring or a cistern, a gathering of water, stays clean; and whoever touches their carcass in it becomes unclean.

**37** And if any of their carcass falls on any seed for sowing that is to be sown, it is clean.

**38** But if water has been put on the seed and part of their carcass falls on it, it is unclean for you.

**39** And if an animal that you may eat dies, whoever touches its carcass will be unclean until evening.

**40** And whoever eats from its carcass shall wash his clothes and be unclean until evening; and whoever carries its carcass shall wash his clothes and be unclean until evening.

## Be Holy

**41** And every swarming thing that swarms on the ground is loathsome. It shall not be eaten.

**42** Whatever goes on its belly, and whatever goes on four, up to whatever has many feet — every swarming thing that swarms on the ground — you shall not eat them, because they are loathsome.

**43** Do not make yourselves loathsome with any swarming thing that swarms, and do not make yourselves unclean by them, and so become unclean.

**44** Because I am the LORD your God. Set yourselves apart and be holy, because I am holy. And do not make yourselves unclean with any swarming thing that moves on the ground.

**45** Because I am the LORD who is bringing you up from the land of Egypt, to be God to you. And you shall be holy, because I am holy."

**46** This is the instruction for animals and birds and every living creature that moves in the water and every creature that swarms on the ground,

**47** to separate between the unclean and the clean, and between the creature that may be eaten and the creature that may not be eaten.

## Notes

- **the chapter never says why** — forty-seven verses of lists, and not one reason. Not health, not cruelty, not idolatry, not anything. The only thing resembling an explanation arrives at the very end and is about God rather than about the animals: *be holy, because I am holy*. Every reason an English reader has probably been given for these rules was supplied by somebody later; the text withholds all of them, and the withholding is deliberate enough to be a feature of the chapter.
- **v3 the two tests** — a split hoof and chewing the cud, and an animal needs both. What makes the section memorable is that the four exceptions are argued *out loud*: camel, rock badger, hare, pig, each with its reason spelled out in the same sentence. The law is not content to list what is banned; it shows its working, and it is the only place in Leviticus that does.
- **v7 the pig** — it gets one verse, the same shape as the camel's and the hare's, and not a syllable more. Of all the animals named here it is the one that became the emblem of the whole system, and the text treats it as the fourth item on a list of four. Whatever weight it later carried is not in these words.
- **vv13–19 the birds cannot all be identified** — twenty names, and the English in every Bible is a best guess for most of them. Several occur only in this list and its twin at Deuteronomy 14, which gives a translator nothing to work with: a word used twice, in a list, with no description attached. *Nesher* may be the eagle or the griffon vulture; *tachmas*, *shalakh*, *racham* and *dukhifat* are essentially unknown. What survives clearly is the shape of the list — birds of prey, carrion birds, water birds — and one creature at the end that is not a bird at all.
- **vv18, 30 the same name in two lists** — *tinshemet* appears in the bird list at v18 and again at v30 among the things that crawl on the ground. It occurs three times in the whole Hebrew Bible, twice in this chapter, in two different categories. English Bibles print two different animals for it, usually an owl and a chameleon, which is a solution rather than a reading. The text uses one word twice and does not remark on it.
- **v19 "and the bat"** — the last entry in a list of birds. The text sorts creatures by how they move and where they live, not by anatomy; anything that flies belongs with flying things. The same logic runs through v20, where insects *go on four* although they have six legs, and v27, where what matters about an animal is that it walks on its paws.
- **vv10, 13, 41 "loathsome"** — *sheqets*, and it is not the same word as *tamei*, unclean. Unclean is a condition, and it can be got rid of: wash, wait until evening, and it is over. *Sheqets* is a verdict, and the chapter also uses it as a verb — *you shall loathe them* (v11, 13) — which is the only thing in these forty-seven verses commanded as an attitude rather than an act.
- **v20 "goes on four"** — insects have six legs. The phrase is not a mistake about anatomy, it is a category: walking on legs rather than standing upright, which is the same distinction v27 draws for animals that go on their paws and v42 draws for whatever goes on its belly. The chapter's categories are ways of moving.
- **v36 "a gathering of water"** — *miqveh mayim*, and the phrase is out of Genesis 1:10: *and the gathering of the waters He called seas*. Here it names the one thing in the chapter that cannot be made unclean by contact. A pot is smashed, an oven is pulled down, a cistern is fine. The word later becomes the name of the ritual bath, *mikveh*, built on this verse.
- **v44 "be holy, because I am holy"** — the first time this sentence appears in the Bible. *Qadosh ani*, **I am holy**, occurs five times in all, and every one of them is in Leviticus. It arrives at the end of a chapter about lizards and fins, and the logic is not that the creatures are evil but that Israel is being made to resemble somebody.
- **v45 "who is bringing you up"** — *ha-ma'aleh*, a participle, not a past tense, and not the usual verb. The standard formula is *I brought you out*, *hotseti*, a finished act. Here it is ongoing and it is *alah*, to go up — the same verb Pharaoh feared at Exodus 1:10 and the one God used to Jacob at Genesis 46:4. The exodus is spoken of as still in progress while the people are standing at Sinai arguing about grasshoppers.
- **v47 "to separate between"** — *le-havdil*, closing the chapter with the verb it was handed in 10:10, which is the verb of the first three days of Genesis. The priest's job and the diet laws turn out to be the same job: keeping apart things that would otherwise run together.
