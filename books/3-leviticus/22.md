# Leviticus 22

## The Priests and the Holy Things

**1** And the LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**2** "Speak to Aaron and to his sons, that they keep themselves apart from the holy things of the sons of Israel, which they set apart for Me, and not profane My holy name. I am the LORD.

**3** Say to them: Throughout your generations, any man of all your offspring who comes near to the holy things that the sons of Israel set apart for the LORD while his uncleanness is on him — that person shall be cut off from My presence. I am the LORD.

**4** No man at all of the offspring of Aaron who has blight or a discharge shall eat of the holy things until he is clean. And whoever touches anything made unclean by a dead person, or a man who has an emission of semen,

**5** or a man who touches any swarming thing that makes him unclean, or any person who makes him unclean, whatever his uncleanness —

**6** the person who touches it shall be unclean until evening, and shall not eat of the holy things unless he has washed his body in water.

**7** And when the sun goes down he will be clean, and afterward he may eat of the holy things, because it is his bread.

**8** One that died on its own or one torn by beasts he shall not eat, and so make himself unclean by it. I am the LORD.

**9** And they shall keep My charge, so that they do not carry sin because of it and die for it when they profane it. I am the LORD who makes them holy.

**10** No outsider shall eat a holy thing. A priest's resident or a hired man shall not eat a holy thing.

**11** But if a priest buys a person, bought with his money, he may eat of it, and those born in his house — they may eat of his bread.

**12** And a priest's daughter who is married to an outsider shall not eat of the holy contributions.

**13** But a priest's daughter who is widowed or divorced, and has no child, and has come back to her father's house as in her youth, may eat of her father's bread. But no outsider shall eat of it.

**14** And if a man eats a holy thing by mistake, he shall add a fifth to it and give the holy thing to the priest.

**15** And they shall not profane the holy things of the sons of Israel, which they raise up to the LORD,

**16** and so make them carry guilt that must be paid for, by eating their holy things. For I am the LORD who makes them holy."

## An Offering Must Be Whole

**17** And the LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**18** "Speak to Aaron and to his sons and to all the sons of Israel, and say to them: Any man at all of the house of Israel, or of the foreigners living in Israel, who brings his offering for any of their vows or any of their freewill offerings, which they bring to the LORD as a burnt offering —

**19** so that it is accepted for you, it must be an unblemished male, from the cattle, from the sheep, or from the goats.

**20** Anything that has a blemish you shall not bring, because it will not be accepted for you.

**21** And if a man brings a sacrifice of peace offering to the LORD to fulfill a special vow, or as a freewill offering, from the herd or from the flock, it must be unblemished to be accepted. There shall be no blemish in it.

**22** Blind, or broken, or maimed, or with a wart, or a scab, or a skin eruption — you shall not bring these to the LORD, and you shall not put an offering by fire from them on the altar to the LORD.

**23** An ox or a sheep with a limb too long or too short you may make into a freewill offering, but for a vow it will not be accepted.

**24** One whose testicles are bruised, or crushed, or torn, or cut, you shall not bring to the LORD, and in your land you shall not do this.

**25** And from the hand of a foreigner you shall not bring any of these as the bread of your God, because their ruin is in them; there is a blemish in them. They will not be accepted for you."

## The Eighth Day

**26** And the LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**27** "When an ox or a sheep or a goat is born, it shall be seven days under its mother, and from the eighth day on it will be accepted as an offering by fire to the LORD.

**28** And an ox or a sheep — you shall not slaughter it and its young on the same day.

**29** And when you sacrifice a thanksgiving sacrifice to the LORD, sacrifice it so that it is accepted for you.

**30** It shall be eaten on that same day; you shall leave none of it until morning. I am the LORD.

**31** And you shall keep My commandments and do them. I am the LORD.

**32** And you shall not profane My holy name, and I will be shown holy among the sons of Israel. I am the LORD who makes you holy,

**33** who brought you out of the land of Egypt to be your God. I am the LORD."

## Notes

- **v2 "keep themselves apart"** — *ve-yinnazru*, from *nazar*, the root of *nazir*, the Nazirite who *separates himself* to the LORD (Numbers 6:2). The men who handle the holy things every day are told to hold back from them whenever they are unclean. Closeness is not a standing right; it is renewed each time they are clean.
- **v3 "cut off from My presence"** — *mi-lefanai*, from before My face. Every other time Leviticus says what a person is cut off from, it is *his people* (7:20, 17:4, 20:17, and eleven more). Here, for a priest, the loss is named differently: not his people, but the face of God.
- **vv10–11 the slave eats; the hired man does not** — the line is drawn around the household, not around freedom. A man the priest bought *with his money* belongs to the priest's house and eats its bread; a hired man, free and paid, only works for it and does not. The Passover draws the same line in the same words: the bought slave may eat, *a resident and a hired man shall not* (Exodus 12:44–45).
- **vv12–13 the priest's daughter** — marriage to a man outside the priesthood takes her out of the house that eats the holy portion. If she is widowed or divorced with no child and comes back *as in her youth*, *ki-ne'ureha*, she is at her father's table again and the right comes back with her. Belonging here is not blood alone; it is whose bread you eat.
- **v14 "add a fifth"** — the same repayment as 5:16 for sinning *against the holy thing*: the value in full, one fifth more, given to the priest.
- **v21 "to fulfill a special vow"** — *le-falle neder*, and the verb is from *pala*, the root of *pele*, **wonder**: the word of 15:11 in Exodus, *doing wonders*. The same verb opens the law of vows in 27:2. The sense is a vow set apart as extraordinary, beyond the ordinary gift.
- **v22 "with a wart"** — *yabbelet* occurs here and nowhere else in the Hebrew Bible. The Greek has *myrmēkiōnta*, **warted**; the other old reading is a running sore, and with one occurrence in a list, nothing in the Hebrew settles it. *Scab* and *skin eruption* are the two words of 21:20: the same faults that keep a priest from the altar keep an animal off it.
- **v24 "in your land you shall not do this"** — the Hebrew does not say what *this* is. It can mean *do not offer such animals*, which repeats the first half of the verse, or *do not do this to animals at all*, which would forbid gelding. Both readings are old, and the words allow either.
- **v25 "a foreigner"** — *ben nekhar*, a son of a foreign land, and not the same person as the *ger* of v18, the foreigner living in Israel, who may bring his own offerings. The man from outside may not supply the animal. *Their ruin is in them* is *mashchatam*, from *shachat*, the verb used of all flesh that *had ruined its way* before the flood (Genesis 6:12).
- **v27 "from the eighth day on"** — seven days with its mother, and on the eighth day it may be offered: the rule for a firstling in Exodus 22:30, now for every animal. The eighth day is the day of a boy's circumcision (12:3) and the day the priests' ordination was complete and their service began (9:1). In this book the eighth day is the day something is first given over to God.
- **v28 "it and its young"** — *oto ve-et beno*, it and its son. The law does not say mother or father; it forbids killing a parent and its offspring on the same day.
- **v32 "I will be shown holy"** — *ve-niqdashti*, the form Moses used at the death of Nadab and Abihu: *through those near Me I will be shown holy* (10:3). The chapter turns on one pair of words: **profane**, *chillel*, four times (vv2, 9, 15, 32), against **holy**. God's name is either profaned or shown holy by how Israel handles what belongs to Him.
