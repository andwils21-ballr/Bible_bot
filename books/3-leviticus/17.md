# Leviticus 17

## Nowhere Else to Kill It

**1** The LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**2** "Speak to Aaron and to his sons and to all the sons of Israel, and say to them: This is what the LORD has commanded:

**3** Any man of the house of Israel who slaughters an ox or a sheep or a goat in the camp, or who slaughters it outside the camp,

**4** and has not brought it to the entrance of the tent of meeting to present it as an offering to the LORD before the dwelling of the LORD — blood shall be counted against that man. He has shed blood, and that man shall be cut off from among his people.

**5** This is so that the sons of Israel will take the sacrifices they have been making out in the open field and bring them to the LORD, to the entrance of the tent of meeting, to the priest, and offer them as peace offerings to the LORD.

**6** The priest shall dash the blood against the altar of the LORD at the entrance of the tent of meeting, and turn the fat into smoke as a soothing aroma to the LORD.

**7** They shall no longer offer their sacrifices to the goat-spirits they have been prostituting themselves to. This shall be a lasting statute for them through their generations.

**8** Say to them: Any man of the house of Israel, or of the guests living among them, who offers up a burnt offering or a sacrifice

**9** and does not bring it to the entrance of the tent of meeting to offer it to the LORD — that man shall be cut off from his people.

## The Life Is in the Blood

**10** Any man of the house of Israel, or of the guests living among them, who eats any blood — I will set My face against the person who eats the blood, and cut that person off from among his people.

**11** For the life of the flesh is in the blood, and I have given it to you on the altar to make atonement for your lives; for it is the blood that makes atonement by the life.

**12** That is why I have said to the sons of Israel: None of you shall eat blood, and the guest living among you shall not eat blood.

**13** Any man of the sons of Israel, or of the guests living among them, who hunts and catches an animal or a bird that may be eaten shall pour out its blood and cover it with earth.

**14** For the life of all flesh — its blood is its life. I said to the sons of Israel: You shall not eat the blood of any flesh, because the life of all flesh is its blood. Anyone who eats it shall be cut off.

**15** Any person, native or guest, who eats an animal that died on its own or was torn by beasts shall wash his clothes and bathe in water, and be unclean until evening, and then he is clean.

**16** But if he does not wash them and does not bathe his body, he carries his guilt."

## Notes

- **v4 "He has shed blood"** — *dam shafakh*, and it is the language of murder. Genesis 9:6: *one who sheds the blood of the human, by the human his blood shall be shed* — the same verb with the same noun. A man who butchers his own ox in the wrong place is described in the vocabulary reserved for killing a person. The verb before it is *yechashev*, **shall be counted**, the accounting word from 7:18 and from Genesis 15:6: something is entered against his name.
- **v7 "the goat-spirits"** — *se'irim*, **hairy ones**, the ordinary Hebrew word for he-goats, here plural with the article and clearly meaning something that can be sacrificed **to**. And it is the same word, in the same form, as the two goats of the previous chapter (16:8). One chapter after the Day of Atonement assigns two *se'irim* by lot, the next forbids sacrificing to the *se'irim*. Whatever they are — the word is used again of the creatures that dance in Babylon's ruins at Isaiah 13:21 — the text will not say more about them than that Israel has been going to them.
- **v7 "prostituting themselves to"** — *zonim achareihem*, from *zanah*, the plain word for prostitution. It is the Bible's standing metaphor for worshipping anything else, and it is not a polite one: the relationship is marriage and the offense is infidelity, not error. Softening it to *going astray* removes the only thing the sentence is doing.
- **vv3–9 the point of the restriction** — an Israelite may not kill an ox anywhere but at the one door. Not because slaughter is wrong, but because sacrifice in the open field is what they have been doing, and to whom. The law does not argue with the practice; it moves it. Everything gets brought to one place where a priest can see it.
- **v11 "the life of the flesh is in the blood"** — *nefesh ha-basar ba-dam hi*, and *nefesh* is the word English Bibles usually print as **soul**. It is not a spirit that can leave a body; it is breath, throat, appetite, the living of a living thing — the word used of animals in Genesis 1 before it is ever used of a person. So this sentence is not metaphysics. It is a statement about what blood **is**: the life itself, in liquid form, which is why it may not be eaten and why it is the one substance that can be put on the altar in place of a life.
- **v11 "makes atonement by the life"** — *ba-nefesh yekhapper*, three words that can be read at least three ways: the blood atones **by means of** the life in it, or **in place of** a life, or **at the cost of** a life. The preposition *be* carries all three and the verse does not choose. It is the single most quoted sentence in Leviticus and its last clause has never been settled.
- **v13 covering the blood of a hunted animal** — a deer or a bird killed out in the hills cannot be brought to the altar, so its blood is poured on the ground and buried. No priest, no altar, nobody watching. The rule reaches the one situation where there is no institution to enforce it, and makes it a matter of what a man does alone on a hillside with a spade.
- **vv8, 10, 12, 13, 15 "the guest"** — *ger*, and the chapter names him in every single paragraph, five times in sixteen verses. The blood law is one of the few in Leviticus that binds the non-Israelite exactly as it binds the native. What is at stake here is not identity; it is the ground everyone is standing on.
- **v10 "I will set My face against"** — *ve-natatti panai*, and God stops speaking through a procedure and speaks in the first person about an individual. Everywhere else the penalty is *he shall be cut off*, passive, with nobody named as doing the cutting. Here the sentence names Him twice over: *I will set My face*, *and I will cut that person off*.
