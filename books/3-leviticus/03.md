# Leviticus 3

## From the Herd

**1** And if his offering is a peace offering — if he is bringing it from the herd, whether male or female, he shall bring it unblemished before the LORD.

**2** And he shall lean his hand on the head of his offering and slaughter it at the entrance of the tent of meeting, and the sons of Aaron, the priests, shall dash the blood against the altar all around.

**3** And he shall bring from the peace offering an offering by fire to the LORD: the fat covering the entrails and all the fat on the entrails,

**4** and the two kidneys with the fat on them at the loins, and the lobe of the liver, which he shall remove with the kidneys.

**5** And the sons of Aaron shall turn it into smoke on the altar, on top of the burnt offering on the burning wood — an offering by fire, a soothing aroma to the LORD.

## From the Flock

**6** And if his offering for a peace offering to the LORD is from the flock, male or female, he shall bring it unblemished.

**7** If he is bringing a sheep as his offering, he shall bring it before the LORD,

**8** and he shall lean his hand on the head of his offering and slaughter it before the tent of meeting, and the sons of Aaron shall dash its blood against the altar all around.

**9** And he shall bring from the peace offering an offering by fire to the LORD: its fat — the whole fat tail, which he shall remove close to the backbone — and the fat covering the entrails and all the fat on the entrails,

**10** and the two kidneys with the fat on them at the loins, and the lobe of the liver, which he shall remove with the kidneys.

**11** And the priest shall turn it into smoke on the altar — food, an offering by fire to the LORD.

## A Goat

**12** And if his offering is a goat, he shall bring it before the LORD,

**13** and he shall lean his hand on its head and slaughter it before the tent of meeting, and the sons of Aaron shall dash its blood against the altar all around.

**14** And he shall bring from it his offering, an offering by fire to the LORD: the fat covering the entrails and all the fat on the entrails,

**15** and the two kidneys with the fat on them at the loins, and the lobe of the liver, which he shall remove with the kidneys.

**16** And the priest shall turn them into smoke on the altar — food, an offering by fire, for a soothing aroma. All fat belongs to the LORD.

**17** A lasting statute through your generations, in all your settlements: you shall eat no fat and no blood.

## Notes

- **v1 "peace offering"** — *zevach shelamim*, and the second word is built on the root behind *shalom*. That root is not primarily about the absence of conflict; it is about a thing being **whole, complete, paid up, settled**. So the name has been rendered *peace offering*, *offering of well-being*, and *fellowship offering*, and each catches part of it. What separates this sacrifice from the two before it is what happens to the meat: the burnt offering goes up entirely, the grain offering feeds the priests, and this one is the offering the worshipper and his household sit down and **eat** (7:15–16). The fat and the blood go to the altar; the rest is dinner.
- **v1 "whether male or female"** — the burnt offering must be *a male*, stated twice in chapter 1 (vv3, 10). Here the sex is explicitly optional, and it is the only offering in the opening chapters where the text says so. Nothing is made of it.
- **vv3, 9, 14 "the fat"** — *chelev*, and it is not the fat marbled through meat. It is the hard suet that sheathes the organs — around the entrails, over the kidneys, across the loins — the fat a butcher can lift off in a sheet. That is the portion the altar takes, every time, in all three versions of the rite, and the list is repeated word for word three times so no one can be uncertain which fat is meant.
- **v9 "the whole fat tail"** — *alyah*, the heavy fat-storing tail of the broad-tailed sheep of the region, which can weigh several pounds on its own and was the prized cut. It is named only for the sheep, because only the sheep has one. And *he-atseh*, the point it is cut at, occurs **once** in the Hebrew Bible, in this verse, so what exactly it names cannot be recovered — the backbone, the rump, the base of the spine have all been proposed from the anatomy rather than from the word.
- **vv11, 16 "food"** — *lechem*, the ordinary word for **bread**, and by extension for food in general. The sentence says, flatly, that what goes up off the altar is God's food. The Hebrew Bible uses this language freely in the ritual laws and then turns on it elsewhere in the sharpest terms available: *If I were hungry I would not tell you, for the world is Mine and all that fills it. Do I eat the flesh of bulls, or drink the blood of goats?* (Psalm 50:12–13). Both sentences are in the same book of scripture, and neither is softened to accommodate the other.
- **v16 "All fat belongs to the LORD"** — four words in Hebrew, *kol chelev la-YHWH*, dropped at the end of the ritual instructions with no argument attached. It is the reason for everything the chapter has just described, and it is stated after the fact rather than before.
- **v17 the one line addressed to everybody** — the chapter is procedure for priests until this verse, which turns and speaks to the whole population, everywhere they live, permanently: **no fat and no blood**. Blood is given a reason later (17:11, *the life of the flesh is in the blood*); the fat never is. Two prohibitions of equal weight in one sentence, and the text explains one of them and not the other.
