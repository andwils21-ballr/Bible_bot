# Leviticus 18

## Not Like Egypt, Not Like Canaan

**1** And the LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**2** "Speak to the sons of Israel and say to them: I am the LORD your God.

**3** You shall not do as they do in the land of Egypt, where you lived; and you shall not do as they do in the land of Canaan, where I am bringing you; and you shall not walk in their statutes.

**4** You shall do My judgments and keep My statutes, to walk in them. I am the LORD your God.

**5** And you shall keep My statutes and My judgments, which a person shall do and live by them. I am the LORD.

## Whose Nakedness Is Not to Be Uncovered

**6** No man shall come near to anyone of his own flesh to uncover nakedness. I am the LORD.

**7** You shall not uncover the nakedness of your father, or the nakedness of your mother. She is your mother; you shall not uncover her nakedness.

**8** You shall not uncover the nakedness of your father's wife. It is your father's nakedness.

**9** You shall not uncover the nakedness of your sister, your father's daughter or your mother's daughter, born at home or born outside.

**10** You shall not uncover the nakedness of your son's daughter or your daughter's daughter, because their nakedness is your own.

**11** You shall not uncover the nakedness of the daughter of your father's wife, born to your father. She is your sister.

**12** You shall not uncover the nakedness of your father's sister. She is your father's flesh.

**13** You shall not uncover the nakedness of your mother's sister, because she is your mother's flesh.

**14** You shall not uncover the nakedness of your father's brother — you shall not come near his wife. She is your aunt.

**15** You shall not uncover the nakedness of your daughter-in-law. She is your son's wife; you shall not uncover her nakedness.

**16** You shall not uncover the nakedness of your brother's wife. It is your brother's nakedness.

**17** You shall not uncover the nakedness of a woman and her daughter. You shall not take her son's daughter or her daughter's daughter to uncover her nakedness. They are her own flesh. It is depravity.

**18** And you shall not take a woman together with her sister, as a rival, to uncover her nakedness while she is alive.

## And These

**19** And you shall not come near a woman in the uncleanness of her monthly separation, to uncover her nakedness.

**20** And you shall not give your emission to your neighbor's wife, to become unclean by her.

**21** And you shall not give any of your children to pass over to Molech; and you shall not profane the name of your God. I am the LORD.

**22** And you shall not lie with a male as one lies with a woman. It is an abomination.

**23** And you shall not give your emission to any animal, to become unclean by it; and a woman shall not stand in front of an animal to mate with it. It is a mixing.

## What the Land Does

**24** Do not make yourselves unclean by any of these, because by all of these the nations I am driving out before you have made themselves unclean.

**25** And the land became unclean, and I called its guilt to account upon it, and the land vomited out those living in it.

**26** But you shall keep My statutes and My judgments, and shall not do any of these abominations — neither the native nor the foreigner living among you —

**27** because the people of the land who were before you did all these abominations, and the land became unclean.

**28** And the land will not vomit you out when you make it unclean, as it vomited out the nation that was before you.

**29** Because anyone who does any of these abominations — the people who do them shall be cut off from among their people.

**30** And you shall keep My charge, not to do any of the abominable statutes that were done before you, and not to make yourselves unclean by them. I am the LORD your God."

## Notes

- **v6 "uncover nakedness"** — *gallot ervah*, and the phrase runs through the chapter twenty-four times, so this rendering keeps it identical every time the Hebrew repeats it. *Ervah* is exposure, the state of being uncovered, and the idiom is a euphemism for sexual relations — Hebrew names the act by naming the shame of being seen. The chapter is built out of one sentence said over and over with the relative changed, which is why it reads like a list rather than an argument.
- **v6 "his own flesh"** — *she'er besaro*, and *she'er* means both **flesh** and **a blood relative**, with no line drawn between the senses. The chapter uses it as its whole principle: these people are not other people, they are you. Verse 10 makes it explicit about a granddaughter — *their nakedness is your own*.
- **vv7–8 the father's nakedness** — twice the text describes an offence against a woman as an offence against a **man**: the father's wife is *your father's nakedness* (v8), the brother's wife is *your brother's nakedness* (v16). The women in those two verses are not blood relatives at all. What the formula tracks in these cases is the marriage, not the bloodline, and it says so by naming the husband.
- **v18 "a woman together with her sister"** — *ishah el achotah*, and this is the hardest verse in the chapter to be sure of. The phrase occurs in seven other places in the Hebrew Bible and in every one of them it is a **fixed idiom meaning one to another**, used of things: the curtains of the tabernacle coupled *one to another* (Exodus 26:3, 5, 6), the frames (26:17), the wings of the living creatures touching *one another* (Ezekiel 1:9, 23; 3:13). Not one of them is about a woman. So this verse can be read as forbidding marriage to a wife's sister — which is what Jacob did, and the word *litsror*, **as a rival**, points that way — or as forbidding taking one woman in addition to another at all. The two readings produce two different laws, and the Hebrew supports both.
- **v21 the one prohibition that is not sexual** — *le-ha'avir la-Molekh*, **to make pass over to Molech**, dropped without transition into the middle of a list about relatives. The verb is *avar*, to cross over; what the passing consists of is not stated here, though 20:14 and later books use fire. And the sentence that follows it is about **speech**: *you shall not profane the name of your God*. Nothing in the chapter explains why this belongs here.
- **v22 "as one lies with a woman"** — *mishkevei ishah*, and the noun is **plural**: the lyings of a woman. The same plural appears in the parallel at 20:13 and nowhere else in the Bible, and what the plural is doing has never been settled. The verse ends with *to'evah*, the word Genesis uses for Egyptians eating with Hebrews (43:32) and for shepherds (46:34) — a term for what a given community will not have among it, which is a different kind of word from *tamei*, unclean, and from *sheqets*, detestable, which chapter 11 used of animals.
- **v23 "It is a mixing"** — *tevel*, built on a root for mixing or confounding, and the sense is the running together of things that belong to separate categories. That is the same logic as chapter 11's clean and unclean animals and 19:19's ban on sowing a field with two kinds of seed: the offence named is not cruelty or harm but **confusion of kinds**. The word occurs in this sense twice in the Bible, here and at 20:12.
- **v23 the woman's clause** — the verse addresses a man and then turns and addresses a woman in the same breath, *and a woman shall not stand in front of an animal*. It is the only sentence in the chapter with a woman as the subject of the prohibition rather than its object. Everything else here is said to men about women.
- **vv25, 28 "the land vomited out"** — *va-taqi ha-arets*, from *qi*, **to vomit**, and it is a physical verb used of a sick body. This is the only place in the Torah where the **land** is the one who acts. Not God expelling the Canaanites, not Israel conquering them: the ground itself is made nauseous and throws off what is on it. And v28 turns it on the audience — *the land will not vomit you out* — which means the new occupants are being told they are on probation with the same soil.
- **v3 Egypt and Canaan, named together** — the chapter opens by naming both the country they came out of and the country they are going into, and forbids the practices of each. It does not say what those practices were. Every account of what Egypt or Canaan actually did comes from somewhere other than this text, and the chapter itself simply says: not like the place behind you, not like the place ahead.
- **v5 "and live by them"** — *va-chai bahem*, four words that became one of the most argued sentences in the Bible: the statutes are given so that a person may **live** by them. Ezekiel 20 quotes the exact phrase three times in one chapter (vv11, 13, 21) and then, at v25, says God gave them statutes that were not good and rules by which they could not live. The Hebrew here says only what it says, and does not define the living.
