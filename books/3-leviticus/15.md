# Leviticus 15

## A Man with a Discharge

**1** And the LORD spoke to Moses and to Aaron, saying,

**2** "Speak to the sons of Israel and say to them: When any man has a discharge from his body, his discharge is unclean.

**3** And this is how his discharge makes him unclean: whether his body runs with it or is stopped up by it, he is unclean.

**4** Every bed the man with the discharge lies on is unclean, and everything he sits on is unclean.

**5** And anyone who touches his bed shall wash his clothes and bathe in water, and be unclean until evening.

**6** And whoever sits on anything the man with the discharge sat on shall wash his clothes and bathe in water, and be unclean until evening.

**7** And whoever touches the body of the man with the discharge shall wash his clothes and bathe in water, and be unclean until evening.

**8** And if the man with the discharge spits on someone who is clean, that person shall wash his clothes and bathe in water, and be unclean until evening.

**9** And anything he rides on is unclean.

**10** And anyone who touches anything that was under him shall be unclean until evening; and whoever carries those things shall wash his clothes and bathe in water, and be unclean until evening.

**11** And if the man with the discharge touches anyone without first rinsing his hands in water, that person shall wash his clothes and bathe in water, and be unclean until evening.

**12** A clay pot that the man with the discharge touches shall be broken, and every wooden vessel shall be rinsed in water.

**13** And when the man with the discharge becomes clean of his discharge, he shall count seven days for his cleansing, and wash his clothes and bathe his body in fresh running water, and he is clean.

**14** And on the eighth day he shall take two turtledoves or two young pigeons and come before the LORD, to the entrance of the tent of meeting, and give them to the priest.

**15** And the priest shall offer them, one as a sin offering and the other as a burnt offering, and make atonement for him before the LORD for his discharge.

## Emissions

**16** And when a man has an emission of semen, he shall bathe his whole body in water, and be unclean until evening.

**17** And every garment and every hide with semen on it shall be washed in water, and be unclean until evening.

**18** And when a man lies with a woman and has an emission of semen, they shall both bathe in water, and be unclean until evening.

## A Woman's Flow

**19** And when a woman has a discharge, and the discharge in her body is blood, she shall be in her monthly separation seven days, and anyone who touches her shall be unclean until evening.

**20** And everything she lies on in her separation is unclean, and everything she sits on is unclean.

**21** And anyone who touches her bed shall wash his clothes and bathe in water, and be unclean until evening.

**22** And anyone who touches anything she sits on shall wash his clothes and bathe in water, and be unclean until evening.

**23** If anything is on her bed or on whatever she is sitting on, whoever touches it shall be unclean until evening.

**24** And if a man lies with her and her separation comes on him, he shall be unclean seven days, and every bed he lies on shall be unclean.

## A Flow Out of Season

**25** And when a woman's flow of blood runs many days at a time that is not her monthly separation, or when it runs past her separation, all the days of her unclean flow she shall be as in the days of her separation. She is unclean.

**26** Every bed she lies on all the days of her flow shall be to her like the bed of her separation, and everything she sits on shall be unclean, like the uncleanness of her separation.

**27** And anyone who touches these things shall be unclean, and shall wash his clothes and bathe in water, and be unclean until evening.

**28** And when she becomes clean of her flow, she shall count seven days for herself, and after that she is clean.

**29** And on the eighth day she shall take two turtledoves or two young pigeons and bring them to the priest, to the entrance of the tent of meeting.

**30** And the priest shall offer one as a sin offering and the other as a burnt offering, and make atonement for her before the LORD for her unclean flow.

## Why This Matters

**31** And you shall keep the sons of Israel separate from their uncleanness, so that they do not die in their uncleanness by defiling My dwelling, which is among them."

**32** This is the instruction for the man with a discharge, and for the man who has an emission of semen and is unclean by it,

**33** and for the woman who is unwell in her separation, and for anyone with a discharge, male or female, and for a man who lies with an unclean woman.

## Notes

- **v2 "a discharge"** — *zav*, from *zuv*, **to flow**, and it is the same verb as the most famous promise in the Torah. The land Israel is being taken to is *zavat chalav u-dvash*, **flowing with milk and honey** (Exodus 3:8). One root covers the abundance of the promised land and the thing a man's body does that puts him outside the camp. Hebrew does not hear two words there; it hears one, used of anything that will not stop coming out.
- **v2 "from his body"** — *mi-besaro*, literally **from his flesh**, and here it is a euphemism: the chapter is about the genitals and says so by not saying so. The same word is used plainly for flesh two verses later. This rendering keeps the Hebrew's own indirection rather than replacing it with clinical language the text declined to use.
- **v3 the hard verse** — *rar besaro et zovo o hechtim besaro mi-zovo*. *Rar* is a word for running or slavering; *hechtim* is built on *chatam*, **to seal up**. So the sentence covers the condition whether the flow is running or blocked, which is medically sensible and grammatically strange, and neither verb occurs often enough to be pinned down. The verse is left rough here because it is rough.
- **uncleanness is not sin** — the distinction the whole chapter depends on, and the one most likely to be misread. Nothing described here is a wrong. Nobody is blamed, nobody is punished, and two of the causes are ordinary marital relations (v18) and a woman's monthly cycle (v19). The remedy is water and sunset. *Tamei* is a **status**, like being wet — it says where a person may go, not what kind of person they are — and the only reason it matters at all is given in v31.
- **v18 both of them, and nothing else** — a couple who have slept together wash and wait until evening. That is the entire law. No offering, no confession, no priest, and no suggestion that anything wrong happened. The verse is remarkable mostly for what it does not contain, given how much later writing has been built on it.
- **v19 "her monthly separation"** — *niddah*, from a root meaning to move away or be put aside. It names the state, not the woman, and the chapter treats it in exactly the same words it used for the man in vv4–12: beds, seats, whoever touches them, wash, wait. The symmetry is deliberate and runs for thirty verses — one flow for a man, one for a woman, the same procedures, the same two birds at the end.
- **vv24 and 20:18 do not agree** — here, a man who lies with a woman in her separation is unclean for seven days, the same as the bed he slept on. At 20:18 the same act ends with both of them **cut off from among their people**. Two penalties for one action, in one book, and neither passage mentions the other. It is the kind of seam the Torah leaves visible, and no note can close it honestly.
- **v25 the woman whose flow does not stop** — a chronic condition, treated at length and with no stigma attached beyond the same rules that apply to everyone else in the chapter. This is the law the woman in the crowd at Mark 5:25 has been living under: the Greek says she was *in a flow of blood twelve years*, using the word the Greek Old Testament uses for *zov*. What she then does is reach through a crowd and take hold of a man's cloak (5:27) — and by vv21–27 here, touching is exactly what transmits. In Mark the transmission runs the other way.
- **v31 the reason, finally** — thirty verses of beds and seats and washing, and then the point: *so that they do not die in their uncleanness by defiling My dwelling, which is among them*. The rules are not about hygiene and not about morality. They exist because of where God has chosen to live. *Mishkani*, **My dwelling**, is the word from Exodus 25:9, and the last three words of the verse are the whole problem — *asher be-tokham*, **which is among them**. Something holy has been set down in the middle of an ordinary camp full of bodies, and the rest follows from that.
