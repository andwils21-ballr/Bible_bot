# Leviticus 6

## When the Wrong Is Against a Neighbor

**1** And the LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**2** "When a person sins and breaks faith with the LORD — he lies to his neighbor about something left in his keeping, or a pledge, or something he seized by force; or he has defrauded his neighbor;

**3** or he found something lost and lied about it, and swore falsely — in any one of all the things a man may do and sin by doing:

**4** when he sins and is guilty, he shall give back what he seized by force, or what he took by fraud, or what was left in his keeping, or the lost thing he found,

**5** or anything at all he swore falsely about. He shall repay it in full and add a fifth to it. He shall give it to its owner on the day of his guilt offering.

**6** And he shall bring his guilt offering to the LORD — an unblemished ram from the flock, at your valuation — to the priest, as a guilt offering.

**7** And the priest shall make atonement for him before the LORD, and he shall be forgiven for any one of all the things a man may do and incur guilt by."

## The Fire That Does Not Go Out

**8** And the LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**9** "Command Aaron and his sons, saying: This is the instruction for the burnt offering. The burnt offering itself shall stay on the hearth on the altar all night until morning, and the fire of the altar shall be kept burning on it.

**10** And the priest shall put on his linen robe, and put linen undergarments on his body, and lift off the ashes that the fire has made of the burnt offering on the altar, and put them beside the altar.

**11** And he shall take off his garments and put on other garments, and carry the ashes outside the camp, to a clean place.

**12** And the fire on the altar shall be kept burning on it; it shall not go out. And the priest shall burn wood on it morning by morning, and arrange the burnt offering on it, and turn into smoke on it the fat of the peace offerings.

**13** Fire shall be kept burning on the altar continually. It shall not go out.

## The Grain Offering, and Who Eats It

**14** And this is the instruction for the grain offering. Aaron's sons shall bring it near before the LORD, in front of the altar.

**15** And one shall lift from it his handful — of the fine flour of the grain offering and of its oil, and all the frankincense that is on the grain offering — and turn its memorial portion into smoke on the altar, a soothing aroma to the LORD.

**16** And what is left of it Aaron and his sons shall eat. It shall be eaten unleavened in a holy place; in the court of the tent of meeting they shall eat it.

**17** It shall not be baked with leaven. I have given it as their share from My offerings by fire. It is most holy, like the sin offering and like the guilt offering.

**18** Every male among Aaron's sons may eat it — a lasting statute through your generations, from the offerings by fire of the LORD. Whatever touches them becomes holy."

## The Priest's Own Offering

**19** And the LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**20** "This is the offering of Aaron and his sons that they shall bring to the LORD on the day he is anointed: a tenth of an ephah of fine flour as a regular grain offering, half of it in the morning and half of it in the evening.

**21** It shall be made on a griddle with oil. You shall bring it soaked, and present it baked in pieces — a grain offering, a soothing aroma to the LORD.

**22** And whichever of his sons is anointed priest in his place shall make it. It is a lasting statute: to the LORD it shall go up in smoke whole.

**23** And every grain offering of a priest shall be burned whole. It shall not be eaten."

## The Sin Offering, and What It Touches

**24** And the LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**25** "Speak to Aaron and his sons, saying: This is the instruction for the sin offering. In the place where the burnt offering is slaughtered the sin offering shall be slaughtered, before the LORD. It is most holy.

**26** The priest who offers it for sin shall eat it. It shall be eaten in a holy place, in the court of the tent of meeting.

**27** Whatever touches its flesh becomes holy; and if any of its blood is spattered on a garment, you shall wash what it was spattered on in a holy place.

**28** And an earthen vessel in which it was boiled shall be broken; and if it was boiled in a bronze vessel, that shall be scoured and rinsed with water.

**29** Every male among the priests may eat it. It is most holy.

**30** But no sin offering shall be eaten if its blood is brought into the tent of meeting to make atonement in the holy place. It shall be burned in the fire."

## Notes

- **the chapter numbers here do not match a Hebrew Bible** — the Hebrew divides this material seven verses later than the English does. Hebrew Leviticus 5 runs to twenty-six verses and carries what English prints as 6:1–7; Hebrew chapter 6 then covers English 6:8–30. This rendering follows the English division throughout, so it can be set beside a printed Bible. Anyone checking it against the Hebrew should subtract seven from the verse numbers in the first section and add seven in the rest.
- **v2 "breaks faith with the LORD"** — *ma'alah ma'al ba-YHWH*, the same charge 5:15 brought against a man who misused the holy things. Here nothing holy has been touched. A man has lied to a neighbor about a deposit, or kept something he found, and the text calls it treachery against **God** — then spends five verses on the neighbor's property and one on the ram. The wrong runs in two directions at once, and the law will not let the vertical one be settled first.
- **v2 "his neighbor"** — *amito*, a word that occurs ten times in the Hebrew Bible: nine of them in Leviticus, and one in Zechariah. It is not the ordinary *rea* of *love your neighbor as yourself* (19:18). It means an associate, someone you stand alongside — and the verb used against him here, *kachash*, to deny or lie about, is the same verb in the same construction at 19:11: *you shall not steal, and you shall not deal falsely, and you shall not lie to one another*.
- **v2 "a pledge"** — *tesumet yad*, literally **a putting of the hand**, and the phrase occurs nowhere else in the Hebrew Bible. Something handed over and closed on — a security, a deposit held against a debt, goods put into a partner's hands. The three nouns before and after it are ordinary and sharp: *pikkadon*, what is left in someone's keeping; *gazel*, what is taken by force. This one sits between them and cannot be pinned down further.
- **v5 "repay it in full"** — *be-rosho*, literally **in its head**, which is Hebrew for the capital sum as against the interest or the fine. The fifth is added on top of it. And the restitution runs to **its owner**, not to the sanctuary: of every offering in these six chapters, this is the only one where somebody outside the priesthood is handed anything back.
- **v5 "on the day of his guilt offering"** — *be-yom ashmato*, **on the day of his guilt**, and the noun means the guilt and the offering brought for it at once. So the sentence either dates the repayment to the day his guilt is established or to the day he brings the ram, and nothing in the verse settles which. Either way the two land together: he cannot pay the man back and leave the altar for later, or square it with God and pay the man when it suits him.
- **vv9, 14, 25 "This is the instruction for"** — *zot torat*, and *torah* is not primarily a law. It is from *yarah*, to throw or shoot, and so to point — instruction, direction, what a teacher hands a student. The word that comes into English as **the Torah**, the name of the whole five books, is used here for the handling procedure of a burnt offering.
- **vv10–11 the ashes** — *deshen*, and it is not the ordinary word for ashes. *Deshen* is what the fire leaves of fat: the same noun means **richness, fatness, the best of a thing** everywhere outside the sacrificial laws — *they drink their fill of the richness of your house* (Psalm 36:8), *my soul is satisfied as with fat and rich food* (Psalm 63:5), *I will saturate the appetite of the priests with richness* (Jeremiah 31:14). The first task of the priest's day is carrying out the previous day's residue, and the word for it is the word for abundance.
- **v10 "put on his linen robe"** — the priest is dressed in the holy linen to lift the ashes off the altar, and in v11 he changes into other clothes to carry them out of the camp. The garments are graded by where they go, not by the dignity of the job: the same linen undergarments made at Exodus 28:42 are worn for this. Somebody has to empty the fireplace, and the text says who, and what he wears, and where he puts it.
- **vv12–13 "it shall not go out"** — said twice in two verses, with *tukad*, **shall be kept burning**, three times across vv9–13. The only thing in the entire sanctuary that is commanded to be continuous is the fire, and keeping it so is manual: somebody carries wood every morning. *Tamid*, **continually**, is the word that will attach to the lamp, the bread and the daily offering — the standing things — and it gets here first.
- **vv18, 27 "whatever touches them becomes holy"** — stated twice, of the grain offering and of the sin offering's flesh, and it runs the opposite way to everything modern intuition expects. Uncleanness is the thing that normally spreads by contact. Here holiness does, and it is not presented as a blessing: v27 continues straight into what to do about blood on your clothes and v28 into smashing the pot it was boiled in. The rule is not that holy things are fragile. It is that they are **contagious**, and what they touch cannot go back to ordinary use.
- **v21 "soaked, and… baked in pieces"** — two words here are barely recoverable. *Murbekhet* occurs three times in the whole Bible, always of this same flour-and-oil preparation, and means something like stirred or soaked through. *Tufinei* occurs **once** — this verse, and nowhere else in Hebrew. The proposals run from a form of *afah*, to bake, to a word for the broken pieces named in the next breath, and the verse offers nothing to decide between them. What can be said is what the priest is handed: flour, oil, a griddle, and an instruction that has not been fully legible for a very long time.
- **vv22–23 what the priest never eats** — the grain offering of an ordinary Israelite is mostly the priests' dinner (vv16–18). The priest's own grain offering goes up **whole**, *kalil*, twice in two verses, and the last line of the section shuts the door: *it shall not be eaten*. The men who live off the altar get nothing back from their own gift, and the only one of them named is Aaron, who has to bring it every day of his life, half in the morning and half at dusk.
- **v26 "the priest who offers it for sin"** — *ha-mechatte*, and the verb is *chata*, **to sin**. In this form it reverses: it means to de-sin something, to purge the sin out of it. The same three letters that name the offence name the cure, the wrong and the remedy, and the animal — and the only thing that tells a reader which is meant is the shape of the verb.
