# Leviticus 8

## Washed and Dressed

**1** And the LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**2** "Take Aaron and his sons with him, and the garments, and the anointing oil, and the bull of the sin offering, and the two rams, and the basket of unleavened bread,

**3** and assemble the whole congregation at the entrance of the tent of meeting."

**4** And Moses did as the LORD commanded him, and the congregation assembled at the entrance of the tent of meeting.

**5** And Moses said to the congregation, "This is what the LORD has commanded to be done."

**6** And Moses brought Aaron and his sons forward and washed them with water.

**7** And he put the tunic on Aaron and tied the sash around him, and clothed him with the robe, and put the ephod on him, and tied the woven band of the ephod around him, fastening it to him.

**8** And he set the breastpiece on him, and put the Urim and the Thummim into the breastpiece.

**9** And he set the turban on his head, and set the gold plate, the holy crown, on the front of the turban, as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**10** And Moses took the anointing oil and anointed the dwelling and everything in it, and set them apart.

**11** And he sprinkled some of it on the altar seven times, and anointed the altar and all its vessels, and the basin and its stand, to set them apart.

**12** And he poured some of the anointing oil on Aaron's head and anointed him, to set him apart.

**13** And Moses brought Aaron's sons forward and clothed them with tunics and tied sashes around them and bound caps on them, as the LORD had commanded Moses.

## The Bull and the Two Rams

**14** And he brought the bull of the sin offering, and Aaron and his sons leaned their hands on its head.

**15** And it was slaughtered, and Moses took the blood and put it with his finger on the horns of the altar all around, and purified the altar; and he poured out the blood at the base of the altar. So he set the altar apart, to make atonement for it.

**16** And he took all the fat that was on the entrails, and the lobe on the liver, and the two kidneys and their fat, and Moses turned them into smoke on the altar.

**17** And he burned the bull — its hide, its flesh and its dung — in the fire outside the camp, as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**18** And he brought the ram of the burnt offering, and Aaron and his sons leaned their hands on the head of the ram.

**19** And it was slaughtered, and Moses dashed the blood against the altar all around.

**20** And he cut the ram into pieces, and Moses turned the head, the pieces and the suet into smoke.

**21** And he washed the entrails and the legs with water, and Moses turned the whole ram into smoke on the altar. It was a burnt offering for a soothing aroma; it was an offering by fire to the LORD, as the LORD had commanded Moses.

## Blood on the Ear, the Thumb, the Toe

**22** And he brought the second ram, the ram of ordination, and Aaron and his sons leaned their hands on the head of the ram.

**23** And it was slaughtered, and Moses took some of its blood and put it on the lobe of Aaron's right ear, and on the thumb of his right hand, and on the big toe of his right foot.

**24** And he brought Aaron's sons forward, and Moses put some of the blood on the lobe of their right ear, and on the thumb of their right hand, and on the big toe of their right foot. And Moses dashed the blood against the altar all around.

**25** And he took the fat — the fat tail and all the fat on the entrails, and the lobe on the liver, and the two kidneys and their fat — and the right thigh.

**26** And from the basket of unleavened bread before the LORD he took one unleavened loaf, one loaf of oiled bread and one wafer, and put them on the fat and on the right thigh,

**27** and he put it all on Aaron's palms and on his sons' palms, and waved them before the LORD.

**28** And Moses took them from their palms and turned them into smoke on the altar, on top of the burnt offering. They were an ordination offering for a soothing aroma; it was an offering by fire to the LORD.

**29** And Moses took the breast and waved it before the LORD. It was Moses' share of the ram of ordination, as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**30** And Moses took some of the anointing oil and some of the blood that was on the altar and sprinkled it on Aaron and his garments, and on his sons and their garments; so he set apart Aaron and his garments, and his sons and their garments with him.

## Seven Days at the Door

**31** And Moses said to Aaron and to his sons, "Boil the flesh at the entrance of the tent of meeting, and eat it there with the bread that is in the basket of ordination, as I commanded: Aaron and his sons shall eat it.

**32** And you shall burn in the fire whatever is left of the flesh and the bread.

**33** And you shall not go out from the entrance of the tent of meeting for seven days, until the day your days of ordination are complete; for it will take seven days to fill your hand.

**34** The LORD commanded what has been done today, to make atonement for you.

**35** And you shall stay at the entrance of the tent of meeting day and night for seven days, and keep the LORD's watch, so that you do not die; for so I was commanded."

**36** And Aaron and his sons did everything the LORD had commanded through Moses.

## Notes

- **v8 the Urim and the Thummim** — they are put into the breastpiece here with no more explanation than Exodus 28:30 gave, and the Bible never provides one. The names are usually connected to *or*, light, and *tam*, complete or whole, and they are handled as objects: put **in**, and later carried, never described. What they did can be partly reconstructed from how they are used elsewhere — a question is asked and an answer comes back one way or the other, and at 1 Samuel 14:37 no answer comes back at all. This verse is the installation of a device whose operation the text assumes everyone knows.
- **v7 "fastening it to him"** — the Hebrew verb here is *va-ye'efod*, made out of the noun **ephod**: to ephod it onto him. It occurs twice in the Bible, at Exodus 29:5 where the ceremony is prescribed and here where it is carried out, and nowhere else. A garment important enough to have a verb coined for putting it on, and the verb is used only for these two sentences about the same garment on the same man.
- **v10 oil before blood** — the first thing Moses does after dressing Aaron is anoint the **building**: the dwelling, everything in it, the altar seven times over, the basin and its stand. Only then, in v12, does he pour oil on Aaron's head. The structure is made holy before the man who will work in it is, and the man is anointed in the middle of the same operation as the furniture.
- **v15 "purified the altar"** — *va-yechatte*, the verb *chata*, **to sin**, in the form that reverses it: to take sin out of a thing. The brand-new altar, which nobody has yet used or defiled, is de-sinned before it is put to work. Holiness in these chapters is not a quality an object has; it is a state something is brought into, and even an unused altar has to be brought there.
- **vv23–24 the ear, the thumb, the toe** — *tenukh*, the soft lobe of the ear, a word that occurs seven times in the Hebrew Bible and nowhere outside two rites: this one, and the restoring of a person healed of a skin disease (14:14, 17, 25, 28). Those two ceremonies are otherwise nothing alike — one installs a man at the center of the camp, the other readmits a man who was living outside it — and they share a gesture found in no other rite: blood on the right ear, the right thumb, the right big toe. Whatever the daub means, the text applies it to going **in**.
- **v27 "on Aaron's palms"** — this is the idiom acted out. Ordination in Hebrew is *filling the hand*, and here Moses piles fat, the right thigh, a loaf, an oiled loaf and a wafer onto the palms of men who are standing there holding them. Then he takes it all back off and burns it. Their hands are filled with exactly what they will handle for the rest of their lives, and they do not keep any of it.
- **v29 the one portion Moses takes** — the breast of the ordination ram goes to **Moses**. He has performed every action in this chapter: the washing, the dressing, the anointing, the slaughtering, the blood, the burning. He is not a priest and never becomes one; his sons do not inherit anything here. For one day the whole priesthood is a man from the tribe of Levi who will hand it to his brother in the morning and go back to being a prophet.
- **v30 oil and blood together** — sprinkled on the men and on their clothes. Every other mixture in these chapters is oil with flour, and blood stays on the altar. Here the two are put on fabric, and the garments are named twice in one verse as things being set apart. What a priest wears is holy in its own right, not by association with him.
- **v31 "Boil the flesh"** — and 6:28 has just ruled that the pot a sin offering is boiled in must be smashed if it is clay, or scoured if it is bronze. The law was given two chapters ago and this is the first meal cooked under it. The text moves from the rule to the cooking pot without remarking on it.
- **v33 "for seven days"** — they are confined to the doorway, day and night, for a week. Nothing is said about what they do during it. The reason given is not training and not purification: *so that you do not die* (v35). It is the first time that warning appears in Leviticus, and chapter 10 is two chapters away.
- **v35 "keep the LORD's watch"** — *u-shmartem et mishmeret YHWH*, the verb with its own noun, which is how Hebrew presses a word: **keep the keeping**. *Mishmeret* is a guard duty, a watch posted, and the same pairing will describe the Levites' permanent job (Numbers 3:7). The priesthood begins with a week of guard duty at a door, and the thing being guarded is not named.
