# Leviticus 7

## The Guilt Offering, and the Priest's Share

**1** And this is the instruction for the guilt offering. It is most holy.

**2** They shall slaughter the guilt offering in the place where they slaughter the burnt offering, and its blood shall be dashed against the altar all around.

**3** He shall offer all its fat: the fat tail, the fat covering the entrails,

**4** and the two kidneys with the fat on them at the loins, and the lobe of the liver, which he shall remove with the kidneys.

**5** And the priest shall turn them into smoke on the altar, an offering by fire to the LORD. It is a guilt offering.

**6** Every male among the priests may eat it. It shall be eaten in a holy place. It is most holy.

**7** The sin offering and the guilt offering have one instruction between them: it belongs to the priest who makes atonement with it.

**8** And the priest who presents a man's burnt offering shall have for himself the hide of the burnt offering he presented.

**9** And every grain offering baked in an oven, and everything made in a pan or on a griddle, belongs to the priest who presents it.

**10** And every grain offering mixed with oil, or dry, belongs to all the sons of Aaron alike.

## The Thanksgiving Offering

**11** And this is the instruction for the peace offering that a man brings to the LORD.

**12** If he brings it for thanksgiving, he shall bring with the thanksgiving sacrifice unleavened loaves mixed with oil, and unleavened wafers spread with oil, and loaves of fine flour soaked and mixed with oil.

**13** He shall bring his offering with loaves of leavened bread, along with his thanksgiving peace offering.

**14** And from it he shall bring one of each kind as a contribution to the LORD. It belongs to the priest who dashes the blood of the peace offering.

**15** And the flesh of his thanksgiving peace offering shall be eaten on the day it is offered. He shall leave none of it until morning.

**16** But if the sacrifice he brings is a vow or a freewill offering, it shall be eaten on the day he brings his sacrifice, and what is left of it may be eaten the next day.

**17** And what is left of the flesh of the sacrifice on the third day shall be burned in the fire.

**18** And if any of the flesh of his peace offering is eaten at all on the third day, it will not be accepted. It will not be credited to the one who brought it. It is foul, and the person who eats it carries his guilt.

**19** And flesh that touches anything unclean shall not be eaten; it shall be burned in the fire. Otherwise, anyone who is clean may eat the flesh.

**20** But the person who eats flesh from the peace offering that belongs to the LORD while he is unclean — that person shall be cut off from his people.

**21** And when a person touches anything unclean — human uncleanness, or an unclean animal, or any unclean loathsome thing — and eats flesh from the peace offering that belongs to the LORD, that person shall be cut off from his people."

## No Fat and No Blood

**22** And the LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**23** "Speak to the sons of Israel, saying: You shall eat no fat of ox or sheep or goat.

**24** The fat of an animal that died on its own, and the fat of one torn by beasts, may be put to any use, but you shall certainly not eat it.

**25** For anyone who eats the fat of an animal from which an offering by fire is brought to the LORD — the person who eats it shall be cut off from his people.

**26** And you shall eat no blood at all, in any of your settlements, whether of bird or of animal.

**27** Any person who eats any blood — that person shall be cut off from his people."

## The Breast and the Right Thigh

**28** And the LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**29** "Speak to the sons of Israel, saying: Whoever offers a peace offering to the LORD shall bring from it his offering to the LORD.

**30** His own hands shall bring the LORD's offerings by fire. He shall bring the fat with the breast, the breast to be waved before the LORD.

**31** And the priest shall turn the fat into smoke on the altar, and the breast shall be Aaron's and his sons'.

**32** You shall give the right thigh to the priest as a contribution from your peace offerings.

**33** The right thigh shall be the share of the son of Aaron who presents the blood and the fat of the peace offering.

**34** For I have taken the breast that is waved and the thigh that is lifted off from the sons of Israel, from their peace offerings, and have given them to Aaron the priest and to his sons as a lasting due from the sons of Israel.

**35** This is the share that comes with the anointing of Aaron and of his sons, from the LORD's offerings by fire, from the day they were brought forward to serve the LORD as priests —

**36** which the LORD commanded to be given them by the sons of Israel on the day He anointed them, a lasting statute through their generations.

**37** This is the instruction for the burnt offering, the grain offering, the sin offering, the guilt offering, the ordination offering, and the peace offering,

**38** which the LORD commanded Moses on Mount Sinai on the day He commanded the sons of Israel to bring their offerings to the LORD, in the wilderness of Sinai.

## Notes

- **v12 "thanksgiving"** — *todah*, from *yadah*, and it is the same root as *hitvaddah*, **to confess**, at 5:5. One verb covers both: the sacrifice for which a man says what God has done for him, and the act by which he says what he has done himself. Joshua uses it of Achan — *give praise to the LORD the God of Israel and make confession to Him* (Joshua 7:19) — where a single Hebrew root does the work of both English words in one breath. Owning the good and owning the wrong are not separate motions in this language.
- **v15 eaten in a day** — the thanksgiving offering must be finished before morning; the vow and the freewill offering get two days (v16). This is the tightest deadline in the sacrificial laws and it has a practical consequence the text does not spell out: an ox and four kinds of bread cannot be eaten by one household between afternoon and dark. The rule does not say *invite people*. It makes it impossible not to.
- **v13 the one leavened thing on the list** — leaven is banned from the altar outright (2:11), and here the thanksgiving offering arrives with **leavened** loaves. They are not burned. They come with the meal and go to the priest and the table, and the prohibition holds exactly where it was drawn: at the fire, not at the feast.
- **v10 "alike"** — *ish ke-achiv*, **a man like his brother**, which is Hebrew for share and share alike. It is the one line in the chapter that divides something equally: the baked and griddled grain offerings go to whichever priest handled them (v9), the hide goes to whoever presented the animal (v8), and the plain flour, cooked by nobody, is split evenly among all of them.
- **v18 "It is foul"** — *piggul*, a word that occurs four times in the Hebrew Bible and never with an explanation: here, at 19:7 of the same third-day meat, in Ezekiel 4:14 of food a priest refuses to put in his mouth, and in Isaiah 65:4 of what is being boiled in the pots of people sitting among graves. Every one of the four is about something eaten. It is the strongest available word for food that has gone wrong, and the law applies it to meat that was holy two days earlier.
- **v18 "eaten at all"** — *he'akhol ye'akhel*, the verb doubled with itself, which is how Hebrew leans on a word. The doubling is not *thoroughly eaten* or *eaten up*; it closes off the excuses — a mouthful is eating.
- **v18 "will not be credited"** — *lo yechashev lo*, from *chashav*, the accounting verb: to reckon, to enter in a ledger. It is the verb of Genesis 15:6, where Abraham's trust *was credited to him as righteousness*. Here it runs the other way. A man brought a whole animal, did every step correctly, and three days later the entry is struck out over how the leftovers were handled.
- **vv20, 21, 25, 27 "cut off from his people"** — *ve-nikhretah*, from *karat*, the verb for cutting — and the same verb used for **making** a covenant, which in Hebrew is *cut* rather than made. What the penalty actually consists of is never stated anywhere in the Torah: not execution, which the law prescribes plainly where it means it, and not exile, which has its own vocabulary. It is announced four times in eight verses over fat, blood and a meal eaten while unclean, and left undefined.
- **v21 "any unclean loathsome thing"** — *sheqets*, the word chapter 11 uses for the creatures Israel must loathe. The list runs from a human source of uncleanness to an animal to this, and the expected word for the third item is *sherets*, a swarming thing, which differs from *sheqets* by one letter. The Hebrew text has *sheqets*, and it is rendered as written.
- **v24 the fat of an animal nobody killed for God** — *nevelah*, one that died on its own, and *terefah*, one torn by a predator. Their fat may be **put to any use** — greased, burned for light, worked into something — and only eating it is forbidden. The prohibition of v23 turns out not to be about the substance. Fat is barred because it is the altar's portion; where there was never going to be an altar, the same fat is just material. *Terefah* is the word behind Jacob's defense to Laban at Genesis 31:39, the standing excuse of a hired shepherd.
- **v30 "his own hands shall bring"** — *yadav tevi'enah*, and the noun is fronted: **his hands**, they shall bring it. The owner cannot send the fat and the breast up with a servant or hand them over at the gate; the one sentence in this chapter about who physically carries the portion says it is the man himself. Everything else in these chapters is done by priests.
- **v30 "to be waved"** — *lehanif oto tenufah*, to wave it a waving, the verb with its own noun. What the motion was is not described here or anywhere else — extended forward and back, or lifted up and set down, both have been argued from the word. What the text does say is that the waved breast and the lifted thigh are the two cuts that leave the altar and go to the priests' families (v34), and 10:14 will say the daughters eat them too.
- **v32 "a contribution"** — *terumah*, from *rum*, to be high: the portion **lifted off** a larger amount and set aside. It is the same verb as lifting the ashes off the altar and lifting the handful out of the flour in 6:10 and 6:15. One motion runs through the whole system — take a part up out of the whole — and the noun built on it becomes the ordinary word for everything given to the sanctuary.
- **v35 "the share that comes with the anointing"** — *mishchat Aharon*, literally **the anointing of Aaron**, used here for what the anointing pays. The oil is not only a ceremony; it is the instrument of a transfer, and this verse is where the text says out loud that the priesthood is a living. The men have no land (Numbers 18:20). What they have is a standing claim on the breast and the right thigh of every peace offering in Israel.
- **v37 "the ordination offering"** — *milluim*, **fillings**, from the idiom that names the making of a priest: *filling the hand*. It is the one item in this closing list with no instructions anywhere behind it — burnt, grain, sin, guilt and peace offerings all have their chapter, and this one does not. Chapter 8 is about to supply it, and there the hands get filled in front of the reader.
