# Leviticus 12

## After a Child Is Born

**1** And the LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**2** "Speak to the sons of Israel, saying: When a woman produces seed and bears a male, she shall be unclean seven days; she shall be unclean as in the days of her monthly separation.

**3** And on the eighth day the flesh of his foreskin shall be circumcised.

**4** And for thirty-three days she shall stay in the blood of her purifying. She shall not touch anything holy and shall not come into the sanctuary, until the days of her purifying are complete.

**5** And if she bears a female, she shall be unclean two weeks, as in her separation; and for sixty-six days she shall stay in the blood of her purifying.

**6** And when the days of her purifying are complete, for a son or for a daughter, she shall bring a year-old lamb for a burnt offering and a young pigeon or a turtledove for a sin offering, to the entrance of the tent of meeting, to the priest.

**7** And he shall present it before the LORD and make atonement for her, and she shall be clean from the flow of her blood. This is the instruction for the woman who gives birth, to a male or to a female.

**8** And if she cannot afford a lamb, she shall take two turtledoves or two young pigeons, one for a burnt offering and one for a sin offering. And the priest shall make atonement for her, and she shall be clean."

## Notes

- **v2 "produces seed"** — *tazria*, and the verb is the one used of **plants**. Genesis 1:12: *vegetation yielding seed*, *esev mazria zera*, the same stem. Hebrew has ordinary words for conceiving and for becoming pregnant, and the chapter opens with neither. It describes a woman bearing a child in the vocabulary of the third day of creation, and this is the only place in the Torah the verb is used of a person.
- **v3 one verse about the baby** — circumcision on the eighth day is dropped into the middle of a law about the mother, in eight Hebrew words, with nothing before or after it. The chapter is not about the child; everything else in it concerns her days, her blood, her offering. The most consequential thing that happens to an Israelite boy gets a subordinate clause, because it is here only to mark where in her count it falls.
- **v4 "the blood of her purifying"** — *demei tohorah*, and it is a strange pair of words. Blood is the substance that makes things unclean everywhere else in Leviticus; here the blood is the purifying. The phrase is not explained, and the situation it describes is neither uncleanness nor cleanness but a third state: she is not contagious any more — nothing here says anyone who touches her is affected — yet she cannot come near what is holy.
- **vv4–5 the count is doubled for a daughter** — seven days and thirty-three for a son, fourteen and sixty-six for a daughter. Exactly twice, in both halves. The text gives no reason whatsoever, and none of the reasons later supplied is in the passage. What can be said is what the numbers do: nothing about the child changes, only the length of the mother's separation.
- **v6 a sin offering, for what** — she brings a *chatat*, the offering of chapters 4 and 5, which is brought for a sin committed by mistake. No sin is named here, and nothing in the chapter says she did anything wrong. Whatever the *chatat* is doing in this law, it is not responding to an act — which is a warning about reading the word *sin offering* as though it always answered a sin.
- **v8 "if she cannot afford a lamb"** — *lo timtsa yadah*, **her hand does not find**, next to 5:7's *lo taggia yado*, **his hand does not reach**. Two different idioms for the same thing in one book, and neither of them mentions money. The sliding scale works the same way it did in chapter 5: the cheaper offering is not a lesser rite, and the verse ends with the identical words the full version ends with — *and she shall be clean*.
- **v8 and the family in Luke 2** — the Greek of Luke 2:24 quotes this verse: *a pair of turtledoves or two young pigeons*. Three verses of that chapter are this law being carried out — circumcision on the eighth day (2:21), the days of purification (2:22), and then the offering, which is the substitute of v8 rather than the lamb of v6. Luke does not comment on which one they brought, and does not need to; anyone who knew this chapter could see it.
