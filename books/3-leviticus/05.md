# Leviticus 5

## Four Ways to Become Guilty

**1** If a person sins — he hears a public call to testify, and he is a witness, whether he saw it or came to know of it — if he does not tell it, he carries his guilt.

**2** Or a person who touches any unclean thing, whether the carcass of an unclean wild animal, of unclean livestock, or of an unclean swarming thing, and it was hidden from him, and he is unclean and guilty;

**3** or if he touches human uncleanness — any uncleanness of a person by which someone becomes unclean — and it was hidden from him, and then he knows, and he is guilty;

**4** or a person who swears, blurting it out with his lips, to do harm or to do good — whatever a man may blurt out in an oath — and it was hidden from him, and then he knows, and he is guilty in one of these:

**5** when he is guilty in one of these, he shall confess how he has sinned,

**6** and he shall bring his guilt offering to the LORD for the sin he has committed — a female from the flock, a lamb or a female goat, as a sin offering — and the priest shall make atonement for him for his sin.

## If He Cannot Afford a Lamb

**7** If he cannot afford a lamb, he shall bring as his guilt offering for his sin two turtledoves or two young pigeons to the LORD, one for a sin offering and one for a burnt offering.

**8** He shall bring them to the priest, who shall present first the one for the sin offering. He shall pinch off its head at the back of its neck without severing it,

**9** and shall sprinkle some of the blood of the sin offering on the side of the altar, and the rest of the blood shall be drained out at the base of the altar. It is a sin offering.

**10** He shall offer the second as a burnt offering, according to the rule. The priest shall make atonement for him for the sin he has committed, and he shall be forgiven.

**11** If he cannot afford two turtledoves or two young pigeons, he shall bring as his offering for his sin a tenth of an ephah of fine flour for a sin offering. He shall put no oil on it and shall lay no frankincense on it, because it is a sin offering.

**12** He shall bring it to the priest, and the priest shall scoop out a handful of it as its memorial portion and turn it into smoke on the altar, on top of the LORD's offerings by fire. It is a sin offering.

**13** The priest shall make atonement for him for the sin he has committed in any one of these, and he shall be forgiven. The rest shall be the priest's, like the grain offering.

## Against the Holy Things

**14** The LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**15** "When a person breaks faith and sins by mistake against any of the holy things of the LORD, he shall bring to the LORD as his guilt offering an unblemished ram from the flock, at the set value in silver shekels, by the shekel of the holy place.

**16** He shall make restitution for his sin against the holy thing, add a fifth to it, and give it to the priest. The priest shall make atonement for him with the ram of the guilt offering, and he shall be forgiven.

**17** If a person sins and does any one of the things the LORD commanded not to be done, and he did not know it, he is guilty and carries his guilt.

**18** He shall bring to the priest an unblemished ram from the flock, at the set value, as a guilt offering. The priest shall make atonement for him for the mistake he made without knowing, and he shall be forgiven.

**19** It is a guilt offering. He is certainly guilty before the LORD."

## Notes

- **v1 the first case is saying nothing** — *qol alah*, the **sound of an oath**: a public adjuration, someone standing up and calling on anyone who knows something to come forward under oath. A man hears it, has the information, and keeps his mouth shut. That is the opening case of the chapter, and it is a sin of omission — no act is committed, nothing is touched, nothing is said. The list that follows moves from silence to accidental contact to words blurted out, which means the chapter is ordered from the least visible offense outward.
- **v5 "he shall confess"** — *hitvaddah*, from *yadah*, and this is the first place in the sacrificial system where the offerer has to **say** anything. Chapters 1 through 4 are entirely hands and knives; the worshipper leans, slaughters, skins, and never speaks. Here, before the animal is brought, he has to state out loud what he did.
- **vv7, 11 "if he cannot afford"** — *lo taggia yado*, literally **his hand does not reach**, which is what Hebrew says instead of *he cannot afford it*. The idiom is about grasp, not money, and the chapter uses it twice to build a sliding scale: a lamb, or failing that two birds, or failing that a tenth of an ephah of flour — about two quarts. The rite is written so that nobody is priced out of it.
- **v11 no oil and no frankincense** — the grain offering of chapter 2 always carries both, and the reason for stripping them here is given in four words: *because it is a sin offering*. Oil and incense are the marks of a gift; this is not a gift. So the poorest man's offering is plain flour with nothing added, and the text makes a point of saying it is not being cheapened — the priest handles it exactly as he handles the full version, and it counts exactly the same.
- **v15 "breaks faith"** — *tim'ol ma'al*, the verb with its own noun, which is how Hebrew presses a word. *Ma'al* is treachery against a trust — it is the word used of a wife suspected by her husband and of Achan taking what was devoted. Applied to the *holy things* it means using for ordinary purposes something that had been set apart, and the remedy is not only the ram but repayment of the value **plus a fifth** (v16). This is the one case in the chapter where somebody is out of pocket.
- **v17 guilty without ever knowing** — the most uncomfortable sentence in the section: *he did not know it, he is guilty and carries his guilt*. Guilt here is not a feeling and not a verdict anyone has reached; it is a condition, like a debt accruing on an account nobody has looked at. And v18 has the priest make atonement for a mistake the man still cannot name — *for the mistake he made without knowing*.
- **v19 "He is certainly guilty"** — *ashom asham*, the verb doubled with its own infinitive, the Hebrew intensifier. And the whole verse is three words long, tacked on after the case has already closed with *he shall be forgiven*. The chapter ends by restating the guilt of a man it has just declared forgiven, and does not explain why.
- **the two words for the offering** — *chatat* and *asham* run side by side through this chapter and the verses do not keep them apart tidily: v6 says *bring his **asham*** and then calls the animal a *chatat*. Both nouns mean the wrong and the remedy at once — sin and sin offering, guilt and guilt offering — and where later tradition separates them into two distinct rites, the text here lets them overlap.
