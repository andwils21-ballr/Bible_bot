# Leviticus 23

**1** The LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**2** "Speak to the sons of Israel and say to them: The appointed times of the LORD, which you shall proclaim as holy assemblies — these are My appointed times.

## The Sabbath

**3** Work may be done for six days, but on the seventh day is a sabbath of complete rest, a holy assembly. You shall do no work at all. It is a sabbath to the LORD in all your dwellings.

## Passover and Unleavened Bread

**4** These are the appointed times of the LORD, holy assemblies, which you shall proclaim at their appointed time.

**5** In the first month, on the fourteenth of the month, between the evenings, is the LORD's Passover.

**6** On the fifteenth day of this month is the Festival of Unleavened Bread to the LORD. Seven days you shall eat unleavened bread.

**7** On the first day you shall have a holy assembly. You shall do no ordinary work.

**8** You shall present an offering by fire to the LORD for seven days. On the seventh day is a holy assembly. You shall do no ordinary work."

## The First Sheaf

**9** The LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**10** "Speak to the sons of Israel and say to them: When you come into the land that I am giving you, and you reap its harvest, you shall bring the first sheaf of your harvest to the priest.

**11** He shall wave the sheaf before the LORD, so that it is accepted for you. On the day after the sabbath the priest shall wave it.

**12** On the day you wave the sheaf you shall offer an unblemished lamb a year old as a burnt offering to the LORD,

**13** and its grain offering, two tenths of fine flour mixed with oil, an offering by fire to the LORD, a soothing aroma; and its drink offering, wine, a fourth of a hin.

**14** You shall eat no bread, roasted grain or fresh grain until that very day, until you have brought the offering of your God. It is a lasting statute throughout your generations in all your dwellings.

## Fifty Days

**15** You shall count for yourselves from the day after the sabbath, from the day you bring the sheaf of the wave offering: there shall be seven full sabbaths.

**16** Until the day after the seventh sabbath you shall count fifty days, and you shall present a new grain offering to the LORD.

**17** From your dwellings you shall bring two loaves of bread for waving, made of two tenths of fine flour. They shall be baked with leaven, as firstfruits to the LORD.

**18** With the bread you shall present seven unblemished lambs a year old, and one young bull, and two rams. They shall be a burnt offering to the LORD, with their grain offering and their drink offerings, an offering by fire, a soothing aroma to the LORD.

**19** You shall offer one male goat as a sin offering, and two lambs a year old as a sacrifice of peace offerings.

**20** The priest shall wave them before the LORD with the bread of the firstfruits, together with the two lambs. They shall be holy to the LORD, for the priest.

**21** On that very day you shall proclaim a holy assembly for yourselves. You shall do no ordinary work. It is a lasting statute in all your dwellings throughout your generations.

**22** When you reap the harvest of your land, you shall not reap all the way to the edge of your field, and you shall not gather the gleanings of your harvest. You shall leave them for the poor and for the guest. I am the LORD your God."

## The First of the Seventh Month

**23** The LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**24** "Speak to the sons of Israel, saying: In the seventh month, on the first of the month, you shall have a rest, a remembrance with horn blasts, a holy assembly.

**25** You shall do no ordinary work, and you shall present an offering by fire to the LORD."

## The Day of Atonement

**26** The LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**27** "But on the tenth of this seventh month is the Day of Atonement. It shall be a holy assembly for you, and you shall afflict yourselves, and you shall present an offering by fire to the LORD.

**28** You shall do no work at all on that very day, because it is a Day of Atonement, to make atonement for you before the LORD your God.

**29** For any person who does not afflict himself on that very day shall be cut off from his people.

**30** Any person who does any work at all on that very day — I will destroy that person from among his people.

**31** You shall do no work at all. It is a lasting statute throughout your generations in all your dwellings.

**32** It is a sabbath of complete rest for you, and you shall afflict yourselves. On the ninth of the month at evening, from evening to evening, you shall keep your sabbath."

## Booths

**33** The LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**34** "Speak to the sons of Israel, saying: On the fifteenth day of this seventh month is the Festival of Booths, seven days to the LORD.

**35** On the first day is a holy assembly. You shall do no ordinary work.

**36** For seven days you shall present an offering by fire to the LORD. On the eighth day you shall have a holy assembly, and you shall present an offering by fire to the LORD. It is a closing assembly. You shall do no ordinary work.

**37** These are the appointed times of the LORD, which you shall proclaim as holy assemblies, for presenting offerings by fire to the LORD — burnt offering and grain offering, sacrifice and drink offerings, each on its own day —

**38** besides the LORD's sabbaths, and besides your gifts, and besides all your vows, and besides all your freewill offerings, which you give to the LORD.

**39** But on the fifteenth day of the seventh month, when you have gathered in the produce of the land, you shall keep the festival of the LORD seven days. On the first day is a rest, and on the eighth day is a rest.

**40** On the first day you shall take for yourselves the fruit of splendid trees, branches of palm trees, boughs of leafy trees, and willows of the brook, and you shall rejoice before the LORD your God seven days.

**41** You shall keep it as a festival to the LORD seven days in the year. It is a lasting statute throughout your generations. In the seventh month you shall keep it.

**42** You shall live in booths for seven days. Every native Israelite shall live in booths,

**43** so that your generations may know that I made the sons of Israel live in booths when I brought them out of the land of Egypt. I am the LORD your God."

**44** Moses told the sons of Israel the appointed times of the LORD.

## Notes

- **v2 "appointed times… holy assemblies"** — *mo'adim*, from *ya'ad*, to fix a meeting: the word of the *tent of meeting*, *ohel mo'ed*, and of the lights in Genesis 1:14, set in the sky *for appointed times*. The calendar is a set of meetings with God, and the sun and moon were made to keep them. *Holy assembly* is *miqra qodesh*, from *qara*, to call: a holy **calling**. The verb in the same verse is *which you shall **call*** them, so the day is named for the summons that gathers the people.
- **v3 "You shall do no work at all"** — the chapter uses two different rules and keeps them apart every time. On the sabbath and the Day of Atonement it is *kol melakhah*, **any work** at all (vv3, 28, 30, 31). On every festival day it is *melekhet avodah*, **ordinary work**, the work of a trade (vv7, 8, 21, 25, 35, 36), which is why Exodus 12:16 could let food be made on the first day of Unleavened Bread. Only the two days of total rest are called *sabbath of complete rest*.
- **v10 "the sheaf"** — *omer*, which in Exodus 16 was a measure of manna, one per person each day. Here it is a sheaf of standing grain, and the first one belongs to God before anyone eats bread from the new harvest (v14).
- **v11 "on the day after the sabbath"** — the text does not say which sabbath: the weekly Sabbath, or the first day of Unleavened Bread (v7), which is kept like one. The two readings put the sheaf, and the fifty days that are counted from it, on different dates, and later Jewish communities divided over exactly this line.
- **vv15–16 "seven full sabbaths… fifty days"** — *sabbath* here also means the week it closes. Seven weeks are counted, and the fiftieth day is the festival. The Greek word for *fiftieth* is *pentēkostē*, and it is the name the New Testament gives this day: *when the day of Pentecost had come* (Acts 2:1).
- **v17 "baked with leaven"** — leaven is banned from the altar (2:11), and for the seven days of Unleavened Bread it is not to be eaten at all. Fifty days later the offering is two loaves **made with leaven**. They are waved before the LORD and go to the priest (v20); they are not burned. The year opens with bread that has had no time to rise and reaches its harvest with bread that has.
- **v22 the edge of the field** — the law of 19:9–10, set down again in the middle of the calendar. The harvest festival is not complete without the part of the harvest that is not taken.
- **v24 "a remembrance with horn blasts"** — *zikhron teru'ah*. *Teru'ah* is a blast or a great shout: it is the shout that brings down Jericho's walls (Joshua 6:5), and the *shofar teru'ah*, the horn blast, of the jubilee (25:9). This verse names no instrument, and it does not say what is remembered. The day is the first of the seventh month; nothing in the text calls it a new year.
- **v27 "the Day of Atonement"** — *yom ha-kippurim*, and the noun is plural: the day of **atonements**. It is the day of chapter 16, set here in the year.
- **v30 "I will destroy that person"** — every other penalty in the chapter is *cut off* (v29). For working on this day God speaks in the first person: *ve-ha'avadti*, **I will destroy**, and it is the only time the five books of Moses use this form.
- **v32 "from evening to evening"** — the only place in the Bible that says outright where a sacred day begins and ends: at evening. The tenth of the month begins on the evening of the ninth. It is the order of Genesis 1: *there was evening, and there was morning*.
- **v36 "a closing assembly"** — *atseret*, from *atsar*, to hold back, to restrain. The eighth day holds the people back one day more after the seven.
- **v40 "the fruit of splendid trees"** — *peri ets hadar*. *Hadar* is splendor, majesty; the Hebrew names the quality and not the tree. The other three are plain: palm branches, leafy boughs, willows that grow by water.
- **v40 "you shall rejoice"** — *u-semachtem*, and it is **the first time in the Bible that God commands people to rejoice**. Genesis and Exodus mention gladness; neither commands it. The command comes with the harvest in, living in booths.
- **vv42–43 "booths… when I brought them out"** — *sukkot*. On the night they left Egypt, the first place Israel camped was a town called *Sukkot*, **Booths** (Exodus 12:37). The festival makes every generation live where the first night out of slavery was spent.
