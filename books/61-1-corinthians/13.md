# 1 Corinthians 13

## Without Love

**1** If I speak in the tongues of men and of angels, but do not have love, I have become a noisy gong or a clanging cymbal.

**2** If I have prophecy and understand all mysteries and all knowledge, and if I have all faith, so as to move mountains, but do not have love, I am nothing.

**3** If I give away all I have to feed others, and if I hand over my body to be burned, but do not have love, I gain nothing.

## What Love Is

**4** Love is patient, love is kind. Love does not envy, does not brag, is not puffed up,

**5** does not behave shamefully, does not seek its own, is not easily angered, does not reckon up wrongs,

**6** does not rejoice at unrighteousness, but rejoices with the truth.

**7** It bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, endures all things.

## Love Never Fails

**8** Love never fails. As for prophecies, they will be done away with; as for tongues, they will cease; as for knowledge, it will be done away with.

**9** For we know in part and we prophesy in part,

**10** but when what is complete comes, what is in part will be done away with.

**11** When I was a child, I spoke like a child, I thought like a child, I reasoned like a child; when I became a man, I did away with the things of a child.

**12** For now we see in a mirror, in a riddle, but then face to face. Now I know in part; then I will know fully, even as I have been fully known.

**13** So now faith, hope and love remain, these three; but the greatest of these is love.

## Notes

- **v3 "if I hand over my body to be burned"** — *paradō*, the verb of *the night when He was handed over* (11:23). The edition reads *kauthēsomai*, **so that I may be burned**, and marks the word as one where manuscripts differ; some English Bibles have **so that I may boast**, *kauchēsōmai*.
- **vv4–5 "is not puffed up… does not seek its own… does not reckon up wrongs"** — each line answers the letter. *Puffed up* is the Corinthians' fault from 4:6 to 8:1; *does not seek its own* is 10:24, *Let no one seek his own good*. *Ou logizetai to kakon*, **does not reckon up wrongs**, is the accounting verb of Romans 4, *blessed is the man whose sin the Lord will never reckon* (Romans 4:8; Psalm 32:2): love keeps no ledger, as God keeps none.
- **v12 "in a mirror, in a riddle, but then face to face"** — the words God used of Moses: *Mouth to mouth I speak with him, plainly and not in riddles* (Numbers 12:8), *ou di' ainigmatōn* in the Greek, the word Paul has here; and *The LORD would speak to Moses face to face, as a man speaks to his companion* (Exodus 33:11). What Moses had, all will have. *Even as I have been fully known* goes back to 8:3, *if anyone loves God, he is known by Him*.
