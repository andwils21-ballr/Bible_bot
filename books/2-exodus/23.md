# Exodus 23

## In Court

**1** You shall not lift up an empty report. You shall not put your hand in with a guilty man to be a violent witness.

**2** You shall not follow the crowd into wrong, and you shall not testify in a dispute so as to side with the crowd and pervert justice.

**3** And you shall not favor a poor man in his dispute.

**4** When you come upon your enemy's ox or his donkey wandering, you shall surely bring it back to him.

**5** When you see the donkey of the one who hates you lying down under its load, do not hold back from him — you shall surely help him with it.

**6** You shall not turn aside the judgment of your needy in his dispute.

**7** Keep far from a false charge. And do not kill the innocent and the righteous, for I will not acquit the guilty.

**8** And you shall not take a bribe, because a bribe blinds the clear-sighted and twists the words of the righteous.

**9** And you shall not press down a foreigner, for you yourselves know the soul of the foreigner — because you were foreigners in the land of Egypt.

## The Seventh

**10** And six years you shall sow your land and gather in its produce.

**11** And the seventh you shall let go and leave it, and the needy of your people shall eat, and what they leave the beast of the field shall eat. So you shall do with your vineyard and with your olive grove.

**12** Six days you shall do your work, and on the seventh day you shall stop — so that your ox and your donkey may rest, and the son of your slave woman and the foreigner may draw breath.

**13** And in all that I have said to you, be watchful. And the name of other gods you shall not mention; it shall not be heard on your mouth.

## Three Times in the Year

**14** Three times in the year you shall hold a festival to Me.

**15** The Festival of Unleavened Bread you shall keep. Seven days you shall eat unleavened bread, as I commanded you, at the appointed time of the month of Abib, because in it you came out of Egypt. And they shall not appear before My face empty-handed.

**16** And the Festival of the Harvest, the firstfruits of your work that you sow in the field. And the Festival of the Gathering, at the going out of the year, when you gather in your work from the field.

**17** Three times in the year all your males shall appear before the face of the Lord, the LORD.

**18** You shall not offer the blood of My sacrifice with anything leavened, and the fat of My festival shall not stay the night until morning.

**19** The first of the firstfruits of your ground you shall bring to the house of the LORD your God. You shall not boil a young goat in its mother's milk.

## The Messenger Before You

**20** Here — I am sending a messenger before you, to guard you on the way and to bring you to the place I have prepared.

**21** Be watchful before him and listen to his voice. Do not rebel against him, for he will not bear your transgression, because My name is within him.

**22** But if you truly listen to his voice and do all that I speak, then I will be an enemy to your enemies and an adversary to your adversaries.

**23** For My messenger will go before you and bring you to the Amorite and the Hittite and the Perizzite and the Canaanite, the Hivite and the Jebusite, and I will wipe them out.

**24** You shall not bow down to their gods and you shall not serve them, and you shall not do as they do. You shall utterly tear them down and utterly break their standing stones.

**25** And you shall serve the LORD your God, and He will bless your bread and your water, and I will take sickness away from among you.

**26** There shall be no woman miscarrying or barren in your land. I will fill out the number of your days.

**27** I will send My terror before you and throw into confusion every people you come among, and I will make all your enemies turn their backs to you.

**28** And I will send the hornet before you, and it shall drive out the Hivite, the Canaanite and the Hittite from before you.

**29** I will not drive them out from before you in one year, or the land would become desolate and the beast of the field multiply against you.

**30** Little by little I will drive them out from before you, until you are fruitful and take the land as your possession.

**31** And I will set your border from the Sea of Reeds to the Sea of the Philistines, and from the wilderness to the River. For I will give the inhabitants of the land into your hand, and you shall drive them out from before you.

**32** You shall not cut a covenant with them or with their gods.

**33** They shall not live in your land, or they will make you sin against Me — for you would serve their gods, and it would become a snare to you.

## Notes

- **v1 "lift up an empty report"** — *lo tissa shema shav*, and the third commandment three chapters back was *lo tissa et shem… la-shav*, **you shall not lift up the name for emptiness** (20:7). Same verb, same noun *shav*; the only difference is *shem*, name, against *shema*, a thing heard — one letter apart in Hebrew. Swearing falsely by God and spreading a rumor are written as the same act performed on different objects.
- **vv3, 6 "you shall not favor a poor man… you shall not turn aside the judgment of your needy"** — both, three verses apart. The court is told not to tilt against the poor man and not to tilt **toward** him either. The second is the one no English reader expects, and it is the reason the first is not charity: what is being protected is the judgment, not the party.
- **v5 "do not hold back from him — you shall surely help him with it"** — the verb is *azav*, which everywhere else means to **leave, abandon, let go**, and it appears here twice with opposite force required by the sentence: you must not *azav* him, you must surely *azav* with him. Either it carries a second sense of loosing a load that survives nowhere else, or the text is damaged. What the law wants is plain regardless, and it is aimed at an enemy: his animal is down, and you are the one who has to get under it with him.
- **v9 "you know the soul of the foreigner"** — the second time in two chapters that the Egypt memory is given as the reason (22:21), and this one adds *nefesh ha-ger*: not his situation, his **soul** — the inside of it. The law assumes its readers still have that knowledge available and can be argued with from it.
- **v11 "you shall let it go"** — *tishmetennah*, from *shamat*, to drop or release something you are holding. It is the root behind *shemittah*, the name the seventh year eventually carried. And the beneficiaries are named in order: the needy of your people first, and whatever they leave, **wild animals**. The land's rest is legislated as somebody else's food.
- **v12 "may draw breath"** — *yinnafesh*, a verb built on *nefesh*, throat and breath and life. The sabbath command here gives no reason about creation and no reason about Egypt: the reason is that the ox, the donkey, the slave woman's son and the foreigner get to breathe. The one person whose rest is not mentioned is the man being commanded.
- **v13 "it shall not be heard on your mouth"** — not *do not worship them*: do not **say the names**. The prohibition is on the sound, and the phrasing puts the hearing in someone else's ear — a name is not to be heard coming off your lips.
- **v14 "three times"** — *shalosh regalim*, and *regel* is a **foot**. Three footings in the year, three times the whole body has to get up and go somewhere. Hebrew counts these festivals by the walking they require, which is why they are pilgrim feasts, and English "times" loses the legs entirely.
- **v19 "you shall not boil a young goat in its mother's milk"** — stated three times in the Torah and never once explained: here, at 34:26, and at Deuteronomy 14:21. No reason, no penalty, no context — at 34:26 it again follows the firstfruits, and at Deuteronomy it follows a rule about carrion. The entire separation of meat from dairy in later Jewish practice rests on a sentence the text gives no argument for.
- **v21 "My name is within him"** — *ki shemi be-qirbo*. The messenger must be obeyed, cannot forgive, and carries the name **inside** him. Nothing in Exodus explains what that means, and the chapter moves straight on to boundaries and battle plans. Set beside 33:2–3, where God offers to send a messenger *instead* of going Himself and Moses refuses, the sentence becomes the question the rest of the book argues about: whether a messenger with the name in him is the same as God going along.
- **v28 "the hornet"** — *tzir'ah*, a word that occurs three times in the Hebrew Bible and all three are this same promise about clearing the land (here, Deuteronomy 7:20, Joshua 24:12). Because it never appears in an ordinary sentence, nothing fixes its meaning: a stinging insect is the traditional reading, and a plague or a terror that goes ahead of an army has been argued from the same letters. The one thing the word always does is drive people out.
- **vv29–30 "little by little"** — *me'at me'at*. The reason given for the slowness is ecological rather than moral: clear the land too fast and it goes to waste, and the wild animals take it over. The conquest is paced to how quickly one nation can actually farm.
