# Exodus 32

## The Calf

**1** And the people saw that Moses was taking a long time to come down from the mountain, and the people gathered against Aaron and said to him, "Rise, make us gods who will go before us — because this Moses, the man who brought us up from the land of Egypt, we do not know what has become of him."

**2** And Aaron said to them, "Tear off the gold rings that are in the ears of your wives, your sons and your daughters, and bring them to me."

**3** And all the people tore off the gold rings that were in their ears and brought them to Aaron.

**4** And he took them from their hand and shaped it with an engraving tool, and made it into a molten calf. And they said, "These are your gods, Israel, who brought you up from the land of Egypt."

**5** And Aaron saw, and built an altar in front of it. And Aaron called out and said, "A festival to the LORD tomorrow."

**6** And they rose early the next day and offered burnt offerings and brought peace offerings. And the people sat down to eat and drink, and rose up to play.

## Leave Me Alone

**7** And the LORD said to Moses, "Go, get down — because your people, whom you brought up from the land of Egypt, have ruined themselves.

**8** They have turned aside quickly from the way I commanded them. They have made themselves a molten calf and bowed down to it and sacrificed to it, and said, 'These are your gods, Israel, who brought you up from the land of Egypt.'"

**9** And the LORD said to Moses, "I have seen this people, and here — it is a stiff-necked people.

**10** And now, leave Me alone, and My anger will burn against them, and I will finish them. And I will make you into a great nation."

**11** And Moses pleaded with the LORD his God and said, "Why, LORD, should Your anger burn against Your people, whom You brought out of the land of Egypt with great power and with a strong hand?

**12** Why should Egypt say, 'With evil intent He brought them out, to kill them in the mountains and to finish them off the face of the ground'? Turn from Your burning anger and relent of the evil against Your people.

**13** Remember Abraham, Isaac and Israel Your servants, to whom You swore by Yourself and said to them, 'I will multiply your seed as the stars of the heavens, and all this land that I have spoken of I will give to your seed, and they shall inherit it forever.'"

**14** And the LORD relented of the evil He had said He would do to His people.

## Down the Mountain

**15** And Moses turned and went down from the mountain, and the two tablets of the Testimony were in his hand — tablets written on both their sides, on one side and on the other they were written.

**16** And the tablets were the work of God, and the writing was the writing of God, cut into the tablets.

**17** And Joshua heard the sound of the people as they shouted, and he said to Moses, "There is a sound of war in the camp."

**18** And he said, "It is not the sound of shouting for victory, and it is not the sound of shouting for defeat. A sound of singing is what I hear."

**19** And it came to pass, when he came near the camp and saw the calf and the dancing, that Moses' anger burned, and he threw the tablets from his hands and shattered them at the foot of the mountain.

**20** And he took the calf they had made and burned it in the fire, and ground it until it was powder, and scattered it on the face of the water, and made the sons of Israel drink it.

**21** And Moses said to Aaron, "What did this people do to you, that you brought such a great sin on them?"

**22** And Aaron said, "Let my lord's anger not burn. You yourself know the people, that they are set on evil.

**23** And they said to me, 'Make us gods who will go before us — because this Moses, the man who brought us up from the land of Egypt, we do not know what has become of him.'

**24** And I said to them, 'Whoever has gold, tear it off.' And they gave it to me, and I threw it into the fire, and out came this calf."

## Who Is for the LORD

**25** And Moses saw the people, that they were let loose — for Aaron had let them loose, to the derision of those who rose against them.

**26** And Moses stood in the gate of the camp and said, "Whoever is for the LORD — to me!" And all the sons of Levi gathered to him.

**27** And he said to them, "This is what the LORD, God of Israel, has said: Each man put his sword on his thigh. Pass back and forth from gate to gate in the camp, and each man kill his brother and each man his companion and each man his neighbor."

**28** And the sons of Levi did according to Moses' word, and about three thousand men of the people fell that day.

**29** And Moses said, "Fill your hands today for the LORD — for each man has gone against his son and against his brother — and so bring a blessing on yourselves today."

## Blot Me Out

**30** And it came to pass the next day that Moses said to the people, "You yourselves have sinned a great sin. And now I will go up to the LORD. Perhaps I can make atonement for your sin."

**31** And Moses returned to the LORD and said, "Please — this people has sinned a great sin, and they have made themselves gods of gold.

**32** And now, if You would forgive their sin — and if not, blot me out, please, from Your book that You have written."

**33** And the LORD said to Moses, "Whoever has sinned against Me, him I will blot out of My book.

**34** And now, go, lead the people to where I told you. Here — My messenger will go before you. And on the day I call to account, I will call them to account for their sin."

**35** And the LORD struck the people because they made the calf — the one Aaron made.

## Notes

- **v1 "this Moses, the man"** — *zeh Mosheh ha-ish*, and the demonstrative does the work: not *Moses*, but **this Moses fellow**. In the same breath they say he is the one who *brought us up from the land of Egypt*, crediting the man rather than God — and in v4 they will say the calf did it, in exactly the same words. The subject changes twice in four verses and the sentence does not.
- **v2 "the gold rings that are in the ears"** — *nizmei ha-zahav*, and *nezem* is the ring Abraham's servant put in Rebekah's nose in Genesis 24:47. The last time gold jewellery came off Egyptians it was plunder carried out at the exodus (12:35). It goes back into the fire here.
- **v4 "These are your gods, Israel"** — *elleh elohekha*, plural verb and plural pronoun, for one calf. *Elohim* is grammatically plural and normally takes singular verbs when it means God; here the verb is plural too, so the sentence commits: **these** brought you up. The people in v1 asked for *gods who will go* — also plural — while looking at one animal.
- **vv4, 24 Aaron tells it twice, and the second version is a lie** — v4: *he took them from their hand and shaped it with an engraving tool, and made it into a molten calf.* Two deliberate acts with a tool. v24, to Moses: *I threw it into the fire, and out came this calf.* The narrator has already told the reader what happened, so the reader watches the high priest of Israel claim the thing made itself. And v35, the last line of the chapter, quietly settles it: *the calf — the one Aaron made*.
- **v6 "rose up to play"** — *le-tzacheq*, the verb built on Isaac's name. Genesis used it for whatever Ishmael was doing in 21:9, for Isaac and Rebekah in 26:8, and for what Potiphar's wife accused Joseph of in 39:14. Its range runs from laughing to mocking to sexual play, and this verse gives no context at all — the same refusal to specify as Genesis 21:9. Whatever it means here, the word is a grandson's name.
- **v9 "stiff-necked"** — *qesheh oref*, hard of **neck**, and this is its first appearance in the Bible. The noun is the back of the neck, and its last use was nine chapters earlier in a promise: *I will make all your enemies turn their backs to you* (23:27), literally give them to you *by the neck*. The part of the body that means an enemy in flight now means this people facing God.
- **v10 "leave Me alone… I will make you into a great nation"** — *hanichah li*. God asks Moses to let go of Him, which concedes that Moses is holding on, and then offers him **Abraham's promise** — *I will make of you a great nation* is word for word what was said at Genesis 12:2. Moses is offered the founding of a new chosen people and spends the next three verses arguing him out of it, using Abraham as the argument.
- **vv12, 14 "relent"** — *nacham*, the root behind Noah's name and behind Genesis 6:6, where *the LORD regretted that He had made man*. Moses asks God to *be sorry* about the evil, and v14 says He was. English "relent" and "repent" both reach for it; the Hebrew word is the one for a change of feeling, and this chapter has it happen to God twice at a man's request.
- **v18 the sound is described three times** — *ein qol anot gevurah, ve-ein qol anot chalushah, qol annot anokhi shomea*. The same verb *anah* three times, and it is a word that means both **to answer** and **to sing**: no sound of answering-in-strength, no sound of answering-in-weakness, a sound of singing. Joshua the soldier hears a battle. Moses, who has just been told what is down there, hears a choir.
- **v20 "made the sons of Israel drink it"** — burned, ground to powder, scattered on water, drunk. Four verbs to destroy an object that could simply have been melted down. The one ritual in the Torah that makes a person drink something is the ordeal of a wife suspected of unfaithfulness (Numbers 5), where the water carries a written curse dissolved into it.
- **v29 "Fill your hands today"** — *mil'u yedkhem*, the exact idiom for **ordaining a priest**, used four times in chapter 29 and translated there as *ordain*. Levi is installed into the priesthood in this verse, and the qualification is the killing just described. The tribe that gets the sanctuary is ordained with swords, on the day the sanctuary's first object was melted down and drunk.
- **v32 "blot me out from Your book"** — the first mention in the Bible of a book God keeps with names in it, and it appears in an unfinished sentence: *if You would forgive their sin* — and then nothing, no main clause, the offer breaking off before it is made. Moses cannot say *forgive them*, only start to. What he can finish is the alternative, and it is that his own name be erased.
