# Exodus 22

## Restitution

**1** When a man steals an ox or a sheep and slaughters it or sells it, he shall pay five oxen in place of the ox and four sheep in place of the sheep.

**2** If the thief is found breaking in and is struck so that he dies, there is no bloodguilt for him.

**3** If the sun has risen on him, there is bloodguilt. He shall make full payment; and if he has nothing, he shall be sold for his theft.

**4** If the stolen thing is found in his hand alive — from ox to donkey to sheep — he shall pay double.

**5** When a man grazes a field or a vineyard, and sends his beast out and it grazes in another man's field, he shall make it good from the best of his own field and the best of his own vineyard.

**6** When fire breaks out and catches in thorns, and a stack of grain or the standing grain or the field is consumed, the one who started the fire shall make full payment.

**7** When a man gives his neighbor silver or goods to keep, and it is stolen from the man's house — if the thief is found, he shall pay double.

**8** If the thief is not found, then the owner of the house shall be brought to God, to swear that he did not put his hand to his neighbor's property.

**9** In every case of breach of trust — over an ox, over a donkey, over a sheep, over a garment, over anything lost, of which one says, "This is it" — the matter of them both shall come before God. Whomever God declares guilty shall pay double to his neighbor.

**10** When a man gives his neighbor a donkey or an ox or a sheep or any animal to keep, and it dies or is injured or is driven off, with no one seeing,

**11** an oath by the LORD shall be between the two of them, that he did not put his hand to his neighbor's property. And the owner shall accept it, and he shall not pay.

**12** But if it was stolen from him, he shall pay its owner.

**13** If it was torn by a beast, he shall bring it as evidence; he shall not pay for what was torn.

**14** And when a man borrows from his neighbor and it is injured or dies, its owner not being with it, he shall make full payment.

**15** If its owner was with it, he shall not pay. If it was hired, it comes at its hire.

## Things Not Tolerated

**16** And when a man seduces a young woman who is not promised in marriage, and lies with her, he shall pay the bride-price for her, as his wife.

**17** If her father utterly refuses to give her to him, he shall weigh out silver according to the bride-price of young women.

**18** A sorceress you shall not let live.

**19** Whoever lies with an animal shall be put to death.

**20** Whoever sacrifices to gods, except to the LORD alone, shall be devoted to destruction.

## The Foreigner, the Widow, the Poor

**21** And you shall not wrong a foreigner and you shall not press him down, for you were foreigners in the land of Egypt.

**22** You shall not afflict any widow or orphan.

**23** If you do afflict him — if he cries out to Me, I will surely hear his cry,

**24** and My anger will burn, and I will kill you with the sword, and your wives will become widows and your children orphans.

**25** If you lend silver to My people, to the poor among you, you shall not be a creditor to him. You shall not lay interest on him.

**26** If you take your neighbor's cloak as a pledge, you shall return it to him before the sun goes down,

**27** because it is his only covering — it is his garment for his skin. In what shall he sleep? And when he cries out to Me, I will hear, for I am gracious.

## What Is Owed to God

**28** You shall not curse God, and you shall not curse a leader among your people.

**29** You shall not delay your full harvest or your outflow. The firstborn of your sons you shall give to Me.

**30** So you shall do with your ox and with your flock: seven days it shall be with its mother, and on the eighth day you shall give it to Me.

**31** And you shall be men of holiness to Me. And flesh torn in the field you shall not eat; you shall throw it to the dog.

## Notes

- **v1 the chapter starts a verse earlier in Hebrew** — this law is numbered **21:37** in the Hebrew Bible, so the whole of Hebrew chapter 22 runs one verse behind this one. The Greek agrees with the English. Anyone reading this alongside a Hebrew text should expect the offset from here to the end of the chapter.
- **vv2–3 "there is no bloodguilt… there is bloodguilt"** — *damim*, blood in the **plural**, which in Hebrew means liability for blood rather than the substance. The right to kill a housebreaker lasts exactly as long as the dark: *if the sun has risen on him*, the same blow is murder. What changes is not the threat but whether the householder could see who he was hitting.
- **vv8–9 "brought to God… whomever God declares guilty"** — *ha-elohim* again, as in 21:6, and here the procedure is spelled out: where there is no thief and no witness, the case goes to the sanctuary and a man swears. The Greek keeps it as God — *before God* — rather than resolving it into human judges. A property dispute with no evidence is settled by an oath nobody can check, which is the point: the only remaining witness is the one being sworn to.
- **vv16–17 "the bride-price"** — *mohar*, the sum a man paid a bride's family. The law gives the father a veto and leaves the payment standing either way: he may refuse the marriage and still collect the full amount. The woman's own wishes are not part of the ruling.
- **v18 "a sorceress"** — *mekhashefah*, and the Hebrew is **feminine singular**. Every other capital law in this stretch is put in the masculine or in a form covering anyone; this one names a woman. The masculine form of the word exists and is used elsewhere in the canon. It is not used here.
- **v21 "for you were foreigners in the land of Egypt"** — *ger*, a resident outsider, the word this rendering refuses to translate as *sojourner*. The commandment does not argue from principle or from the foreigner's rights. It argues from **memory**: you know what this feels like from the inside. It is the only reason given, and the book will give it again.
- **vv23–24 "your wives will become widows and your children orphans"** — the sentence is the crime handed back. Afflict a widow and an orphan, and the punishment named is that your own household becomes widows and orphans. Nothing else in this chapter is framed that way; every other penalty is payment, death, or destruction.
- **vv26–27 "In what shall he sleep?"** — a rhetorical question, in the middle of a statute. The law is about a poor man's cloak taken as security on a loan, and instead of stating a rule it asks what the man is supposed to do at night, then says God will hear him if he cries out, *ki chanun ani* — **for I am gracious**. A legal code stops to picture one cold man and gives God's character as the enforcement.
- **v28 "You shall not curse God, and… a leader"** — *elohim* and *nasi*, and the Greek read the first word as a plural: **Θεούς**, *gods*. Paul quotes the second half in Greek at his trial — *you shall not speak evil of a ruler of your people* (Acts 23:5) — in the Septuagint's wording rather than the Hebrew's, after insulting the high priest and being told who he was.
- **v29 "your outflow… the firstborn of your sons"** — *dim'akha*, and the ordinary meaning of *dim'a* is a **tear**, the kind you weep; here it has to mean the juice that runs from a press, wine or oil. The word for what comes out of an eye is the word for what comes out of crushed fruit. Then the verse turns without a pause to firstborn sons, and — unlike 13:13, which says *every firstborn of man among your sons you shall redeem* — says nothing about redemption. The book carries both sentences and does not reconcile them here.
