# Exodus 27

## The Altar

**1** "And you shall make the altar of acacia wood, five cubits long and five cubits wide — the altar shall be square — and three cubits its height.

**2** And you shall make its horns on its four corners. Its horns shall be of one piece with it, and you shall overlay it with bronze.

**3** And you shall make its pots for clearing its ashes, and its shovels and its basins and its forks and its fire pans. All its vessels you shall make of bronze.

**4** And you shall make for it a grating, a network of bronze, and on the network you shall make four bronze rings at its four corners.

**5** And you shall set it under the ledge of the altar, below, so that the network reaches halfway up the altar.

**6** And you shall make poles for the altar, poles of acacia wood, and overlay them with bronze.

**7** And its poles shall be put into the rings, and the poles shall be on the two sides of the altar when it is carried.

**8** Hollow, of boards, you shall make it. As He showed you on the mountain, so they shall make it.

## The Court

**9** And you shall make the court of the dwelling. For the south side, facing south, hangings for the court of fine twisted linen, a hundred cubits long for the one side,

**10** and its twenty pillars and their twenty sockets of bronze, the hooks of the pillars and their bands of silver.

**11** And so for the north side, in its length, hangings a hundred long, and its twenty pillars and their twenty sockets of bronze, the hooks of the pillars and their bands of silver.

**12** And for the width of the court on the west side, hangings of fifty cubits, their pillars ten and their sockets ten.

**13** And the width of the court on the east side, facing the sunrise, fifty cubits.

**14** And fifteen cubits of hangings for the one shoulder, their pillars three and their sockets three.

**15** And for the other shoulder, fifteen of hangings, their pillars three and their sockets three.

**16** And for the gate of the court, a screen of twenty cubits, of blue and purple and crimson and fine twisted linen, the work of an embroiderer; their pillars four and their sockets four.

**17** All the pillars of the court all around shall be banded with silver, their hooks of silver and their sockets of bronze.

**18** The length of the court, a hundred cubits, and the width fifty by fifty, and the height five cubits, of fine twisted linen; and their sockets of bronze.

**19** All the vessels of the dwelling, in all its service, and all its pegs and all the pegs of the court, shall be bronze.

## The Lamp

**20** And you — you shall command the sons of Israel, and let them bring you clear oil of beaten olives for the light, to set up a lamp continually.

**21** In the tent of meeting, outside the veil that is over the Testimony, Aaron and his sons shall set it in order from evening until morning before the LORD. A lasting statute for their generations, from the sons of Israel."

## Notes

- **v2 "its horns… of one piece with it"** — *qarnot*, horns, projecting from the four corners and beaten out of the same block, not fixed on. Nothing here says what they are for, but the law already assumed it: *you shall take him from My altar to die* (21:14) only makes sense if a man could run to the altar and expect to be safe. Centuries later Adonijah does exactly that — *he rose and went and took hold of the horns of the altar* (1 Kings 1:50). A frightened man's first move was to grab these, and the chapter that describes them never explains why.
- **v3 "its pots for clearing its ashes"** — one Hebrew word, *ledashno*, built from *deshen*, the **fat ash** left by a burnt offering. The verb made from the noun means to take the noun **away**: to de-ash. English does this too — to *dust* a shelf is to remove dust, to *bone* a fish is to remove bones — and the first item on the altar's equipment list is the bucket for carrying out what is left after the fire.
- **v8 "hollow, of boards"** — *nevuv luchot*. The altar of the whole sanctuary is a bronze-plated wooden box with nothing inside it. And the word for the boards is *luchot*, the same noun as the **tablets** of stone Moses is on the mountain to collect (24:12). The two objects the chapter's vocabulary links are the empty altar and the written law.
- **v8 "as He showed you on the mountain"** — the fourth time in three chapters that the instruction ends by pointing back at something Moses was shown (25:9, 25:40, 26:30, and here). Nothing in Exodus describes what he saw. The pattern is invoked four times and never once depicted.
- **vv9–18 the sanctuary sits inside a fence** — a hundred cubits by fifty, five cubits high, of plain white linen. Everything gold and blue and crimson is inside it; from outside, the entire structure is a long white wall a little taller than a man, with nothing visible over the top.
- **v16 "the gate of the court"** — twenty cubits of blue, purple and crimson, in three hundred cubits of undyed linen. It is the only color anywhere on the outside, and it marks the one way in. The same embroiderer's work covers the door of the tent (26:36), so a person walking inward passes three colored screens, each finer than the last, and never sees a plain door.
- **v17, 19 the metals are graded by distance** — gold for everything inside the veil, gold and silver at the frames, silver bands and bronze sockets around the court, bronze for the altar, bronze for every peg in the ground. The further from the ark, the cheaper the metal, with no verse saying so. The scheme is only visible if you read the inventory as a map.
- **v18 "fifty by fifty"** — the court has just been given as a hundred long and fifty wide, and then the summary says the width is *fifty by fifty*. The phrase does not fit the measurements above it, and the Hebrew as it stands cannot be made to. It is one of the places where the building specification does not add up.
- **v20 "clear oil of beaten olives"** — *zakh katit*: olives **crushed** rather than pressed, which yields the first and cleanest oil and very little of it. The lamp in front of God is fuelled by the most wasteful grade available.
- **v21 "in the tent of meeting"** — *ohel mo'ed*, and this is its **first appearance in the Bible**. The structure has been the *mishkan*, the dwelling, and the *ohel*, the tent; now it gets the name it will carry through the rest of the Torah, built on *mo'ed*, an appointed meeting — the same root as *I will meet with you there* at 25:22. And the first duty attached to it is keeping a light burning **from evening until morning**: the one thing that must never stop is required only at night.
