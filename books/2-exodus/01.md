# Exodus 1

**1** And these are the names of the sons of Israel coming to Egypt with Jacob, each man and his household they came:

**2** Reuben, Simeon, Levi, and Judah;

**3** Issachar, Zebulun, and Benjamin;

**4** Dan and Naphtali, Gad and Asher.

**5** And all the souls who came out of Jacob's loins were seventy souls. And Joseph was in Egypt.

**6** And Joseph died, and all his brothers, and all that generation.

**7** And the sons of Israel were fruitful and swarmed and multiplied and grew numerous beyond measure, and the land was filled with them.

## A New King

**8** And a new king rose over Egypt, who did not know Joseph.

**9** And he said to his people, "Look — the people of the sons of Israel are more numerous and stronger than we are.

**10** Come, let us deal shrewdly with them — otherwise they will multiply, and it will happen that if war breaks out they will be added to those who hate us, and fight against us, and go up out of the land."

**11** And they set over them chiefs of forced labor, to afflict them with their burdens. And they built store cities for Pharaoh: Pithom and Rameses.

**12** But the more they afflicted them, the more they multiplied and the more they broke out. And they dreaded the sons of Israel.

**13** And Egypt made the sons of Israel serve with crushing labor.

**14** And they made their lives bitter with hard service — in mortar and in bricks, and in every service in the field — all their service in which they made them serve, with crushing labor.

## Shiphrah and Puah

**15** And the king of Egypt said to the Hebrew midwives, of whom the name of one was Shiphrah and the name of the second was Puah,

**16** and he said, "When you deliver the Hebrew women, look at the two stones: if it is a son, put him to death, and if it is a daughter, she may live."

**17** And the midwives feared God, and they did not do as the king of Egypt spoke to them, and they let the boys live.

**18** And the king of Egypt called for the midwives and said to them, "Why have you done this thing, and let the boys live?"

**19** And the midwives said to Pharaoh, "Because the Hebrew women are not like the Egyptian women — they are vigorous. Before the midwife comes to them, they have given birth."

**20** And God did well by the midwives, and the people multiplied and grew very numerous.

**21** And it came to pass, because the midwives feared God, that He made them households.

**22** And Pharaoh commanded all his people, saying, "Every son that is born, throw him into the Nile, and every daughter you may let live."

## Notes

- **v1 "And these are the names"** — *ve-elleh shemot*, and the book starts with **and**. It does not begin; it continues, picking up Genesis 46:8, which opened its own list with almost the same words. The Hebrew name of this book is *Shemot*, **Names**, taken from this first line — so the book English calls Exodus is called Names by the people whose book it is.
- **v1 "each man and his household"** — *ish u-veito*, the distributive *ish* again: each one with his own. The same construction that made Genesis 7:2 look, in English, as though the animals were married.
- **v5 "seventy souls"** — the figure from Genesis 46:27, and *yots'ei yerekh Ya'aqov*, **those who came out of Jacob's loins**, is that chapter's phrase too (46:26). The last verse of Genesis put Joseph in a coffin in Egypt; the fifth verse of Exodus says *and Joseph was in Egypt*, and then kills him in the next line. The seam is deliberate and it is only two verses wide.
- **v7 five verbs, and all of them are creation's** — *paru va-yishretsu va-yirbu va-ya'atsmu bi-me'od me'od*. **Fruitful** and **multiply** are the blessing of Genesis 1:28. **Swarmed**, *sharats*, is the word for what the waters do on the fifth day, 1:20–21 — used here of people. And *va-timmale ha-arets otam*, **the land was filled with them**, is 1:28's *fill the earth*, arriving at last.

  It is also the promise to Abraham coming true. *Va-ya'atsmu* is *atsum*, the word of Genesis 18:18 — *a great and numerous nation*. The blessing reaches its fulfilment in the first paragraph of the book about slavery, and that fulfilment is precisely what the next verse treats as a problem.
- **v8 "who did not know Joseph"** — *asher lo yada et Yosef*, and *yada*, to know, is the verb Genesis ran on: Adam knew Eve, Joseph made himself known to his brothers. A whole book of knowing ends, and the new reign begins with a man who does not.
- **v9 "more numerous and stronger than we are"** — *rav ve-atsum mimmennu*, and *atsum* is the same word as v7 and as the promise in Genesis 18:18. Pharaoh is quoting God's blessing back as a threat, without knowing it. He is the first person in the Bible to be frightened by a promise.
- **v10 "let us deal shrewdly"** — *nitchakkemah*, from *chakham*, to be wise. Egypt's answer to a divine promise is a policy meeting. And the plan's stated fear is not that they will rise up but that they will **leave**: *ve-alah min ha-arets*, and go up out of the land — *alah*, the verb God used to Jacob in Genesis 46:4, *I will surely bring you up*. Pharaoh's nightmare and God's promise are the same sentence.
- **v11 "Pithom and Rameses"** — Genesis 47:11 settled Jacob's family *in the best of the land, in the land of Rameses*, as a gift. Four hundred years later the same name is a store city they are whipped into building. Genesis never points this out. Exodus does not either. The word simply comes back.
- **v12 "the more they afflicted them, the more they multiplied and the more they broke out"** — *yifrots*, from *parats*, to burst through a wall — the verb of Jacob's wealth in Genesis 30:43 and of the baby who shoved past his brother to be born in 38:29. Oppression is applied and the response is the creation blessing again, harder. Then *va-yaqutsu*, they **dreaded** them, a verb that carries loathing and sickness at once.
- **vv13, 14 "crushing labor"** — *be-farekh*, twice, framing the sentence. *Perekh* is rare and its force is harshness or breaking; the word itself is hard to say. And *va-yemareru et chayyeihem*, **they made their lives bitter** — *marar*. Eleven chapters later, at the first Passover, Israel is told to eat the lamb *with **bitter herbs***, *merorim*, from this root. The meal remembers the word.
- **v15 the midwives are named, and the king is not** — Shiphrah and Puah. The most powerful man on earth is *the king of Egypt* and then *Pharaoh*, a title, never a name. Two midwives who disobey him are named twice each. The text records who it thinks is worth remembering.
- **v16 "the two stones"** — *ha-ovnayim*, the same word Jeremiah 18:3 uses for a potter's wheel: a physical object, likely a birth-stool. The phrase just marks the point in delivery when the child's sex became visible.
- **v17 "and the midwives feared God"** — the first recorded disobedience of a king in the Bible, and the reason given is fear of God rather than courage or pity. They are not Israelites by anything the text says; they are called *the Hebrew midwives*, which can mean midwives who are Hebrew or midwives to the Hebrews, and the Hebrew will not decide.
- **v19 "they are vigorous"** — *ki chayot hennah*. *Chayah* is the ordinary word for a living creature, and in Genesis it is the word for **beasts** as opposed to livestock. So the sentence they feed Pharaoh is either *they are full of life* or, said to a man who already despises them, *they are like animals*. He accepts it. The midwives may be lying, flattering his contempt, or both, and the text does not say.
- **v21 "He made them households"** — *va-ya'as lahem battim*. God gives the two women who preserved other people's children families of their own. *Bayit*, house, is also a dynasty in Hebrew — what David is promised in 2 Samuel 7.
- **v22 "throw him into the Nile"** — *ha-ye'orah*, the Egyptian loanword for the Nile that Genesis 41 used for Pharaoh's dream. The river that made Egypt rich in Joseph's story is turned into a method of disposal. And the command is given to *all his people*, not to officials — every Egyptian is now deputized.
