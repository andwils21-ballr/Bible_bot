# Exodus 36

## The Only Time Israel Has to Be Told to Stop

**1** "And Bezalel and Oholiab shall work, and every man with a wise heart in whom the LORD has put wisdom and understanding to know how to do all the work of the service of the holy place, according to all that the LORD has commanded."

**2** And Moses called Bezalel and Oholiab and every man with a wise heart in whose heart the LORD had put wisdom — everyone whose heart lifted him to come near to the work, to do it.

**3** And they took from before Moses the whole contribution that the sons of Israel had brought for the work of the service of the holy place, to do it. And the people kept bringing him freewill offerings, morning after morning.

**4** And all the wise men doing all the work of the holy place came, each from the work he was doing,

**5** and said to Moses, "The people are bringing far more than the service needs for the work that the LORD commanded to be done."

**6** And Moses gave an order, and they had word carried through the camp: "Let no man or woman do any more work for the contribution of the holy place." And the people were held back from bringing,

**7** and what had come in was enough for all the work, to do it — and there was more than enough.

## The Curtains and the Tent

**8** And every one with a wise heart among those doing the work made the dwelling of ten curtains of fine twisted linen, and blue and purple and crimson. With cherubim, the work of a designer, he made them.

**9** The length of one curtain, twenty-eight cubits, and the width four cubits, for one curtain. One measure for all the curtains.

**10** And he joined five of the curtains to one another, and five curtains he joined to one another.

**11** And he made loops of blue on the edge of the outermost curtain of the first set; he did the same on the edge of the outermost curtain of the second set.

**12** Fifty loops he made on the one curtain, and fifty loops he made on the edge of the curtain that is in the second set, the loops facing one another.

**13** And he made fifty clasps of gold and joined the curtains to one another with the clasps, and the dwelling was one.

**14** And he made curtains of goats' hair for a tent over the dwelling. Eleven curtains he made them.

**15** The length of one curtain, thirty cubits, and four cubits the width of one curtain. One measure for the eleven curtains.

**16** And he joined five of the curtains by themselves, and six of the curtains by themselves.

**17** And he made fifty loops on the edge of the outermost curtain in the first set, and fifty loops he made on the edge of the curtain of the second set.

**18** And he made fifty clasps of bronze to join the tent together, to be one.

**19** And he made a covering for the tent of rams' skins dyed red, and a covering of fine leather above it.

## The Frames

**20** And he made the frames for the dwelling of acacia wood, standing upright.

**21** Ten cubits the length of a frame, and a cubit and a half the width of one frame.

**22** Two tenons for each frame, fitted to one another. So he did for all the frames of the dwelling.

**23** And he made the frames for the dwelling: twenty frames for the south side, facing south.

**24** And forty sockets of silver he made under the twenty frames — two sockets under one frame for its two tenons, and two sockets under the next frame for its two tenons.

**25** And for the second side of the dwelling, on the north side, he made twenty frames,

**26** and their forty sockets of silver — two sockets under one frame and two sockets under the next frame.

**27** And for the rear of the dwelling, toward the sea, he made six frames.

**28** And two frames he made for the corners of the dwelling, at the rear.

**29** And they were doubled below, and together they were whole to the top of each, at the first ring. So he did for both of them, for the two corners.

**30** And there were eight frames, and their sockets of silver, sixteen sockets — two sockets, two sockets, under each frame.

**31** And he made bars of acacia wood: five for the frames of the one side of the dwelling,

**32** and five bars for the frames of the second side of the dwelling, and five bars for the frames of the dwelling at the rear, toward the sea.

**33** And he made the middle bar to run through the middle of the frames from end to end.

**34** And he overlaid the frames with gold, and made their rings of gold as holders for the bars, and overlaid the bars with gold.

## The Veil and the Screen

**35** And he made the veil of blue and purple and crimson and fine twisted linen. The work of a designer he made it, with cherubim.

**36** And he made for it four pillars of acacia and overlaid them with gold, their hooks of gold, and cast for them four sockets of silver.

**37** And he made a screen for the entrance of the tent, of blue and purple and crimson and fine twisted linen, the work of an embroiderer,

**38** and its five pillars and their hooks; and he overlaid their tops and their bands with gold, and their five sockets were bronze.

## Notes

- **vv5–7 the one place in the Bible where giving has to be stopped** — the craftsmen walk off the job to report a problem, and the problem is that too much is coming in. Moses issues the only order of its kind in the Torah: *let no man or woman do any more work for the contribution*. Six chapters earlier the same people handed over the same gold as fast as it was asked for, and Aaron cast it into a calf (32:3–4). Nothing here points back at that. The book simply puts the two scenes in the same building and lets a reader notice that the fault was never the generosity.
- **v6 "they had word carried through the camp"** — *va-ya'aviru qol*, literally **they made a voice pass over**. The verb is *avar*, the crossing-over verb: what the LORD does through Egypt on the night of the Passover (12:12), what Israel does through the sea. Here it is what a proclamation does through a camp — the announcement travels the way a person travels, from one end to the other.
- **v7 "and there was more than enough"** — *ve-hoter*, one word, from *yatar*, to remain over. It is the surplus word: what is left of the Passover lamb in the morning (12:10), what the hail has left for the locusts to eat (10:15). The building account of the sanctuary ends its collection with the same word the plague narrative used for leftovers, and it is the only time in Exodus the leftover is a good thing.
- **vv8–38 every command has become a past tense** — chapter 26 gave all of this in the second person future: *you shall make ten curtains*, *you shall make fifty clasps of gold*. Here every one of those sentences returns as *va-ya'as*, **and he made**. Thirty verses of instruction are repeated almost word for word with only the verb changed, and the repetition is the point — Hebrew narrative reports obedience by saying the command again with the tense moved. Nothing is summarized; the reader is made to walk the whole list twice.
- **v8 "the work of a designer" and v37 "the work of an embroiderer"** — *ma'aseh choshev* and *ma'aseh roqem*, two distinct crafts, and the text assigns them by rank of location. The designer's work — from *chashav*, to devise or calculate — goes on the ten curtains and on the veil that hides the ark (v35). The embroiderer's work goes on the screen at the outer entrance (v37). The nearer a fabric gets to the ark, the higher the craft named on it.
- **v25 "the second side of the dwelling"** — *tsela*, and the noun is **rib**. It is the word for what is taken out of the man in Genesis 2:21–22 — *He took one of his ribs… and the LORD God built the rib that He had taken from the man into a woman*. Hebrew uses it for the flank of a building as naturally as for the flank of a body, and the verb attached to it in Genesis is *banah*, to build. The dwelling has ribs and sides; the first woman is constructed out of one.
- **vv27, 32 "toward the sea"** — *yammah*, from *yam*, sea. Hebrew has no neutral word for west: west is where the Mediterranean is. The other directions in these verses work the same way — *negev* for south is the name of the dry country below Judah, and *teimanah* is the right hand of someone facing the sunrise. A Hebrew compass is a description of standing in one specific place and looking east.
- **v29 "they were whole to the top of each"** — *tammim*, from *tamam*, to be complete or without defect. It is the word for Noah, *tamim in his generations* (Genesis 6:9), and the standard for an animal fit to be offered. Applied here to two corner planks, it means they ran the full height without a break — the same word for a man's integrity, a lamb's soundness, and a joint that does not gap.
