    <h2>Notes on what's compressed, and what's left out</h2>
    <p><strong>Shapes.</strong> Square-cornered boxes are individual people the narrative follows. Pill shapes are peoples and nations &mdash; a name that has become a population. Dashed boxes bundle names that appear once in a genealogical list and are never seen again as characters; they are real, but the text gives them no story of their own.</p>
    <p><strong>The fading dots</strong> under the lowest name on each branch stand for the descendants the text never counts. Every line here runs on past the last box.</p>
    <p><strong>Perizzite isn't on this tree at all.</strong> Unlike Hittite, Amorite, Hivite, and Jebusite, it never appears in Genesis 10's genealogy of Canaan. Its name most likely describes a way of life &mdash; people of the open, unwalled country &mdash; rather than a bloodline, so it has no ancestor to hang from here.</p>
    <p>This tree stops at what's directly relevant to Israel's own story. It leaves out the deeper reaches of Japheth's line, most of Genesis 36's long list of Edomite chiefs, and every genealogy past Jacob's twelve sons.</p>
    <p class="cite">Sources: Genesis 10 (the Table of Nations), Genesis 11:10&ndash;32, 19:36&ndash;38, 22:20&ndash;24, 25:1&ndash;18, 35:22&ndash;26, 36:1&ndash;19; Exodus 3:1, 3:8.</p>
