# Exodus 5

**1** And afterward Moses and Aaron came and said to Pharaoh, "This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: Send My people away, and let them hold a feast to Me in the wilderness."

**2** And Pharaoh said, "Who is the LORD, that I should listen to His voice and send Israel away? I do not know the LORD, and I will not send Israel away either."

**3** And they said, "The God of the Hebrews has met with us. Let us go, please, a three days' journey into the wilderness and sacrifice to the LORD our God, or He may strike us with plague or with the sword."

**4** And the king of Egypt said to them, "Moses and Aaron, why are you pulling the people away from their work? Get back to your burdens!"

**5** And Pharaoh said, "Look — the people of the land are many now, and you would have them rest from their burdens."

## Make the Work Heavier

**6** And that same day Pharaoh commanded the slave drivers of the people and their foremen:

**7** "You shall not go on giving the people straw to make bricks as before. Let them go and gather straw for themselves.

**8** And the quota of bricks they were making before, you shall lay on them. You shall not reduce it, because they are slack. That is why they are crying out, saying, 'Let us go and sacrifice to our God.'

**9** Let the work be heavy on the men, and let them do it, and let them not seek out lies."

**10** And the slave drivers of the people and their foremen went out and said to the people, "This is what Pharaoh says: I am not giving you straw.

**11** You go and get straw for yourselves wherever you can find it — because nothing is being reduced from your work."

**12** And the people scattered through all the land of Egypt to gather stubble for straw.

**13** And the slave drivers pressed them, saying, "Finish your work — a day's portion in its day, just as when there was straw."

**14** And the foremen of the sons of Israel, whom Pharaoh's slave drivers had set over them, were beaten, and they were asked, "Why have you not finished your fixed amount of brickmaking, yesterday and today, as you did before?"

## Look on Us and Judge

**15** And the foremen of the sons of Israel came and cried out to Pharaoh, "Why do you do this to your servants?

**16** No straw is given to your servants, and they say to us, 'Make bricks!' And here — your servants are being beaten, and it is your own people who are at fault."

**17** And he said, "Slack — you are slack! That is why you say, 'Let us go and sacrifice to the LORD.'

**18** And now go, work! And straw will not be given to you, and you will hand over the full count of bricks."

**19** And the foremen of the sons of Israel saw that they were in trouble when they were told, "You shall not reduce anything from your bricks — a day's portion in its day."

**20** And they met Moses and Aaron, who were standing there to meet them as they came out from Pharaoh.

**21** And they said to them, "May the LORD look on you and judge, because you have made us stink in the eyes of Pharaoh and in the eyes of his servants — putting a sword in their hand to kill us."

**22** And Moses went back to the LORD and said, "My Lord, why have You done harm to this people? Why did You ever send me?

**23** From the moment I came to Pharaoh to speak in Your name, he has done harm to this people — and rescue, You have not rescued Your people."

## Notes

- **v2 "I do not know the LORD"** — *lo yadati*. This book keeps turning on that verb. A king arose who did **not know** Joseph (1:8). God **knew** (2:25). Moses' killing became **known** (2:14). Now Pharaoh says the one thing that makes him the enemy: he does not know this name. Everything from here to chapter 14 is God answering it — the phrase *that you may know that I am the LORD* runs through the plagues like a refrain.
- **v3 "The God of the Hebrews has met with us"** — the words God gave Moses at the bush (3:18), repeated verbatim. But look at what the brothers add on their own: *or He may strike us with plague or with the sword*. God did not say that. They are arguing that letting them go protects Pharaoh's workforce, and they have invented the threat to do it.
- **v4 "pulling the people away"** — *tafri'u*, from *para*, to let loose or let go slack. It is the verb for undoing restraint. Pharaoh is accusing them of loosening something he has tied down.
- **v4 "your burdens"** — *sivlotekhem*, the word from 1:11 and 2:11, where Moses first went out and saw his brothers' **burdens**. Pharaoh throws the same noun back at him.
- **v7 "straw to make bricks"** — *teven* and *levenim*. In Hebrew the two words sound almost the same, and the chapter hammers them together nine times. What is being described is a work order, and it clicks like one.
- **v12 "to gather stubble for straw"** — *le-qoshesh qash la-teven*. They are not given straw, so they gather *qash*, stubble — the leftovers. The Hebrew even makes the verb and the noun the same sound. They are scavenging chaff to meet a brick quota.
- **v9 "Let the work be heavy"** — *tikhbad*, from *kaved*, heavy. It is the root already used for Moses' **heavy** mouth (4:10) and, in the chapters ahead, for Pharaoh's **heavy** heart. The same weight sits on the slave, the prophet, and the king.
- **v13 "a day's portion in its day"** — *devar yom be-yomo*. The phrase for a fixed daily allotment. Israel will hear it again in the wilderness at 16:4, where the thing measured out daily is not bricks but bread.
- **v16 "it is your own people who are at fault"** — the Hebrew of this clause is compressed and has been read two ways: that Pharaoh's own people are the ones in the wrong, or that the fault is being laid on the foremen and so on Pharaoh's people by extension. *Chatat* is the word for sin or fault, and the sentence does not say clearly whose. The foremen are accusing the crown to its face, and the grammar is as careful as they must have been.
- **v17 "Slack — you are slack!"** — *nirpim attem nirpim*, the word doubled. Pharaoh answers a complaint about beatings by repeating an insult from his own memo (v8). He is not reasoning; he is quoting himself.
- **v21 "you have made us stink"** — *hiv'ashtem et rechenu*, literally you have **made our smell stink**. It is a physical verb: *ba'ash* is what the Nile does when the fish die in it (7:18, 7:21) and what the hoarded manna does overnight (16:20). The foremen reach for the ugliest word available.
- **v23 "and rescue, You have not rescued"** — *ve-hatsel lo hitsalta*, the doubled construction Hebrew uses to press a verb down hard. And the verb is *natsal* — the one God used at the bush: *I have come down **to rescue** them* (3:8). Moses ends the chapter by taking God's own word and handing it back empty. The book's first prayer is an accusation.
