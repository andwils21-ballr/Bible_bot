# Exodus 7

**1** The LORD said to Moses, "See, I have made you God to Pharaoh, and Aaron your brother will be your prophet.

**2** You shall speak everything I command you, and Aaron your brother shall speak to Pharaoh, so that he sends the sons of Israel out of his land.

**3** I Myself will make Pharaoh's heart hard, and I will multiply My signs and My wonders in the land of Egypt.

**4** Pharaoh will not listen to you, and I will set My hand against Egypt, and I will bring out My divisions, My people the sons of Israel, from the land of Egypt with great acts of judgment.

**5** Egypt will know that I am the LORD, when I stretch out My hand against Egypt and bring the sons of Israel out from among them."

**6** Moses and Aaron did as the LORD commanded them — so they did.

**7** Moses was eighty years old and Aaron eighty-three years old when they spoke to Pharaoh.

## The Staff That Swallowed the Staffs

**8** The LORD said to Moses and to Aaron:

**9** "When Pharaoh says to you, 'Produce a wonder for yourselves,' then say to Aaron, 'Take your staff and throw it down before Pharaoh' — and it will become a serpent."

**10** Moses and Aaron came to Pharaoh and did so, as the LORD had commanded. Aaron threw down his staff before Pharaoh and before his servants, and it became a serpent.

**11** Pharaoh also called for the wise men and the sorcerers, and they too — the magicians of Egypt — did the same with their secret arts.

**12** Each one threw down his staff, and they became serpents. Aaron's staff swallowed their staffs.

**13** Pharaoh's heart was strong, and he did not listen to them, just as the LORD had said.

## The First Blow

**14** The LORD said to Moses, "Pharaoh's heart is heavy. He refuses to send the people away.

**15** Go to Pharaoh in the morning — look, he is going out to the water — and stand to meet him on the bank of the Nile. Take in your hand the staff that turned into a snake.

**16** Say to him, 'The LORD, the God of the Hebrews, sent me to you, saying: Send My people away, so they may serve Me in the wilderness. And here — you have not listened, until now.

**17** This is what the LORD says: By this you will know that I am the LORD. Here — I am striking the water that is in the Nile with the staff that is in my hand, and it will turn to blood.

**18** The fish that are in the Nile will die, and the Nile will stink, and Egypt will be unable to drink water from the Nile.'"

**19** The LORD said to Moses, "Say to Aaron: Take your staff and stretch out your hand over the waters of Egypt — over their rivers, over their canals, over their pools, and over every gathering of their water — and they will become blood. There will be blood in all the land of Egypt, even in the wooden and the stone vessels."

**20** Moses and Aaron did so, as the LORD commanded. He raised the staff and struck the water that was in the Nile, before the eyes of Pharaoh and before the eyes of his servants, and all the water in the Nile turned to blood.

**21** The fish that were in the Nile died, and the Nile stank, and Egypt could not drink water from the Nile. The blood was in all the land of Egypt.

**22** The magicians of Egypt did the same with their secret arts, and Pharaoh's heart was strong, and he did not listen to them, just as the LORD had said.

**23** Pharaoh turned and went into his house, and he did not set his heart to this either.

**24** All Egypt dug around the Nile for water to drink, because they could not drink from the water of the Nile.

**25** Seven days were filled after the LORD struck the Nile.

## Notes

- **v1 "I have made you God to Pharaoh, and Aaron your brother will be your prophet"** — the plainest definition of a prophet the Bible gives, and it is given as a job description rather than a doctrine. A prophet is not the source of the message. He is the mouth for it. The arrangement was set up in 4:16, where Aaron is *a mouth for you* and Moses is *God for him*; here the second word, *navi*, **prophet**, is supplied.
- **v3 "I will make Pharaoh's heart hard"** — *aqsheh*, from *qashah*, to be hard or stiff. This is the **third** Hebrew verb for what happens to Pharaoh's heart, and all three appear within eleven verses of each other: *qashah* here, **hard**; *chazaq* in v13, **strong**; *kaved* in v14, **heavy**. English flattens all three into *hardened*. The Hebrew keeps them apart, and it also keeps track of who does it — God hardens in v3, and in v13 and v22 the heart simply *was strong*, with no one named.
- **v9 "a serpent"** — *tannin*, and this is not the word from chapter 4. There the staff became a *nachash*, an ordinary snake. Here it becomes a *tannin* — the word for a sea-monster, a great reptile, the creature God makes on the fifth day of creation in Genesis 1:21. The sign has been upgraded for the throne room. Then in v15 the narrator refers back to it as *the staff that turned into a **nachash***, using the smaller word again. The text does not harmonize itself.
- **v11 "with their secret arts"** — the Hebrew word here is *belahatehem*, built on *lahat*, flame or blade. Eleven verses later, at v22, the same phrase is spelled *belatehem*, built on *lat*, secrecy or concealment. One letter apart, two different pictures — flashing and hiding — and the chapter uses both.
- **v11 "the magicians of Egypt"** — *chartummim*, a loanword from Egyptian rather than a Hebrew coinage. The narrator uses the Egyptians' own professional title. They are not frauds in this account: they do what Aaron does.
- **v12 "Aaron's staff swallowed their staffs"** — *va-yivla*, from *bala*, to swallow. Egypt's magic is not shown to be false; it is shown to be eaten. The same verb returns in the song after the sea: *You stretched out Your right hand — **the earth swallowed them*** (15:12).
- **v17 "By this you will know that I am the LORD"** — the answer to Pharaoh's question in 5:2, *Who is the LORD? I do not know the LORD*. He asked to be introduced. The plagues are the introduction, and this sentence is its formula — it recurs through the whole sequence.
- **v19 "even in the wooden and the stone vessels"** — the blow does not stop at the river. It reaches the water already drawn and standing in the house. Egypt's life is organised around the Nile, and the first plague follows that organisation indoors.
- **v18 "the Nile will stink"** — *u-va'ash*, the same verb the Israelite foremen used against Moses two chapters earlier: *you have made us **stink** in the eyes of Pharaoh* (5:21). What they accused him of doing to them, the Nile now does in fact.
- **v23 "he did not set his heart to this either"** — *lo shat libbo gam la-zot*. Not defiance; indifference. He went home. The word *either* is doing real work: the text is counting, and it expects the reader to notice that this has now happened more than once.
- **v25 "seven days were filled"** — the only interval given in the plague sequence. The narrative almost never tells you how long anything lasts, and here it stops to say a week passed with the river undrinkable before anything else happened.
