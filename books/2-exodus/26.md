# Exodus 26

## The Ten Curtains

**1** "The dwelling you shall make of ten curtains of fine twisted linen, and blue and purple and crimson. With cherubim, the work of a designer, you shall make them.

**2** The length of one curtain, twenty-eight cubits, and the width of one curtain, four cubits. One measure for all the curtains.

**3** Five of the curtains shall be joined to one another, and five curtains joined to one another.

**4** You shall make loops of blue on the edge of the outermost curtain of the first set, and do the same on the edge of the outermost curtain of the second set.

**5** Fifty loops you shall make on the one curtain, and fifty loops you shall make on the edge of the curtain that is in the second set, the loops facing one another.

**6** You shall make fifty clasps of gold, and join the curtains to one another with the clasps, and the dwelling shall be one.

## The Tent Over It

**7** You shall make curtains of goats' hair for a tent over the dwelling. Eleven curtains you shall make of them.

**8** The length of one curtain, thirty cubits, and the width of one curtain, four cubits. One measure for the eleven curtains.

**9** You shall join five of the curtains by themselves and six of the curtains by themselves, and you shall fold the sixth curtain double at the front of the tent.

**10** You shall make fifty loops on the edge of the outermost curtain of the first set, and fifty loops on the edge of the curtain of the second set.

**11** You shall make fifty clasps of bronze, and put the clasps into the loops and join the tent, and it shall be one.

**12** The overhang left over from the curtains of the tent — the half curtain that is left over — shall hang over the back of the dwelling.

**13** The cubit on this side and the cubit on that side, of what is left over in the length of the curtains of the tent, shall hang over the sides of the dwelling, on this side and on that, to cover it.

**14** You shall make a covering for the tent of rams' skins dyed red, and a covering of fine leather above that.

## The Frames

**15** You shall make the frames for the dwelling of acacia wood, standing upright.

**16** Ten cubits the length of a frame, and a cubit and a half the width of each frame.

**17** Two tenons for each frame, fitted to one another. So you shall do for all the frames of the dwelling.

**18** You shall make the frames for the dwelling: twenty frames for the south side, facing south.

**19** Forty sockets of silver you shall make under the twenty frames — two sockets under one frame for its two tenons, and two sockets under the next frame for its two tenons.

**20** For the second side of the dwelling, the north side, twenty frames,

**21** and their forty sockets of silver — two sockets under one frame and two sockets under the next frame.

**22** For the rear of the dwelling, toward the west, you shall make six frames.

**23** Two frames you shall make for the corners of the dwelling at the rear.

**24** They shall be paired below, and together they shall be flush at the top, at the first ring. So it shall be for both of them; they shall be for the two corners.

**25** There shall be eight frames, and their sockets of silver, sixteen sockets — two sockets under one frame and two sockets under the next frame.

**26** You shall make bars of acacia wood: five for the frames of the one side of the dwelling,

**27** and five bars for the frames of the second side of the dwelling, and five bars for the frames of the side of the dwelling at the rear, toward the west.

**28** The middle bar, in the middle of the frames, shall run through from end to end.

**29** The frames you shall overlay with gold, and their rings you shall make of gold, as holders for the bars. You shall overlay the bars with gold.

**30** You shall raise up the dwelling according to its ruling, which you were shown on the mountain.

## The Veil and the Screen

**31** You shall make a veil of blue and purple and crimson and fine twisted linen. The work of a designer shall make it, with cherubim.

**32** You shall hang it on four pillars of acacia overlaid with gold, their hooks of gold, on four sockets of silver.

**33** You shall hang the veil under the clasps, and bring in there, inside the veil, the ark of the Testimony. The veil shall divide for you between the Holy Place and the Holy of Holies.

**34** You shall put the cover on the ark of the Testimony in the Holy of Holies.

**35** You shall set the table outside the veil, and the lampstand opposite the table, on the south side of the dwelling; and the table you shall put on the north side.

**36** You shall make a screen for the entrance of the tent, of blue and purple and crimson and fine twisted linen, the work of an embroiderer.

**37** You shall make for the screen five pillars of acacia and overlay them with gold, their hooks of gold, and cast for them five sockets of bronze."

## Notes

- **vv1, 31, 36 two grades of craft** — the Hebrew names the workmanship each time and does not use the same word twice by accident. The inner curtains and the veil are *ma'aseh choshev*, the work of a **designer** — from *chashav*, to think, calculate, devise — and both carry cherubim. The screen at the outer door is *ma'aseh roqem*, the work of an **embroiderer**, and has no figures on it. The further in, the more the fabric is credited to thinking rather than needlework.
- **vv1, 7 two structures, two names** — *mishkan*, the **dwelling**, is the inner layer of ten linen curtains; *ohel*, the **tent**, is the eleven goats'-hair curtains thrown over it. English usually calls the whole thing the tabernacle and loses the distinction, but the chapter keeps them apart line by line: gold clasps for the dwelling, bronze clasps for the tent.
- **v3 "joined to one another"** — the Hebrew is *ishah el achotah*, **a woman to her sister**, and it says it four times in this chapter about curtains, loops and tenons. It is simply how Hebrew says *each to the next* when the noun is grammatically feminine; the matching masculine idiom is *ish el achiv*, a man to his brother. No woman and no sister is meant, which is exactly why a literal English rendering would say something the Hebrew does not.
- **vv5, 24 "facing one another… paired below"** — the whole structure is described by how its parts meet: loops opposite loops, tenons fitted, frames doubled at the corner, one bar running the full length. The sanctuary is specified almost entirely as a set of joints.
- **v14 "fine leather"** — *techashim*, and nobody knows what animal this is. Dolphin, dugong, badger, antelope and a word for a color or a tanning process have all been proposed, from the Greek onward. It is the outermost layer, the one exposed to weather and the only part of the structure anyone outside would see, and the word for it cannot be recovered.
- **v15 "standing upright"** — *omdim*, a participle hung on the end of a sentence about timber. Acacia is a desert tree, scrubby and thorny; the wood the whole frame is cut from is the toughest thing that grows where these people are. The verse says the boards **stand**, and the same root is what a person does before God.
- **v24 "flush at the top"** — *tammim*, and it is the word the book uses for a person who is whole: Noah was *tamim* in his generations (Genesis 6:9), Abraham was told *be tamim* (17:1), Jacob was an *ish tam* (25:27). Here it is a carpentry term for two boards meeting cleanly with no gap. The moral word and the joinery word are the same word, and the joinery is the plain sense.
- **v30 "according to its ruling"** — *ke-mishpato*, the same noun that opened the law code five chapters back: *these are the rulings you shall set before them* (21:1). A judgment and a set of assembly instructions are the same kind of thing in this language — the settled, correct way a matter goes.
- **v31 "a veil… with cherubim"** — *parokhet*, which the Greek renders *katapetasma*. That is the word in Matthew 27:51: *and here — the veil of the temple was torn in two, from top to bottom.* And the figures woven into it are the third appearance of cherubim in three chapters of this canon: posted at the garden gate in Genesis 3:24, beaten into the lid of the ark in 25:18, and now embroidered across the one door nobody may pass. Wherever a boundary is absolute, this book puts cherubim on it.
- **v33 "the Holy of Holies"** — *qodesh ha-qodashim*, and Hebrew has no word for *most*. To make a superlative it repeats the noun in the plural and hangs it off itself: holy of the holies, the same construction as king of kings and song of songs. The innermost room is named by a grammatical trick for saying *the most*.
