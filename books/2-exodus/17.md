# Exodus 17

## Massah and Meribah

**1** And the whole assembly of the sons of Israel set out from the wilderness of Sin by their stages, at the LORD's command, and camped at Rephidim. And there was no water for the people to drink.

**2** And the people quarreled with Moses and said, "Give us water, and let us drink." And Moses said to them, "Why do you quarrel with me? Why do you test the LORD?"

**3** And the people thirsted there for water, and the people grumbled against Moses and said, "Why is it that you brought us up from Egypt — to kill me and my children and my livestock with thirst?"

**4** And Moses cried out to the LORD, saying, "What shall I do with this people? A little more and they will stone me."

**5** And the LORD said to Moses, "Cross over in front of the people, and take with you some of the elders of Israel. And your staff, with which you struck the Nile — take it in your hand, and go.

**6** Here — I will be standing before you there on the rock at Horeb. And you shall strike the rock, and water will come out of it, and the people will drink." And Moses did so before the eyes of the elders of Israel.

**7** And he called the name of the place Massah and Meribah, because of the quarreling of the sons of Israel, and because they tested the LORD, saying, "Is the LORD among us or not?"

## Amalek at Rephidim

**8** And Amalek came and fought with Israel at Rephidim.

**9** And Moses said to Joshua, "Choose men for us, and go out, fight with Amalek. Tomorrow I will be standing on the top of the hill with the staff of God in my hand."

**10** And Joshua did as Moses had said to him, to fight with Amalek. And Moses, Aaron, and Hur went up to the top of the hill.

**11** And it was that when Moses raised his hand, Israel prevailed; and when he let his hand rest, Amalek prevailed.

**12** And Moses' hands were heavy. And they took a stone and put it under him, and he sat on it. And Aaron and Hur held up his hands, one on this side and one on that side. And his hands were steady until the sun went down.

**13** And Joshua weakened Amalek and his people by the mouth of the sword.

**14** And the LORD said to Moses, "Write this as a memorial in the book, and set it in the ears of Joshua: I will utterly blot out the memory of Amalek from under the heavens."

**15** And Moses built an altar and called its name The LORD Is My Banner.

**16** And he said, "For a hand upon the throne of Yah: the LORD is at war with Amalek from generation to generation."

## Notes

- **v2, v7 "quarreled… tested"** — the whole first half of the chapter is built out of two verbs, and the place gets named for both. *Riv* is not bickering; it is the word for bringing a legal case, the verb behind every courtroom scene in the prophets. *Nasah* is to put something to the proof. The place becomes **Massah** (Testing) and **Meribah** (Quarrel) — the names are the verbs, frozen. The Greek translators did not transliterate them the way English does. They wrote Πειρασμός and Λοιδόρησις, *Testing* and *Reviling*, so a Greek reader gets the meaning and loses the place.
- **v3 "to kill me and my children and my livestock"** — the Hebrew slips into the singular mid-sentence. The people speak as a crowd in the first half of the verse and as one man in the second: *my* children, *my* cattle, *my* thirst. English versions usually level it to *us*. The text is letting a nation talk like a single frightened household.
- **v5 "your staff, with which you struck the Nile"** — the verse names the staff by what it did last. That blow turned a river into blood and made water undrinkable (7:20). The same wood is now raised against a rock to make water come out. Nothing in the verse comments on this; it just makes sure the reader knows which staff.
- **v6 "I will be standing before you there on the rock"** — God takes up a position on the thing that is about to be hit. The verse gives no explanation, and no later verse in Exodus returns to it.
- **v6 "before the eyes of the elders"** — the Hebrew has the elders as witnesses, matching v5 where Moses is told to bring them. The Greek says he did it *before the sons of Israel* — the whole people watching instead of a chosen few. One of the two traditions has widened the audience.
- **v7 "Is the LORD among us or not?"** — six words in Hebrew, and they are the reason the place has a name. Not *did God bring us out* — that is settled — but whether He is **here**, in the middle of the camp, today. This is the question the tabernacle occupies the last third of the book answering.
- **v9 Joshua** — his first appearance, introduced without a word of explanation, as though the reader already knows him. His name is *Yehoshua*, **the LORD saves**. The Greek Old Testament spells it **Ἰησοῦς** — which is, letter for letter, the way the New Testament spells Jesus. They are the same name. A Greek reader met the general at Rephidim under the spelling he would later meet in the Gospels, and English gives them two different names.
- **v12 "his hands were steady"** — *emunah*, and this is the word the rest of the Bible uses for **faithfulness**, the noun behind *amen*. Its plain sense is sitting in the open here: something that holds its position and does not give way. Faith, in the language, starts as a property of a held-up arm.
- **v14 "a memorial… the memory of Amalek"** — *zikkaron* and *zekher*, the same root twice in one verse. Moses is ordered to write a permanent record of the people whose record is to be erased. The erasing is done by writing it down.
- **vv15–16 "My Banner… the throne of Yah"** — *nes* is a pole raised where everyone can see it, a rallying signal, not a flag in the modern sense. Then v16 opens with *kes*, which is not the ordinary Hebrew word for throne (*kisse*) and occurs **nowhere else in the Bible**. The sentence is broken enough that the ancient translators did not read it as a throne at all: the Greek has *the Lord makes war on Amalek with a hidden hand*, which takes the same consonants from *kasah*, to cover. The Greek also renders the altar's name *the Lord my refuge* rather than *my banner* — the consonants of *nes*, banner, and *nus*, to flee for safety, are the same two letters. Two verses, and the oldest translation we have disagrees with the Hebrew on both.
