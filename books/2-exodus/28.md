# Exodus 28

## The Ephod

**1** "You — bring near to yourself Aaron your brother, and his sons with him, from among the sons of Israel, to serve Me as priests: Aaron, Nadab and Abihu, Eleazar and Ithamar, Aaron's sons.

**2** You shall make holy garments for Aaron your brother, for glory and for beauty.

**3** You shall speak to all the wise of heart, whom I have filled with a spirit of wisdom, and they shall make Aaron's garments to set him apart, to serve Me as priest.

**4** These are the garments they shall make: a breastpiece and an ephod and a robe, a checkered tunic, a turban and a sash. They shall make holy garments for Aaron your brother and for his sons, to serve Me as priests.

**5** They shall take the gold and the blue and the purple and the crimson and the fine linen.

**6** They shall make the ephod of gold, blue, purple, crimson and fine twisted linen, the work of a designer.

**7** It shall have two shoulder pieces joined at its two ends, and so it shall be joined together.

**8** The woven band of its ephod, which is on it, shall be of the same work and of one piece with it: gold, blue, purple, crimson and fine twisted linen.

**9** You shall take two onyx stones and engrave on them the names of the sons of Israel —

**10** six of their names on the one stone, and the names of the remaining six on the second stone, in the order of their birth.

**11** The work of a stone-cutter, engravings of a seal, you shall engrave the two stones with the names of the sons of Israel. Set in gold settings you shall make them.

**12** You shall put the two stones on the shoulder pieces of the ephod, stones of remembrance for the sons of Israel. Aaron shall carry their names before the LORD on his two shoulders, as a remembrance.

**13** You shall make settings of gold,

**14** and two chains of pure gold — twisted like cords you shall make them — and you shall fasten the corded chains to the settings.

## The Breastpiece

**15** You shall make a breastpiece of judgment, the work of a designer. Like the work of the ephod you shall make it: of gold, blue, purple, crimson and fine twisted linen you shall make it.

**16** Square it shall be, folded double, a span its length and a span its width.

**17** You shall set in it a setting of stones, four rows of stones. A row of carnelian, topaz and emerald — the first row.

**18** The second row: turquoise, sapphire and diamond.

**19** The third row: jacinth, agate and amethyst.

**20** The fourth row: beryl, onyx and jasper. They shall be set in gold in their settings.

**21** The stones shall be according to the names of the sons of Israel, twelve according to their names — engravings of a seal, each with its name, for the twelve tribes.

**22** You shall make on the breastpiece chains of pure gold, twisted like cords.

**23** You shall make on the breastpiece two rings of gold, and put the two rings on the two ends of the breastpiece.

**24** You shall put the two gold cords on the two rings, at the ends of the breastpiece.

**25** The two ends of the two cords you shall put on the two settings, and put them on the shoulder pieces of the ephod, toward its front.

**26** You shall make two rings of gold and set them on the two ends of the breastpiece, on its edge that faces the ephod, inward.

**27** You shall make two rings of gold and put them on the two shoulder pieces of the ephod, low down, at the front, close to its seam, above the woven band of the ephod.

**28** They shall bind the breastpiece by its rings to the rings of the ephod with a cord of blue, so that it rests on the woven band of the ephod, and the breastpiece will not come loose from the ephod.

**29** Aaron shall carry the names of the sons of Israel on the breastpiece of judgment, on his heart, when he goes into the holy place, as a remembrance before the LORD always.

**30** You shall put into the breastpiece of judgment the Urim and the Thummim, and they shall be on Aaron's heart when he goes in before the LORD. Aaron shall carry the judgment of the sons of Israel on his heart before the LORD always.

## The Robe

**31** You shall make the robe of the ephod entirely of blue.

**32** The opening for his head shall be in the middle of it. Its opening shall have a binding of woven work all around — like the opening of a coat of mail it shall be — so that it is not torn.

**33** On its hem you shall make pomegranates of blue and purple and crimson, all around its hem, and bells of gold between them all around:

**34** a gold bell and a pomegranate, a gold bell and a pomegranate, on the hem of the robe all around.

**35** It shall be on Aaron when he serves, and its sound shall be heard when he goes into the holy place before the LORD and when he comes out — and he shall not die.

## The Plate, and the Rest

**36** You shall make a plate of pure gold, and engrave on it, with the engravings of a seal: **Holy to the LORD**.

**37** You shall fasten it with a cord of blue, and it shall be on the turban — on the front of the turban it shall be.

**38** It shall be on Aaron's forehead, and Aaron shall carry the guilt of the holy things that the sons of Israel set apart, in all their holy gifts. It shall be on his forehead always, for their acceptance before the LORD.

**39** You shall weave the tunic in checker work of fine linen, and make a turban of fine linen, and a sash you shall make, the work of an embroiderer.

**40** For Aaron's sons you shall make tunics, and make them sashes, and make them caps, for glory and for beauty.

**41** You shall clothe Aaron your brother with them, and his sons with him, and anoint them and ordain them and set them apart, and they shall serve Me as priests.

**42** Make them linen undergarments to cover their naked flesh, reaching from the waist to the thighs.

**43** They shall be on Aaron and on his sons when they come into the tent of meeting, or when they come near the altar to serve in the holy place, so that they do not bear guilt and die. A lasting statute for him and for his seed after him."

## Notes

- **vv2, 40 "for glory and for beauty"** — *le-khavod u-le-tif'aret*, and it is said twice: once of Aaron's garments and once of his sons'. *Kavod* is the heavy word — weight, wealth, honor, the glory that settles on Sinai in 24:16. The clothes are not described as functional or as protective. Their stated purpose is that the man wearing them should be **weighty and beautiful to look at**.
- **v3 "the wise of heart… a spirit of wisdom"** — *chakhmei lev*, filled with *ruach chokhmah*. Hebrew has no separate word for technical skill: the dyers, weavers and goldsmiths who will build this are called **wise**, and the thing filling them is the same spirit and the same wisdom the rest of the canon uses for understanding God. Craftsmanship and wisdom are one word here, and the book does not treat that as a metaphor.
- **vv12, 29, 38 Aaron carries three things** — the same verb, *nasa*, to lift and bear, three times in one chapter, and each time on a different part of his body. The **names** of the tribes go on his **shoulders** (v12). The **judgment** of the tribes goes on his **heart** (v30). The **guilt** of their holy things goes on his **forehead** (v38). Nothing in the chapter says this is a pattern. The verb says it.
- **v15 "a breastpiece of judgment"** — *choshen mishpat*, the same *mishpat* as the rulings of chapter 21 and the specification of the tabernacle in 26:30. What hangs on the high priest's chest is named for a legal decision, and v30 makes that explicit: he carries *the judgment of the sons of Israel* on his heart.
- **vv17–20 twelve stones, and most of the names are unrecoverable** — the Hebrew lists *odem, pitdah, bareqet, nofekh, sappir, yahalom, leshem, shevo, achlamah, tarshish, shoham, yashfeh*. A few are reasonably secure; several are pure guesswork, and English Bibles disagree with each other row by row. *Sappir* is most likely lapis lazuli rather than the modern sapphire. What is certain is the arrangement: four rows of three, one per tribe, each cut with a name.
- **v30 "the Urim and the Thummim"** — left untranslated, because nobody knows what they were. The words look like plurals of *ur*, light, and *tam*, whole or complete, so *lights and perfections* is a fair guess at the letters and no help at all with the objects. They are introduced here with no description, no instruction for making them, and no explanation — the one item in the entire sanctuary the text does not tell Moses how to build.
- **vv33–35 bells on the hem** — gold bells alternating with woven pomegranates, and the reason given is not decoration: *its sound shall be heard when he goes in… and he shall not die*. The holiest man in Israel is fitted with a noise-maker so that his entering can be heard, and the alternative named is his death. Nothing says who is listening.
- **v32 "like the opening of a coat of mail"** — *ke-fi tachra*, and *tachra* occurs in the Bible only here and in the matching verse at 39:23. A garment of some kind, reinforced at the neck; armor is the traditional guess from the context and cannot be confirmed. The instruction attached to it is clear enough: the collar is to be bound so that it **cannot tear**.
- **v36 "a plate of pure gold… Holy to the LORD"** — *tzitz*, a word that elsewhere means a **blossom** or a shining thing. Two words are cut into it, *qodesh la-YHWH*, and they sit on the forehead of a man who is at that moment carrying the guilt of everything the nation has dedicated. The label and the load are on the same square inch.
- **v41 "and ordain them"** — the Hebrew is *u-milleta et yadam*, **and you shall fill their hand**. It is the fixed idiom for installing a priest, used through the Torah, and what the hand is filled with is never stated in the phrase itself — chapter 29 will put portions of the ordination ram into their palms. English "ordain" comes by way of the Latin; the Hebrew describes a gesture.
- **v42 "linen undergarments to cover their naked flesh"** — and this is the answer to 20:26, eight chapters earlier: *you shall not go up by steps onto My altar, so that your nakedness is not uncovered on it*. The problem raised at the very first altar law is solved here by tailoring. It is the last item in a chapter about glory and beauty, and it is underwear.
