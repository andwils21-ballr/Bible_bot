# Exodus 29

## Washing and Clothing

**1** "This is what you shall do for them, to set them apart to serve Me as priests. Take one bull of the herd and two rams, unblemished,

**2** and unleavened bread, and unleavened cakes mixed with oil, and unleavened wafers spread with oil. Of fine wheat flour you shall make them.

**3** You shall put them in one basket and bring them near in the basket — along with the bull and the two rams.

**4** Aaron and his sons you shall bring near to the entrance of the tent of meeting, and wash them with water.

**5** You shall take the garments and clothe Aaron with the tunic and the robe of the ephod and the ephod and the breastpiece, and fasten the ephod on him with its woven band.

**6** You shall set the turban on his head, and put the holy crown on the turban.

**7** You shall take the anointing oil and pour it on his head, and anoint him.

**8** His sons you shall bring near, and clothe them with tunics.

**9** You shall gird them with sashes — Aaron and his sons — and bind caps on them. The priesthood shall be theirs by a lasting statute. You shall fill the hand of Aaron and the hand of his sons.

## The Bull and the Two Rams

**10** You shall bring the bull near before the tent of meeting, and Aaron and his sons shall lean their hands on the bull's head.

**11** You shall slaughter the bull before the LORD, at the entrance of the tent of meeting.

**12** You shall take some of the bull's blood and put it on the horns of the altar with your finger, and all the rest of the blood you shall pour out at the base of the altar.

**13** You shall take all the fat that covers the innards, and the lobe of the liver, and the two kidneys and the fat that is on them, and turn them to smoke on the altar.

**14** The flesh of the bull and its hide and its dung you shall burn in fire outside the camp. It is a sin offering.

**15** You shall take the one ram, and Aaron and his sons shall lean their hands on the ram's head.

**16** You shall slaughter the ram, and take its blood and throw it against the altar all around.

**17** The ram you shall cut into its pieces, and wash its innards and its legs, and put them with its pieces and with its head.

**18** You shall turn the whole ram to smoke on the altar. It is a burnt offering to the LORD, a soothing aroma, an offering by fire to the LORD.

**19** You shall take the second ram, and Aaron and his sons shall lean their hands on the ram's head.

**20** You shall slaughter the ram, and take some of its blood and put it on the lobe of Aaron's right ear and on the lobe of the right ear of his sons, and on the thumb of their right hand and on the big toe of their right foot. You shall throw the blood against the altar all around.

**21** You shall take some of the blood that is on the altar, and some of the anointing oil, and sprinkle it on Aaron and on his garments, and on his sons and on his sons' garments with him. He shall be holy, and his garments, and his sons and his sons' garments with him.

## What Fills the Hand

**22** You shall take from the ram the fat, and the fat tail, and the fat that covers the innards, and the lobe of the liver, and the two kidneys and the fat that is on them, and the right thigh — for it is a ram of ordination —

**23** and one round loaf of bread, and one cake of oiled bread, and one wafer, from the basket of unleavened bread that is before the LORD.

**24** You shall put all of it on Aaron's palms and on the palms of his sons, and wave them as a wave offering before the LORD.

**25** You shall take them from their hands and turn them to smoke on the altar, on top of the burnt offering, as a soothing aroma before the LORD. It is an offering by fire to the LORD.

**26** You shall take the breast of the ram of Aaron's ordination, and wave it as a wave offering before the LORD. It shall be your portion.

**27** You shall make holy the breast of the wave offering and the thigh of the contribution — what was waved and what was lifted — from the ram of ordination, from what is Aaron's and from what is his sons'.

**28** It shall belong to Aaron and to his sons as a lasting due from the sons of Israel, because it is a contribution. It shall be a contribution from the sons of Israel, out of their peace offerings — their contribution to the LORD.

**29** The holy garments of Aaron shall belong to his sons after him, to be anointed in them and to have their hands filled in them.

**30** Seven days shall the priest who succeeds him from among his sons wear them, the one who comes into the tent of meeting to serve in the holy place.

**31** The ram of ordination you shall take, and boil its flesh in a holy place.

**32** Aaron and his sons shall eat the flesh of the ram, and the bread that is in the basket, at the entrance of the tent of meeting.

**33** They shall eat the things by which atonement was made, to fill their hands, to set them apart. An outsider shall not eat, because they are holy.

**34** If any of the flesh of the ordination, or of the bread, is left until morning, then you shall burn what is left in fire. It shall not be eaten, because it is holy.

**35** You shall do for Aaron and for his sons in this way, according to all that I have commanded you. Seven days you shall fill their hands.

**36** A bull as a sin offering you shall make each day for atonement, and you shall purge the altar by making atonement on it, and anoint it to set it apart.

**37** Seven days you shall make atonement on the altar and set it apart, and the altar shall be most holy. Whatever touches the altar shall become holy.

## The Continual Offering

**38** This is what you shall offer on the altar: two lambs a year old, day by day, continually.

**39** The one lamb you shall offer in the morning, and the second lamb you shall offer between the evenings.

**40** A tenth of fine flour mixed with a quarter of a hin of beaten oil, and a drink offering of a quarter of a hin of wine, for the one lamb.

**41** The second lamb you shall offer between the evenings. Like the grain offering of the morning and like its drink offering you shall do for it, as a soothing aroma, an offering by fire to the LORD.

**42** A continual burnt offering for your generations, at the entrance of the tent of meeting before the LORD — where I will meet with you, to speak to you there.

**43** I will meet there with the sons of Israel, and it shall be made holy by My glory.

**44** I will set apart the tent of meeting and the altar, and Aaron and his sons I will set apart, to serve Me as priests.

**45** I will dwell among the sons of Israel, and I will be their God.

**46** They shall know that I am the LORD their God, who brought them out of the land of Egypt to dwell among them. I am the LORD their God."

## Notes

- **vv9, 29, 33, 35 "fill the hand"** — *mille yad*, four times in this chapter, and 28:41 made it the word for ordaining a priest. Here the chapter finally says what the hand is filled with: in v24 the fat, the tail, the liver, the kidneys, the right thigh and three kinds of bread are all piled **onto Aaron's palms**, and he holds them out. The ram itself is *eil ha-millu'im*, the **ram of fillings**. English "ordain" is an abstraction; the Hebrew is a man standing with his arms full.
- **v1 "unblemished"** — *temimim*, the plural of *tam*. The word Genesis uses for Noah (6:9), commands to Abraham (17:1), and calls Jacob (25:27), and the word Exodus 26:24 uses for two boards meeting flush. A moral description, a carpentry term and a livestock grade are one word, and the animal sense is the concrete one the others are built on.
- **vv10, 15, 19 "lean their hands on the head"** — *samakh*, and it is not a light touch: the verb is used for leaning your weight on something, propping a thing up so it does not fall. Three times, before each killing, the men who are being ordained put their weight on the animal's skull. The text never says what the gesture means.
- **v20 "the lobe of the right ear… the thumb… the big toe"** — blood is put at three points, and they are the ends of the body: the part that hears, the part that works, the part that walks. Nothing in the chapter comments on the choice. The word for the ear lobe, *tenukh*, occurs in only two rites in the Torah: this ordination, carried out in Leviticus 8, and the cleansing of someone healed of a skin disease (Leviticus 14:14). Making a priest and readmitting an outcast are marked on the body the same way.
- **vv18, 25, 41 "a soothing aroma"** — *reach nichoach*, from *nuach*, **to rest** — the root behind Noah's name, the ark coming to rest, and the dove finding nowhere to set her foot. English often prints *a pleasing aroma*, but the word is built from rest: the smoke **soothes** something. Its first appearance in this canon is Genesis 8:21, where God smells it after the flood and promises never again.
- **vv24, 27 "waved… lifted"** — two motions with two nouns. *Tenufah* is from *nuf*, to swing or wave; *terumah* is from *rum*, to lift, the same word as the contribution that funded the sanctuary in 25:2. One portion is swung, one is raised, and the two together are what the priests get to keep. The gestures are named more precisely than the theology behind them.
- **v34 "burn what is left in fire"** — the leftovers of an ordination meal cannot be saved, and the reason given is that they are **holy**, not that they would spoil. Holiness in this system is a property that makes food dangerous rather than valuable, and the same rule governs the manna in chapter 16, which bred worms if kept overnight.
- **v37 "whatever touches the altar shall become holy"** — holiness here behaves like a charge that transfers on contact. Nothing says this is desirable. The chapter that ends by ordering underwear to protect priests from dying (28:42–43) treats the altar as something that makes whatever brushes it no longer ordinary.
- **vv38–42 "continually… between the evenings"** — two lambs a day forever, one at dawn and one *bein ha-arbayim*, **between the two evenings**, a phrase the Hebrew never defines and Jewish practice has argued over since. The sanctuary's basic rhythm is not festivals or pilgrimages: it is two animals a day, every day, marking the ends of the daylight.
- **vv45–46 "to dwell among them"** — the last two verses answer the whole question. *Shakhan*, the verb behind *mishkan*, twice: **I will dwell among the sons of Israel**, and then, as the stated reason for the exodus itself, *who brought them out of the land of Egypt* **to dwell among them**. Four chapters of joinery, cloth and blood, and the purpose given at the end is residence. The point of getting them out of Egypt was to move in.
