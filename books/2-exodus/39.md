# Exodus 39

## The Ephod

**1** And from the blue and the purple and the crimson they made the finely worked garments for serving in the holy place, and they made the holy garments that were for Aaron, as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**2** And he made the ephod of gold, blue and purple and crimson and fine twisted linen.

**3** And they hammered out sheets of gold and cut them into threads, to work into the blue and into the purple and into the crimson and into the fine linen — the work of a designer.

**4** Shoulder pieces they made for it, joined together; at its two ends it was joined.

**5** And the woven band of its ephod, which was on it, was of one piece with it and of the same work: gold, blue and purple and crimson and fine twisted linen, as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**6** And they made the onyx stones, set in gold settings, engraved with the engravings of a seal with the names of the sons of Israel.

**7** And he set them on the shoulder pieces of the ephod as stones of remembrance for the sons of Israel, as the LORD had commanded Moses.

## The Breastpiece

**8** And he made the breastpiece, the work of a designer, like the work of the ephod: gold, blue and purple and crimson and fine twisted linen.

**9** Square it was; they made the breastpiece folded double — a span its length and a span its width, folded double.

**10** And they filled it with four rows of stone. A row of carnelian, topaz and emerald — the first row.

**11** And the second row: turquoise, sapphire and diamond.

**12** And the third row: jacinth, agate and amethyst.

**13** And the fourth row: beryl, onyx and jasper — set in gold settings in their mountings.

**14** And the stones were according to the names of the sons of Israel, twelve according to their names, engravings of a seal, each with its own name, for the twelve tribes.

**15** And they made on the breastpiece chains at the edges, of pure gold, twisted like cords.

**16** And they made two gold settings and two gold rings, and put the two rings on the two ends of the breastpiece.

**17** And they put the two gold cords on the two rings at the ends of the breastpiece.

**18** And the two ends of the two cords they put on the two settings, and put them on the shoulder pieces of the ephod, toward its front.

**19** And they made two rings of gold and set them on the two ends of the breastpiece, on its edge, on the inner side facing the ephod.

**20** And they made two rings of gold and put them on the two shoulder pieces of the ephod, low down, at the front, close to its seam, above the woven band of the ephod.

**21** And they bound the breastpiece by its rings to the rings of the ephod with a cord of blue, so that it rested on the woven band of the ephod and the breastpiece would not come loose from the ephod, as the LORD had commanded Moses.

## The Robe and the Rest

**22** And he made the robe of the ephod, the work of a weaver, entirely of blue.

**23** And the opening of the robe was in the middle of it, like the opening of a coat of mail, with a binding around its opening, so that it would not be torn.

**24** And on the hem of the robe they made pomegranates of blue and purple and crimson, twisted.

**25** And they made bells of pure gold and put the bells between the pomegranates on the hem of the robe all around, between the pomegranates:

**26** a bell and a pomegranate, a bell and a pomegranate, on the hem of the robe all around, for serving — as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**27** And they made the tunics of fine linen, the work of a weaver, for Aaron and for his sons,

**28** and the turban of fine linen, and the ornamented caps of fine linen, and the linen undergarments of fine twisted linen,

**29** and the sash of fine twisted linen and blue and purple and crimson, the work of an embroiderer — as the LORD had commanded Moses.

**30** And they made the plate of the holy crown of pure gold, and wrote on it an inscription with the engravings of a seal: **Holy to the LORD**.

**31** And they put on it a cord of blue, to fasten it on the turban above, as the LORD had commanded Moses.

## They Bring It All to Moses

**32** And all the work of the dwelling of the tent of meeting was finished. And the sons of Israel did according to all that the LORD had commanded Moses; so they did.

**33** And they brought the dwelling to Moses: the tent and all its furnishings, its clasps, its frames, its bars, and its pillars and its sockets;

**34** and the covering of rams' skins dyed red, and the covering of fine leather, and the veil of the screen;

**35** the ark of the Testimony and its poles, and the cover;

**36** the table, all its vessels, and the bread of the Presence;

**37** the pure lampstand, its lamps — the lamps set in their row — and all its vessels, and the oil for the light;

**38** and the gold altar, and the anointing oil, and the fragrant incense, and the screen for the entrance of the tent;

**39** the bronze altar and the bronze grating that belongs to it, its poles and all its vessels; the basin and its stand;

**40** the hangings of the court, its pillars and its sockets, and the screen for the gate of the court, its cords and its pegs, and all the vessels for the service of the dwelling, for the tent of meeting;

**41** the finely worked garments for serving in the holy place — the holy garments for Aaron the priest and the garments of his sons, for serving as priests.

**42** According to all that the LORD had commanded Moses, so the sons of Israel did all the work.

**43** And Moses saw all the work, and here — they had done it; as the LORD had commanded, so they had done. And Moses blessed them.

## Notes

- **The sentence that will not stop — seven times** — *ka'asher tsivvah YHWH et Mosheh*, **as the LORD had commanded Moses**, closes vv1, 5, 7, 21, 26, 29 and 31: seven times in thirty-one verses, once at the end of each piece of clothing. Then the chapter says it three more times in a different shape, at v32, v42 and v43. A reader in English feels a refrain. What the Hebrew is doing is closer to a legal endorsement — each item signed off as it is handed over, the way a manifest is initialled line by line.
- **vv32, 43 the end of Exodus is written in the words of the beginning of Genesis** — three verbs, in the same order, with the same noun for the work. Genesis 2:2, *va-yekhal Elohim… melakhto asher asah*, God **finished** the work he had made; Exodus 39:32, *va-tekhel kol avodat ha-mishkan*, all the work of the dwelling was **finished**. Genesis 1:31, *va-yar Elohim et kol asher asah ve-hinneh tov me'od*, God **saw** all that he had made, and here — very good; Exodus 39:43, *va-yar Mosheh et kol ha-melakhah ve-hinneh asu otah*, Moses **saw** all the work, and here — they had done it. Genesis 2:3, God **blessed**; Exodus 39:43, Moses **blessed** them. A tent is finished in the vocabulary of a world being finished, and nothing in either passage points it out.
- **v3 "they hammered out sheets of gold and cut them into threads"** — the only description in the Bible of how any material in the sanctuary was actually manufactured. Everywhere else the text says *he made* and moves on. Here it stops to explain that the gold in the cloth is real gold: beaten into foil, cut into strips, and worked in among the blue and the purple as though it were yarn. Four chapters of gold objects, and this is the one sentence about technique.
- **v23 "like the opening of a coat of mail"** — *ke-fi tachra*, and *tachra* occurs twice in the Hebrew Bible, at 28:32 and here, both times in this same comparison about this same garment. Nothing else in the language defines it. Something with a reinforced neck-hole that does not tear — armor is the usual guess, and it is a guess. What the verse is certain about is the requirement: *so that it would not be torn*. The robe of the high priest is built to resist the one thing a man does to his clothes when someone dies.
- **vv24–26 "a bell and a pomegranate, a bell and a pomegranate"** — the Hebrew repeats the pair rather than stating the pattern, which is how the alternation is described: not *alternating*, but the thing said twice so the reader walks the hem. Chapter 28 gave the reason and it is not ornament — *its sound shall be heard when he goes into the holy place… and he shall not die* (28:35). The most elaborate garment in the book is fitted with a noise-maker, and the alternative named is death.
- **v30 "Holy to the LORD"** — *qodesh la-YHWH*, two words, and they are the only writing anywhere in the sanctuary. The tablets inside the ark are written, but they are shut in a box under a lid; these two words are worn on the forehead, in gold, facing out. Everything else in nine chapters of instruction is an object. This alone is a sentence.
- **v37 "the pure lampstand"** — *ha-menorah ha-tehorah*. The adjective normally describes the **gold** — *pure gold*, a dozen times in these chapters. Here it has slid onto the lampstand itself, as though the object had taken on the quality of its metal. And *nerot ha-ma'arakhah*, the lamps of the **row**: *ma'arakhah* is a setting-out in order, the word used of the rows of bread on the table and of an army drawn up in line of battle.
- **v43 "And Moses blessed them"** — no words are recorded. Aaron and his sons will later be handed the exact three lines to bless with, each one naming the LORD (Numbers 6:24–26); Moses here is given nothing to say. The Torah does report wordless blessings elsewhere — Jacob blesses Pharaoh twice and the text never says how (Genesis 47:7, 10) — but the occasion is unusual: almost every blessing in Genesis is spoken over someone leaving, or about what is still to come. This one is said to people who have just finished a job, about the job.
