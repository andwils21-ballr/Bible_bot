# Exodus 15

**1** Then Moses and the sons of Israel sang this song to the LORD, and they said:

I will sing to the LORD, for He is high, high above —  
horse and its rider He has thrown into the sea.

**2** My strength and my song is Yah,  
and He has become my deliverance.  
This is my God, and I will praise Him,  
the God of my father, and I will lift Him high.

**3** The LORD is a man of war.  
The LORD is His name.

**4** Pharaoh's chariots and his army He cast into the sea,  
and the pick of his officers were drowned in the Sea of Reeds.

**5** The deeps covered them.  
They went down into the depths like a stone.

**6** Your right hand, LORD, is majestic in power.  
Your right hand, LORD, shatters the enemy.

**7** And in the greatness of Your rising You tear down those who stand against You.  
You send out Your burning anger — it eats them like stubble.

**8** And by the breath of Your nostrils the waters were heaped up.  
The flowing waters stood like a wall.  
The deeps congealed in the heart of the sea.

**9** The enemy said, "I will chase, I will overtake, I will divide the spoil.  
My appetite will be full of them.  
I will draw my sword. My hand will drive them out."

**10** You blew with Your breath — the sea covered them.  
They sank like lead in the mighty waters.

**11** Who is like You among the gods, LORD?  
Who is like You — majestic in holiness,  
fearful in praises, doing wonders?

**12** You stretched out Your right hand.  
The earth swallowed them.

**13** In Your faithful love You led the people You redeemed.  
In Your strength You guided them to Your holy dwelling.

**14** The nations have heard, and they shake.  
Labor pains grip those who live in Philistia.

**15** Now the chiefs of Edom are terrified.  
Trembling seizes the mighty men of Moab.  
All who live in Canaan melt in fear.

**16** Terror and dread fall on them.  
By the might of Your arm they are struck still as stone —  
until Your people cross over, LORD,  
until the people You made Your own cross over.

**17** You will bring Your people in and plant them on the mountain of Your inheritance —  
the place You made for Your dwelling, LORD,  
the sanctuary, Lord, that Your hands established.

**18** The LORD will reign forever and ever.

**19** For Pharaoh's horses went into the sea with his chariots and his horsemen, and the LORD brought the waters of the sea back over them. But the sons of Israel had walked on dry ground in the middle of the sea.

**20** And Miriam the prophetess, Aaron's sister, took the tambourine in her hand, and all the women went out after her with tambourines and with dancing.

**21** And Miriam sang back to the men:

Sing to the LORD, for He is high, high above —  
horse and its rider He has thrown into the sea.

## Bitter Water

**22** And Moses made Israel set out from the Sea of Reeds, and they went out into the wilderness of Shur, and they walked three days in the wilderness and found no water.

**23** And they came to Marah, and they could not drink water from Marah, because it was bitter. That is why its name was called Marah.

**24** And the people grumbled against Moses, saying, "What are we to drink?"

**25** And he cried out to the LORD, and the LORD showed him a tree, and he threw it into the water, and the water became sweet. There He made for them a statute and a rule, and there He tested them.

**26** He said, "If you will listen hard to the voice of the LORD your God, and do what is right in His eyes, and give ear to His commands and keep all His statutes, then every sickness I put on Egypt I will not put on you — because I am the LORD your healer."

**27** And they came to Elim, and there were twelve springs of water and seventy palm trees, and they camped there by the water.

## Notes

- **v1 "Then Moses... sang"** — the Hebrew verb is *yashir*, which is the **future** form: *then Moses **will** sing*. Hebrew does use this form for past narration in poetry, and the sense here is past. But the oddity is real and old: readers as far back as the Talmud have taken it as a hint that the song is also still to be sung.
- **v1 "for He is high, high above"** — *ga'oh ga'ah*, the verb doubled. *Ga'ah* is to rise up, to be lifted, and it is used of water swelling. The first line of the song describes God with the word for a rising tide.
- **v2 "Yah"** — the short form of the divine name, and **this is its first appearance in the Bible**. Not *YHWH* but *Yah*, two letters, the form that survives in *hallelu-Yah* and at the end of names like Isaiah and Elijah. The oldest poem in the book uses the oldest form of the name.
- **v2 "my strength and my song"** — *ozzi ve-zimrat*, and *zimrat* will not settle. It is the word for song or music, but there is a second root spelled the same that means strength or protection — which would make the line *my strength and my might*. Both readings are defensible and both are ancient.
- **v2 "I will praise Him"** — *ve-anvehu*, from a root that gives both *naveh*, a dwelling or pasture, and a verb for beautifying. So the line has been read *I will glorify Him* and *I will make Him a dwelling*. Verse 13 then uses the noun outright — *Your holy **dwelling*** — which suggests the poet had the second sense in earshot.
- **v3 "The LORD is a man of war"** — *ish milchamah*, blunt and unhedged. The Greek Septuagint could not leave it and rendered it *the Lord, crushing wars*. The Hebrew says man of war, and this rendering keeps it.
- **vv14–16 the nations ahead** — Philistia, Edom, Moab, Canaan: every people the song names lies ahead of Israel, not behind. The song stands at the sea and sees the whole road to the land. Forty years later, in Jericho, Rahab says it back almost word for word: *the **terror** of you has **fallen** on us, and all who live in the land **melt** before you, for we have heard how the LORD dried up the water of the Sea of Reeds* (Joshua 2:9–10).
- **v15 "the mighty men of Moab"** — *eilei Moav*, literally **the rams of Moab**, the ram that leads the flock. The same word names a country's leading men in Ezekiel 17:13, *the mighty of the land*; the Greek has *rulers*.
- **v5, v16 "like a stone"** — the Egyptians go down *like a stone* in v5; the nations of Canaan are made still *like a stone* in v16. The song uses one image twice, for sinking and for freezing in place.
- **v8 "by the breath of Your nostrils"** — *ruach appekha*. The prose account said an east wind blew all night (14:21). The poem says God exhaled. Same event, and *ruach* is the same word: wind, breath, spirit.
- **v8 "the deeps congealed"** — *tehomot*, the same word as the deep over which the Spirit hovered in Genesis 1:2, and *qafa*, to thicken or curdle. The poem's picture is not of a corridor with two walls. It is of water turning solid.
- **v9 "I will chase, I will overtake, I will divide the spoil"** — six short verbs in a row with nothing between them, and every one is first person. The enemy's whole speech is a man listing what he is about to take. The next verse is one breath from God, and it ends.
- **v11 "among the gods"** — *ba-elim*. The song does not say the other gods do not exist; it asks who among them compares. That is what the Hebrew says, and it is older than the theology that later grew around it.
- **v12 "the earth swallowed them"** — *tivla'emo*, from *bala*. Aaron's staff **swallowed** the magicians' staffs in 7:12 with this verb. The contest that began with a swallowing ends with one.
- **v13 "Your faithful love"** — *chesed*, the hardest word in the Hebrew Bible to render: loyalty, kindness, covenant-faithfulness, the thing a party does because of a bond rather than because it must. No single English word holds it.
- **v17 "the mountain of Your inheritance... the sanctuary"** — a temple, on a mountain, built by God's hands. There is no temple yet, no land, and no Jerusalem. The song is standing somewhere later than the story it tells.
- **v20 "Miriam the prophetess, Aaron's sister"** — she is called Aaron's sister, not Moses'. She is also called a **prophetess**, the first woman given that title in the Bible, and she is unnamed back in chapter 2 when she watches the basket.
- **v21 "Miriam sang back to the men"** — *va-ta'an lahem*, she **answered** them, and the pronoun is masculine, pointing back to Moses and the men who sang in v1. The women answer in song, the way the women answer one another in 1 Samuel 18:7. The Greek reads *Miriam led them*.
- **v21 "Sing to the LORD"** — Miriam's version is the song's first line with one change: Moses' *I will sing* becomes her **imperative**, *Sing!* Some readers take her couplet as the original and the long song as the expansion. What can be said is that the book gives the same words twice and gives her the command form.
- **v23 "Marah, because it was bitter"** — *marah*, from *marar*. It is the root of *and they made their lives **bitter*** in 1:14, and of the *bitter herbs*, *merorim*, on the Passover table in 12:8. Three days out of Egypt, the first thing they find is the taste they just left.
- **v25 "the LORD showed him a tree"** — *va-yorehu*, from *yarah*, to point, to throw, to instruct — and it is the root of *torah*. God **instructs** him with a tree, and in the same verse gives *a statute and a rule*. The pun is not decorative: the first teaching in the wilderness is about water.
- **vv25–26 "there He tested them"** — the test is the sentence that follows: listen, do what is right, keep His commands. It is the first time God tests Israel, and the next two uses of the verb say what testing is for: the manna, *so that I may test them, whether they will walk in My instruction or not* (16:4), and Sinai, *God has come to test you* (20:20).
- **v26 "I am the LORD your healer"** — *rof'ekha*, from *rafa*, the root in the name Raphael. The promise is framed as an exemption: the diseases of Egypt will not follow them. The sentence assumes the plagues were aimed, not general.
- **v27 "twelve springs and seventy palm trees"** — twelve for the tribes and seventy for the elders of Israel and the nations of Genesis 10 is how later readers heard it; the text just gives the count. What the verse does is put a place of abundant water immediately after a place of bitter water, and say they camped there.
