# Exodus 37

## The Ark

**1** Bezalel made the ark of acacia wood — two and a half cubits its length, and a cubit and a half its width, and a cubit and a half its height.

**2** He overlaid it with pure gold inside and outside, and made a molding of gold on it all around.

**3** He cast for it four rings of gold on its four feet — two rings on its one side and two rings on its other side.

**4** He made poles of acacia wood and overlaid them with gold.

**5** He put the poles into the rings on the sides of the ark, to carry the ark.

**6** He made a cover of pure gold — two and a half cubits its length and a cubit and a half its width.

**7** He made two cherubim of gold. Hammered work he made them, at the two ends of the cover:

**8** one cherub at the end on this side and one cherub at the end on that side. Out of the cover itself he made the cherubim, at its two ends.

**9** The cherubim were spreading their wings upward, covering the cover with their wings, their faces each toward his brother. Toward the cover were the faces of the cherubim.

## The Table

**10** He made the table of acacia wood — two cubits its length and a cubit its width and a cubit and a half its height.

**11** He overlaid it with pure gold and made a molding of gold for it all around.

**12** He made for it a rim a handbreadth wide all around, and made a molding of gold for its rim all around.

**13** He cast for it four rings of gold and put the rings on the four corners that belong to its four legs.

**14** Close to the rim were the rings, holders for the poles, to carry the table.

**15** He made the poles of acacia wood and overlaid them with gold, to carry the table.

**16** He made the vessels that are on the table — its dishes and its pans and its bowls and the jars the drink offering is poured from — of pure gold.

## The Lampstand

**17** He made the lampstand of pure gold. Hammered work he made the lampstand — its base and its shaft; its cups, its bulbs and its blossoms were of one piece with it.

**18** Six branches going out from its sides — three branches of the lampstand from its one side and three branches of the lampstand from its other side.

**19** Three cups shaped like almond blossoms on the one branch, a bulb and a blossom; and three cups shaped like almond blossoms on the next branch, a bulb and a blossom. So for the six branches going out from the lampstand.

**20** On the lampstand itself, four cups shaped like almond blossoms, its bulbs and its blossoms;

**21** and a bulb under the first two branches of one piece with it, and a bulb under the next two branches of one piece with it, and a bulb under the next two branches of one piece with it — for the six branches going out from it.

**22** Their bulbs and their branches were of one piece with it; all of it one hammered work of pure gold.

**23** He made its seven lamps, and its tongs and its fire pans, of pure gold.

**24** Of a talent of pure gold he made it, with all its vessels.

## The Altar of Incense, and the Oil

**25** He made the altar of incense of acacia wood — a cubit its length and a cubit its width, square, and two cubits its height. Its horns were of one piece with it.

**26** He overlaid it with pure gold — its roof and its walls all around, and its horns — and made a molding of gold for it all around.

**27** Two rings of gold he made for it under its molding, on its two sides, on its two flanks, as holders for poles to carry it with.

**28** He made the poles of acacia wood and overlaid them with gold.

**29** He made the holy anointing oil and the pure fragrant incense, the work of a perfumer.

## Notes

- **v1 "Bezalel made the ark"** — chapter 36 reported the curtains, the frames and the veil with no one named: *and every one with a wise heart among those doing the work made…*, and then thirty verses of *and he made*, subject unstated. The ark is the one object in the sanctuary the text attaches a name to. Whoever cut the boards, this is the thing Bezalel is recorded as having made with his own hands.
- **v9 "their faces each toward his brother"** — *ish el achiv*, **a man toward his brother**. Biblical Hebrew has no word for *each other*; it borrows the language of kinship and uses it for objects — planks fitted to one another (26:3), curtains facing one another, and here two gold figures turned face to face. The reciprocal is always built out of a relationship word, so the grammar of two things facing is the grammar of two brothers.
- **vv19–20 "cups shaped like almond blossoms"** — *meshuqqadim*, from *shaqed*, the almond. The almond is the first tree in the land to flower, weeks ahead of anything else, and its name is spelled with the same consonants as *shoqed*, **watching, staying awake**. Jeremiah is shown *a branch of an almond*, and the answer is *I am watching over My word to do it* (Jeremiah 1:11–12) — the whole exchange is that one pair of words. The lampstand that stands burning in the dark of the sanctuary is shaped, four times over, into the tree whose name means staying awake.
- **v24 "of a talent of pure gold"** — *kikkar*, and the word means a **round flat disc**: a loaf of bread, a district of flat country. It is what Lot looks out over in Genesis 13:10, *the whole kikkar of the Jordan* — the Round, the plain. Weight got its name from the shape the metal was cast in. About seventy-five pounds of gold, hammered out of one piece, with nothing joined and nothing soldered.
- **v26 "its roof and its walls"** — *gag* and *qirot*, the ordinary words for the roof and the walls of a house. The incense altar is eighteen inches square and three feet high, and the Hebrew describes it in the vocabulary of architecture, as though it were a building rather than a piece of furniture. Its four raised corners are *qarnot*, **horns**, which is the animal word. A small house with horns, standing in front of the veil.
- **v29 "the work of a perfumer"** — *ma'aseh roqeach*, and it is the last craft named in the chapter and the only one that is not carpentry, metalwork or weaving. Everything else in the sanctuary is shaped by hand; the oil and the incense are compounded. 30:32–33 makes the recipe itself restricted — anyone who mixes it to the same formula for ordinary use is cut off from his people. It is the one object in the tent that can be copied exactly, which is why it is the one the law has to forbid copying.
