# Exodus 4

**1** Moses answered and said, "But look — they will not believe me and will not listen to my voice, for they will say, 'The LORD did not appear to you.'"

**2** The LORD said to him, "What is that in your hand?" He said, "A staff."

**3** He said, "Throw it on the ground." He threw it on the ground, and it became a snake, and Moses ran from it.

**4** The LORD said to Moses, "Stretch out your hand and take hold of its tail." He stretched out his hand and seized it, and it became a staff in his palm —

**5** "so that they may believe that the LORD, the God of their fathers, appeared to you — the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob."

**6** The LORD said to him further, "Put your hand into your cloak." He put his hand into his cloak, and drew it out, and here — his hand was blighted, white as snow.

**7** He said, "Put your hand back into your cloak." He put his hand back into his cloak and drew it out of his cloak, and here — it had gone back like the rest of his flesh.

**8** "And it will come to pass, if they do not believe you and do not listen to the voice of the first sign, they will believe the voice of the second sign.

**9** And it will come to pass, if they do not believe even these two signs and do not listen to your voice, you shall take some of the water of the Nile and pour it on the dry ground, and the water you take from the Nile will become blood on the dry ground."

## I Am Heavy of Mouth

**10** Moses said to the LORD, "Please, my Lord, I am not a man of words — not yesterday, not the day before, not since You began speaking to Your servant. For I am heavy of mouth and heavy of tongue."

**11** The LORD said to him, "Who put a mouth in a man? Or who makes him mute, or deaf, or seeing, or blind? Is it not I, the LORD?

**12** And now go, and I Myself will be with your mouth, and I will teach you what to say."

**13** He said, "Please, my Lord — send someone else."

**14** The anger of the LORD burned against Moses, and He said, "Is there not Aaron your brother, the Levite? I know that he can certainly speak. And here — he is even coming out to meet you, and he will see you and be glad in his heart.

**15** You shall speak to him and put the words in his mouth, and I Myself will be with your mouth and with his mouth, and I will teach you both what you are to do.

**16** He will speak for you to the people, and it will come to pass that he will be a mouth for you, and you will be God for him.

**17** You shall take this staff in your hand, with which you will do the signs."

## Back to Egypt

**18** Moses went and returned to Jether his father-in-law and said to him, "Let me go, please, and return to my brothers who are in Egypt, and see whether they are still alive." Jethro said to Moses, "Go in peace."

**19** The LORD said to Moses in Midian, "Go, return to Egypt, for all the men who were seeking your life are dead."

**20** Moses took his wife and his sons and set them on the donkey, and returned to the land of Egypt. Moses took the staff of God in his hand.

**21** The LORD said to Moses, "When you go back to Egypt, see all the wonders I have put in your hand, and do them before Pharaoh. I Myself will make his heart strong, and he will not send the people away.

**22** You shall say to Pharaoh, 'This is what the LORD says: Israel is My son, My firstborn.

**23** I said to you, send My son away so he may serve Me, and you refused to send him away. Here — I am killing your son, your firstborn.'"

## A Bridegroom of Blood

**24** And it came to pass on the road, at the lodging place, that the LORD met him and sought to put him to death.

**25** Zipporah took a flint and cut off her son's foreskin and touched it to his feet, and said, "A husband of blood you are to me."

**26** He let him go. Then she said, "A husband of blood," because of the circumcision.

**27** The LORD said to Aaron, "Go to meet Moses in the wilderness." He went and met him at the mountain of God, and kissed him.

**28** Moses told Aaron all the words of the LORD who had sent him, and all the signs He had commanded him.

**29** Moses and Aaron went and gathered all the elders of the sons of Israel.

**30** Aaron spoke all the words that the LORD had spoken to Moses, and did the signs in the sight of the people.

**31** The people believed. They heard that the LORD had attended to the sons of Israel and that He had seen their affliction, and they bowed down and worshiped.

## Notes

- **v2 "What is that in your hand?"** — in the written consonants the Hebrew question is a single word, *mazzeh*; the traditional reading splits it into two, *mah zeh*, **what is this**. The scribes preserved both — the letters as written (*ketiv*) and the words as read aloud (*qere*) — rather than choosing. Where the two differ, the tradition kept the written text untouched and put the reading in the margin, and English translations are silently picking one of the two.
- **v2 "A staff"** — a shepherd's stick, the ordinary tool of the job Moses has been doing for forty years. God does not hand him anything. The whole demonstration is done with what he already brought.
- **v3 "it became a snake"** — the sign is not just a wonder; Egypt's crown carries a rearing cobra on the forehead, and Moses is told to grab it by the tail. Handling a snake by the tail is the wrong end, and that appears to be the point.
- **v6 "his hand was blighted, white as snow"** — *metsora'at*, the condition Leviticus 13 legislates, rendered *blight* there too. English Bibles usually print *leprous*, but the biblical condition is not the modern disease of that name; it covers a range of skin, fabric, and wall discolorations that make a person or thing unfit to approach the sanctuary. The sign makes Moses untouchable and then undoes it, twice, in two verses.
- **v9 "will become blood on the dry ground"** — the third sign is a small private version of what becomes the first plague in 7:20. Here it is a handful of river water poured out in front of the elders.
- **v10 "I am heavy of mouth and heavy of tongue"** — *kevad peh u-khevad lashon*. Heavy, weighted, hard to move. The Hebrew does not name a condition, and it has been read as a stutter, as a speech impairment, and as simple lack of eloquence. What the text says is that his mouth is heavy. The same root *kaved* is the one used later for Pharaoh's **heavy** heart.
- **v11 "Who put a mouth in a man?"** — Moses says his mouth does not work; God's answer is that He is the one who makes mouths, working and not. It does not fix the mouth. It moves the question.
- **v13 "send someone else"** — the Hebrew doubles the verb, *shelach... tishlach*, "send... you will send." After four objections this is Moses' plain request, and the last thing he says before God gets angry.
- **v14 "the anger of the LORD burned against Moses"** — and the anger produces Aaron. The punishment for refusing is that he now has to share the job with his brother. Nothing else happens; God keeps talking in the same breath.
- **v16 "you will be God for him"** — *ve-attah tihyeh lo le-Elohim*. Aaron is the mouth, Moses is the one whose words the mouth carries. The same arrangement is restated in 7:1, where Aaron is called Moses' **prophet**. It is the clearest working definition of a prophet the Bible gives: not the source of the message, the mouth for it.
- **v18 "Jether his father-in-law"** — the name is spelled *Yeter* in the first half of this verse and *Yitro* (Jethro) in the second half, in the same sentence. He is also called Reuel in 2:18. The book does not reconcile the names.
- **v20 "the staff of God"** — it was *a staff* in v2, when the question was what Moses had in his hand. Eighteen verses later the narrator calls it the staff of God. The object has not changed.
- **v21 "I Myself will make his heart strong"** — *va-ani achazzeq et libbo*. God says this before Pharaoh has appeared in the story or refused anything. Three different Hebrew verbs are used across the plague accounts for what happens to Pharaoh's heart — *chazaq* (make strong), *kaved* (make heavy), *qashah* (make hard) — and the book distributes them between God doing it and Pharaoh doing it to himself. The first statement of it, here, is God's.
- **v22 "Israel is My son, My firstborn"** — the first time God calls the nation His son. It sets up the exchange in the next verse and the last plague at the end of it: a firstborn for a firstborn, announced before a single plague has fallen.
- **vv24–26 "a husband of blood"** — three verses, and they are among the most obscure in the Bible. The pronouns are unanchored: *the LORD met **him** and sought to put **him** to death* — Moses or the son, the Hebrew does not say. *She touched it to **his feet*** — whose feet, and *feet* is elsewhere a euphemism. *Chatan damim* is not a phrase that occurs anywhere else, and *chatan* specifically names the male relation marriage creates — a groom or son-in-law — not a general word for husband. What is clear is the sequence: God has just called Israel His firstborn son, and immediately a son's blood on a doorpost of flesh turns death aside — which is the Passover, eight chapters early.
- **v31 "the LORD had attended to the sons of Israel"** — *paqad*, the word from 3:16, and from Joseph's deathbed at the end of Genesis. The people believe when they hear it. They have been waiting on that verb for four hundred years.
