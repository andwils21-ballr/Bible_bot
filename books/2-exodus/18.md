# Exodus 18

## Jethro Comes to the Mountain

**1** And Jethro, priest of Midian, Moses' father-in-law, heard all that God had done for Moses and for Israel His people, that the LORD had brought Israel out of Egypt.

**2** And Jethro, Moses' father-in-law, took Zipporah, Moses' wife, after her sending away,

**3** and her two sons — the name of the one was Gershom, for he had said, "I have been a foreigner in a foreign land,"

**4** and the name of the other was Eliezer, "for the God of my father was my help, and He rescued me from Pharaoh's sword" —

**5** and Jethro, Moses' father-in-law, came with his sons and his wife to Moses, to the wilderness where he was camped, at the mountain of God.

**6** And he said to Moses, "I, your father-in-law Jethro, am coming to you — and your wife, and her two sons with her."

**7** And Moses went out to meet his father-in-law, and bowed down and kissed him, and each asked the other how he was, and they came into the tent.

**8** And Moses recounted to his father-in-law everything the LORD had done to Pharaoh and to Egypt on Israel's account, all the hardship that had found them on the way, and how the LORD rescued them.

**9** And Jethro thrilled over all the good the LORD had done for Israel, whom He had rescued from the hand of Egypt.

**10** And Jethro said, "Blessed is the LORD, who rescued you from the hand of Egypt and from the hand of Pharaoh, who rescued the people from under the hand of Egypt.

**11** Now I know that the LORD is greater than all the gods — for in the very thing in which they acted arrogantly against them."

**12** And Jethro, Moses' father-in-law, took a burnt offering and sacrifices for God. And Aaron came, and all the elders of Israel, to eat bread with Moses' father-in-law before God.

## The Thing Is Too Heavy for You

**13** And it came to pass the next day that Moses sat to judge the people, and the people stood over Moses from the morning until the evening.

**14** And Moses' father-in-law saw everything he was doing for the people, and said, "What is this thing you are doing for the people? Why do you sit alone, and all the people are standing over you from morning until evening?"

**15** And Moses said to his father-in-law, "Because the people come to me to inquire of God.

**16** When they have a matter, it comes to me, and I judge between a man and his neighbor, and I make known the statutes of God and His instructions."

**17** And Moses' father-in-law said to him, "The thing you are doing is not good.

**18** You will surely wither — you, and this people with you — because the thing is too heavy for you. You cannot do it alone.

**19** Now listen to my voice. I will advise you, and may God be with you. You be for the people in front of God, and bring the matters to God.

**20** And you shall warn them about the statutes and the instructions, and make known to them the way they are to walk in and the work they are to do.

**21** And you shall look out from all the people men of ability, who fear God, men of truth, who hate a cut taken by force — and set them over them as officers of thousands, officers of hundreds, officers of fifties, and officers of tens.

**22** And they shall judge the people at all times. Every great matter they shall bring to you, and every small matter they shall judge themselves. Lighten it from upon you, and let them carry it with you.

**23** If you do this thing, and God commands you, then you will be able to stand, and also all this people will come to its place in peace."

**24** And Moses listened to the voice of his father-in-law, and did all that he had said.

**25** And Moses chose men of ability from all Israel and made them heads over the people — officers of thousands, officers of hundreds, officers of fifties, and officers of tens.

**26** And they judged the people at all times. The hard matter they would bring to Moses, and every small matter they would judge themselves.

**27** And Moses sent his father-in-law off, and he went to his own land.

## Notes

- **"Moses' father-in-law"** — *choten Mosheh*, **thirteen times in twenty-seven verses**, often in the same breath as his name: *Jethro, Moses' father-in-law*. It is not how Hebrew narrative usually works; a named character normally keeps his name. The chapter is insisting on the relationship rather than the man, and the man it insists on is a Midianite priest.
- **v2 "after her sending away"** — *achar shilucheha*, from *shalach*, the verb Moses shouted at Pharaoh in every plague: **send my people away**. Somewhere between chapter 4, where Zipporah is travelling to Egypt with him, and here, Moses sent his wife away. The book never tells that story. It uses the word and moves on, and then closes the chapter in v27 with the same verb again, this time for her father.
- **vv3–4 Gershom and Eliezer** — both names are sentences. *Ger* is a resident outsider, so **Gershom** holds the words *a foreigner there*, and the verse spells it out: *I have been a foreigner in a foreign land*. **Eliezer** is *my God is help*, and the verse spells that out too: *the God of my father was my help*. Moses named his first son for exile and his second for rescue, in that order, and the two names put together are the plot of the book.
- **v9 "thrilled"** — *va-yichad*, and the verb is so rare that its meaning has to be argued. One root gives *rejoice*; another gives *be sharp*, which in a person is the prickle that runs over the skin. The Greek translators took neither: they wrote ἐξέστη, **he was beside himself, out of his mind with it**. What is certain is that a strong physical reaction is meant, and that no single English word covers the range.
- **v11 "for in the very thing in which they acted arrogantly against them"** — the sentence stops. There is no main clause in the Hebrew, and the Greek does not supply one either; the oldest translation is as stranded as the original. The verb is *zid*, to **boil up and over** — the root of *nazid*, the stew Jacob was cooking when Esau sold his birthright (Genesis 25:29). Pride in this language is a pot left on the fire.
- **v12 "and Aaron came, and all the elders of Israel"** — a priest of Midian offers the sacrifice, and Israel's leadership comes to eat at his table. This is before Sinai, before Aaron is a priest, before there is a law about who may do this. The guest list is given in full and **Moses is not on it**.
- **v18 "you will surely wither"** — *navol tibbol*, the verb doubled for force. It is what a leaf does on a dying branch and what a flower does in the heat — not collapse, not exhaustion, but the slow drying-out of something that was green. And the reason given is that the matter is *kaved*, **heavy**: the same word as Pharaoh's heavy heart, the LORD's heavy glory, and Moses' heavy hands six verses earlier.
- **v19 "listen to my voice"** — *shema be-qoli*. The book uses this phrase at its hinges: the man in the garden was condemned for listening to the voice of his wife, and Abraham was commanded to listen to the voice of Sarah. Here a foreign priest says it to Moses about how to run a court, and v24 records that he did.
- **v21 "men of truth, who hate a cut taken by force"** — *anshe emet*, from the same root as the *steady* hands of 17:12 — a man of *emet* is one who holds. And *betsa* is not an abstraction: the root means to **cut off a piece**, to break something and take your portion. It is profit pictured as a slice torn out of someone else's loaf. The Greek missed this and wrote *hating arrogance*.
- **v22 "and let them carry it with you"** — *nasa*, the load verb. The whole speech treats judging as freight: too heavy for one man, lightened by dividing it, carried by others alongside him. Nothing is said about authority or rank. It is about weight.
