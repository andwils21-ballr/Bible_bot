# Exodus 2

**1** And a man from the house of Levi went and took a daughter of Levi.

**2** And the woman conceived and bore a son. And she saw him, that he was good, and she hid him three months.

**3** And she could hide him no longer, and she took for him a basket of papyrus and daubed it with tar and with resin, and put the child in it, and set it in the reeds on the bank of the Nile.

**4** And his sister stationed herself at a distance, to know what would be done to him.

**5** And Pharaoh's daughter came down to bathe at the Nile, and her young women were walking beside the Nile. And she saw the basket among the reeds, and she sent her maid and took it.

**6** And she opened it and saw him — the child — and here, a boy crying. And she had compassion on him, and said, "This is one of the Hebrews' children."

**7** And his sister said to Pharaoh's daughter, "Shall I go and call you a nursing woman from the Hebrews, and she will nurse the child for you?"

**8** And Pharaoh's daughter said to her, "Go." And the girl went and called the child's mother.

**9** And Pharaoh's daughter said to her, "Take this child away and nurse him for me, and I myself will give your wages." And the woman took the child and nursed him.

**10** And the child grew, and she brought him to Pharaoh's daughter, and he became a son to her. And she called his name Moses, and said, "Because I drew him out of the water."

## He Turned This Way and That

**11** And it came to pass in those days that Moses grew up, and he went out to his brothers and saw their burdens. And he saw an Egyptian man striking a Hebrew man, one of his brothers.

**12** And he turned this way and that, and saw that there was no man, and he struck down the Egyptian and hid him in the sand.

**13** And he went out on the second day, and here — two Hebrew men fighting. And he said to the one in the wrong, "Why do you strike your neighbor?"

**14** And he said, "Who made you a chief and a judge over us? Are you saying you will kill me as you killed the Egyptian?" And Moses was afraid and said, "Surely the thing is known."

**15** And Pharaoh heard this thing and sought to kill Moses. And Moses fled from before Pharaoh and settled in the land of Midian. And he sat down by a well.

## The Well in Midian

**16** And the priest of Midian had seven daughters. And they came and drew water and filled the troughs to water their father's flock.

**17** And the shepherds came and drove them off, and Moses rose and saved them, and watered their flock.

**18** And they came to Reuel their father, and he said, "Why have you come so quickly today?"

**19** And they said, "An Egyptian man delivered us from the hand of the shepherds, and he even drew, drew water for us, and watered the flock."

**20** And he said to his daughters, "And where is he? Why did you leave the man? Call him, and let him eat bread."

**21** And Moses was willing to stay with the man, and he gave Zipporah his daughter to Moses.

**22** And she bore a son, and he called his name Gershom, for he said, "I have been a foreigner in a foreign land."

**23** And it came to pass in those many days that the king of Egypt died. And the sons of Israel groaned from the labor and cried out, and their cry for help went up to God from the labor.

**24** And God heard their groaning, and God remembered His covenant with Abraham, with Isaac, and with Jacob.

**25** And God saw the sons of Israel. And God knew.

## Notes

- **v2 "she saw him, that he was good"** — *va-tere oto ki tov hu*, and that is the sentence of the first day of creation: *and God saw the light, that it was good* (Genesis 1:4). A mother looking at a baby she is about to hide gets the words God used looking at the light.
- **v3 "a basket of papyrus"** — the Hebrew is *tevat gome*, and *tevah* is the word for **Noah's ark**. It occurs in the Bible for those two objects and nothing else. Both are containers, not boats; both are sealed against water — Noah's *covered with covering*, this one daubed with tar and resin; both carry a survivor through a flood sent to kill. The second one is three feet long.
- **v3 "in the reeds"** — *ba-suf*. The sea Israel walks through in 13:18 is *Yam Suf*, the **Sea of Reeds**. The child is hidden among the *suf* and will bring a nation out through the *suf*, and Hebrew says it with the same word both times.
- **v5 "Pharaoh's daughter came down to bathe at the Nile"** — her father has just made the Nile an instrument for drowning boys (1:22). She goes down to it, finds one of them, and takes him home. The text lets the two sentences sit four verses apart without comment.
- **v6 "This is one of the Hebrews' children"** — she knows exactly what she is doing, says so out loud, and does it anyway. She is the daughter of the man whose policy it is.
- **v9 "I myself will give your wages"** — the baby's own mother is hired, and paid by the palace, to nurse her own son. Pharaoh's daughter does not know it. The sister arranged it in one sentence (v7) and nobody in the scene is named except the child at the end.
- **v10 "Moses"** — *Mosheh*, and Pharaoh's daughter explains it in Hebrew: *ki min ha-mayim meshitihu*, **because I drew him out of the water**, from *mashah*, to draw out. But the form of the name is not passive. *Mosheh* is an **active** participle — not *the drawn-out one* but **the one who draws out**. She names him for what was done to him and the grammar names him for what he will do.
- **v11 "their burdens"** — *sivlotam*, the word from 1:11, where taskmasters were set over them *to afflict them with their **burdens***. Moses goes out and sees the exact noun the policy was written in.
- **v12 "he turned this way and that, and saw that there was no man"** — *va-yifen koh va-khoh*. He checks for witnesses before he acts, which tells the reader what he already thinks of the act. And *ein ish*, **no man** — the next day there turn out to have been witnesses after all.
- **v14 "Who made you a chief and a judge over us?"** — the question is meant as an insult and it is also a job description. Forty years later he is exactly that, appointed by someone else. And *akhen noda ha-davar*, **the thing is known** — *yada*, the verb the last chapter used for the king who did **not** know Joseph.
- **v15 "And he sat down by a well"** — the third time in the Bible a man sits at a foreign well and leaves engaged. Abraham's servant met Rebekah at one (Genesis 24), Jacob met Rachel at one (Genesis 29), and the shape repeats here down to the details: a traveler, a well, women with a flock, water drawn, a run home with news, a welcome, a marriage.
- **v17 "and Moses rose and saved them"** — *va-yoshi'an*, from *yasha*, to save or deliver. It is the root behind the names Joshua and Jesus, and this is the first thing in the Bible Moses does with it — for seven Midianite women he has never met. The same verb reports the sea in 14:30: *and the LORD **saved** Israel that day*.
- **v19 "An Egyptian man delivered us"** — they take him for an Egyptian, and they are not wrong about how he looks. He was raised in the palace. Two verses later he says his own name for himself: *a foreigner in a foreign land*.
- **v22 "Gershom"** — *ger hayiti be-erets nokhriyyah*, **I have been a foreigner in a foreign land**, and the name carries *ger* in it. A man born a Hebrew, raised an Egyptian, married in Midian, names his son for belonging nowhere.
- **vv23–25 four verbs, and then the sentence stops** — God **heard** their groaning, **remembered** His covenant, **saw** the sons of Israel — *va-yeda Elohim*, **and God knew**.

  And there the verse ends, with no object. Knew what? The Hebrew does not say, and every English Bible that prints *and God took notice of them* or *and God acknowledged them* has supplied something the text withholds. After four hundred years of silence the book says only that God knew, and then changes the subject to a burning bush.
