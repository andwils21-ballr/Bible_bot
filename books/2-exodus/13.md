# Exodus 13

**1** And the LORD spoke to Moses:

**2** "Set apart to Me every firstborn, the opener of every womb among the sons of Israel, in man and in beast. It is Mine."

**3** And Moses said to the people, "Remember this day, on which you came out of Egypt, out of the house of slaves, because with a strong hand the LORD brought you out of here. And nothing leavened shall be eaten.

**4** Today you are going out, in the month of Aviv.

**5** And it will be, when the LORD brings you to the land of the Canaanite and the Hittite and the Amorite and the Hivite and the Jebusite, which He swore to your fathers to give you — a land flowing with milk and honey — that you shall keep this service in this month.

**6** Seven days you shall eat unleavened bread, and on the seventh day there will be a feast to the LORD.

**7** Unleavened bread shall be eaten through the seven days, and nothing leavened shall be seen with you, and no leaven shall be seen with you in all your territory.

**8** And you shall tell your son on that day, 'It is because of what the LORD did for me when I came out of Egypt.'

**9** And it will be a sign for you on your hand, and a reminder between your eyes, so that the instruction of the LORD may be in your mouth — because with a strong hand the LORD brought you out of Egypt.

**10** And you shall keep this statute at its appointed time, from year to year.

## Every Firstborn Is Mine

**11** "And it will be, when the LORD brings you to the land of the Canaanite, as He swore to you and to your fathers, and gives it to you,

**12** that you shall set apart to the LORD every opener of the womb. And every firstling dropped by an animal that belongs to you — the males are the LORD's.

**13** And every firstling of a donkey you shall redeem with a lamb, and if you do not redeem it, you shall break its neck. And every firstborn of man among your sons you shall redeem.

**14** And it will be, when your son asks you tomorrow, 'What is this?' — you shall say to him, 'With a strong hand the LORD brought us out of Egypt, out of the house of slaves.

**15** And it came to pass, when Pharaoh made it hard to send us away, that the LORD killed every firstborn in the land of Egypt, from the firstborn of man to the firstborn of beast. That is why I sacrifice to the LORD every male that opens the womb, and every firstborn of my sons I redeem.'

**16** And it will be a sign on your hand and a band between your eyes, because with a strong hand the LORD brought us out of Egypt."

## The Long Way Round

**17** And it came to pass, when Pharaoh sent the people away, that God did not lead them by the way of the land of the Philistines, though it was near — because God said, "The people may change their minds when they see war, and go back to Egypt."

**18** And God took the people around by the way of the wilderness, to the Sea of Reeds. And the sons of Israel went up out of the land of Egypt in ranks.

**19** And Moses took the bones of Joseph with him, because he had made the sons of Israel swear, saying, "God will surely attend to you, and you shall bring my bones up from here with you."

**20** And they set out from Succoth and camped at Etham, at the edge of the wilderness.

**21** And the LORD went before them by day in a pillar of cloud to lead them on the way, and by night in a pillar of fire to give them light — so they could travel by day and by night.

**22** The pillar of cloud did not move away by day, nor the pillar of fire by night, from before the people.

## Notes

- **v2 "the opener of every womb"** — *peter kol rechem*, and the Hebrew is anatomical rather than ceremonial. Not the eldest son but the one that opened the body. That is why the rule covers animals in the same breath, and why v12 repeats it for a firstling *dropped* by a beast.
- **v4 "the month of Aviv"** — *aviv* means the barley in the ear, green and not yet hard. Four chapters back, the hail struck the flax and the barley *because the barley was **aviv*** (9:31). The month of the exodus is named for the crop stage that dated the seventh plague, and the same word does both jobs.
- **v3, 14 "the house of slaves"** — *beit avadim*. Not a condition but an address: the house you lived in. And *avadim* is the same word as the *service* Israel is now to render the LORD in v5 — *avodah*. They leave one master's house to serve another, and Hebrew uses one root for both.
- **v8 "what the LORD did for **me**"** — not *for them*, not *for us*: **for me**. A man who was not there says the exodus happened to him personally. This single word is the hinge of the whole Passover liturgy, which requires each person in every generation to say it in the first person.
- **v9 "a sign on your hand, and a reminder between your eyes"** — the sentence later Judaism takes literally, as the leather boxes worn on the arm and forehead. Whether the verse was meant as a figure of speech or an instruction is not decidable from the words; both readings are old.
- **v16 "a band between your eyes"** — *totafot*, and the word occurs **three times in the entire Hebrew Bible**: here, Deuteronomy 6:8, and Deuteronomy 11:18 — all three in this same formula and nowhere else. Its meaning is unrecovered. Bands, frontlets, ornaments have all been proposed. Nothing in Hebrew settles it, because the word has no other occurrence to be compared with.
- **v13 "you shall break its neck"** — the donkey cannot be sacrificed, because it is not a clean animal; but it is a firstling and so belongs to God. The law gives two ways to hand it over: buy it back with a lamb, or destroy it. There is no option to keep it.
- **v17 "God did not lead them... the people may change their minds"** — two different roots that come out on the page as the same three letters, **נחם**. *Nacham* here is *lead them*; *yinnachem* in the second half is *change their mind, relent*. A reader of the consonants sees the same word twice in one verse, doing opposite work.
- **v17 "though it was near"** — the coastal road to Canaan was a week's walk. The text says plainly that God chose the long way on purpose, and gives the reason: a people fresh out of slavery will turn around the first time they are shot at.
- **v18 "in ranks"** — *chamushim*, and the word is genuinely uncertain. It is spelled with the same consonants as *chamishim*, **fifty** — the word used for the officers over *fifties* in 18:21 and for the *fifty* loops of the tabernacle curtain in 26:5. So the phrase has been read as *in battle order*, *in fives*, and *armed*. The letters do not choose.
- **v19 "the bones of Joseph"** — Genesis ends with Joseph making this exact demand: *God will surely attend to you, and you shall bring my bones up from here* (50:25). The phrase he used, *paqod yifqod*, is the one God turned into the past tense at the bush — *I have surely attended*. Four hundred years later Moses walks out of Egypt carrying a coffin, and the book records it in one verse between two travel notes.
- **v21 "a pillar of cloud... a pillar of fire"** — the text does not describe two things. It is one presence, seen as cloud in daylight and as fire in the dark, and v22 says neither ever left. What it does is not protect or fight. It makes travel possible at any hour.
