# Exodus 12

**1** The LORD said to Moses and to Aaron in the land of Egypt:

**2** "This month is for you the head of months. It is the first for you, of the months of the year.

**3** Speak to the whole assembly of Israel: On the tenth of this month, let them take for themselves, each man, a lamb for a father's house — a lamb for a household.

**4** If the household is too small for a lamb, then he and his neighbor nearest his house shall take one, by the number of people. You shall count for the lamb according to what each one eats.

**5** A lamb without blemish, male, a year old, shall be yours. You may take it from the sheep or from the goats.

**6** You shall keep it until the fourteenth day of this month, and the whole congregation of the assembly of Israel shall slaughter it between the evenings.

**7** They shall take some of the blood and put it on the two doorposts and on the lintel, on the houses where they eat it.

**8** They shall eat the meat that night, roasted with fire, with unleavened bread, with bitter herbs they shall eat it.

**9** Do not eat any of it raw, or boiled at all in water, but roasted with fire — its head with its legs and with its inner parts.

**10** You shall not leave any of it until morning. Whatever is left of it until morning you shall burn with fire.

**11** This is how you shall eat it: your waists belted, your sandals on your feet, and your staff in your hand. You shall eat it in haste. It is a Passover to the LORD.

**12** I will pass through the land of Egypt that night, and I will strike down every firstborn in the land of Egypt, from man to beast. Against all the gods of Egypt I will carry out judgments. I am the LORD.

**13** The blood will be a sign for you on the houses where you are. I will see the blood, and I will pass over you, and there will be no blow of destruction against you when I strike the land of Egypt.

## A Memorial

**14** This day will be a memorial for you, and you shall keep it as a feast to the LORD. Through your generations, as a lasting statute, you shall keep it.

**15** Seven days you shall eat unleavened bread. On the very first day you shall put away leaven from your houses, because anyone who eats what is leavened, from the first day to the seventh day — that person will be cut off from Israel.

**16** On the first day there will be a holy assembly, and on the seventh day a holy assembly for you. No work shall be done on them, except what every person must eat — only that may be prepared for you.

**17** You shall keep the Unleavened Bread, because on this very day I brought your divisions out of the land of Egypt. You shall keep this day through your generations, as a lasting statute.

**18** In the first month, on the fourteenth day of the month in the evening, you shall eat unleavened bread, until the twenty-first day of the month in the evening.

**19** For seven days no leaven shall be found in your houses, because anyone who eats what is leavened — that person will be cut off from the assembly of Israel, whether a guest or a native of the land.

**20** You shall eat nothing leavened. In all your dwellings you shall eat unleavened bread."

## Draw Out and Take

**21** Moses called for all the elders of Israel and said to them, "Draw out and take for yourselves flocks for your families, and slaughter the Passover.

**22** You shall take a bunch of hyssop and dip it in the blood that is in the basin, and touch the lintel and the two doorposts with the blood that is in the basin. None of you shall go out from the doorway of his house until morning.

**23** The LORD will pass through to strike Egypt, and He will see the blood on the lintel and on the two doorposts, and the LORD will pass over the doorway, and He will not let the destroyer come into your houses to strike.

**24** You shall keep this thing as a decree for you and for your sons forever.

**25** And it will come to pass, when you come to the land that the LORD will give you as He promised, that you shall keep this service.

**26** And it will come to pass, when your sons say to you, 'What does this service mean to you?' —

**27** you shall say, 'It is the sacrifice of the Passover to the LORD, who passed over the houses of the sons of Israel in Egypt when He struck Egypt, and He rescued our houses.'" And the people bowed down and worshiped.

**28** The sons of Israel went and did as the LORD had commanded Moses and Aaron — so they did.

## At Midnight

**29** And it came to pass at midnight that the LORD struck down every firstborn in the land of Egypt, from the firstborn of Pharaoh sitting on his throne to the firstborn of the captive who was in the dungeon, and every firstborn of the animals.

**30** Pharaoh got up in the night, he and all his servants and all Egypt, and there was a great cry in Egypt, because there was no house where there was not someone dead.

**31** He called for Moses and Aaron at night and said, "Get up, get out from among my people, both you and the sons of Israel! Go, serve the LORD as you said.

**32** Take your flocks and your herds too, as you said, and go. Bless me also."

**33** Egypt pressed hard on the people to hurry them out of the land, because they said, "We are all dead men."

**34** The people carried their dough before it was leavened, their kneading bowls bound up in their cloaks on their shoulders.

**35** The sons of Israel did as Moses said, and they asked from Egypt objects of silver and objects of gold and clothing.

**36** The LORD gave the people favor in the eyes of Egypt, and they let them have what they asked. They stripped Egypt bare.

**37** The sons of Israel set out from Rameses toward Succoth, about six hundred thousand men on foot, besides children.

**38** A great mixed crowd also went up with them, and flocks and herds, a very great amount of livestock.

**39** They baked the dough they had brought out of Egypt into flat cakes of unleavened bread, because it had not risen — because they were driven out of Egypt and could not wait, and they had not even made provisions for themselves.

**40** The time the sons of Israel lived in Egypt was four hundred and thirty years.

**41** And it came to pass at the end of four hundred and thirty years — on this very day all the divisions of the LORD went out from the land of Egypt.

**42** It is a night of watching for the LORD, to bring them out of the land of Egypt. This is that night for the LORD, a watching for all the sons of Israel through their generations.

## One Law

**43** The LORD said to Moses and Aaron, "This is the statute of the Passover. No foreigner shall eat of it.

**44** But every man's slave, bought with money — when you have circumcised him, then he may eat of it.

**45** A resident and a hired man shall not eat of it.

**46** It shall be eaten in one house. You shall not take any of the meat outside the house, and you shall not break a bone of it.

**47** The whole assembly of Israel shall keep it.

**48** When a guest lives with you and would keep the Passover to the LORD, let every male belonging to him be circumcised, and then he may come near to keep it, and he will be like a native of the land. But no uncircumcised person shall eat of it.

**49** There shall be one law for the native and for the guest living among you."

**50** All the sons of Israel did as the LORD had commanded Moses and Aaron — so they did.

**51** And it came to pass on this very day that the LORD brought the sons of Israel out of the land of Egypt by their divisions.

## Notes

- **v2 "This month is for you the head of months"** — before any instruction about the meal, the calendar is reset. The first thing given to a people who are still slaves is not freedom but a new way of counting time, and the count starts from the month they leave. Everything in their year will now be measured from this.
- **v5 "from the sheep or from the goats"** — the Hebrew word *seh* covers both, and the verse says so outright. Centuries of art and translation have made this a lamb; the text permits a kid, and Genesis 22 and the goat of the Day of Atonement both stand behind the choice.
- **v6 "between the evenings"** — *bein ha-arbayim*, two evenings, and nobody has ever been certain which two. It has been read as the time between sunset and full dark, and as the time between the sun's decline and sunset. The dispute is old and the Hebrew does not decide it.
- **v8 "with bitter herbs"** — *merorim*, from *marar*, to be bitter. It is the root of the sentence in 1:14: *and they made their lives **bitter** with hard slavery*. The taste on the table that night is the name of what they are leaving.
- **v9 "or boiled at all in water"** — the word is *bashal*, to boil, and the prohibition is emphatic. **Deuteronomy 16:7**, giving the same festival, says *u-vishalta ve-akhalta* — **you shall boil it and eat it**, the same verb, as a command. Both sentences are in the Bible. Later tradition reconciled them by widening *bashal* to mean cooking of any kind; the plainest reading of the two verses is that they disagree.
- **v11 "your waists belted, your sandals on your feet, your staff in your hand"** — the meal is eaten dressed to leave. It is the only sacrifice in the Bible with a dress code, and the code is traveling clothes.
- **v12 "against all the gods of Egypt I will carry out judgments"** — the only verse in the sequence that names what the plagues are against. Not a king and not a nation: the gods.
- **v13 "I will pass over you"** — *u-fasachti*, and this is the verb the festival is named for. Its meaning is genuinely unsettled. It can be read as *pass over*, or *skip*, or *limp*; but **Isaiah 31:5** puts it in a chain with three protective verbs — *shielding and rescuing, **pasoach** and delivering* — which suggests something closer to **standing over to protect** than to walking past. English chose *pass over* long ago, and the name of the feast in every European language follows it.
- **v13 "no blow of destruction"** — and in v23 *the destroyer*, *ha-mashchit*. The text speaks of the destroyer as something the LORD does not *let* into the houses. Who or what it is, the book does not say.
- **v22 "the blood that is in the basin"** — *ba-saf*, and *saf* means both a basin and a **threshold**. So the blood is in a bowl, or it is on the doorstep. Both readings are ancient, and either makes sense of the scene: the doorway is marked on three sides, and possibly on four.
- **v22 "none of you shall go out from the doorway of his house until morning"** — the one thing Israel must actively do that night is stay inside. The deliverance is not something they take part in. It happens over their heads while they eat standing up.
- **v26 "when your sons say to you, 'What does this service mean to you?'"** — the second instruction in three chapters to prepare an answer for a child (see 10:2). The ritual is designed from the outset around a question it expects to be asked, and the seder's four questions grow directly out of this verse and its parallels in 13:8 and 13:14.
- **v29 "the firstborn of the captive who was in the dungeon"** — in 11:5 the bottom of the scale was the slave girl behind the millstones. Here it is a prisoner in a pit. The announcement and the event name different people at the low end, and the effect is the same: the reach goes all the way down.
- **v30 "there was no house where there was not someone dead"** — the double negative is in the Hebrew, and it lands harder than a plain statement would. And *tse'aqah gedolah*, a **great cry**, is the phrase used of Israel's suffering in 2:23 and 3:7.
- **v32 "Bless me also"** — the last words Pharaoh speaks in the book. The man who asked *Who is the LORD? I do not know the LORD* (5:2) ends by asking the LORD's prophet for a blessing, in the middle of the night, with his son dead.
- **v33 "Egypt pressed hard on the people"** — *va-techezaq mitsrayim*, from *chazaq*, **to be strong**. It is the hardening verb, and here it is Egypt being strong — not to hold Israel, but to push them out the door. The word that kept them in is the word that expels them.
- **v34 "their kneading bowls bound up in their cloaks"** — *mish'arotam*, and the last time that word appeared, the frogs were in them (8:3). What Egypt's plague filled, Israel now carries out on its shoulders.
- **v36 "they stripped Egypt bare"** — *va-yenatslu*, from *natsal*, the verb for rescue. God came down *to rescue* Israel from Egypt's hand (3:8); Israel now *rescues* Egypt of its silver. The book has been setting this word against itself since the bush, and this is where the two senses meet.
- **v38 "a great mixed crowd"** — *erev rav*, from a root meaning to mix. Whoever they were, the people who leave Egypt are not all Israelites, and the text says so in the same breath as the census number. The law four verses later, *one law for the native and for the foreigner*, is written for people who are already walking with them.
- **v39 "because they were driven out"** — *gorshu*, and that is the fourth appearance of *garash* since 6:1. Unleavened bread is explained here not as a symbol but as a consequence: nobody had time to let dough rise.
- **v41 "on this very day"** — *be-etsem ha-yom ha-zeh*, and the same phrase closes the chapter at v51. *Etsem* is the word for a bone, and by extension the substance or very thing itself. The idiom means *on that exact day*, and the chapter uses it three times, fixing the date the way the first verse fixed the month.
- **v46 "you shall not break a bone of it"** — *ve-etsem lo tishberu vo*. **John 19:36** quotes this line of the Passover law about the body of Jesus on the cross: *a bone of him shall not be broken*.
- **v49 "one law for the native and for the guest"** — *torah achat*. Immediately after a rule excluding the uncircumcised, the text states the principle that the rule is not about ancestry. The door is circumcision, not birth, and once through it a guest is *like a native of the land*.
