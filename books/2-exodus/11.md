# Exodus 11

**1** And the LORD said to Moses, "One more blow I will bring on Pharaoh and on Egypt. After that he will send you away from here. When he sends you away, it will be complete — he will drive you out of here altogether.

**2** Speak now in the ears of the people, and let every man ask from his neighbor, and every woman from her neighbor, objects of silver and objects of gold."

**3** And the LORD gave the people favor in the eyes of Egypt. Also the man Moses was very great in the land of Egypt, in the eyes of Pharaoh's servants and in the eyes of the people.

**4** And Moses said, "This is what the LORD says: About midnight I am going out into the middle of Egypt.

**5** And every firstborn in the land of Egypt will die — from the firstborn of Pharaoh sitting on his throne to the firstborn of the slave girl who is behind the millstones, and every firstborn of the animals.

**6** And there will be a great cry in all the land of Egypt, such as there has never been and such as there will never be again.

**7** But against all the sons of Israel not a dog will sharpen its tongue — not against man or beast — so that you may know that the LORD makes a distinction between Egypt and Israel.

**8** And all these servants of yours will come down to me and bow to me, saying, 'Get out, you and all the people who follow you!' And after that I will go out." And he went out from Pharaoh in burning anger.

**9** And the LORD said to Moses, "Pharaoh will not listen to you — so that My wonders may be many in the land of Egypt."

**10** And Moses and Aaron did all these wonders before Pharaoh, and the LORD made Pharaoh's heart strong, and he did not send the sons of Israel out of his land.

## Notes

- **v1 "one more blow"** — *nega*, and the ordinary meaning of the word is a **touch**. It is used for a skin affliction and for a stroke of judgment, but the root is contact. After hail and locusts and darkness, the last one is named as a touch.
- **v1 "he will drive you out of here altogether"** — *garesh yegaresh*, the verb doubled for force, and it is the third time in three chapters. God promised it in 6:1 — *with a strong hand he will **drive them out***. Pharaoh did it to Moses and Aaron personally in 10:11. Here it is promised for the whole people. The king's own characteristic act becomes the mechanism of his defeat.
- **v2 "let every man ask from his neighbor"** — *re'ehu*, his **neighbor**, and the verb is *sha'al*, to ask. Older English Bibles print *borrow*, which turns the scene into a deception. The Hebrew does not say borrow. It says ask, and the next verse says they were given the things because Egypt looked on them with favor. At 3:22 only the women were told to ask; now it is everyone.
- **v3 "the man Moses was very great in the land of Egypt"** — the narrator steps outside the story for one clause to say how Moses was regarded. It is the only such assessment in the book, and it is startling: the wanted fugitive of chapter 2 is now a figure of standing in Pharaoh's own court, among *the servants* who had already turned on their king in 10:7.
- **v4 "About midnight I am going out into the middle of Egypt"** — no staff, no Aaron, no outstretched hand. Every other plague came through an instrument. This one God performs in person, and the verb He uses for it is *yatsa*, **to go out** — the same verb used throughout the book for Israel going out of Egypt. God goes out into Egypt so that Israel can go out of it.
- **v5 "from the firstborn of Pharaoh... to the firstborn of the slave girl behind the millstones"** — the top and the bottom of Egyptian society, named by where they sit: one on a throne, one behind a handmill. The slave girl is as Egyptian as the king, and the text makes a point of including her loss.
- **v6 "a great cry"** — *tse'aqah gedolah*. It is Israel's word. The sons of Israel *groaned from the labor and cried out*, and *their cry went up to God* (2:23); God said *I have heard their cry* (3:7). Egypt now makes the same sound. The book does not comment; it just uses the word again.
- **v7 "not a dog will sharpen its tongue"** — *lo yecheratz kelev leshono*, and the idiom has no clean English. *Charatz* means to cut, sharpen, decide. Something between a dog whetting its tongue and a dog so much as growling. Whatever the precise picture, the claim is total silence on Israel's side of the night while Egypt is screaming.
- **v8 "and bow to me"** — Moses says Pharaoh's officials will bow **to him**, not to God. It is the most personal thing he has said in the whole contest, and it comes immediately before the only sentence in the book that reports his temper.
- **v8 "in burning anger"** — *ba-chori af*, literally in the **burning of the nose**, which is how Hebrew says rage. This is the first time Moses is angry. It is also the last conversation: he told Pharaoh in 10:29 that he would not see his face again, and he does not.
- **v9 "so that My wonders may be many"** — the stated reason for the whole sequence, placed at the end of it rather than the beginning. The plagues were not ten attempts that failed nine times. By this account the number was the point.
