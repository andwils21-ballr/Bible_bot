# Exodus 38

## The Altar and the Basin

**1** And he made the altar of burnt offering of acacia wood — five cubits its length and five cubits its width, square, and three cubits its height.

**2** And he made its horns on its four corners; its horns were of one piece with it. And he overlaid it with bronze.

**3** And he made all the vessels of the altar — the pots and the shovels and the basins, the forks and the fire pans. All its vessels he made of bronze.

**4** And he made for the altar a grating, a network of bronze, under its ledge below, reaching halfway up.

**5** And he cast four rings at the four ends of the bronze grating, as holders for the poles.

**6** And he made the poles of acacia wood and overlaid them with bronze.

**7** And he put the poles into the rings on the sides of the altar, to carry it with them. Hollow, of boards, he made it.

**8** And he made the basin of bronze and its stand of bronze — out of the mirrors of the women who served in ranks at the entrance of the tent of meeting.

## The Court

**9** And he made the court. For the south side, facing south, the hangings of the court of fine twisted linen, a hundred cubits;

**10** their pillars twenty and their sockets twenty, of bronze; the hooks of the pillars and their bands, silver.

**11** And for the north side, a hundred cubits; their pillars twenty and their sockets twenty, of bronze; the hooks of the pillars and their bands, silver.

**12** And for the side toward the sea, hangings of fifty cubits; their pillars ten and their sockets ten; the hooks of the pillars and their bands, silver.

**13** And for the front side, toward the sunrise, fifty cubits.

**14** Hangings of fifteen cubits for the one shoulder; their pillars three and their sockets three.

**15** And for the second shoulder — on this side of the gate of the court and on that — hangings of fifteen cubits; their pillars three and their sockets three.

**16** All the hangings of the court all around were of fine twisted linen.

**17** And the sockets for the pillars were bronze; the hooks of the pillars and their bands, silver, and the overlay of their tops, silver; and they were banded with silver, all the pillars of the court.

**18** And the screen of the gate of the court was the work of an embroiderer — blue and purple and crimson and fine twisted linen — twenty cubits long, and its height, along its width, five cubits, matching the hangings of the court.

**19** And their pillars four and their sockets four, of bronze; their hooks silver, and the overlay of their tops and their bands, silver.

**20** And all the pegs for the dwelling and for the court all around were bronze.

## The Reckoning

**21** These are the accounts of the dwelling, the dwelling of the Testimony, which were counted at Moses' mouth — the service of the Levites, by the hand of Ithamar son of Aaron the priest.

**22** And Bezalel son of Uri, son of Hur, of the tribe of Judah, made everything that the LORD commanded Moses;

**23** and with him Oholiab son of Ahisamach, of the tribe of Dan — a craftsman and a designer, and an embroiderer in blue and in purple and in crimson and in fine linen.

**24** All the gold used for the work, in all the work of the holy place — the gold of the offering was twenty-nine talents and seven hundred and thirty shekels, by the shekel of the holy place.

**25** And the silver of those counted in the assembly: a hundred talents and one thousand seven hundred and seventy-five shekels, by the shekel of the holy place —

**26** a beka a head, half a shekel by the shekel of the holy place, for everyone who crossed over to those counted, from twenty years old and upward: six hundred and three thousand five hundred and fifty men.

**27** And the hundred talents of silver were for casting the sockets of the holy place and the sockets of the veil — a hundred sockets for the hundred talents, a talent to a socket.

**28** And of the thousand seven hundred and seventy-five he made hooks for the pillars, and overlaid their tops, and banded them.

**29** And the bronze of the offering was seventy talents and two thousand four hundred shekels.

**30** And with it he made the sockets for the entrance of the tent of meeting, and the bronze altar and the bronze grating that belongs to it, and all the vessels of the altar,

**31** and the sockets of the court all around and the sockets of the gate of the court, and all the pegs of the dwelling and all the pegs of the court all around.

## Notes

- **v4 "under its ledge"** — *karkob*, a word that occurs twice in the Hebrew Bible and both times of this one altar (27:5 and here). Nothing else in the language uses it, so nothing else can settle what part of the altar it names. A rim, a ledge, a projecting border partway up have all been proposed from the geometry of the verse — the grating hangs *under* it and reaches halfway. The shape is guessed from the measurement, not from the word.
- **v7 "Hollow, of boards, he made it"** — *nevuv luchot*, three words, and they are the whole description of what the altar actually is. Not a block of stone and not a solid casting: a bronze-plated wooden box with nothing inside it. And *luchot* is the same noun as the **tablets** Moses carries down the mountain in 34:29. The same word for the stone God wrote on and for the planks the fire is built on.
- **v8 "out of the mirrors of the women who served in ranks"** — *be-mar'ot ha-tsove'ot asher tsave'u*, and the verb is *tsava*, the word behind *tseva'ot*, **hosts** — the armies of Israel, the host of heaven, the title *the LORD of hosts*. It is a military word for massing and mustering, and here it is used twice in one clause of women stationed at the door of the tent. What their service was, the text does not say; the word it picks for it is the word for an army in formation. And the metal is theirs: bronze mirrors, handed over and melted, become the basin the priests wash in before they are allowed to live through the approach.
- **vv14–15 "for the one shoulder"** — *katef*, the shoulder, used for the stretch of curtain flanking the gate. Hebrew builds architecture out of anatomy without marking it as figurative: a building has shoulders and sides, and *tsela*, rib, is the word for its flank (36:25). English has kept one of these — the *shoulder* of a road — and lost the rest.
- **v21 "the accounts… which were counted"** — *pequdei… asher puqqad*, and *paqad* is one of the load-bearing verbs of the book. It is what God does at the bush and in the promise Joseph made his brothers — *paqod paqadti*, **I have surely attended to you** (3:16, and Genesis 50:24). It means to muster, to number, to call to account, to visit with attention. What is being done to this pile of metal is the same verb that is done to a nation.
- **v26 "a beka a head"** — *beqa la-gulgolet*, and *gulgolet* is the **skull**. Hebrew counts people by skulls the way English counts cattle by head. The word traveled into Aramaic and out the other side: the place John's gospel names *Golgotha* is glossed there, in Greek, as **the Place of a Skull** (John 19:17).
- **vv25–28 the numbers check** — this is the only itemized account of expenditure in the Torah, and its arithmetic closes. Six hundred and three thousand five hundred and fifty men at half a shekel each comes to 301,775 shekels. The text says a hundred talents went into the sockets and 1,775 shekels into the hooks; subtract the hooks and 300,000 shekels are left for the hundred talents. So the chapter quietly defines its own unit: **three thousand shekels to the talent**, a figure stated nowhere in the Bible and recoverable only by doing the sum. And the hundred talents are spent at exactly one talent a socket — about seventy-five pounds of silver under each upright, holding up a tent.
- **v24 gold, silver, bronze, and which is which** — the three metals sort themselves by distance from the ark. Gold is everything inside the veil and on it. Silver, the census money, is cast into the sockets the whole structure stands in — every man counted in Israel is, in metal, part of the foundation. Bronze is the outer court, the altar where the burning happens, and the pegs driven into the dirt. The further out a reader walks, the baser the metal gets, and the ordering is never stated; it is only ever built.
