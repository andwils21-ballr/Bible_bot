# Exodus 19

## A Kingdom of Priests

**1** In the third month after the sons of Israel went out of the land of Egypt, on that very day, they came to the wilderness of Sinai.

**2** And they set out from Rephidim and came to the wilderness of Sinai, and they camped in the wilderness. And Israel camped there in front of the mountain.

**3** And Moses went up to God, and the LORD called to him from the mountain, saying, "This is what you shall say to the house of Jacob and tell the sons of Israel:

**4** You yourselves have seen what I did to Egypt, and how I carried you on eagles' wings and brought you to Myself.

**5** And now, if you will truly listen to My voice and keep My covenant, then you will be My own treasure out of all the peoples — for all the earth is Mine.

**6** And you will be for Me a kingdom of priests and a holy nation. These are the words you shall speak to the sons of Israel."

**7** And Moses came and called the elders of the people, and set before them all these words that the LORD had commanded him.

**8** And all the people answered together and said, "Everything the LORD has spoken we will do." And Moses brought back the people's words to the LORD.

## Set a Boundary Around the Mountain

**9** And the LORD said to Moses, "Here — I am coming to you in the thickness of the cloud, so that the people may hear when I speak with you, and may trust you forever as well." And Moses told the people's words to the LORD.

**10** And the LORD said to Moses, "Go to the people and make them holy today and tomorrow, and let them wash their clothes.

**11** And let them be ready for the third day, because on the third day the LORD will come down before the eyes of all the people, on Mount Sinai.

**12** And you shall set a boundary for the people all around, saying, 'Watch yourselves — do not go up the mountain, and do not touch its edge. Anyone who touches the mountain shall be put to death.

**13** No hand shall touch it: he shall be stoned or shot through — animal or man, he shall not live.' When the ram's horn sounds long, they may come up the mountain."

**14** And Moses went down from the mountain to the people, and made the people holy, and they washed their clothes.

**15** And he said to the people, "Be ready for three days. Do not go near a woman."

## The Third Day

**16** And it came to pass on the third day, when it was morning, that there were thunders and lightnings, and a heavy cloud on the mountain, and the sound of a horn, very strong. And all the people in the camp trembled.

**17** And Moses brought the people out of the camp to meet God, and they took their stand at the foot of the mountain.

**18** And Mount Sinai was smoking, all of it, because the LORD had come down on it in fire. And its smoke went up like the smoke of a kiln, and the whole mountain trembled violently.

**19** And the sound of the horn went on and grew very strong. Moses would speak, and God would answer him in a voice.

**20** And the LORD came down on Mount Sinai, to the top of the mountain. And the LORD called Moses to the top of the mountain, and Moses went up.

**21** And the LORD said to Moses, "Go down, warn the people — or they will break through to the LORD to look, and many of them will fall.

**22** And the priests too, who come near to the LORD, must make themselves holy, or the LORD will break out against them."

**23** And Moses said to the LORD, "The people cannot come up Mount Sinai, because You Yourself warned us, saying, 'Set a boundary around the mountain and make it holy.'"

**24** And the LORD said to him, "Go down, and come back up — you, and Aaron with you. But the priests and the people must not break through to come up to the LORD, or He will break out against them."

**25** And Moses went down to the people, and said to them —

## Notes

- **v2 "and Israel camped there"** — the verse uses the verb twice, and changes number between them. *They camped* in the wilderness, plural, the way the book has said it at every stop since Egypt; then *Israel camped* in front of the mountain, **singular**, one subject with one verb. A crowd arrives in the wilderness and a nation arrives at the mountain, and the whole change is a letter.
- **v4 "on eagles' wings"** — *nesher*. The bird is not certainly an eagle: the word is used of a carrion bird that goes bald (Micah 1:16), which fits the griffon vulture, the great soaring scavenger of that country, better than any eagle. Both are enormous, both ride the thermals without beating a wing. The picture the Hebrew reaches for is the carrying, not the species, and English has quietly chosen the nobler bird.
- **v5 "My own treasure"** — *segullah*, and it is not a poetic word. It is the term for a king's **private** wealth, the hoard that is his personally and not the crown's, kept separate from the treasury he administers. God has just said *all the earth is Mine* — the whole estate — and then sets one people aside as the thing He owns the other way.
- **v6 "a holy nation"** — *goy qadosh*. Israel is almost always *am*, a people; *goy* is the standard word for everybody else, the nations. Here, at the moment of the covenant, the book calls Israel a *goy* — the same category as everyone it will spend the rest of the canon being distinguished from, with one adjective doing all the work.
- **v8 "Everything the LORD has spoken we will do"** — *na'aseh*. The Greek translators had a text that said more: *we will do* **and we will hear**. That fuller form is the famous one, and in the Hebrew it does not appear until 24:7. Whether the Greek anticipated it or the Hebrew dropped it, the two oldest witnesses disagree about what Israel said when it agreed.
- **v9 "in the thickness of the cloud"** — *av*, a dense mass of cloud, the word for weather that has body to it. The Greek reads *in a pillar of cloud*, importing the column that led them out of Egypt. The Hebrew is not describing a shape; it is describing opacity. God comes in the part of the cloud you cannot see into.
- **vv12–13 "do not touch its edge… no hand shall touch it"** — Hebrew has one masculine pronoun for *him* and *it*, and *har*, mountain, is masculine. *Bo*, "it", in v13 carries the same ending as *be-qatsehu*, "its edge", in v12, and the Greek uses one word, *autou*, for both. So v13 repeats v12: whoever touches the mountain dies. What makes the mountain deadly is not the rock. It is that *the LORD had come down on it in fire* (v18). God tells Moses to *make it holy* (v23), and the warning is that the people must not *break through to the LORD* (v21). Holiness passes to the place where God is: *the place you are standing on is holy ground* (3:5). The mountain carries the same danger as the holy things in the dwelling, which the Kohathites may not touch or even see, or they die (Numbers 4:15, 20).
- **v13 "when the ram's horn sounds long, they may come up"** — after two verses of absolute prohibition, the same sentence ends by granting exactly what it forbade, and nothing in the chapter explains the signal or reports it being given. The horn is *yovel* — the word that names the **Jubilee**, the fiftieth year announced by blowing it. The Greek has a different text here entirely: *when the voices and the trumpets and the cloud depart from the mountain, then they may go up.*
- **v15 "Do not go near a woman"** — God told Moses two things in vv10–11: make them holy, wash their clothes. Moses relays both in v14 and then adds a third that he was not given. The text reports the addition without comment, and every later reader who has taken this as God's requirement is quoting Moses.
- **vv16, 18 "trembled"** — *charad*, the verb for the shudder that goes through a body. In v16 it is the people who tremble; in v18 the Hebrew says **the whole mountain** trembled. The Greek says the people did, both times. One letter separates *ha-am*, the people, from *ha-har*, the mountain, and the two traditions took it different ways — leaving a real question about whether the mountain in this chapter is shaking or only the crowd at the bottom of it.
- **vv21–24 "break through… break out"** — two different verbs, aimed in opposite directions. *Haras* is what you do to a wall: tear it down, demolish it. *Parats* is to burst a breach and come pouring through. The people are warned not to demolish the boundary from below, and the danger named is that God will burst out of it from above. The barrier is described as holding back pressure on both sides.
- **v25 "and said to them —"** — the chapter stops mid-sentence. Moses goes down and opens his mouth and the verse ends; the next words in the book are God's, beginning the Ten Commandments. The speech Moses was about to give is never recorded.
