# Exodus 16

**1** They set out from Elim, and the whole assembly of the sons of Israel came to the wilderness of Sin, which is between Elim and Sinai, on the fifteenth day of the second month after they came out of the land of Egypt.

**2** The whole assembly of the sons of Israel grumbled against Moses and against Aaron in the wilderness.

**3** The sons of Israel said to them, "If only we had died by the hand of the LORD in the land of Egypt, when we sat by the pot of meat, when we ate bread until we were full — because you have brought us out into this wilderness to kill this whole assembly with hunger."

**4** The LORD said to Moses, "Here — I am raining down bread from the sky for you. The people shall go out and gather a day's portion in its day, so that I may test them, whether they will walk in My instruction or not.

**5** It will be on the sixth day that they shall prepare what they bring in, and it will be double what they gather day by day."

**6** Moses and Aaron said to all the sons of Israel, "This evening you will know that it was the LORD who brought you out of the land of Egypt,

**7** and in the morning you will see the weight of the LORD's glory, because He has heard your grumblings against the LORD. Who are we, that you should grumble against us?"

**8** Moses said, "It will happen when the LORD gives you meat to eat in the evening and bread in the morning to fill you, because the LORD has heard your grumblings against Him. Who are we? Your grumblings are not against us, but against the LORD."

**9** Moses said to Aaron, "Say to the whole assembly of the sons of Israel, 'Come near before the LORD, because He has heard your grumblings.'"

**10** And it came to pass, as Aaron was speaking to the whole assembly of the sons of Israel, that they turned toward the wilderness — and here, the glory of the LORD appeared in the cloud.

**11** The LORD spoke to Moses:

**12** "I have heard the grumblings of the sons of Israel. Speak to them: 'At twilight you will eat meat, and in the morning you will be full of bread, and you will know that I am the LORD your God.'"

## What Is It?

**13** And it came to pass in the evening that quail came up and covered the camp, and in the morning there was a layer of dew around the camp.

**14** The layer of dew went up, and here — on the face of the wilderness a fine, flaking thing, fine as frost on the ground.

**15** The sons of Israel saw it and said to one another, "What is it?" — because they did not know what it was. Moses said to them, "It is the bread that the LORD has given you to eat.

**16** This is what the LORD has commanded: gather of it, each according to what he eats, an omer a head, according to the number of your people. Each of you shall take for those in his tent."

**17** The sons of Israel did so, and they gathered — some more, some less.

**18** They measured it by the omer, and the one who gathered much had nothing over, and the one who gathered little had no lack. Each had gathered according to what he ate.

**19** Moses said to them, "Let no one leave any of it until morning."

**20** But they did not listen to Moses, and some men left part of it until morning — and it bred worms and stank. Moses was angry with them.

**21** They gathered it morning by morning, each according to what he ate. When the sun grew hot, it melted.

## The Seventh Day

**22** And it came to pass on the sixth day that they gathered double bread, two omers for each person. All the chiefs of the assembly came and told Moses.

**23** He said to them, "This is what the LORD spoke: tomorrow is a rest, a holy sabbath to the LORD. Bake what you will bake and boil what you will boil, and all that is left over set aside for yourselves, to keep until morning."

**24** They set it aside until morning, as Moses had commanded, and it did not stink, and there was no worm in it.

**25** Moses said, "Eat it today, because today is a sabbath to the LORD. Today you will not find it in the field.

**26** Six days you shall gather it, and on the seventh day, the sabbath, there will be none."

**27** And it came to pass on the seventh day that some of the people went out to gather, and they found none.

**28** The LORD said to Moses, "How long will you refuse to keep My commands and My instructions?

**29** See that the LORD has given you the sabbath — that is why He gives you bread for two days on the sixth day. Each of you stay where you are. Let no one go out from his place on the seventh day."

**30** The people rested on the seventh day.

**31** The house of Israel called its name manna. It was like coriander seed, white, and its taste was like a wafer with honey.

**32** Moses said, "This is what the LORD has commanded: a full omer of it is to be kept through your generations, so that they may see the bread I fed you in the wilderness when I brought you out of the land of Egypt."

**33** Moses said to Aaron, "Take one jar and put a full omer of manna in it, and set it down before the LORD, to be kept through your generations."

**34** As the LORD commanded Moses, Aaron set it down before the Testimony, to be kept.

**35** The sons of Israel ate the manna forty years, until they came to an inhabited land. They ate the manna until they came to the edge of the land of Canaan.

**36** The omer is a tenth of an ephah.

## Notes

- **v2 "grumbled"** — the Hebrew here carries a **ketiv/qere**: the consonants as written are one form of the verb, and the reading tradition supplies another. The scribes kept both rather than choose. It is the first of many complaints in the wilderness, and the text cannot settle how to spell it.
- **v3 "when we sat by the pot of meat"** — one pot, and slaves remembering it as plenty. The complaint is not that Egypt was good; it is that hunger is worse than slavery. Two chapters ago they preferred serving Egypt to dying at the sea (14:12). The argument has not changed, only the threat.
- **v4 "so that I may test them, whether they will walk in My instruction"** — *torah*, before there is any law. The first test is not obedience to a command but whether people can take one day's food and not hoard.
- **v7 "the weight of the LORD's glory"** — *kevod*, from *kaved*, **heavy**. It is the root of Pharaoh's heavy heart and of God's *getting weight* on Pharaoh at the sea (14:4). Here it is what the people will see in a cloud. The same three letters have now carried stubbornness, honor, and visible presence.
- **v14 "a fine, flaking thing"** — *daq mechuspas*, and *mechuspas* occurs **nowhere else in the Hebrew Bible**. Nothing can be compared with it. Flaky, scaly, granular, peeled have all been proposed from the shape of the root and from the ancient translations. The word describing what the food looked like is a word nobody can define.
- **v15 "What is it?"** — *man hu*. That is the question, and in v31 it becomes the name: *they called its name **manna***, in Hebrew simply *man*. The thing they ate for forty years is called What-Is-It. *Manna* is the Greek form of the word (Numbers 11:6), and English took it from there, which is why the English name loses the joke: a nation was fed on a question.
- **v18 "the one who gathered much had nothing over, and the one who gathered little had no lack"** — **2 Corinthians 8:15** quotes this verse exactly, in an appeal for a collection: *as it is written, the one who had much did not have too much, and the one who had little did not have too little.* Paul is using the manna as an argument about money.
- **v20 "it bred worms and stank"** — *va-yiv'ash*, the stink verb again. The Nile stank with dead fish (7:21), the land stank with dead frogs (8:14), and Israel's foremen accused Moses of making them stink (5:21). Hoarded bread joins the list.
- **v21 "when the sun grew hot, it melted"** — the food has a deadline at both ends. It cannot be gathered late, and it cannot be kept. Everything about it is designed to prevent storage.
- **v23 "a rest, a holy sabbath to the LORD"** — **the sabbath is commanded here, before Sinai**, and it is not introduced as new. Moses says *this is what the LORD spoke*, as though the people should already know. The commandment at 20:8 will say *remember*.
- **v29 "let no one go out from his place"** — the rule is given without explanation and became the basis for a whole body of later law about how far one may walk on the sabbath. The verse itself just says stay put.
- **v31 "like coriander seed, white, and its taste was like a wafer with honey"** — the only description of it. Numbers 11:8 will say it tasted of oil-cake instead. The book that records both does not reconcile them.
- **v33 "one jar"** — *tsintsenet*, another word that occurs nowhere else. A jar of an unknown kind, holding an unmeasurable food, kept in front of a Testimony that has not yet been given.
- **v34 "before the Testimony"** — the ark does not exist yet; it is built in chapter 37. The verse is written by someone standing later, placing the jar where it will eventually go.
- **v36 "the omer is a tenth of an ephah"** — an editor's footnote inside the verse. By the time this sentence was added, readers no longer knew how much an omer was, and someone converted it into a measure they did know.
