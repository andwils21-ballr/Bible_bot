# Exodus 14

**1** And the LORD spoke to Moses:

**2** "Speak to the sons of Israel, and let them turn back and camp before Pi-hahiroth, between Migdol and the sea, before Baal-zephon. Opposite it you shall camp, by the sea.

**3** And Pharaoh will say of the sons of Israel, 'They are wandering in confusion in the land. The wilderness has closed in on them.'

**4** And I will make Pharaoh's heart strong, and he will chase after them, and I will get My weight on Pharaoh and on all his army, and Egypt will know that I am the LORD." And they did so.

**5** And the king of Egypt was told that the people had fled, and the heart of Pharaoh and his servants turned against the people, and they said, "What is this we have done, sending Israel away from serving us?"

**6** And he harnessed his chariot and took his people with him.

**7** And he took six hundred chosen chariots, and all the chariots of Egypt, and officers over all of them.

**8** And the LORD made the heart of Pharaoh king of Egypt strong, and he chased after the sons of Israel — and the sons of Israel were marching out in open defiance.

**9** And Egypt chased after them and overtook them camped by the sea — all Pharaoh's horses and chariots and his horsemen and his army — at Pi-hahiroth, before Baal-zephon.

## Stand Still and See

**10** And Pharaoh drew near, and the sons of Israel lifted their eyes, and here — Egypt marching after them. And they were very afraid, and the sons of Israel cried out to the LORD.

**11** And they said to Moses, "Was it for lack of graves in Egypt that you took us away to die in the wilderness? What is this you have done to us, taking us out of Egypt?

**12** Is this not what we said to you in Egypt: 'Leave us alone and let us serve Egypt'? For it would be better for us to serve Egypt than to die in the wilderness."

**13** And Moses said to the people, "Do not be afraid. Stand still and see the deliverance of the LORD, which He will work for you today — for the Egyptians you see today, you will never see them again.

**14** The LORD will fight for you, and you — be silent."

**15** And the LORD said to Moses, "What are you crying out to Me for? Speak to the sons of Israel, and let them move on.

**16** And you — lift up your staff and stretch out your hand over the sea and split it, and the sons of Israel will come into the middle of the sea on dry ground.

**17** And I — here, I am making the heart of Egypt strong, and they will come in after them. And I will get My weight on Pharaoh and on all his army, on his chariots and on his horsemen.

**18** And Egypt will know that I am the LORD, when I get My weight on Pharaoh, on his chariots and on his horsemen."

## The Sea

**19** And the messenger of God who was going before the camp of Israel moved and went behind them, and the pillar of cloud moved from before them and stood behind them.

**20** And it came between the camp of Egypt and the camp of Israel. On Egypt's side it was cloud and darkness; on Israel's it lit up the night — and neither camp came near the other all night.

**21** And Moses stretched out his hand over the sea, and the LORD drove the sea back with a strong east wind all night, and made the sea into dry ground, and the waters were split.

**22** And the sons of Israel came into the middle of the sea on dry ground, and the waters were a wall for them on their right and on their left.

**23** And Egypt chased and came in after them — all Pharaoh's horses, his chariots and his horsemen — into the middle of the sea.

**24** And it came to pass in the morning watch that the LORD looked down on the camp of Egypt through the pillar of fire and cloud, and threw the camp of Egypt into panic.

**25** And He bound the wheels of their chariots, and they dragged along heavily. And the Egyptians said, "Let us flee from Israel, because the LORD is fighting for them against Egypt."

**26** And the LORD said to Moses, "Stretch out your hand over the sea, and let the waters come back over Egypt, over its chariots and over its horsemen."

**27** And Moses stretched out his hand over the sea, and at daybreak the sea came back to its full flow. The Egyptians fled — straight into it — and the LORD shook the Egyptians off into the middle of the sea.

**28** And the waters came back and covered the chariots and the horsemen, all of Pharaoh's army that had come after them into the sea. Not one of them was left.

**29** But the sons of Israel had walked on dry ground in the middle of the sea, with the waters a wall for them on their right and on their left.

**30** And the LORD saved Israel that day from the hand of Egypt, and Israel saw the Egyptians lying dead on the seashore.

**31** And Israel saw the great hand that the LORD had worked against Egypt, and the people feared the LORD, and they believed in the LORD and in Moses His servant.

## Notes

- **v4 "I will get My weight on Pharaoh"** — *ikkavdah*, and this is the root the book has been building all along: *kaved*, **heavy**. It is the word for Pharaoh's **heavy** heart (7:14), for Moses' **heavy** mouth (4:10), for the **heavy** hail and the **heavy** plague (9:18, 9:3). Here the same root means **glory** — because in Hebrew, glory is weight, what has substance and cannot be brushed aside. The word that described Pharaoh's stubbornness now describes God's honor, taken *on* him. English needs two unrelated words; Hebrew uses one and the whole book turns on it.
- **v5 "the heart of Pharaoh and his servants turned"** — *va-yehafekh*, to overturn. Not hardened this time. Flipped. And the reason given is economic in the plainest terms: *we have lost our workforce*.
- **v8 "in open defiance"** — *be-yad ramah*, literally **with a high hand**: a hand raised, not hidden. Numbers 33:3 uses the phrase for this same departure and adds *in the sight of all Egypt*; Numbers 15:30 uses it for a sin done deliberately, in the open. Egypt thinks they *fled* (v5); the narrator says they marched.
- **v11 "Was it for lack of graves in Egypt?"** — the first complaint in the wilderness, and it is sarcasm. Egypt is the land of tombs; it is what Egypt is famous for. They are telling Moses there was no shortage of places to be buried back there.
- **v12 "it would be better for us to serve Egypt"** — *la'avod*, the same verb as serving the LORD. The argument of the whole book, stated by the people in one sentence: they are not choosing between slavery and freedom, but between masters, and they would rather have the one that fed them.
- **v14 "and you — be silent"** — *tacharishun*, from *charash*, to be silent, also to be deaf. It is the only instruction Israel is given at the sea, and it is to do nothing and say nothing.
- **v16 "and split it"** — *u-veqa'ehu*, from *baqa*. It is the flood's verb: in Genesis 7:11 *all the fountains of the great deep **were split open***, and the water came up to drown the world. Here the same verb opens water to save a people. The one time the Bible uses *baqa* of a sea before this, everyone died.
- **v20 "cloud and darkness... it lit up the night"** — the Hebrew says only *and there was the cloud and the darkness, and it lit up the night*, without saying which camp had which. The pillar had just moved behind Israel (v19), and by night it was fire (13:21), so the light fell on Israel's side and the dark on Egypt's. The Greek reads *darkness and gloom, and the night passed*, with no light at all.
- **v21 "a strong east wind all night"** — *ruach qadim*, and it is the wind from 10:13, which blew *all that day and all that night* to bring the locusts. Same wind, same duration, opposite service. Also worth hearing: *ruach* is breath, wind and spirit, and the sea is opened by the same word that hovers over the water in Genesis 1:2.
- **vv21–22 "dry ground"** — two words, and both reach back to Genesis. *Charavah*, the dry ground the sea is turned into (v21), is where everything died in the flood: *all that was on the dry ground died* (Genesis 7:22). *Yabbashah*, the dry ground Israel walks on (v22, v29), is the word of the third day of creation: *let the waters be gathered, and let the dry ground appear* (Genesis 1:9). The crossing is told in the vocabulary of the world's making and of its drowning.
- **v22 "the waters were a wall"** — *chomah*, a city wall. Not a bank or a heap: the word for fortification. Israel walks through a corridor with defensive walls on both sides, and the thing defending them is the sea.
- **v24 "the morning watch"** — the last third of the night, before dawn. The whole crossing happens in the dark, and the destruction comes at the moment it becomes possible to see.
- **v25 "He bound the wheels"** — the Hebrew is *va-yasar*, He **removed** the wheels; the Greek has He **bound** them, a reading one letter away in Hebrew.
- **v25 "dragged along heavily"** — *bi-khvedut*, from *kaved* again. The Egyptians are now the ones moving under weight. The root has run from Pharaoh's heart, to God's glory in v4, to the dragging of the chariots — three turns of one word inside twenty verses.
- **v27 "back to its full flow"** — *le-eitano*, from *eitan*, the word for a stream that never runs dry (Deuteronomy 21:4; Amos 5:24, *an ever-flowing stream*). The sea returns to what it always is. **Shook off** is *va-yena'er*, the verb for shaking out a garment (Nehemiah 5:13).
- **v30 "the LORD saved Israel"** — *va-yosha*, from *yasha*, the root behind the names Joshua and Jesus. Moses first used it for seven Midianite women at a well (2:17). Here it is used of a nation, and v13 had already promised it as a noun: *the **deliverance** of the LORD*, *yeshu'at YHWH*.
- **v30 "Israel saw the Egyptians lying dead"** — Moses had promised, *the Egyptians you see today, you will never see them again* (v13). The last time Israel sees them is here, on the shore.
- **v31 "they believed in the LORD and in Moses His servant"** — the book's first statement that the people believe. It comes after the rescue, not before it, and it names Moses alongside God. In chapter 4 the question was whether they would believe him at all.
