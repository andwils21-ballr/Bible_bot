# Exodus 30

## The Altar of Incense

**1** "And you shall make an altar for burning incense. Of acacia wood you shall make it.

**2** A cubit its length and a cubit its width — square it shall be — and two cubits its height. Its horns shall be of one piece with it.

**3** And you shall overlay it with pure gold: its top and its sides all around, and its horns. And you shall make for it a molding of gold all around.

**4** And two rings of gold you shall make for it, beneath its molding, on its two sides — you shall make them on its two flanks — as holders for the poles to carry it with.

**5** And you shall make the poles of acacia wood and overlay them with gold.

**6** And you shall set it in front of the veil that is by the ark of the Testimony, in front of the cover that is over the Testimony, where I will meet with you.

**7** And Aaron shall burn fragrant incense on it. Morning by morning, when he tends the lamps, he shall burn it.

**8** And when Aaron sets up the lamps between the evenings, he shall burn it — incense continually before the LORD, for your generations.

**9** You shall not offer on it strange incense, or a burnt offering, or a grain offering; and you shall not pour a drink offering on it.

**10** And Aaron shall make atonement on its horns once a year. With the blood of the sin offering of atonement, once a year, he shall make atonement on it, for your generations. It is most holy to the LORD."

## A Ransom for His Life

**11** And the LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**12** "When you take a head count of the sons of Israel, to number them, then each shall give a ransom for his life to the LORD when they are numbered — so there will be no plague among them when they are numbered.

**13** This they shall give, everyone who passes over to those counted: half a shekel by the shekel of the sanctuary — the shekel is twenty gerahs — half a shekel as a contribution to the LORD.

**14** Everyone who passes over to those counted, from twenty years old and upward, shall give the contribution of the LORD.

**15** The rich shall not give more, and the poor shall not give less, than the half shekel, when giving the contribution of the LORD to make atonement for your lives.

**16** And you shall take the atonement silver from the sons of Israel and give it for the service of the tent of meeting. And it shall be a remembrance for the sons of Israel before the LORD, to make atonement for your lives."

## The Basin

**17** And the LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**18** "And you shall make a basin of bronze, and its stand of bronze, for washing. And you shall set it between the tent of meeting and the altar, and put water there.

**19** And Aaron and his sons shall wash their hands and their feet from it.

**20** When they come into the tent of meeting they shall wash with water, and they shall not die — or when they come near the altar to serve, to turn an offering by fire into smoke for the LORD.

**21** And they shall wash their hands and their feet, and they shall not die. And it shall be a lasting statute for them, for him and for his seed, through their generations."

## The Anointing Oil

**22** And the LORD spoke to Moses, saying,

**23** "And you — take for yourself the finest spices: five hundred of liquid myrrh, and half as much fragrant cinnamon, two hundred and fifty, and two hundred and fifty of fragrant cane,

**24** and five hundred of cassia, by the shekel of the sanctuary, and a hin of olive oil.

**25** And you shall make of it a holy anointing oil, a blended compound, the work of a perfumer. A holy anointing oil it shall be.

**26** And with it you shall anoint the tent of meeting and the ark of the Testimony,

**27** and the table and all its vessels, and the lampstand and its vessels, and the altar of incense,

**28** and the altar of burnt offering and all its vessels, and the basin and its stand.

**29** And you shall set them apart, and they shall be most holy. Whatever touches them shall become holy.

**30** And Aaron and his sons you shall anoint, and set them apart to serve Me as priests.

**31** And to the sons of Israel you shall say: 'This shall be a holy anointing oil to Me through your generations.

**32** On a person's flesh it shall not be poured, and you shall not make any like it in its proportions. It is holy; holy it shall be to you.

**33** Anyone who blends any like it, or who puts any of it on an outsider, shall be cut off from his people.'"

## The Incense

**34** And the LORD said to Moses, "Take for yourself spices — stacte and onycha and galbanum — spices, and pure frankincense. Each shall be in equal measure.

**35** And you shall make of it incense, a compound, the work of a perfumer, salted, pure, holy.

**36** And you shall grind some of it fine, and put some of it before the Testimony in the tent of meeting, where I will meet with you. It shall be most holy to you.

**37** And the incense you make — you shall not make any in its proportions for yourselves. It shall be holy to you for the LORD.

**38** Anyone who makes any like it, to smell it, shall be cut off from his people."

## Notes

- **v9 "strange incense"** — *qetoret zarah*, and the adjective is the one that comes back to kill. In Leviticus 10:1 the two men named first in 28:1 as the sons being ordained — **Nadab and Abihu** — take their fire pans, put incense on them, and bring near before the LORD *esh zarah*, **strange fire**, *which He had not commanded*. Same word, same altar, two chapters after they are dressed for the job. The prohibition is written here, in a verse about equipment, before either of them has done anything.
- **vv7–8 "morning by morning… between the evenings"** — the incense is tied to the lamps: it is burned when Aaron trims them at dawn and when he lights them at dusk. It is not a separate rite with its own hours. Twice a day someone crosses the room to do the wicks, and the smoke goes up while he is in there anyway.
- **v12 "when you take a head count"** — *ki tissa et rosh*, literally **when you lift the head of** the sons of Israel, which is the ordinary Hebrew for taking a census. And the law attached to it is startling: each man must pay *kofer nafsho*, **a ransom for his life**, *so there will be no plague among them when they are numbered*. Counting people is treated as dangerous in itself, and the danger is stated without explanation. The tax is not a fee for the count; it is protection from it.
- **v15 "the rich shall not give more, and the poor shall not give less"** — the ransom is a flat half shekel and it is forbidden to pay differently in either direction. A wealthy man may not buy a larger share of the atonement, and a poor man's is not smaller. It is the one payment in the Torah where the amount is fixed against the payer's means in both directions.
- **v16 "the atonement silver… for the service of the tent"** — the money does not disappear into a treasury. It is spent on the building, and 38:27 will account for it: the silver sockets the whole frame stands in are cast from this. The tabernacle's foundations are the ransom money of the men who were counted.
- **vv20–21 "and they shall not die"** — said twice in two verses, about washing hands and feet. The same sentence closed chapter 28 about the linen undergarments. Three times now the instruction for a garment or a basin ends by naming the alternative, and the alternative is always death. Nothing in these chapters treats proximity to the sanctuary as safe.
- **vv23–25, 34–35 two recipes, given in full** — the anointing oil and the incense are the only formulas in the Torah written out with quantities: myrrh, cinnamon, fragrant cane, cassia and olive oil for the one; stacte, onycha, galbanum and frankincense for the other. Several of the ingredients cannot be identified with confidence — *shecheleth*, onycha, is usually taken as the operculum of a sea snail, and *nataf*, stacte, means only **a drop**. The proportions are exact and the substances are half-unknown.
- **vv32–33, 37–38 the recipes are capital secrets** — both close the same way: make any in these proportions for yourself, and you are **cut off from your people**. And v38 names the offence precisely — anyone who makes any like it *to smell it*. Not to sell, not to worship with: to enjoy. The penalty attaches to private pleasure in a smell.
- **v25 "the work of a perfumer"** — *ma'aseh roqeach*, the same construction as the designer and the embroiderer in chapter 26. The sanctuary's trades are named one by one: weaver, designer, embroiderer, stone-cutter, goldsmith, perfumer. Every one of them is called *wise* in 28:3.
- **v35 "salted"** — *memullach*, from *melach*, salt. What a salted incense is, nobody can say: proposals run from literally salted, to well-mixed, to seasoned in the sense of blended, and the Hebrew gives no help. It sits in a list of three adjectives — salted, pure, holy — where the other two are plain.
