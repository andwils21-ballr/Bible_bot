# Exodus 6

**1** The LORD said to Moses, "Now you will see what I will do to Pharaoh. For with a strong hand he will send them away, and with a strong hand he will drive them out of his land."

**2** God spoke to Moses and said to him, "I am the LORD.

**3** I appeared to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob as God Almighty — and by My name, the LORD, I was not known to them.

**4** I also established My covenant with them, to give them the land of Canaan, the land where they lived as guests.

**5** I have also heard the groaning of the sons of Israel, whom the Egyptians are enslaving, and I have remembered My covenant.

**6** Therefore say to the sons of Israel: I am the LORD. I will bring you out from under the burdens of Egypt, and I will rescue you from their slavery, and I will redeem you with an outstretched arm and with great acts of judgment.

**7** I will take you to Myself as a people, and I will be God to you. You will know that I am the LORD your God, who brings you out from under the burdens of Egypt.

**8** I will bring you to the land that I raised My hand to give to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob, and I will give it to you as a possession. I am the LORD."

**9** Moses spoke this to the sons of Israel, and they did not listen to Moses, out of shortness of breath and hard slavery.

**10** The LORD spoke to Moses:

**11** "Go, speak to Pharaoh king of Egypt, and let him send the sons of Israel out of his land."

**12** Moses spoke before the LORD, "Look — the sons of Israel have not listened to me. How then will Pharaoh listen to me? My lips are still as covered as if they were uncircumcised."

**13** The LORD spoke to Moses and to Aaron and gave them a charge concerning the sons of Israel and concerning Pharaoh king of Egypt, to bring the sons of Israel out of the land of Egypt.

## These Are the Heads of Their Fathers' Houses

**14** These are the heads of their fathers' houses. The sons of Reuben, Israel's firstborn: Hanoch and Pallu, Hezron and Carmi. These are the families of Reuben.

**15** The sons of Simeon: Jemuel and Jamin and Ohad and Jachin and Zohar, and Shaul the son of the Canaanite woman. These are the families of Simeon.

**16** These are the names of the sons of Levi, by their generations: Gershon and Kohath and Merari. The years of the life of Levi were a hundred and thirty-seven years.

**17** The sons of Gershon: Libni and Shimei, by their families.

**18** The sons of Kohath: Amram and Izhar and Hebron and Uzziel. The years of the life of Kohath were a hundred and thirty-three years.

**19** The sons of Merari: Mahli and Mushi. These are the families of the Levites by their generations.

**20** Amram took Jochebed his father's sister as his wife, and she bore him Aaron and Moses and Miriam their sister. The years of the life of Amram were a hundred and thirty-seven years.

**21** The sons of Izhar: Korah and Nepheg and Zichri.

**22** The sons of Uzziel: Mishael and Elzaphan and Sithri.

**23** Aaron took Elisheba, daughter of Amminadab, sister of Nahshon, as his wife, and she bore him Nadab and Abihu, Eleazar and Ithamar.

**24** The sons of Korah: Assir and Elkanah and Abiasaph. These are the families of the Korahites.

**25** Eleazar son of Aaron took one of the daughters of Putiel as his wife, and she bore him Phinehas. These are the heads of the fathers' houses of the Levites, by their families.

**26** This is the Aaron and Moses to whom the LORD said, "Bring the sons of Israel out of the land of Egypt by their divisions."

**27** They are the ones who spoke to Pharaoh king of Egypt, to bring the sons of Israel out of Egypt. This is the Moses and Aaron.

**28** And it came to pass on the day the LORD spoke to Moses in the land of Egypt,

**29** that the LORD spoke to Moses: "I am the LORD. Speak to Pharaoh king of Egypt everything I say to you."

**30** Moses said before the LORD, "Look — my lips are still as covered as if they were uncircumcised. How then will Pharaoh listen to me?"

## Notes

- **v1 "with a strong hand he will send them away"** — *be-yad chazakah*. At the bush God said Pharaoh would not let them go *not unless by a strong hand* (3:19), and the Hebrew left it open whose hand that was. Here the sentence reads most naturally as **Pharaoh's**: the strong hand is on him, and it makes him not merely release them but *drive them out*. The last plague ends exactly that way.
- **v3 "by My name, the LORD, I was not known to them"** — the hardest verse in the chapter, because Genesis contradicts it on its face. God says *I am the LORD* to Abraham in Genesis 15:7, and Genesis 4:26 says that already in Adam's grandson's day *men began to call on the name of the LORD*. The Hebrew here is a passive, *lo noda'ti*, **I was not made known** — which is not quite the same as saying they never heard the syllables. Readers have taken it three ways: that the name was spoken but its meaning was not yet demonstrated; that the clause is a question — *and by My name was I not known to them?*; or that the verse comes from a strand of the tradition that told it differently. The Hebrew allows all three. It does not settle it.
- **vv6–7 "I will bring out... rescue... redeem... take"** — four promises, each a separate Hebrew verb, each beginning *I will*: *ve-hotseti*, *ve-hitsalti*, *ve-ga'alti*, *ve-laqachti*. They are the backbone of the Passover seder, where the four cups are counted off against these four verbs. Whatever the ritual's age, the four are really there, in a row, in this verse.
- **v6 "I will rescue you"** — *ve-hitsalti*, from *natsal* — the exact verb Moses threw back at God in the last line of chapter 5: *and rescue, You have not rescued*. God's first word in reply uses it. The two chapters are one conversation.
- **v6 "with an outstretched arm"** — *bi-zroa netuyah*. Egyptian royal art shows the pharaoh with arm raised to strike; the phrase puts that image on someone else. It becomes a fixed expression for the exodus and is still being quoted centuries later.
- **v9 "out of shortness of breath"** — *mi-qotser ruach*. *Ruach* is breath, wind, spirit. The phrase can mean impatience, or it can mean what it plainly says: people worked past the point of being able to take in a sentence. Given *and hard slavery* right beside it, the physical reading is the likelier one.
- **v12 "as if they were uncircumcised"** — *aral sefatayim*. In chapter 4 Moses said he was **heavy of mouth**; now he says something different. *Aral* is the circumcision word, appearing two chapters after Zipporah's flint knife — the same word used of fruit not yet fit to eat in Leviticus 19:23. It marks something closed off, not yet fit for use. He is not repeating the old complaint. He has found a worse one.
- **vv14–25 the genealogy stops at Levi** — it begins dutifully with Reuben and Simeon, the first two sons, and then reaches Levi, the third, and simply stays there. Everyone after that is Levi's line. The list is not a census; it exists to place Moses and Aaron, and it abandons the other nine tribes the moment it has done so.
- **v20 "and Miriam their sister"** — the Hebrew names only *Aaron and Moses*; the Greek adds *and Miriam their sister*, and Numbers 26:59 gives all three as Amram and Jochebed's children.
- **v20 "Amram took Jochebed his father's sister"** — his aunt. Leviticus 18:12 forbids exactly this: *you shall not uncover the nakedness of your father's sister*. The text records the parentage of Moses without comment, and the law that would later prohibit it is in the same five books.
- **v23 "Elisheba, daughter of Amminadab, sister of Nahshon"** — Aaron's wife is given three identifiers where most wives here get none. Nahshon is the chief of Judah, and the line runs to David. The priesthood and the kingship are married into each other in one verse.
- **v26 "This is the Aaron and Moses"... v27 "This is the Moses and Aaron"** — the names are given in one order, then the other, in consecutive verses. The Hebrew is deliberate: the speaking is credited to them jointly, and the text refuses to settle which brother goes first.
- **v30 repeats v12** — the genealogy interrupts a conversation, and when it ends, the text picks the conversation up by restating Moses' objection word for word. The seam is visible, and nobody smoothed it.
