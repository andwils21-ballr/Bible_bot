# Exodus 9

**1** And the LORD said to Moses, "Go to Pharaoh and speak to him: This is what the LORD, the God of the Hebrews, says: Send My people away, so they may serve Me.

**2** For if you refuse to send them away, and you go on holding them,

**3** here — the hand of the LORD is coming against your livestock that are in the field: against the horses, the donkeys, the camels, the herd and the flock — a very heavy plague.

**4** And the LORD will make a distinction between the livestock of Israel and the livestock of Egypt, and not one thing belonging to the sons of Israel will die."

**5** And the LORD set an appointed time, saying, "Tomorrow the LORD will do this thing in the land."

**6** And the LORD did this thing the next day, and all the livestock of Egypt died, and of the livestock of the sons of Israel not one died.

**7** And Pharaoh sent, and here — not so much as one of Israel's livestock had died. And Pharaoh's heart was heavy, and he did not send the people away.

## Soot from the Kiln

**8** And the LORD said to Moses and to Aaron, "Take for yourselves handfuls of soot from a kiln, and let Moses throw it toward the sky before the eyes of Pharaoh.

**9** And it will become fine dust over all the land of Egypt, and it will become boils breaking out in blisters on man and on beast in all the land of Egypt."

**10** And they took the soot of the kiln and stood before Pharaoh, and Moses threw it toward the sky, and it became boils, blisters breaking out on man and on beast.

**11** And the magicians could not stand before Moses because of the boils, for the boils were on the magicians and on all Egypt.

**12** And the LORD made Pharaoh's heart strong, and he did not listen to them, just as the LORD had said to Moses.

## Hail and Fire

**13** And the LORD said to Moses, "Rise early in the morning and station yourself before Pharaoh and say to him: This is what the LORD, the God of the Hebrews, says: Send My people away, so they may serve Me.

**14** For this time I am sending all My blows to your heart, and against your servants and against your people — so that you may know that there is none like Me in all the earth.

**15** For by now I could have stretched out My hand and struck you and your people with plague, and you would have been wiped from the earth.

**16** But for this very reason I have kept you standing: to show you My power, and so that My name may be told in all the earth.

**17** You are still lording it over My people, refusing to send them away.

**18** Here — about this time tomorrow I am raining down very heavy hail, such as there has never been in Egypt from the day it was founded until now.

**19** And now, send word — bring your livestock into shelter, and everything you have in the field. Every man and beast found in the field and not gathered into the house — the hail will come down on them and they will die."

**20** Whoever among Pharaoh's servants feared the word of the LORD made his servants and his livestock flee into the houses.

**21** And whoever did not set his heart to the word of the LORD left his servants and his livestock in the field.

**22** And the LORD said to Moses, "Stretch out your hand toward the sky, and there will be hail in all the land of Egypt — on man and on beast and on every plant of the field in the land of Egypt."

**23** And Moses stretched out his staff toward the sky, and the LORD gave thunder and hail, and fire went down to the earth. And the LORD rained hail on the land of Egypt.

**24** And there was hail, and fire flashing in the middle of the hail, very heavy — such as there had never been in all the land of Egypt since it became a nation.

**25** And the hail struck, in all the land of Egypt, everything that was in the field, from man to beast. And the hail struck every plant of the field and shattered every tree of the field.

**26** Only in the land of Goshen, where the sons of Israel were, there was no hail.

## The LORD Is the Righteous One

**27** And Pharaoh sent and called for Moses and Aaron and said to them, "I have sinned this time. The LORD is the righteous one, and I and my people are the guilty ones.

**28** Plead with the LORD — there has been enough of God's thunder and hail — and I will send you away, and you will not go on standing here."

**29** And Moses said to him, "As I go out of the city I will spread out my hands to the LORD. The thunder will stop and the hail will be no more, so that you may know that the earth belongs to the LORD.

**30** But as for you and your servants, I know that you do not yet fear the LORD God."

**31** And the flax and the barley were struck, because the barley was in the ear and the flax was in bud.

**32** And the wheat and the spelt were not struck, because they are late.

**33** And Moses went out of the city, away from Pharaoh, and spread out his hands to the LORD, and the thunder and the hail stopped, and rain no longer poured on the earth.

**34** And Pharaoh saw that the rain and the hail and the thunder had stopped, and he went on sinning, and he made his heart heavy — he and his servants.

**35** And Pharaoh's heart was strong, and he did not send the sons of Israel away, just as the LORD had said through Moses.

## Notes

- **v3 "the hand of the LORD is coming"** — the Hebrew word is *hoyah*, a participle built on *hayah*, **to be**, and this spelling of it occurs nowhere else in the Bible. It is the verb behind the name given at the bush. What is announced as coming against the herds is, in the letters, a form of *I will be*.
- **v3 "a very heavy plague"** — *kaved me'od*. Watch where the weight goes in this chapter: a **heavy** plague here, **heavy** hail in v18 and v24, and Pharaoh's **heavy** heart in v7 and v34. The same adjective is on the man and on the weather.
- **v6 "all the livestock of Egypt died"** — and then there are Egyptian livestock again in v19, and horses at the sea in chapter 14. The Hebrew *kol* need not be exhaustive, and v3 had already limited the plague to the animals *in the field*. Still, the sentence as written says all, and the tension is in the text rather than in the translation.
- **v8 "soot from a kiln"** — *piach kivshan*. The *kivshan* is the furnace where bricks are fired. Israel has been making bricks since chapter 1 and scrounging stubble to fire them since chapter 5. Moses takes a handful of the waste from that process and throws it in the air, and it settles on Egypt as a disease. The instrument of the slavery becomes the plague.
- **v11 "the magicians could not stand before Moses"** — their second defeat, and worse than the first. At the gnats they could not perform (8:18). Now they cannot even be in the room. The professionals who opened the contest by matching Aaron staff for staff are written out of it here and never appear again.
- **v12 "the LORD made Pharaoh's heart strong"** — **the first time in the book that God is stated as the one doing it.** Up to now the text has said only that the heart *was* strong (7:13, 7:22, 8:19) or *was* heavy (9:7), with no subject named, or has said outright that Pharaoh did it himself (8:15, 8:32). God announced in 7:3 that He would. This verse is where He is finally the grammatical subject of the act — after five refusals Pharaoh made on his own.
- **v16 "I have kept you standing"** — *he'emadtikha*, from *amad*, to stand. Not *raised you up* in the sense of creating a man for a purpose, but *kept you in place* — left you on your feet. Paul quotes this verse in **Romans 9:17** and builds an argument about election on it; the Greek he quotes uses a stronger verb, *exēgeira*, I raised you up.
- **v17 "you are still lording it over My people"** — *mistolel*, and this word appears nowhere else in the Bible. It is built on a root for a raised road or heaped-up embankment, so the sense is something like *making yourself high against*. Its precise force is unrecoverable; the shape of it is clear enough.
- **v23 "the LORD gave thunder"** — Hebrew has no separate word for thunder. It says *qolot*, **voices**. So what the LORD gives is voices, and Pharaoh in v28 asks for an end to *the voices of God*. English has to choose one or the other; the Hebrew hears them as the same thing.
- **v24 "fire flashing in the middle of the hail"** — *mitlaqachat*, a reflexive form: fire **taking hold of itself**. Ice and fire in one weather system, and the Hebrew describes the fire as self-feeding inside it.
- **v27 "I have sinned this time. The LORD is the righteous one"** — the fullest confession any Egyptian makes, and it uses the legal vocabulary exactly: *tsaddiq* is the one in the right in a dispute, *resha'im* the ones in the wrong. Pharaoh concedes the verdict. Six verses later he reverses it, and the text notes that his servants joined him this time.
- **vv31–32 "the barley was in the ear and the flax was in bud"** — a farmer's aside, and the only thing in the plague sequence that dates it. Barley heads out and flax buds in the same few weeks; wheat and spelt ripen later and were still low enough to survive. The detail also names the season: the barley is *aviv*, and *aviv* is what the month of the exodus is called in 13:4.
- **v34 "he and his servants"** — the first time the hardening is plural. In v20 some of Pharaoh's servants had feared the word of the LORD and brought their animals in. By the end of the chapter that distinction has closed, and the court is with him.
