# Exodus 20

## The Ten Words

**1** And God spoke all these words, saying:

**2** "I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of slaves.

**3** You shall have no other gods before My face.

**4** You shall not make for yourself a carved image, or any form of what is in the heavens above, or on the earth below, or in the waters under the earth.

**5** You shall not bow down to them and you shall not serve them — because I, the LORD your God, am a jealous God, calling the guilt of fathers to account on sons, on the third and on the fourth generation of those who hate Me,

**6** and doing loyal love to thousands, for those who love Me and keep My commandments.

**7** You shall not lift up the name of the LORD your God for emptiness, because the LORD will not leave unpunished the one who lifts up His name for emptiness.

**8** Remember the sabbath day, to keep it holy.

**9** Six days you shall work and do all your labor,

**10** and the seventh day is a sabbath to the LORD your God. You shall not do any labor — you, or your son, or your daughter, your male slave or your female slave, or your animal, or the foreigner who is inside your gates.

**11** For in six days the LORD made the heavens and the earth, the sea and everything in them, and He rested on the seventh day. Therefore the LORD blessed the sabbath day and made it holy.

**12** Honor your father and your mother, so that your days may be long on the ground that the LORD your God is giving you.

**13** You shall not murder.

**14** You shall not commit adultery.

**15** You shall not steal.

**16** You shall not answer against your neighbor as a false witness.

**17** You shall not covet your neighbor's house. You shall not covet your neighbor's wife, or his male slave, or his female slave, or his ox, or his donkey, or anything that is your neighbor's."

## Do Not Let God Speak With Us

**18** And all the people were seeing the thunders and the torches and the sound of the horn and the mountain smoking. And the people saw, and they shook, and they stood far off.

**19** And they said to Moses, "You speak with us, and we will listen. But do not let God speak with us, or we will die."

**20** And Moses said to the people, "Do not be afraid, because God has come in order to test you, and so that the fear of Him will be on your faces, so that you do not sin."

**21** And the people stood far off, and Moses drew near to the thick darkness where God was.

## An Altar of Earth

**22** And the LORD said to Moses, "This is what you shall say to the sons of Israel: You yourselves have seen that I spoke with you from the heavens.

**23** You shall not make gods of silver alongside Me, and gods of gold you shall not make for yourselves.

**24** An altar of earth you shall make for Me, and you shall sacrifice on it your burnt offerings and your peace offerings, your flock and your herd. In every place where I cause My name to be remembered, I will come to you and bless you.

**25** And if you make Me an altar of stones, you shall not build them cut — because you have waved your sword over it and profaned it.

**26** And you shall not go up by steps onto My altar, so that your nakedness is not uncovered on it."

## Notes

- **v1 "all these words"** — the text does not call them commandments. It calls them *devarim*, **words**, and 34:28 names them exactly: *the ten words*, which the Greek renders *tous deka logous* — the source of *Decalogue*. English gained "the Ten Commandments" from the Latin tradition; neither original language says *command* anywhere in the title.
- **v3 "before My face"** — *al panai*, and the preposition is genuinely open. It can mean *in My presence*, *in preference to Me*, *in front of Me* as a rival stands in front of a rival, or simply *besides Me*. Each reading forbids something slightly different, and the Hebrew does not choose. The Greek chose: *except Me*.
- **v5 "a jealous God… calling to account"** — *qanna* is the word for a husband's jealousy over his wife, not for envy of a stranger's goods; it is possessive, and it belongs to a relationship that already exists. And *poqed* is the wide verb from Genesis 21:1, where the LORD *attended to* Sarah — to muster, to number, to call in what is owed. Guilt is being counted, the way a commander counts troops.
- **v6 "loyal love to thousands"** — the two halves of the sentence are not the same size. Guilt reaches the third and fourth generation; *chesed* reaches **thousands** — and the Hebrew does not say thousands of what, so it may be thousands of people or thousands of generations. Either way the asymmetry is the point, and it is deliberate enough that Deuteronomy repeats it word for word.
- **v7 "lift up the name… for emptiness"** — *nasa*, to pick up and carry, the same verb as carrying a load or bearing guilt. This is not primarily about swearing in the sense of cursing. It is about **taking the name up onto yourself** — swearing by it, invoking it, acting in it — when what you are carrying it for is *shav*: emptiness, worthlessness, a thing with nothing inside it. An oath kept is the name well carried; an oath broken is the name carried for nothing.
- **v12 "Honor"** — *kabbed*, the causative of **heavy**. Literally: *make your father and mother weigh something*. The same root has run through Exodus as Pharaoh's hardened heart, the LORD's glory, Moses' tired hands, and the burden of judging that was too heavy for one man. Here it becomes a command, and its opposite in Hebrew is not disrespect but *qalal*, to treat as light.
- **v13 "murder"** — *ratsach*, and it is not the general word for killing. Hebrew has *harag* and *hemit* for that, and uses them for war, for execution, for God putting someone to death. *Ratsach* is the word the law uses for the killings that send a man to a city of refuge — unlawful killing, including by accident. The commandment is narrower than "do not kill" and the language marks the difference.
- **vv13–15 the order is not fixed** — the Hebrew runs murder, adultery, theft. **The Greek Old Testament runs adultery, theft, murder.** This is not a scribal slip; it is how the oldest translation of Exodus reads. And when the New Testament quotes the list — Luke 18:20, Romans 13:9, James 2:11 — **adultery comes first every time**, following the Greek against the Hebrew. Three writers in three different books, all reciting the Ten Commandments in an order most English readers have never heard.
- **v17 the last commandment is longer in Greek** — the Hebrew forbids coveting the house first and the wife second. The Greek reverses them, putting the **wife first**, and adds a possession the Hebrew does not list: *his field*. Deuteronomy's Hebrew version also puts the wife first. The three witnesses agree on what is forbidden and disagree on what comes first in a man's inventory.
- **v18 "the people were seeing the thunders"** — *qolot* means thunders and voices with the same word, and the verb is **seeing**. The Greek did not tidy it either: *the people saw the voice*. Both traditions kept a sentence in which sound is something you look at.
- **v25 "you have waved your sword over it"** — the word is *cherev*, **sword**, not chisel, and it is the ordinary word for the weapon. The reason dressed stone profanes an altar is given in the tool's name: the thing that shapes the block is the thing that kills a man. An altar is to be made of earth, or of stones nobody has touched with a blade.
