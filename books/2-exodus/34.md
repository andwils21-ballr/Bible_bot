# Exodus 34

## The Second Tablets

**1** The LORD said to Moses, "Cut for yourself two tablets of stone like the first ones, and I will write on the tablets the words that were on the first tablets, which you shattered.

**2** Be ready in the morning, and come up in the morning to Mount Sinai, and stand there for Me on the top of the mountain.

**3** No one shall come up with you, and no one shall be seen anywhere on the mountain; and the flocks and the herds shall not graze in front of that mountain."

**4** He cut two tablets of stone like the first ones. Moses rose early in the morning and went up Mount Sinai, as the LORD had commanded him, and took the two tablets of stone in his hand.

**5** The LORD came down in the cloud and stood with him there, and called out the name of the LORD.

**6** The LORD passed before his face and called out: "The LORD, the LORD — God merciful and gracious, slow to anger, and abundant in loyal love and truth,

**7** keeping loyal love for thousands, carrying guilt and rebellion and sin — and not leaving it unpunished, calling the guilt of fathers to account on sons and on sons' sons, on the third and on the fourth generation."

**8** Moses hurried and bowed to the ground and knelt.

**9** He said, "If now I have found favor in Your eyes, my Lord, let my Lord go among us — because it is a stiff-necked people — and forgive our guilt and our sin, and take us as Your own."

## The Words of the Covenant

**10** He said, "Here — I am cutting a covenant. In front of all your people I will do wonders that have not been created in all the earth or in any nation. All the people among whom you are will see the work of the LORD, because it is a fearful thing that I am doing with you.

**11** Keep what I am commanding you today. Here — I am driving out from before you the Amorite and the Canaanite and the Hittite and the Perizzite and the Hivite and the Jebusite.

**12** Watch yourself, or you will cut a covenant with the inhabitants of the land you are coming into, and it will become a snare among you.

**13** You shall tear down their altars and shatter their standing stones and cut down their sacred poles.

**14** For you shall not bow down to another god — because the LORD, whose name is Jealous, is a jealous God.

**15** Or you will cut a covenant with the inhabitants of the land, and they will whore after their gods and sacrifice to their gods, and one will invite you and you will eat of his sacrifice.

**16** You will take some of his daughters for your sons, and his daughters will whore after their gods and make your sons whore after their gods.

**17** You shall not make molten gods for yourself.

**18** The Festival of Unleavened Bread you shall keep. Seven days you shall eat unleavened bread, as I commanded you, at the appointed time of the month of Abib, because in the month of Abib you came out of Egypt.

**19** Everything that first opens the womb is Mine, and all your livestock that bears a male — the firstling of ox and sheep.

**20** The firstling of a donkey you shall redeem with a lamb, and if you do not redeem it, you shall break its neck. Every firstborn of your sons you shall redeem. They shall not appear before My face empty-handed.

**21** Six days you shall work, and on the seventh day you shall stop. In plowing and in harvest you shall stop.

**22** The Festival of Weeks you shall make for yourself, the firstfruits of the wheat harvest; and the Festival of the Gathering at the turn of the year.

**23** Three times in the year all your males shall appear before the face of the Lord, the LORD, God of Israel.

**24** For I will dispossess nations before you and widen your border, and no one will covet your land when you go up to appear before the face of the LORD your God three times in the year.

**25** You shall not slaughter the blood of My sacrifice with anything leavened, and the sacrifice of the festival of Passover shall not stay the night until morning.

**26** The first of the firstfruits of your ground you shall bring to the house of the LORD your God. You shall not boil a young goat in its mother's milk.

**27** The LORD said to Moses, "Write down these words — because by these words I have cut a covenant with you and with Israel."

**28** He was there with the LORD forty days and forty nights. He ate no bread and drank no water. He wrote on the tablets the words of the covenant, the ten words.

## The Skin of His Face

**29** And it came to pass, when Moses came down from Mount Sinai — and the two tablets of the Testimony were in Moses' hand as he came down from the mountain — that Moses did not know that the skin of his face had grown horns from speaking with Him.

**30** Aaron and all the sons of Israel saw Moses, and here — the skin of his face had grown horns. They were afraid to come near him.

**31** Moses called to them, and Aaron and all the leaders of the assembly came back to him, and Moses spoke to them.

**32** Afterward all the sons of Israel came near, and he commanded them everything the LORD had spoken with him on Mount Sinai.

**33** Moses finished speaking with them, and he put a veil over his face.

**34** When Moses came in before the LORD to speak with Him, he would take the veil off until he came out. He would come out and tell the sons of Israel what he had been commanded.

**35** The sons of Israel would see Moses' face, that the skin of Moses' face had grown horns. Moses would put the veil back over his face until he came in to speak with Him.

## Notes

- **v1 "Cut for yourself two tablets"** — the first set were *the work of God, and the writing was the writing of God* (32:16). This time the stone is Moses' own work; he quarries and shapes them and carries them up. The replacement covenant is written on material a man made.
- **v6 "slow to anger"** — *erekh appayim*, literally **long of nostrils**. In Hebrew anger lives in the nose: *charah af*, the burning of the nostril, is what happens to Moses in 32:19 and to God throughout. A long nose is one that takes a long time to flare. The English idiom is a temperature; the Hebrew is a distance.
- **vv6–7 the formula, and the sentence that breaks it** — this list of thirteen words is quoted back at God across the rest of the Hebrew Bible more than any other sentence in the Torah. And it does not end where a reader expects. *Carrying guilt and rebellion and sin* — the verb is *nasa*, the load verb Aaron carries guilt with in 28:38 — and then immediately: *ve-naqqeh lo yenaqqeh*, **and clearing, He will not clear**. The two halves are not reconciled. Mercy is stated in the fullest terms available and then, in the same breath, limited, and the text offers no formula for how both hold.
- **v9 Moses turns the reason inside out** — in 33:3 God gave the refusal and its grounds: *I will not go up among you, because you are a stiff-necked people.* Here Moses asks for the opposite using the identical clause: *let my Lord go among us — **because** it is a stiff-necked people.* Same three words, same causal particle, reversed. What God gave as a reason to stay away, Moses gives as the reason to come.
- **v10 "wonders"** — *nifla'ot*, from *pala*, the root Moses used one chapter earlier when he said that God going with them is what would make Israel *marked out* from every people (33:16). The answer to that argument is given in the same root: distinctness was the request, and wonders are what is promised.
- **v13 "their sacred poles"** — *asherim*, wooden objects standing for the goddess Asherah, and the Hebrew names her. English Bibles print "groves" (following the Greek and the Latin) or "Asherah poles"; what the word is, exactly — a living tree, a carved pole, an image — the text never describes.
- **vv19–20 the redemption clause is here** — at 22:29 the law said flatly *the firstborn of your sons you shall give to Me* with nothing about buying them back. Here the same law is given with the clause attached: a donkey is redeemed with a lamb or its neck is broken, and *every firstborn of your sons you shall redeem*. The book carries both versions and this is the one with a way out.
- **v21 "In plowing and in harvest you shall stop"** — the sabbath law is given a second time with one clause added, and it names the two weeks of the year when stopping costs most. A farmer can rest in a slack season without it meaning anything. The verse specifies the days when it does.
- **vv27–28 who wrote the second tablets** — v1 has God say *I will write on the tablets*. v27 has God tell Moses *write down these words*. Then v28: *and **he** wrote on the tablets the words of the covenant, the ten words*, with no subject named. The Hebrew leaves it open, and the two instructions in the same chapter point different ways. This rendering keeps the pronoun as the Hebrew has it.
- **vv29, 30, 35 "the skin of his face had grown horns"** — *qaran or panav*, and the verb is built on *qeren*, **horn**. It is used of an animal's horns everywhere else in the Bible; this is the only place it is a verb, and the only place it is said of a face. Light radiating in beams is the reading behind "shone," and it is a guess from the shape of a horn rather than from any other use of the word. The Latin translated it *cornuta*, horned, which is why Michelangelo's Moses has horns. The Hebrew says horn and says nothing else.
- **vv33–35 the veil** — *masveh*, a noun that occurs in the Bible only in these three verses. And the sequence is the reverse of what a reader expects: he takes it **off** to speak with God and puts it **on** to speak with Israel. Paul reads the passage this way in Greek — *not like Moses, who put a covering over his face so that the sons of Israel would not look at the end of what was fading* (2 Corinthians 3:13) — but Exodus never says the shining faded, and never says why the veil was worn.
