# Exodus 24

## All the Words We Will Do

**1** And to Moses He said, "Go up to the LORD — you and Aaron, Nadab and Abihu, and seventy of the elders of Israel — and bow down from a distance.

**2** And Moses alone shall come near to the LORD. They shall not come near, and the people shall not go up with him."

**3** And Moses came and recounted to the people all the words of the LORD and all the rulings. And all the people answered with one voice and said, "All the words the LORD has spoken we will do."

**4** And Moses wrote down all the words of the LORD. And he rose early in the morning and built an altar at the foot of the mountain, and twelve standing stones for the twelve tribes of Israel.

**5** And he sent the young men of the sons of Israel, and they offered burnt offerings and sacrificed bulls as peace offerings to the LORD.

**6** And Moses took half the blood and put it in basins, and half the blood he threw against the altar.

**7** And he took the book of the covenant and read it in the ears of the people. And they said, "All that the LORD has spoken we will do, and we will listen."

**8** And Moses took the blood and threw it on the people, and said, "Here — the blood of the covenant that the LORD has cut with you on all these terms."

## They Ate and They Drank

**9** And Moses went up, and Aaron, Nadab and Abihu, and seventy of the elders of Israel.

**10** And they saw the God of Israel, and under His feet was something like brickwork of sapphire, like the very body of the sky for purity.

**11** And on the nobles of the sons of Israel He did not lay a hand. And they looked upon God, and they ate and they drank.

## Forty Days in the Cloud

**12** And the LORD said to Moses, "Come up to Me on the mountain, and be there, and I will give you the tablets of stone — the instruction and the commandment — which I have written to teach them."

**13** And Moses rose, and Joshua his attendant, and Moses went up to the mountain of God.

**14** And to the elders he said, "Stay here for us until we return to you. And here — Aaron and Hur are with you. Whoever is a master of words, let him go to them."

**15** And Moses went up the mountain, and the cloud covered the mountain.

**16** And the weight of the LORD's glory settled on Mount Sinai, and the cloud covered it six days. And on the seventh day He called to Moses from inside the cloud.

**17** And the look of the LORD's glory was like a consuming fire on the top of the mountain, before the eyes of the sons of Israel.

**18** And Moses went into the middle of the cloud and went up the mountain. And Moses was on the mountain forty days and forty nights.

## Notes

- **vv3, 7 "we will do" … "we will do, and we will listen"** — the people answer twice, and the second answer is longer by one word. In v3 it is *na'aseh*, **we will do**. In v7, after the book has been read out, it is *na'aseh ve-nishma*, **we will do and we will hear** — agreement first, understanding second, in that order. That fuller form is the famous one. It is also what the **Greek** already has back at 19:8, where the Hebrew has only *we will do*: one of the two traditions puts the full pledge at the start of the Sinai account, the other holds it back until the terms have actually been read.
- **v4 "twelve standing stones"** — *matzevot*, and one chapter ago, at 23:24, the same word carried a command: *you shall utterly break their standing stones*. The pillar is the standard object of Canaanite worship, and Moses sets up twelve of them at the foot of the mountain on the morning the covenant is ratified. The text neither defends this nor notices it.
- **v5 "he sent the young men"** — *na'arei*, not priests. Aaron is standing right there and is not the one who offers. The priesthood does not exist yet; it is legislated four chapters later. The sacrifices that seal the covenant are made by unnamed young men.
- **vv6, 8 the blood is divided** — half into basins, half thrown against the altar, and then the basins' half thrown **on the people**. The altar stands for one party and the crowd is the other, and the same blood goes onto both. Nothing else in the Torah does this; blood is normally put on an altar, an object, or a priest, never flung over a congregation.
- **v8 "the blood of the covenant"** — *dam ha-berit*, which the Greek renders *to haima tēs diathēkēs*. At the last supper, over the cup, Matthew has Jesus say *τοῦτο γάρ ἐστιν τὸ αἷμά μου τῆς διαθήκης* — **this is my blood of the covenant** (26:28). The phrase is this verse's, word for word in Greek, with one word added.
- **v10 "brickwork of sapphire"** — *livnat ha-sappir*, and *livnah* is the ordinary word for a **brick**: the thing Israel was making in Egypt with mortar and straw in 1:14 and 5:7. The pavement under God's feet is described with the word for the item these men spent their lives producing as slaves, now made of precious stone. (The stone itself is *sappir*, which in this period most likely names **lapis lazuli** rather than what a jeweller today calls sapphire; the identification cannot be settled from the language.)
- **v10 "like the very body of the sky"** — *ke-etzem ha-shamayim*, and *etzem* is **bone**. Hebrew uses it for the substance of a thing, the thing itself — the same idiom behind *on that very day*. What is under His feet is the sky's own bone, for clearness. The verse describes the floor and says nothing whatever about what they saw above it.
- **v11 "He did not lay a hand on them"** — *lo shalach yado*, and the Hebrew is an idiom, not a gesture: to **send the hand** against someone is to strike them. It is the phrase God uses at Genesis 22:12, *do not lay a hand on the boy*. So the verse is not reporting that God kept still; it is reporting that He did **not strike them** — which takes for granted that seeing Him should have been fatal, and says so only by denying it. Then seventy-four men have a meal on the mountain. In 33:20 God will tell Moses that no one can see His face and live; this chapter says these men saw Him and ate.
- **v11 "the nobles"** — *atzilei*, and the word is genuinely unsettled. It occurs three times in the Hebrew Bible and means something different in each: the **joints of the arm** at Ezekiel 13:18, the **far corners** of the earth at Isaiah 41:9, and here, a group of men. What ties them is the root *atzal*, to **set aside, reserve** — the verb God uses in Numbers 11:17 when He takes some of the spirit that is on Moses and reserves it for these same seventy elders. The men are named in v9; the noun for them means the ones set apart.
- **v14 "a master of words"** — *ba'al devarim*, literally a **master of words**, and in Hebrew that is not a talker: it is the party to a lawsuit, the man who has a matter to argue. The sentence means *anyone with a dispute, take it to them*. Moses leaves the court running while he is gone, with Aaron holding it. The next time Aaron is asked to handle the people on his own, in chapter 32, it goes differently.
- **v16 "six days… and on the seventh day He called"** — the cloud covers the mountain for six days and the voice comes on the seventh. The pattern is the creation week's, and nothing in the verse points at it; the shape is simply there, at the moment the plan for the sanctuary is about to be given.
