# Exodus 10

**1** And the LORD said to Moses, "Go to Pharaoh, for I Myself have made his heart heavy and the heart of his servants, so that I may set these signs of Mine among them,

**2** and so that you may tell in the ears of your son and your son's son what I did in Egypt, and My signs that I set among them — and you will know that I am the LORD."

**3** And Moses and Aaron came to Pharaoh and said to him, "This is what the LORD, the God of the Hebrews, says: How long will you refuse to humble yourself before Me? Send My people away, so they may serve Me.

**4** For if you refuse to send My people away, here — tomorrow I am bringing locusts into your territory.

**5** And they will cover the eye of the land, and no one will be able to see the land. And they will eat what is left of what escaped — what the hail left you — and they will eat every tree that is growing for you out of the field.

**6** And your houses will be full, and the houses of all your servants and the houses of all Egypt — such as your fathers and your fathers' fathers have not seen, from the day they were on the ground until this day." And he turned and went out from Pharaoh.

**7** And Pharaoh's servants said to him, "How long will this man be a snare to us? Send the men away, and let them serve the LORD their God. Do you not yet know that Egypt is destroyed?"

**8** And Moses and Aaron were brought back to Pharaoh, and he said to them, "Go, serve the LORD your God. Who and who are going?"

**9** And Moses said, "With our young and with our old we will go. With our sons and with our daughters, with our flocks and with our herds we will go, because it is a feast of the LORD for us."

**10** And he said to them, "May the LORD be with you the way I send you and your little ones away! Look — you have evil in front of your faces.

**11** Not so. Go, the men among you, and serve the LORD — that is what you are asking for." And he drove them out from Pharaoh's presence.

## The Locusts

**12** And the LORD said to Moses, "Stretch out your hand over the land of Egypt for the locusts, and let them come up over the land of Egypt and eat every plant of the land, everything the hail left."

**13** And Moses stretched out his staff over the land of Egypt, and the LORD drove an east wind through the land all that day and all that night. Morning came, and the east wind had carried the locusts.

**14** And the locusts came up over all the land of Egypt and settled in all the territory of Egypt — very heavy. Before them there had never been locusts like them, and after them there will not be.

**15** And they covered the eye of the whole land, and the land went dark. And they ate every plant of the land and all the fruit of the tree that the hail had left, and nothing green was left on the tree or on the plants of the field in all the land of Egypt.

**16** And Pharaoh hurried to call for Moses and Aaron and said, "I have sinned against the LORD your God and against you.

**17** And now, lift my sin just this once, and plead with the LORD your God, and let Him only take this death away from me."

**18** And he went out from Pharaoh and pleaded with the LORD.

**19** And the LORD turned the wind around — a very strong sea wind — and it carried the locusts and drove them into the Sea of Reeds. Not one locust was left in all the territory of Egypt.

**20** And the LORD made Pharaoh's heart strong, and he did not send the sons of Israel away.

## Darkness

**21** And the LORD said to Moses, "Stretch out your hand toward the sky, and there will be darkness over the land of Egypt — and the darkness will be felt."

**22** And Moses stretched out his hand toward the sky, and there was thick darkness in all the land of Egypt for three days.

**23** No one saw his brother, and no one got up from where he was for three days. But for all the sons of Israel there was light in their dwellings.

**24** And Pharaoh called to Moses and said, "Go, serve the LORD. Only your flocks and your herds are to be left behind. Your little ones may go with you too."

**25** And Moses said, "You yourself must also put sacrifices and burnt offerings in our hands, and we will make them for the LORD our God.

**26** And our livestock will go with us too — not a hoof will be left behind — because we will take from them to serve the LORD our God, and we do not know how we are to serve the LORD until we get there."

**27** And the LORD made Pharaoh's heart strong, and he was not willing to send them away.

**28** And Pharaoh said to him, "Get away from me! Watch yourself — do not see my face again, because on the day you see my face you will die."

**29** And Moses said, "You have spoken rightly. I will not see your face again."

## Notes

- **v1 "I Myself have made his heart heavy and the heart of his servants"** — God states it outright, and includes the court. The narrator had already reported the plural at 9:34, where Pharaoh made his heart heavy *he and his servants*. Both accounts are in the book; neither is dropped.
- **v2 "what I did in Egypt"** — the Hebrew verb is *hit'allalti*, from *alal*, and it is not a neutral word. It means to deal harshly with, to make a mockery of, to toy with someone. English translations soften it, and the softening is understandable, but the verb the text chose is the one you would use of a stronger party having its way with a weaker one.
- **v2 "tell in the ears of your son and your son's son"** — the first command in the book to **tell the story**, and it arrives before the story has finished happening. The plagues are being staged, the text says here, partly so there will be something to recite. Everything the Passover liturgy later does is authorised by this verse.
- **v3 "refuse to humble yourself"** — *le'anot*, from *anah*, to be bowed down or afflicted. It is the root of *oni*, the **affliction** God says He has seen in 3:7. Pharaoh is being asked to accept the very thing he has been imposing, in the same word.
- **v5 "they will cover the eye of the land"** — *ein ha-arets*, the **eye** of the land. Hebrew uses *ayin* for the visible surface of a thing. The idiom does double work here, because what the locusts take away is precisely the ability to see the ground.
- **v7 "How long will this man be a snare to us?"** — Pharaoh's own officials turn first, and the word they use, *moqesh*, is a bird trap. Then: *Do you not yet know that Egypt is destroyed?* They use the same *not yet* Moses used to the king's face in 9:30. The court has begun speaking Moses' language.
- **v8 "Who and who are going?"** — *mi va-mi*, the interrogative doubled. It is blunt, almost rude, and it is the sound of a negotiation being reopened on worse terms.
- **v10 "you have evil in front of your faces"** — the Hebrew is short and menacing, and it will not resolve. *Ra'ah* means evil or harm; it can be a warning about what lies ahead of them, an accusation about what they intend, or a threat about what he will do. All three readings fit the words.
- **v11 "and he drove them out"** — *va-yegaresh*, and this is the verb from 6:1, where God said Pharaoh would **drive them out** of his land with a strong hand. Pharaoh performs the word here in miniature, on two men, thinking he is winning.
- **v13 "an east wind... all that day and all that night"** — *ruach qadim*. Hold this one: at the sea in 14:21 the LORD drives the water back *with a strong **east wind** all night* — the same wind, the same duration, the same word. The wind that brings the locusts in is the wind that opens the sea.
- **v19 "a very strong sea wind... into the Sea of Reeds"** — and then the wind reverses and dumps the locusts in *Yam Suf*. The place where Egypt's army will drown is named here first, and what drowns in it first is insects.
- **v15 "the land went dark"** — *va-techshakh ha-arets*. The locusts blacken the sky, and that is the last thing the ninth plague needs; the next one simply removes the light. The text moves from a darkness made of bodies to a darkness made of nothing.
- **v21 "the darkness will be felt"** — *ve-yamesh choshekh*, from a root for groping with the hands. Darkness thick enough to touch. And *choshekh* is the word lying on the face of the deep in Genesis 1:2, before God says *let there be light*. The plague is a piece of the world put back the way it was before the first day.
- **v23 "no one got up from where he was"** — three days without seeing a face, without standing up. It is the only plague with no physical damage at all: nothing is killed, nothing is eaten, nothing is broken. Egypt is simply stopped.
- **v26 "not a hoof will be left behind"** — *lo tisha'er parsah*. Pharaoh has been bargaining piece by piece — the men only, then the children too, now everything but the animals. Moses answers with the smallest unit he can name.
- **v26 "we do not know how we are to serve the LORD until we get there"** — the honest sentence in the middle of the argument. Moses is not withholding a plan. There is no plan yet. The instructions for worship do not exist until Sinai.
