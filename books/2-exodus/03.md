# Exodus 3

**1** And Moses was shepherding the flock of Jethro his father-in-law, the priest of Midian. And he led the flock to the far side of the wilderness and came to the mountain of God, to Horeb.

**2** And the messenger of the LORD appeared to him in a flame of fire from the middle of a bush. And he looked, and here — the bush was burning with fire, and the bush was not consumed.

**3** And Moses said, "Let me turn aside now and see this great sight — why the bush does not burn up."

**4** And the LORD saw that he had turned aside to see, and God called to him from the middle of the bush and said, "Moses, Moses." And he said, "Here I am."

**5** And He said, "Do not come near here. Take your sandals off your feet, for the place you are standing on is holy ground."

**6** And He said, "I am the God of your father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob." And Moses hid his face, for he was afraid to look at God.

## I Have Seen, I Have Seen

**7** And the LORD said, "I have seen, I have seen the affliction of My people who are in Egypt, and I have heard their cry because of their slave drivers, for I know their pains.

**8** And I have come down to rescue them from the hand of Egypt and to bring them up from that land to a good and wide land, to a land flowing with milk and honey — to the place of the Canaanite and the Hittite and the Amorite and the Perizzite and the Hivite and the Jebusite.

**9** And now, here — the cry of the sons of Israel has come to Me, and I have also seen the crushing with which Egypt is crushing them.

**10** And now come, and I will send you to Pharaoh, and bring out My people, the sons of Israel, from Egypt."

**11** And Moses said to God, "Who am I, that I should go to Pharaoh and that I should bring out the sons of Israel from Egypt?"

**12** And He said, "Because I will be with you. And this is the sign for you that I Myself have sent you: when you bring the people out from Egypt, you will serve God on this mountain."

## What Is His Name?

**13** And Moses said to God, "Here I am, going to the sons of Israel, and I will say to them, 'The God of your fathers has sent me to you,' and they will say to me, 'What is His name?' What shall I say to them?"

**14** And God said to Moses, "I WILL BE WHAT I WILL BE." And He said, "This is what you shall say to the sons of Israel: I WILL BE has sent me to you."

**15** And God said further to Moses, "This is what you shall say to the sons of Israel: the LORD, the God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you. This is My name forever, and this is My remembrance for all generations.

**16** Go and gather the elders of Israel and say to them, 'The LORD, the God of your fathers, appeared to me — the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob — saying: I have surely attended to you and to what is being done to you in Egypt.

**17** And I have said: I will bring you up out of the affliction of Egypt to the land of the Canaanite and the Hittite and the Amorite and the Perizzite and the Hivite and the Jebusite, to a land flowing with milk and honey.'

**18** And they will listen to your voice. And you shall come, you and the elders of Israel, to the king of Egypt, and you shall say to him, 'The LORD, the God of the Hebrews, has met with us. And now let us go, please, a three days' journey into the wilderness, and let us sacrifice to the LORD our God.'

**19** And I Myself know that the king of Egypt will not let you go — not unless by a strong hand.

**20** And I will stretch out My hand and strike Egypt with all My wonders that I will do in its midst, and after that he will send you out.

**21** And I will give this people favor in the eyes of Egypt, and it will come to pass that when you go, you will not go empty-handed.

**22** And every woman will ask of her neighbor and of the woman staying in her house for objects of silver and objects of gold and clothing, and you will put them on your sons and on your daughters — and you will strip Egypt bare."

## Notes

- **v1 "to the mountain of God, to Horeb"** — the narrator calls it the mountain of God before anything has happened there. Moses is out looking for grazing and walks into a place the storyteller already knows the name of.
- **v2 "a bush"** — *seneh*. Outside this chapter the word appears as a common noun once in the whole Hebrew Bible, in Moses' own blessing at the end of Deuteronomy: *the favor of the One who dwells in the **bush*** (Deuteronomy 33:16). And it is one letter from *Sinai*. The Hebrew never says the two words are related; it just puts God in a *seneh* on the mountain that becomes *Sinai*.
- **v2 "the messenger of the LORD"** — *mal'akh YHWH*. The messenger appears in v2 and then is not mentioned again: by v4 it is the LORD who sees and God who calls, out of the same bush. The text slides from one to the other without explaining.
- **v4 "Moses, Moses"** — the doubled name is how this book calls someone at the moment everything changes. It happened to Abraham at the binding of Isaac (Genesis 22:11) and to Jacob leaving for Egypt (Genesis 46:2), and both answered exactly as Moses does here: *hinneni*, **here I am**.
- **v5 "holy ground"** — *admat qodesh*. Up to this point in the Bible God has made **time** holy — the seventh day (Genesis 2:3) — but never a place. This is the first holy ground, and it is a patch of wilderness in Midian with a shepherd standing on it, not a temple.
- **v7 "I have seen, I have seen"** — *ra'oh ra'iti*. Hebrew doubles a verb with its own infinitive to press it down hard, and there is no neat English for it. The point is that God is answering the end of chapter 2: *God saw*, *God knew*, and now He says so out loud, three times over — seen, heard, known.
- **v8 "a land flowing with milk and honey"** — the first of twenty-one times this phrase is used in the Bible, and it is spoken here before Israel has any land at all. Milk means herds that graze, honey means fruit trees. It describes country you can live off without irrigating it — which is to say, the opposite of Egypt.
- **v8 "to rescue them"** — *le-hatsilo*, from *natsal*. Keep the verb in mind: it comes back in the last verse of the chapter, pointed the other way.
- **v11 "Who am I"** — Moses' first objection is not about Pharaoh or the people; it is about himself. He asks who he is. God does not answer the question — the reply in v12 is about who **God** is.
- **v12 "this is the sign for you... you will serve God on this mountain"** — a sign is normally proof given in advance. This one can only be checked after the thing it was supposed to prove has already happened. Moses is being told to go first and find out afterward that he was sent.
- **v14 "I WILL BE WHAT I WILL BE"** — *ehyeh asher ehyeh*, three words built on *hayah*, **to be**. *Ehyeh* is first person, and it is not a present tense: Hebrew has no present tense for this verb. It can be read *I am who I am*, *I will be what I will be*, or *I will be whatever I choose to be*, and nothing in the grammar settles it. Two verses earlier God told Moses *ki **ehyeh** imakh* — **I will be with you**. The name He gives is the same word as the promise He just made.
- **v15 "the LORD"** — the Hebrew here is the four letters *YHWH*, built from the same verb *to be* as *ehyeh* in v14, in the third person: **He is** or **He will be**. Jewish practice from antiquity does not pronounce it, and reads *Adonai*, **my Lord**, instead — which is why English Bibles print LORD in capitals. Where you see LORD in this rendering, the Hebrew has the four letters.
- **v16 "I have surely attended to you"** — *paqod paqadti*, another doubled verb. It is the exact phrase Joseph used on his deathbed at the end of Genesis: *God will surely attend to you and bring you up from this land* (Genesis 50:24–25). Joseph said *paqod yifqod*, **He will surely attend**. Here God says *paqod paqadti*, **I have surely attended**. Same two words, four hundred years later, switched from future to past.
- **v19 "not unless by a strong hand"** — the Hebrew is compressed and has been read two ways: that Pharaoh will not let them go **even when faced with** a strong hand, or that he will not let them go **except by** one. The difference is whether the strong hand is God's or Pharaoh's, and the words themselves allow both.
- **v22 "you will strip Egypt bare"** — *ve-nitsaltem et mitsrayim*, from *natsal*, the same verb as **rescue** in v8. God says He has come down to *natsal* Israel from Egypt's hand, and closes by saying Israel will *natsal* Egypt. English needs two different words for it. Hebrew uses one, and the second use is the first one turned around.
