# Exodus 8

**1** The LORD said to Moses, "Go to Pharaoh and say to him, 'This is what the LORD says: Send My people away, so they may serve Me.

**2** If you refuse to send them away, here — I am striking your whole territory with frogs.

**3** The Nile will swarm with frogs, and they will come up and come into your house, and into your bedroom, and onto your bed, and into the house of your servants, and among your people, and into your ovens and into your kneading bowls.

**4** On you and on your people and on all your servants the frogs will come up.'"

**5** The LORD said to Moses, "Say to Aaron: Stretch out your hand with your staff over the rivers, over the canals, and over the pools, and bring the frogs up over the land of Egypt."

**6** Aaron stretched out his hand over the waters of Egypt, and the frog came up and covered the land of Egypt.

**7** The magicians did the same with their secret arts, and they brought frogs up over the land of Egypt.

**8** Pharaoh called for Moses and Aaron and said, "Plead with the LORD to take the frogs away from me and from my people, and I will send the people away, and they will sacrifice to the LORD."

**9** Moses said to Pharaoh, "Claim the honor over me: for when shall I plead for you and for your servants and for your people, to cut off the frogs from you and from your houses, so that they are left only in the Nile?"

**10** He said, "Tomorrow." He said, "As you say — so that you may know that there is none like the LORD our God.

**11** The frogs will turn aside from you and from your houses and from your servants and from your people. They will be left only in the Nile."

**12** Moses and Aaron went out from Pharaoh, and Moses cried out to the LORD about the matter of the frogs that He had set on Pharaoh.

**13** The LORD did according to Moses' word, and the frogs died out of the houses, out of the courtyards, and out of the fields.

**14** They piled them up, heap after heap, and the land stank.

**15** Pharaoh saw that there was relief, and he made his own heart heavy, and he did not listen to them, just as the LORD had said.

## The Finger of God

**16** The LORD said to Moses, "Say to Aaron: Stretch out your staff and strike the dust of the earth, and it will become gnats in all the land of Egypt."

**17** They did so. Aaron stretched out his hand with his staff and struck the dust of the earth, and the gnats were on man and on beast. All the dust of the earth became gnats in all the land of Egypt.

**18** The magicians did the same with their secret arts, to bring out the gnats — and they could not. The gnats were on man and on beast.

**19** The magicians said to Pharaoh, "This is the finger of God." Pharaoh's heart was strong, and he did not listen to them, just as the LORD had said.

## A Separation Between My People and Your People

**20** The LORD said to Moses, "Rise early in the morning and station yourself before Pharaoh — look, he is going out to the water — and say to him, 'This is what the LORD says: Send My people away, so they may serve Me.

**21** For if you are not sending My people away, here — I am letting loose against you and against your servants and against your people and against your houses the swarms. The houses of Egypt will be full of the swarms, and also the ground they stand on.

**22** On that day I will set apart the land of Goshen, where My people are standing, so that no swarms are there — so that you may know that I am the LORD in the midst of the land.

**23** I will put a ransom between My people and your people. Tomorrow this sign will happen.'"

**24** The LORD did so, and heavy swarms came into Pharaoh's house and the house of his servants, and in all the land of Egypt the land was ruined because of the swarms.

**25** Pharaoh called for Moses and Aaron and said, "Go, sacrifice to your God — in the land."

**26** Moses said, "It would not be right to do so, because what we sacrifice to the LORD our God is detestable to Egypt. If we sacrifice what is detestable to Egypt before their eyes, will they not stone us?

**27** A three days' journey we will go into the wilderness, and we will sacrifice to the LORD our God as He tells us."

**28** Pharaoh said, "I myself will send you away, and you will sacrifice to the LORD your God in the wilderness — only you shall not go very far away. Plead for me."

**29** Moses said, "Here — I am going out from you, and I will plead with the LORD, and the swarms will turn aside from Pharaoh, from his servants, and from his people tomorrow. Only let Pharaoh not go on trifling, by not sending the people away to sacrifice to the LORD."

**30** Moses went out from Pharaoh and pleaded with the LORD.

**31** The LORD did according to Moses' word, and He took the swarms away from Pharaoh, from his servants, and from his people. Not one was left.

**32** Pharaoh made his heart heavy this time too, and he did not send the people away.

## Notes

- **On the verse numbers** — the Hebrew text divides these chapters differently: what is printed here as 8:1–4 stands in the Hebrew as 7:26–29, so the Hebrew chapter 8 runs to 28 verses and this one to 32. This rendering follows the English division, so a reader can set it beside a King James Bible. Nothing of the text is added or lost either way.
- **v3 "the Nile will swarm with frogs"** — *ve-sharats ha-ye'or*. *Sharats* is the swarming verb of the fifth day of creation — *let the waters **swarm** with swarms of living creatures* (Genesis 1:20) — and it is one of the five verbs used of Israel multiplying in Egypt in Exodus 1:7. The Nile was where Hebrew boys were drowned. Now the same river swarms, and what comes out of it goes into Pharaoh's bed.
- **v3 "into your ovens and into your kneading bowls"** — the plague is aimed at the places a household cannot do without. Bread cannot be made. What the text lists is not horror but the shutting down of ordinary domestic life, item by item.
- **v6 "and the frog came up and covered the land"** — the Hebrew noun is **singular**: *va-ta'al ha-tsefardea*, and the frog came up. Hebrew can use a singular collectively, so this is grammar rather than a single enormous animal, but the effect is not lost on the reader who sees it — one creature, covering Egypt.
- **v9 "Claim the honor over me"** — *hitpa'er alai*, an odd idiom built on *pa'ar*, to glorify or beautify. Moses is handing Pharaoh the advantage: *you name the hour*. It is a piece of courtesy with a hook in it, and it sets up what Pharaoh does next.
- **v10 "Tomorrow"** — offered any moment he likes, Pharaoh picks the day after. He chooses one more night in a house full of frogs rather than be relieved on someone else's schedule. The text reports the word and says nothing about it.
- **v14 "heap after heap, and the land stank"** — *chomarim chomarim*, the noun doubled, which is how Hebrew says *piles and piles*. And *va-tiv'ash*, the stink verb again — the Nile stank in 7:21, the foremen said Moses made them stink in 5:21. Relief, in this chapter, smells worse than the plague.
- **v15 "he made his own heart heavy"** — *ve-hakhbed et libbo*. Up to here the heart has been strong (7:13, 7:22) or God has said He would harden it (7:3). **This is the first time Pharaoh does it to himself**, and the Hebrew is an emphatic form that puts the act squarely on him. He does it again in the last verse of the chapter.
- **v17 "the dust of the earth"** — *afar ha-arets*, the stuff man was formed from in Genesis 2:7. Aaron strikes it and it becomes vermin. The previous sign struck water; this one strikes the ground itself.
- **v18 "to bring out the gnats — and they could not"** — *le-hotsi*, the same verb used throughout this book for bringing Israel **out** of Egypt. The magicians attempt a bringing-out and fail at it. It is the first thing in the contest that Egypt cannot do.
- **v19 "This is the finger of God"** — *etsba Elohim hi*. Egypt's own experts say it, not Moses, and they say *Elohim*, God, rather than the name. It is the first concession from the other side, and Pharaoh ignores his own advisers. The same phrase returns for the writing of the tablets at 31:18.
- **v21 "the swarms"** — *he-arov*, from a root meaning to mix. The word says a mixture, not which creatures, and ancient readers already disagreed: some took it as flies, others as a mixed mass of wild animals. The Hebrew declines to specify, and this rendering keeps it unspecified.
- **v23 "I will put a ransom between My people and your people"** — *pedut*, and this is not the ordinary word for a division or a boundary. *Pedut* means a ransom, a redemption, the price paid to get someone back. Some read it here as a sign of deliverance, others emend it toward a word for distinction. What the received text says is that what stands between the two peoples is a ransom.
- **v26 "what we sacrifice is detestable to Egypt"** — *to'evat mitsrayim*, and Genesis has used that exact phrase twice about these two peoples: Egyptians could not eat bread with Hebrews, *for that is detestable to Egypt* (43:32), and *every shepherd is detestable to Egypt* (46:34). Moses is not inventing an excuse; he is quoting a fact the book established four hundred years earlier.
- **v28 "only you shall not go very far away"** — the Hebrew doubles the verb, *harcheq lo tarchiqu*, distancing you shall not distance. Pharaoh has moved from *no* to haggling over mileage. It is the first concession, and it is about geography rather than freedom.
- **v29 "let Pharaoh not go on trifling"** — *hatel*, to mock, to deal falsely, to play someone. Moses says it to the king's face, and the last verse of the chapter proves him right.
