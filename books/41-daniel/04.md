# Daniel 4

## The King's Letter

**1** King Nebuchadnezzar, to all peoples, nations and languages who live in all the earth: May your peace be great!

**2** It has seemed good to me to tell the signs and wonders that the Most High God has done for me.

**3** How great are His signs,  
and how mighty His wonders!  
His kingdom is an everlasting kingdom,  
and His dominion is from generation to generation.

## The Dream of the Tree

**4** I, Nebuchadnezzar, was at ease in my house and flourishing in my palace.

**5** I saw a dream, and it made me afraid; the thoughts on my bed and the visions of my head alarmed me.

**6** So I made a decree that all the wise men of Babylon be brought in before me, so that they might make known to me the interpretation of the dream.

**7** Then the magicians, the enchanters, the Chaldeans and the diviners came in, and I told them the dream, but they could not make its interpretation known to me.

**8** At last Daniel came in before me, whose name is Belteshazzar after the name of my god, and in whom is the spirit of the holy gods; and I told him the dream:

**9** "Belteshazzar, chief of the magicians, I know that the spirit of the holy gods is in you and no mystery is too hard for you. Tell me the visions of my dream that I saw, and its interpretation.

**10** These were the visions of my head on my bed: I was looking, and there was a tree in the middle of the earth, and its height was great.

**11** The tree grew and became strong; its top reached to heaven, and it could be seen to the end of the whole earth.

**12** Its leaves were beautiful and its fruit abundant, and on it was food for all. Under it the beasts of the field found shade, and the birds of the heavens lived in its branches, and all flesh was fed from it.

**13** I was looking in the visions of my head on my bed, and a watcher, a holy one, came down from heaven.

**14** He called out loudly and said this: 'Cut down the tree and cut off its branches, strip off its leaves and scatter its fruit. Let the beasts flee from under it and the birds from its branches.

**15** But leave the stump of its roots in the earth, in a band of iron and bronze, in the grass of the field. Let him be wet with the dew of heaven, and let his portion be with the beasts in the grass of the earth.

**16** Let his heart be changed from a man's, and let a beast's heart be given to him, and let seven times pass over him.

**17** The sentence is by the decree of the watchers, and the decision by the word of the holy ones, so that the living may know that the Most High rules over the kingdom of men and gives it to whom He will, and sets over it the lowliest of men.'

**18** This is the dream that I, King Nebuchadnezzar, saw. Now you, Belteshazzar, tell me its interpretation, because none of the wise men of my kingdom can make known to me the interpretation; but you are able, because the spirit of the holy gods is in you."

## The Interpretation

**19** Then Daniel, whose name was Belteshazzar, was appalled for a while, and his thoughts alarmed him. The king said, "Belteshazzar, do not let the dream or its interpretation alarm you." Belteshazzar answered, "My lord, may the dream be for those who hate you, and its interpretation for your enemies!

**20** The tree that you saw, which grew and became strong, whose top reached to heaven and which could be seen in all the earth,

**21** whose leaves were beautiful and its fruit abundant, and on which was food for all, under which the beasts of the field lived and in whose branches the birds of the heavens dwelt —

**22** it is you, O king, who have grown and become strong. Your greatness has grown and reached to heaven, and your dominion to the end of the earth.

**23** As for the king seeing a watcher, a holy one, coming down from heaven and saying, 'Cut down the tree and destroy it, but leave the stump of its roots in the earth, in a band of iron and bronze, in the grass of the field; let him be wet with the dew of heaven, and let his portion be with the beasts of the field, until seven times pass over him' —

**24** this is the interpretation, O king, and it is the decree of the Most High that has come upon my lord the king:

**25** you will be driven from among men, and your dwelling will be with the beasts of the field. You will be made to eat grass like oxen, and you will be wet with the dew of heaven, and seven times will pass over you, until you know that the Most High rules over the kingdom of men and gives it to whom He will.

**26** As it was commanded to leave the stump of the roots of the tree, your kingdom will be restored to you from the time you know that Heaven rules.

**27** Therefore, O king, let my counsel be acceptable to you: break off your sins by doing what is right, and your iniquities by showing mercy to the poor. Perhaps your ease will be prolonged."

## Seven Times

**28** All this came upon King Nebuchadnezzar.

**29** At the end of twelve months he was walking on the roof of the royal palace of Babylon.

**30** The king said, "Is this not great Babylon, which I have built as a royal house by the might of my power and for the glory of my majesty?"

**31** While the word was still in the king's mouth, a voice fell from heaven: "To you it is said, King Nebuchadnezzar: the kingdom has departed from you.

**32** You will be driven from among men, and your dwelling will be with the beasts of the field. You will be made to eat grass like oxen, and seven times will pass over you, until you know that the Most High rules over the kingdom of men and gives it to whom He will."

**33** At that very moment the word was fulfilled on Nebuchadnezzar. He was driven from among men and ate grass like oxen, and his body was wet with the dew of heaven, until his hair grew like eagles' feathers and his nails like birds' claws.

## The King Praises the King of Heaven

**34** At the end of the days I, Nebuchadnezzar, lifted my eyes to heaven, and my understanding returned to me. I blessed the Most High, and I praised and honored Him who lives forever,  
for His dominion is an everlasting dominion,  
and His kingdom is from generation to generation.

**35** All the inhabitants of the earth are counted as nothing,  
and He does as He wills with the host of heaven  
and with the inhabitants of the earth.  
No one can hold back His hand  
or say to Him, "What have You done?"

**36** At that very time my understanding returned to me, and for the glory of my kingdom my majesty and my splendor returned to me. My counselors and my lords sought me out, and I was established again in my kingdom, and still more greatness was added to me.

**37** Now I, Nebuchadnezzar, praise and exalt and honor the King of heaven, for all His works are truth and His ways are justice, and He is able to humble those who walk in pride.

## Notes

- **vv1–3 the king's letter** — in the Hebrew text these three verses end chapter 3 (3:31–33), and v4 here is its 4:1; the English numbering is followed. The chapter is Nebuchadnezzar's own letter: he says *I* while he tells the dream (vv4–18), the story speaks of him from outside while he is mad (vv28–33), and he says *I* again when his understanding returns (v34). *His kingdom is an everlasting kingdom* (v3) is the psalm's *Your kingdom is an everlasting kingdom* (Psalm 145:13), in a Babylonian king's mouth.
- **v4 "at ease… flourishing"** — *shelē*, **at ease**, is the root of Daniel's last words to him, *perhaps your ease* (*shelevah*) *will be prolonged* (v27). *Ra'nan*, **flourishing**, is the word for a green, leafy tree, as in *under every leafy tree* (Deuteronomy 12:2). The king describes himself as a tree before he dreams of one.
- **vv8–9, 18 "the spirit of the holy gods"** — *ruach elahin qaddishin*. *Elahin* is plural, and so is *holy*. The plural is the book's word for gods (2:11, 47; 3:14), and in the same breath the king names *my god* (v8). Hebrew can put a plural adjective with the one God: *He is a holy God* is *Elohim qedoshim*, **holy** plural (Joshua 24:19). The Greek has **the holy Spirit of God**. Choice in the report.
- **vv10–12, 20–22 the tree** — Ezekiel's cedar: *In its boughs all the birds of the heavens made their nests; under its branches all the beasts of the field gave birth* (Ezekiel 31:6), cut down because it grew proud of its height (Ezekiel 31:10–12). Jesus' mustard seed grows into a tree *so that the birds of the sky come and nest in its branches* (Matthew 13:32).
- **vv13, 17, 23 "a watcher, a holy one"** — *ir*, from the root for being awake, found only in this chapter. The Greek does not translate it; it writes *eir*. 1 Enoch's name for the angels who came down to earth is *the Watchers* (1 Enoch 1:5).
- **vv16, 23, 25, 32 "seven times"** — *shiv'ah iddanin*. *Iddan* is the word of 2:21, *He changes times and seasons*, and the chapter does not say how long a *time* is. The Old Greek, the second Greek version of Daniel, has *seven years*.
- **vv17, 25, 32 "the Most High rules over the kingdom of men"** — the sentence is said three times, by the watcher, by Daniel and by the voice from heaven, and the third time it is done. *The lowliest of men* (v17) is *shefal*, the verb of the king's last words: He *is able to humble* (*hashpalah*) those who walk in pride (v37). In v26 the same truth is *Heaven rules*: *Heaven* stands for God, as in the question *The baptism of John — where was it from? From heaven, or from men?* (Matthew 21:25).
- **v27 "break off your sins by doing what is right"** — *chatayakh be-tsidqah feruq*. *Tsidqah* is the Aramaic of *tsedaqah*, **righteousness**, found in the Bible's Aramaic only here; *peruq* is **break off**, or **redeem**. The second half names the poor, *your iniquities by showing mercy to the poor*. The Greek reads both halves as giving to the poor: **redeem your sins by almsgiving**. Choice in the report.
- **v35 "What have You done?"** — Job asked it of God, *Who will say to Him, "What are You doing?"* (Job 9:12), and Ecclesiastes of a king, *who may say to him, "What are you doing?"* (Ecclesiastes 8:4). Here a king says it of God. *Counted as nothing* is Isaiah's word for the nations, *counted by Him as less than nothing* (Isaiah 40:17).
