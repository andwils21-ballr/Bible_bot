# Daniel 5

## Belshazzar's Feast

**1** King Belshazzar made a great feast for a thousand of his lords, and he drank wine in front of the thousand.

**2** As he tasted the wine, Belshazzar ordered that the vessels of gold and silver that Nebuchadnezzar his father had taken out of the temple in Jerusalem be brought, so that the king and his lords, his wives and his secondary wives might drink from them.

**3** Then they brought the vessels of gold that had been taken out of the temple, the house of God in Jerusalem, and the king and his lords, his wives and his secondary wives drank from them.

**4** They drank wine and praised the gods of gold and silver, bronze, iron, wood and stone.

## The Hand

**5** At that very moment the fingers of a human hand came out and wrote opposite the lampstand on the plaster of the wall of the king's palace, and the king watched the hand as it wrote.

**6** Then the king's color changed, and his thoughts alarmed him; the joints of his hips came loose, and his knees knocked together.

**7** The king cried out loudly for the enchanters, the Chaldeans and the diviners to be brought in. The king said to the wise men of Babylon, "Whoever reads this writing and tells me its interpretation will be clothed in purple, with a chain of gold around his neck, and will rule as third in the kingdom."

**8** Then all the king's wise men came in, but they could not read the writing or make known its interpretation to the king.

**9** Then King Belshazzar was greatly alarmed, his color changed, and his lords were bewildered.

**10** Because of the words of the king and his lords, the queen entered the banquet hall. The queen said, "O king, live forever! Do not let your thoughts alarm you or your color change.

**11** There is a man in your kingdom in whom is the spirit of the holy gods. In the days of your father, light and insight and wisdom like the wisdom of the gods were found in him, and King Nebuchadnezzar your father, your father the king, made him chief of the magicians, enchanters, Chaldeans and diviners,

**12** because an excellent spirit, knowledge and insight to interpret dreams, explain riddles and untie knots were found in this Daniel, whom the king named Belteshazzar. Now let Daniel be called, and he will tell the interpretation."

## Daniel Before the King

**13** Then Daniel was brought in before the king. The king said to Daniel, "Are you that Daniel, one of the exiles of Judah, whom the king my father brought from Judah?

**14** I have heard of you that the spirit of the gods is in you, and that light and insight and excellent wisdom are found in you.

**15** Just now the wise men, the enchanters, were brought in before me to read this writing and make known to me its interpretation, but they could not tell the interpretation of the matter.

**16** But I have heard of you that you can give interpretations and untie knots. Now if you can read the writing and make known to me its interpretation, you will be clothed in purple, with a chain of gold around your neck, and you will rule as third in the kingdom."

**17** Then Daniel answered the king, "Keep your gifts for yourself, and give your rewards to someone else. But I will read the writing to the king and make known to him the interpretation.

**18** O king, the Most High God gave Nebuchadnezzar your father the kingdom and greatness, glory and majesty.

**19** Because of the greatness He gave him, all peoples, nations and languages trembled and feared before him. Whom he wanted, he killed, and whom he wanted, he kept alive; whom he wanted, he raised up, and whom he wanted, he brought low.

**20** But when his heart was lifted up and his spirit grew hard so that he acted proudly, he was brought down from his royal throne, and his glory was taken from him.

**21** He was driven from among men, and his heart was made like a beast's, and his dwelling was with the wild donkeys. He was made to eat grass like oxen, and his body was wet with the dew of heaven, until he knew that the Most High God rules over the kingdom of men and sets over it whom He will.

**22** But you, his son, Belshazzar, have not humbled your heart, though you knew all this.

**23** You have lifted yourself up against the Lord of heaven. The vessels of His house were brought before you, and you and your lords, your wives and your secondary wives drank wine from them. You praised the gods of silver and gold, bronze, iron, wood and stone, which do not see or hear or know; but you have not honored the God in whose hand is your breath, and to whom all your ways belong.

**24** Then the hand was sent from Him, and this writing was inscribed.

## The Writing

**25** This is the writing that was inscribed: MENE, MENE, TEKEL, PARSIN.

**26** This is the interpretation of the matter: MENE — God has numbered your kingdom and brought it to an end.

**27** TEKEL — you have been weighed in the balances and found wanting.

**28** PERES — your kingdom is divided and given to the Medes and Persians."

**29** Then Belshazzar gave the order, and they clothed Daniel in purple, with a chain of gold around his neck, and proclaimed about him that he should rule as third in the kingdom.

**30** That very night Belshazzar the Chaldean king was killed.

**31** Darius the Mede received the kingdom, at about sixty-two years of age.

## Notes

- **vv2, 11, 13, 18, 22 "Nebuchadnezzar his father… his son"** — the chapter calls Nebuchadnezzar Belshazzar's *father* six times and Belshazzar *his son* once. *Av*, **father**, is also used of a forefather. Jeremiah had given Babylon three generations: *All the nations will serve him, and his son and his son's son, until the time of his own land comes* (Jeremiah 27:7).
- **vv2–4 "the vessels… the gods of gold and silver, bronze, iron, wood and stone"** — the vessels Nebuchadnezzar put in the treasure house of his god (1:2) are brought out for a drinking party, and the drinkers praise gods made of the statue's metals (2:32–33), with wood and stone added. The *wives and secondary wives* are *shegal* and *lechenah*; the queen of v10 is *malketa*, another word, and she is not at the table.
- **vv6, 12, 16 "the joints of his hips came loose… untie knots"** — *qitrei charseh mishtarayin*, **the knots of his loins were untied** (v6). Daniel is then praised as one who can *untie knots*, *mesharei qitrin* (vv12, 16), the same two words. The king's knots come undone, and Daniel is the man who unties them.
- **v11 "the spirit of the holy gods"** — the words of 4:8, 9, 18, now in the queen's mouth, and in v14 *the spirit of the gods*. The Greek has *the Spirit of God* in both verses. Choice in the report on chapter 4.
- **vv19–22 "he brought low… you have not humbled your heart"** — *shefal*, the verb Nebuchadnezzar's letter ended with, *He is able to humble those who walk in pride* (4:37). The king who *brought low* whom he wanted was himself brought low, and to his son Daniel says, *you… have not humbled* (*hashpelt*) *your heart, though you knew all this*.
- **v21 "with the wild donkeys"** — a detail chapter 4 does not have. There the king's dwelling was *with the beasts of the field* (4:25, 32).
- **v23 "which do not see or hear or know… the God in whose hand is your breath"** — the law's description of the gods of exile, *wood and stone, which neither see nor hear nor eat nor smell* (Deuteronomy 4:28). *Nishmah*, **breath**, is the Aramaic of the breath God blew into the first human, *the breath of lives* (Genesis 2:7).
- **vv25–28 MENE, MENE, TEKEL, PARSIN** — three words that are weights: *mene* is the *mina* (Ezekiel 45:12), *teqel* is the Aramaic of *shekel*, and *peres* is from the verb **divide**. Daniel reads each as a verb: *menah*, **numbered**; *teqilta*, **weighed**; *perisat*, **divided**. *Peres* also sounds like *Paras*, **Persia**, the last word of v28. The writing has *mene* twice and *parsin*, plural; the Greek has each word once: *Mane, thekel, phares*.
- **vv30–31 "That very night… Darius the Mede"** — the night of the feast. In the Hebrew text v31 is 6:1; the English numbering is followed. *Darius the Mede* is found only in Daniel (here and 11:1; 9:1 makes him a Mede by descent); the Darius of Ezra is *king of Persia* (Ezra 4:24).
