# Daniel 14

## Bel

**1** King Astyages was gathered to his fathers, and Cyrus the Persian received his kingdom.

**2** Daniel was the king's companion and was honored above all his friends.

**3** The Babylonians had an idol named Bel, and every day they spent on it twelve measures of fine flour, forty sheep and six jars of wine.

**4** The king worshipped it and went every day to bow down to it, but Daniel bowed down to his own God. The king said to him, "Why do you not bow down to Bel?"

**5** He said, "Because I do not worship idols made by hands, but the living God, who created heaven and earth and has dominion over all flesh."

**6** The king said to him, "Do you not think Bel is a living god? Do you not see how much he eats and drinks every day?"

**7** Daniel laughed and said, "Do not be deceived, O king. This is clay inside and bronze outside, and it has never eaten anything."

**8** The king was angry. He called his priests and said to them, "If you do not tell me who is eating up these provisions, you will die.

**9** But if you show that Bel is eating them, Daniel will die, because he has blasphemed against Bel." Daniel said to the king, "Let it be as you say."

**10** There were seventy priests of Bel, besides their wives and children. The king went with Daniel into the temple of Bel.

**11** The priests of Bel said, "We are going outside. You, O king, set out the food and mix the wine and set it there; then shut the door and seal it with your signet ring.

**12** When you come in the morning, if you do not find that Bel has eaten everything, let us die; otherwise Daniel, who is telling lies about us."

**13** They were unconcerned, for they had made a hidden entrance under the table, and through it they used to go in regularly and consume the offerings.

**14** When they had gone out, the king set out the food for Bel. Then Daniel ordered his servants to bring ashes, and they sifted them over the whole temple in the presence of the king alone. Then they went out, shut the door, sealed it with the king's signet ring, and went away.

**15** In the night the priests came as usual, with their wives and children, and they ate everything and drank it all.

**16** Early in the morning the king rose, and Daniel with him.

**17** The king said, "Are the seals unbroken, Daniel?" He said, "They are unbroken, O king."

**18** As soon as the doors were opened, the king looked at the table and cried out with a loud voice, "You are great, O Bel, and there is no deceit in you at all!"

**19** But Daniel laughed and held the king back from going in, and he said, "Look at the floor, and see whose footprints these are."

**20** The king said, "I see the footprints of men and women and children."

**21** Then the king was angry, and he arrested the priests with their wives and children, and they showed him the secret doors through which they used to come in and consume what was on the table.

**22** The king put them to death, and he handed Bel over to Daniel, who destroyed it and its temple.

## The Dragon

**23** There was also a great dragon, which the Babylonians worshipped.

**24** The king said to Daniel, "You cannot say that this is not a living god. So bow down to it."

**25** Daniel said, "I will bow down to the Lord my God, for He is the living God.

**26** But you, O king, give me permission, and I will kill the dragon without sword or club." The king said, "I give it to you."

**27** Then Daniel took pitch, fat and hair, boiled them together and made cakes, and he put them into the dragon's mouth. The dragon ate them and burst open. Daniel said, "See what you have been worshipping!"

**28** When the Babylonians heard it, they were furious and turned against the king, saying, "The king has become a Jew. He has pulled down Bel, killed the dragon and slaughtered the priests."

**29** They came to the king and said, "Hand Daniel over to us, or we will kill you and your household."

**30** The king saw that they were pressing him hard, and under compulsion he handed Daniel over to them.

## The Lions' Den Again

**31** They threw him into the den of lions, and he was there six days.

**32** There were seven lions in the den, and every day they had been given two human bodies and two sheep; but now nothing was given them, so that they would devour Daniel.

**33** Now the prophet Habakkuk was in Judea. He had made a stew and broken bread into a bowl, and he was going into the field to take it to the reapers.

**34** The messenger of the Lord said to Habakkuk, "Take the meal you have to Babylon, to Daniel, in the den of lions."

**35** Habakkuk said, "Lord, I have never seen Babylon, and I do not know the den."

**36** Then the messenger of the Lord took him by the crown of his head, and carrying him by the hair of his head, set him down in Babylon above the den, with the rushing of his spirit.

**37** Habakkuk called out, "Daniel! Daniel! Take the meal that God has sent you."

**38** Daniel said, "You have remembered me, O God, and You have not forsaken those who love You."

**39** So Daniel got up and ate, and the messenger of God at once returned Habakkuk to his own place.

**40** On the seventh day the king came to mourn for Daniel. He came to the den and looked in, and Daniel was sitting there.

**41** He cried out with a loud voice, "You are great, O Lord, God of Daniel, and there is no other besides You!"

**42** Then he pulled Daniel out, and he threw into the den those who had tried to destroy him, and they were devoured at once before his eyes.

## Notes

- **v1 "King Astyages… Cyrus the Persian"** — this chapter, like chapter 13, has no Hebrew or Aramaic; the text is Theodotion's Greek. The Old Greek opens differently: *From the prophecy of Habakkuk son of Joshua, of the tribe of Levi. There was a man, a priest, whose name was Daniel son of Abal, a companion of the king of Babylon*. The king there is not named.
