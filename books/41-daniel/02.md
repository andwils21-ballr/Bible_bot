# Daniel 2

## The King's Dream

**1** In the second year of the reign of Nebuchadnezzar, Nebuchadnezzar had dreams; his spirit was beaten, and his sleep left him.

**2** The king ordered that the magicians, the enchanters, the sorcerers and the Chaldeans be called to tell the king his dreams. They came and stood before the king.

**3** The king said to them, "I have had a dream, and my spirit is beaten with wanting to know the dream."

**4** The Chaldeans spoke to the king in Aramaic: "O king, live forever! Tell your servants the dream, and we will tell the interpretation."

**5** The king answered the Chaldeans, "My word is firm: if you do not make known to me the dream and its interpretation, you will be torn limb from limb, and your houses will be made a dunghill.

**6** But if you tell the dream and its interpretation, you will receive from me gifts and rewards and great honor. So tell me the dream and its interpretation."

**7** They answered a second time, "Let the king tell his servants the dream, and we will tell its interpretation."

**8** The king answered, "I know for certain that you are trying to gain time, because you see that my word is firm:

**9** if you do not make the dream known to me, there is one sentence for you. You have agreed among yourselves to speak lying and corrupt words before me until the time changes. So tell me the dream, and I will know that you can tell me its interpretation."

**10** The Chaldeans answered the king, "There is no man on earth who can tell the king's matter, because no king, however great and powerful, has asked such a thing of any magician or enchanter or Chaldean.

**11** What the king asks is too hard, and there is no one else who can tell it before the king except the gods, whose dwelling is not with flesh."

**12** At this the king became angry and very furious, and he ordered all the wise men of Babylon to be destroyed.

**13** The decree went out, and the wise men were to be killed; and they looked for Daniel and his companions to kill them.

**14** Then Daniel spoke with prudence and tact to Arioch, the king's chief of the slaughterers, who had gone out to kill the wise men of Babylon.

**15** He said to Arioch, the king's officer, "Why is the decree from the king so harsh?" Then Arioch made the matter known to Daniel.

**16** Daniel went in and asked the king to give him time, so that he could tell the king the interpretation.

## The Mystery Revealed

**17** Then Daniel went to his house and made the matter known to Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah, his companions,

**18** to seek compassion from the God of heaven concerning this mystery, so that Daniel and his companions would not be destroyed with the rest of the wise men of Babylon.

**19** Then the mystery was revealed to Daniel in a vision of the night, and Daniel blessed the God of heaven.

**20** Daniel said,  
"Blessed be the name of God  
from everlasting to everlasting,  
for wisdom and might are His.

**21** He changes times and seasons;  
He removes kings and sets up kings;  
He gives wisdom to the wise  
and knowledge to those who have understanding.

**22** He reveals deep and hidden things;  
He knows what is in the darkness,  
and the light dwells with Him.

**23** To You, God of my fathers,  
I give thanks and praise,  
for You have given me wisdom and might,  
and now You have made known to me what we asked of You;  
You have made known to us the king's matter."

## The Dream and Its Interpretation

**24** So Daniel went in to Arioch, whom the king had appointed to destroy the wise men of Babylon. He went and said to him, "Do not destroy the wise men of Babylon. Bring me in before the king, and I will tell the king the interpretation."

**25** Then Arioch quickly brought Daniel in before the king and said to him, "I have found a man among the exiles of Judah who will make known the interpretation to the king."

**26** The king said to Daniel, whose name was Belteshazzar, "Are you able to make known to me the dream I saw and its interpretation?"

**27** Daniel answered the king, "No wise men, enchanters, magicians or diviners can tell the king the mystery the king has asked about.

**28** But there is a God in heaven who reveals mysteries, and He has made known to King Nebuchadnezzar what will be in the end of days. Your dream and the visions of your head on your bed were these:

**29** As for you, O king, on your bed your thoughts turned to what would be after this, and He who reveals mysteries has made known to you what will be.

**30** As for me, this mystery has been revealed to me not because of any wisdom in me more than in anyone living, but so that the interpretation may be made known to the king, and you may know the thoughts of your heart.

**31** "You were watching, O king, and you saw a statue, one great statue. That statue was huge and its brightness extraordinary; it stood in front of you, and its appearance was terrifying.

**32** The head of that statue was of fine gold, its chest and its arms of silver, its belly and its thighs of bronze,

**33** its legs of iron, its feet partly of iron and partly of clay.

**34** You watched while a stone was cut out, not by hands, and it struck the statue on its feet of iron and clay and crushed them.

**35** Then the iron, the clay, the bronze, the silver and the gold were crushed all together and became like chaff from the summer threshing floors; the wind carried them away, and not a trace of them was found. But the stone that struck the statue became a great mountain and filled the whole earth.

**36** "This was the dream, and we will tell the king its interpretation.

**37** You, O king, are the king of kings, to whom the God of heaven has given the kingdom, the power, the might and the glory.

**38** Wherever they live, the sons of men, the beasts of the field and the birds of the heavens, He has given them into your hand and made you ruler over them all. You are the head of gold.

**39** After you another kingdom will arise, lower than yours, and then a third kingdom, of bronze, which will rule over all the earth.

**40** A fourth kingdom will be strong as iron. Just as iron crushes and shatters everything, like iron that smashes, it will crush and smash all these.

**41** As you saw the feet and the toes, partly of potter's clay and partly of iron, it will be a divided kingdom; but some of the firmness of iron will be in it, just as you saw the iron mixed with the soft clay.

**42** As the toes of the feet were partly iron and partly clay, part of the kingdom will be strong and part of it brittle.

**43** As you saw the iron mixed with soft clay, they will mix with one another by the seed of men, but they will not hold together, one with the other, just as iron does not mix with clay.

**44** In the days of those kings the God of heaven will set up a kingdom that will never be destroyed, and the kingdom will not be left to another people. It will crush all these kingdoms and bring them to an end, and it will stand forever,

**45** just as you saw that a stone was cut from the mountain, not by hands, and that it crushed the iron, the bronze, the clay, the silver and the gold. A great God has made known to the king what will be after this. The dream is certain, and its interpretation trustworthy."

## The King Bows Down

**46** Then King Nebuchadnezzar fell on his face and worshipped Daniel, and he ordered that an offering and soothing aromas be offered to him.

**47** The king said to Daniel, "Truly your God is God of gods and Lord of kings, and a revealer of mysteries, since you have been able to reveal this mystery."

**48** Then the king made Daniel great and gave him many great gifts. He made him ruler over the whole province of Babylon and chief prefect over all the wise men of Babylon.

**49** At Daniel's request the king appointed Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego over the affairs of the province of Babylon, while Daniel remained at the king's gate.

## Notes

- **v1 "In the second year of the reign of Nebuchadnezzar"** — the four were to be trained *for three years* before they stood before the king (1:5, 18), and here, in the king's second year, Daniel is already counted among the wise men (v13). The book does not set the two dates side by side or explain how they fit.
- **vv1–3, 10–11 Pharaoh's dream again** — *va-tippa'em ruchi*, **my spirit is beaten** (v3), is letter for letter what is said of Pharaoh, *his spirit was beaten* (Genesis 41:8). The magicians cannot answer, as Egypt's could not: *there is no one to interpret it* (Genesis 41:15). Then a young man from among the exiles is brought in, says the wisdom is not his own (v30), as Joseph said *Not I. God will answer* (Genesis 41:16), and is set over the province (v48), as Joseph was *over all the land of Egypt* (Genesis 41:41). Arioch is *chief of the slaughterers*, the title of Potiphar (Genesis 37:36).
- **v4 "in Aramaic"** — *aramit*, and from the next words the book itself is in Aramaic, through 7:28; it returns to Hebrew in chapter 8. The same word stands before the Aramaic of Ezra: *The letter was written in Aramaic* (Ezra 4:7).
- **vv5, 8 "My word is firm"** — *millta minni azda*. *Azda* is found only in these two verses, and it is read as **settled, certain**: the king has given his word and will not take it back. In v9 he gives the reason: *tell me the dream, and I will know that you can tell me its interpretation*. The Greek reads *the word has gone from me*, that is, the king has forgotten the dream.
- **vv11, 27–28 "the gods, whose dwelling is not with flesh… a God in heaven who reveals mysteries"** — the Chaldeans say only gods could tell the dream, and gods do not live among mortals. Daniel's answer is a God who does tell it. *Raz*, **mystery**, a secret that is made known, comes eight times in the chapter; the Greek is *mystērion*, the word of *the mysteries of the kingdom of heaven* (Matthew 13:11).
- **v28 "in the end of days"** — *be-acharit yomayya*, the Aramaic of Jacob's words to his sons, *I will tell you what will befall you in the end of days* (Genesis 49:1), and of Balaam's (Numbers 24:14). In v29 the same thing is *what would be after this*.
- **vv34–35, 45 the stone** — *cut out, not by hands*: no one quarries it. It was cut *from the mountain* (v45), and it *became a great mountain and filled the whole earth* (v35). It strikes the statue at its weakest part, the feet, and all the metals fall together. The Greek has *from a mountain* already in v34.
- **vv37–38 "the king of kings… the beasts of the field"** — Ezekiel gives Nebuchadnezzar the same title (Ezekiel 26:7), and Jeremiah the same grant: *I have given him even the beasts of the field to serve him* (Jeremiah 27:6). The head of gold is told that his kingdom was given to him.
- **v43 "they will mix with one another by the seed of men"** — *mit'arvin lehevon bi-zra anasha*. *Seed* is offspring, so the mixing may be by marriage between peoples or royal houses; the verse says no more than that. *Davqin*, **hold together**, is the Aramaic of *cling*: iron and clay can be pressed together, but they do not cling.
- **vv46–47 "an offering and soothing aromas… God of gods"** — *minchah ve-nichochin*, the words of the altar; Ezra has *nichochin* for what is offered *to the God of heaven*, *sacrifices of soothing aroma* (Ezra 6:10). The king offers them to Daniel, then calls Daniel's God *God of gods and Lord of kings*, the words of Moses with one changed: *God of gods and Lord of lords* (Deuteronomy 10:17).
