# Daniel 3

## The Statue of Gold

**1** King Nebuchadnezzar made a statue of gold, sixty cubits high and six cubits wide. He set it up on the plain of Dura, in the province of Babylon.

**2** Then King Nebuchadnezzar sent to gather the satraps, the prefects and the governors, the counselors, the treasurers, the judges, the magistrates and all the officials of the provinces, to come to the dedication of the statue that King Nebuchadnezzar had set up.

**3** Then the satraps, the prefects and the governors, the counselors, the treasurers, the judges, the magistrates and all the officials of the provinces gathered for the dedication of the statue that King Nebuchadnezzar had set up, and they stood before the statue that Nebuchadnezzar had set up.

**4** The herald called out loudly, "You are commanded, peoples, nations and languages:

**5** when you hear the sound of the horn, the flute, the zither, the lyre, the harp, the pipes and every kind of music, you are to fall down and worship the statue of gold that King Nebuchadnezzar has set up.

**6** Whoever does not fall down and worship will at that very moment be thrown into the burning fiery furnace."

**7** So at that time, as soon as all the peoples heard the sound of the horn, the flute, the zither, the lyre, the harp and every kind of music, all the peoples, nations and languages fell down and worshipped the statue of gold that King Nebuchadnezzar had set up.

## The Accusation

**8** At that time certain Chaldeans came forward and denounced the Jews.

**9** They said to King Nebuchadnezzar, "O king, live forever!

**10** You, O king, have made a decree that everyone who hears the sound of the horn, the flute, the zither, the lyre, the harp, the pipes and every kind of music shall fall down and worship the statue of gold,

**11** and whoever does not fall down and worship shall be thrown into the burning fiery furnace.

**12** There are certain Jews whom you have appointed over the affairs of the province of Babylon, Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego. These men pay no attention to you, O king. They do not serve your gods, and they do not worship the statue of gold that you have set up."

**13** Then Nebuchadnezzar, in rage and fury, ordered Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego to be brought, and these men were brought before the king.

**14** Nebuchadnezzar said to them, "Is it true, Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego, that you do not serve my gods or worship the statue of gold that I have set up?

**15** Now if you are ready, when you hear the sound of the horn, the flute, the zither, the lyre, the harp, the pipes and every kind of music, to fall down and worship the statue I have made — but if you do not worship, at that very moment you will be thrown into the burning fiery furnace. Who is the god who will rescue you from my hands?"

**16** Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego answered the king, "Nebuchadnezzar, we have no need to answer you in this matter.

**17** If our God whom we serve is able to rescue us from the burning fiery furnace, then from your hand, O king, He will rescue us.

**18** But if not, know this, O king: we will not serve your gods or worship the statue of gold that you have set up."

## The Furnace

**19** Then Nebuchadnezzar was filled with fury, and the look of his face changed toward Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego. He ordered the furnace heated seven times more than it was usually heated.

**20** He ordered some of the strongest men in his army to bind Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego and throw them into the burning fiery furnace.

**21** Then these men were bound in their cloaks, their tunics, their hats and their other clothes, and they were thrown into the burning fiery furnace.

**22** Because the king's order was so harsh and the furnace so extremely hot, the flame of the fire killed the men who took up Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego.

**23** These three men, Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego, fell bound into the burning fiery furnace.

**24** Then King Nebuchadnezzar was astonished and rose in haste. He said to his counselors, "Did we not throw three men bound into the fire?" They answered the king, "It is true, O king."

**25** He said, "But I see four men loose, walking in the middle of the fire, and they are not harmed; and the appearance of the fourth is like a son of the gods."

**26** Then Nebuchadnezzar came near the door of the burning fiery furnace and said, "Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego, servants of the Most High God, come out, and come here!" Then Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego came out from the middle of the fire.

**27** The satraps, the prefects, the governors and the king's counselors gathered and looked at these men: the fire had had no power over their bodies, the hair of their heads was not singed, their cloaks were not changed, and the smell of fire was not on them.

**28** Nebuchadnezzar said, "Blessed be the God of Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego, who has sent His messenger and rescued His servants, who trusted in Him. They set the king's word aside and gave up their bodies rather than serve or worship any god except their own God.

**29** So I make a decree: any people, nation or language that says anything against the God of Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego shall be torn limb from limb, and his house made a dunghill, because there is no other god who is able to rescue like this."

**30** Then the king promoted Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego in the province of Babylon.

## Notes

- **v1 "a statue of gold"** — *tselem*, the word for the statue of the dream (2:31–35), where only the head was gold and the head was Nebuchadnezzar (2:38). This statue is gold from head to foot. The Greek dates it *in the eighteenth year* of Nebuchadnezzar.
- **vv2–15 the lists, again and again** — the chapter repeats itself: the eight ranks of officials twice (vv2–3), the six instruments four times (vv5, 7, 10, 15), *the statue… that Nebuchadnezzar set up* eight times, *the burning fiery furnace* eight times. Three of the instruments have Greek names: *qatros* is *kitharis*, the zither; *pesanterin* is *psaltērion*, the harp; *sumponyah* is *symphōnia*, the pipes. All three are found only in this chapter.
- **v8 "denounced the Jews"** — *akhalu qartseihon di Yehudaye*, **ate the pieces of the Jews**, the Aramaic way of saying slander. The same words are used of the men who accuse Daniel (6:24).
- **vv15–18 "If our God whom we serve is able… But if not"** — the friends answer in the shape of the king's own sentence. He said *if you are ready* (*hen itheikhon atidin*)… *but if you do not* (*ve-hen la*); they say *if our God… is able* (*hen itai Elahana… yakhil*)… *but if not* (*ve-hen la*). *Itai* with a verb is *is* doing it, as in *Are you able* (2:26) and *you do not serve* (v14). He asked *who is the god who will rescue you from my hands?*, and they answer that their refusal does not depend on the answer. The accents of the Hebrew text pause after *hen itai*, which reads **If it is so, our God… is able**; the Greek has **For there is a God whom we serve, able to rescue us**. Choice in the report.
- **v19 "the look of his face changed"** — *tselem anpohi*, **the image of his face**: the statue's word, now used of the king's own face. *Shena*, **change**, comes three times: his face changed (v19), their cloaks were not changed (v27), and they *changed* the king's word, here *set the king's word aside* (v28).
- **v21 "their cloaks, their tunics, their hats"** — rare words. *Sarbal* is found only here and in v27, and *pattish* only here; *karbela* is related to the verb of David *clothed* in a robe of fine linen (1 Chronicles 15:27). What each garment was is uncertain. The Greek has *sarabara*, *tiaras* and *leg coverings*.
- **v23 the Greek addition** — at this point the Greek has sixty-seven verses the Aramaic does not: the three walking in the flame and praising God; the prayer of Azariah, *we have sinned and transgressed*; the furnace fed with naphtha, pitch, tow and brush until the flame streams forty-nine cubits above it and burns the Chaldeans near it; the angel of the Lord coming down into the furnace and making its middle like a whistling wind of dew; and the song of the three, *Blessed are You, Lord, God of our fathers*, calling on all creation to bless the Lord. The Greek then goes on with v24, the king hearing them sing.
- **v25 "like a son of the gods"** — *bar elahin*. *Elahin* is the plural the king and the Chaldeans use for gods (2:11, 47; 3:14), and in v28 the king himself names the fourth: *His messenger*. The Greek has *a son of God*. Choice in the report.
- **v26 "servants of the Most High God"** — *Elaha illa'ah*, the king's name for the God of Israel here and in 4:2. It is the Aramaic of the title of Melchizedek's God, *God Most High* (Genesis 14:18).
- **v27 "the smell of fire was not on them"** — Isaiah's promise in the event: *when you walk through fire, you will not be burned, and the flame will not consume you* (Isaiah 43:2). The fire that did not singe their hair killed the men who threw them in (v22).
