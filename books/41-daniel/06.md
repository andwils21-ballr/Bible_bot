# Daniel 6

## The Plot

**1** It pleased Darius to set over the kingdom a hundred and twenty satraps, to be throughout the whole kingdom,

**2** and over them three administrators, of whom Daniel was one, so that these satraps would give account to them and the king would suffer no loss.

**3** Then this Daniel distinguished himself above the administrators and the satraps, because an excellent spirit was in him, and the king planned to set him over the whole kingdom.

**4** Then the administrators and the satraps looked for a charge to find against Daniel in the affairs of the kingdom, but they could find no charge or fault, because he was faithful, and no negligence or fault was found in him.

**5** Then these men said, "We will not find any charge against this Daniel unless we find it against him in the law of his God."

**6** Then these administrators and satraps came thronging to the king and said to him, "King Darius, live forever!

**7** All the administrators of the kingdom, the prefects and the satraps, the counselors and the governors, have agreed that the king should establish a statute and enforce an injunction: whoever makes a petition to any god or man for thirty days, except to you, O king, shall be thrown into the den of lions.

**8** Now, O king, establish the injunction and sign the document, so that it cannot be changed, according to the law of the Medes and Persians, which cannot pass away."

**9** So King Darius signed the document and the injunction.

## Daniel's Prayer

**10** When Daniel knew that the document had been signed, he went to his house. In its upper room he had windows open toward Jerusalem, and three times a day he knelt on his knees and prayed and gave thanks before his God, as he had done before.

**11** Then these men came thronging and found Daniel making petition and pleading before his God.

**12** Then they came near and spoke before the king about the king's injunction: "Did you not sign an injunction that anyone who makes a petition to any god or man for thirty days, except to you, O king, shall be thrown into the den of lions?" The king answered, "The thing stands firm, according to the law of the Medes and Persians, which cannot pass away."

**13** Then they answered before the king, "Daniel, who is one of the exiles of Judah, pays no attention to you, O king, or to the injunction you have signed, but three times a day he makes his petition."

**14** When the king heard these words, he was greatly distressed, and he set his mind on Daniel to rescue him; until the sun went down he labored to save him.

**15** Then these men came thronging to the king and said to the king, "Know, O king, that it is a law of the Medes and Persians that no injunction or statute that the king establishes can be changed."

## The Den

**16** Then the king gave the order, and they brought Daniel and threw him into the den of lions. The king said to Daniel, "Your God, whom you serve continually, He will rescue you!"

**17** A stone was brought and laid on the mouth of the den, and the king sealed it with his own signet ring and with the signet rings of his lords, so that nothing might be changed concerning Daniel.

**18** Then the king went to his palace and spent the night fasting; no diversions were brought before him, and his sleep fled from him.

**19** At dawn, at first light, the king rose and went in haste to the den of lions.

**20** As he came near the den, he cried out to Daniel in an anguished voice. The king said to Daniel, "Daniel, servant of the living God, has your God, whom you serve continually, been able to rescue you from the lions?"

**21** Then Daniel spoke with the king: "O king, live forever!

**22** My God sent His messenger and shut the mouths of the lions, and they have not harmed me, because I was found innocent before Him; and before you too, O king, I have done no harm."

**23** Then the king was very glad, and he ordered that Daniel be taken up out of the den. So Daniel was taken up out of the den, and no harm was found on him, because he had trusted in his God.

**24** The king gave the order, and those men who had denounced Daniel were brought and thrown into the den of lions, they, their sons and their wives; and before they reached the bottom of the den the lions overpowered them and crushed all their bones.

## The King's Decree

**25** Then King Darius wrote to all peoples, nations and languages who live in all the earth: "May your peace be great!

**26** I make a decree that in all the dominion of my kingdom people are to tremble and fear before the God of Daniel,  
for He is the living God,  
and He endures forever;  
His kingdom shall never be destroyed,  
and His dominion shall be to the end.

**27** He rescues and saves;  
He does signs and wonders  
in heaven and on earth,  
He who has rescued Daniel  
from the power of the lions."

**28** So this Daniel prospered in the reign of Darius and in the reign of Cyrus the Persian.

## Notes

- **v1 the numbering** — in the Hebrew text chapter 6 begins one verse earlier, with *Darius the Mede received the kingdom* (5:31 here), so English 6:1–28 is Hebrew 6:2–29. The English numbering is followed.
- **vv4, 23 "he was faithful… he had trusted in his God"** — *mehemin* and *heimin*, one root. Daniel is found to be *faithful* to the king, so no fault can be found, and he comes out of the den unharmed *because he had trusted* in his God. In 2:45 the same root made the interpretation *trustworthy*.
- **vv6, 11, 15 "came thronging"** — *hargishu*, three times, each time as a body. The verb in Hebrew is found only in *Why do the nations rage* (*ragshu*, Psalm 2:1), where the nations and their rulers set themselves against the LORD.
- **v7 "All the administrators of the kingdom… have agreed"** — there were three administrators, *of whom Daniel was one* (v2). The plotters tell the king that all of them agree, and the one left out is the man the law is made to catch.
- **vv8, 12, 15 "the law of the Medes and Persians, which cannot pass away"** — Esther's court has the same law: *let it be written among the laws of Persia and Media so that it may not pass away* (Esther 1:19), and *a writing written in the king's name and sealed with the king's signet ring cannot be revoked* (Esther 8:8). The king is caught by his own signature.
- **v10 "windows open toward Jerusalem… three times a day"** — Solomon's prayer had asked God to hear the exiles when they *pray to You toward their land… the city that You have chosen and the house that I have built for Your name* (1 Kings 8:48). The psalm prays three times: *Evening and morning and at noon I complain and moan, and He hears my voice* (Psalm 55:17).
- **vv17–18 the stone and the night** — a stone on the mouth of the den, sealed so that *nothing might be changed*, as the tomb was made secure *sealing the stone* (Matthew 27:66). *Dachavan*, **diversions**, is found only in v18, and what it was is uncertain; the Greek has *food*. The Greek also adds to v18: *and God shut the mouths of the lions, and they did not harm Daniel*.
- **vv20, 26 "the living God"** — *Elaha chayya*, the God David named against Goliath, *the ranks of the living God* (1 Samuel 17:26), and Hezekiah against Assyria, *to defy the living God* (2 Kings 19:4). Here a Persian king names Him twice.
- **vv22–23 "they have not harmed me… I have done no harm… no harm was found on him"** — *chabal*, three times: the lions did not *harm* him, he did the king no *harm*, and no *harm* was found on him, as no *harm* was found on the three in the fire (3:25). Hebrews names the shutting of lions' mouths among the deeds of faith (Hebrews 11:33).
