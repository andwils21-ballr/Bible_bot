# Daniel 8

## The Vision

**1** In the third year of the reign of King Belshazzar a vision appeared to me, Daniel, after the one that had appeared to me at first.

**2** I saw in the vision, and as I watched I was in Susa the citadel, in the province of Elam; I saw in the vision, and I was by the Ulai canal.

**3** I lifted my eyes and saw a ram standing in front of the canal. It had two horns; both horns were high, but one was higher than the other, and the higher one came up last.

**4** I saw the ram charging west and north and south. No beast could stand before it, and no one could rescue from its power. It did as it pleased and made itself great.

**5** As I was considering, a male goat came from the west across the face of the whole earth without touching the ground, and the goat had a conspicuous horn between its eyes.

**6** It came to the ram with the two horns, which I had seen standing in front of the canal, and ran at it in the fury of its strength.

**7** I saw it reach the ram. It was enraged against it and struck the ram and broke its two horns, and the ram had no strength to stand before it. It threw the ram down to the ground and trampled on it, and no one could rescue the ram from its power.

**8** The male goat made itself exceedingly great; but when it was strong, the great horn was broken, and in its place came up four conspicuous horns toward the four winds of heaven.

**9** Out of one of them came a horn, starting small, which grew exceedingly great toward the south, toward the east, and toward the beautiful land.

**10** It grew great, up to the host of heaven, and it threw some of the host and some of the stars down to the earth and trampled on them.

**11** It made itself great even up to the Prince of the host; the continual offering was taken away from Him, and the place of His sanctuary was thrown down.

**12** A host will be given over, together with the continual offering, because of transgression; it will throw truth to the ground, and it will act and prosper.

**13** Then I heard a holy one speaking, and another holy one said to the one who was speaking, "How long is the vision — the continual offering, the transgression that desolates, and the giving over of both the sanctuary and the host to be trampled?"

**14** He said to me, "For two thousand three hundred evenings and mornings; then the sanctuary will be set right."

## Gabriel Explains

**15** When I, Daniel, had seen the vision and was seeking to understand it, there stood before me one who looked like a man.

**16** I heard a human voice between the banks of the Ulai, which called out, "Gabriel, make this man understand the vision."

**17** He came near where I stood, and when he came I was terrified and fell on my face. He said to me, "Understand, son of man, that the vision is for the time of the end."

**18** While he was speaking with me, I fell into a deep sleep with my face to the ground; but he touched me and set me on my feet.

**19** He said, "I am about to make known to you what will happen in the last part of the wrath, for it concerns the appointed time of the end.

**20** The ram you saw with the two horns — they are the kings of Media and Persia.

**21** The shaggy male goat is the king of Greece, and the great horn between its eyes is the first king.

**22** As for the horn that was broken, and the four that stood up in its place: four kingdoms will stand up from his nation, but not with his power.

**23** At the latter end of their rule, when the transgressors have reached their full measure, a king will arise, hard of face and skilled in riddles.

**24** His power will be mighty, but not by his own power. He will cause astonishing destruction, and he will succeed in what he does; he will destroy mighty men and the people of the holy ones.

**25** By his cunning he will make deceit prosper under his hand, and in his heart he will make himself great; while they are at ease he will destroy many. He will even stand up against the Prince of princes, but he will be broken, and not by any hand.

**26** The vision of the evenings and the mornings that has been told is true. But you, seal up the vision, for it refers to many days from now."

**27** I, Daniel, was overcome and lay sick for days. Then I rose and did the king's business. I was appalled by the vision, and no one understood it.

## Notes

- **vv1–2 "In the third year… in Susa the citadel"** — the book returns to Hebrew here and stays in it to the end. Susa is the city where Esther's story takes place, *Susa the citadel* (Esther 1:2). *Uval*, **canal**, is found only in this chapter.
- **vv20–21 "Media and Persia… Greece"** — this time the kingdoms are named. *Yavan*, **Greece**, is Javan son of Japheth (Genesis 10:2), and *Madai*, Media, is his brother. The two horns of the ram are one kingdom of two peoples, the higher coming up last (v3).
- **v9 "the beautiful land"** — *ha-tsevi*, **the beauty**, used alone for the land of Israel. Ezekiel calls it *the most beautiful of all lands*, *tsevi hi le-khol ha-aratsot* (Ezekiel 20:6).
- **vv11, 25 "the Prince of the host… the Prince of princes"** — *sar ha-tsava*, the words of the figure who met Joshua, *the commander of the army of the LORD* (Joshua 5:14). The sanctuary in v11 is *His*, and *Prince of princes* is built like *God of gods* (2:47). In chapters 10–12 *sar* is also the title of heavenly princes, among them Michael (10:13, 21; 12:1).
- **v12 "A host will be given over… because of transgression"** — the hardest verse of the chapter. Its words can be read in more than one way: the host given over *with* the continual offering or *against* it, and the transgression the people's or the horn's. Its verbs also turn from what Daniel saw to what will be. The Greek has *and sin was given upon the sacrifice, and righteousness was thrown to the ground*.
- **v13 "the one who was speaking… the transgression that desolates"** — *la-palmoni*, **to so-and-so**, found only here: the two words *peloni almoni* run together, the name Boaz did not say, *Turn aside, friend* (Ruth 4:1). *Ha-pesha shomem*, **the transgression that desolates**, comes back as *shiqquts meshomem* (9:27; 11:31; 12:11), which Jesus quotes from the Greek: *the abomination of desolation, spoken of through Daniel the prophet* (Matthew 24:15).
- **v14 "two thousand three hundred evenings and mornings"** — *erev boqer*, **evening morning**, the way the days of creation are counted: *it was evening, and it was morning — day one* (Genesis 1:5). The number can be read as 2,300 days, or as 2,300 of the continual offerings of v13, made morning and evening (Exodus 29:38–39), which would be 1,150 days. The Greek adds *days*. *Nitsdaq*, **set right**, from the root of *righteous*, is found only here; the Greek has *cleansed*.
- **vv15–18 "one who looked like a man… Gabriel… son of man"** — *gaver*, **man**, and the name that follows, *Gavri'el*, is built on it. He calls Daniel *ben adam*, **son of man**, as God called Ezekiel (Ezekiel 2:1), and as with Ezekiel the prophet falls on his face and is set on his feet (Ezekiel 1:28; 2:2). Gabriel comes again in Luke (Luke 1:19, 26).
- **vv23–25 "hard of face and skilled in riddles… not by any hand"** — *az panim*, the words of the curse in Deuteronomy, *a nation hard of face* (Deuteronomy 28:50). *Riddles* (*chidot*) were Daniel's own gift (5:12). He is broken *be-efes yad*, **without a hand**, as the stone was cut out *not by hands* (2:34, 45).
