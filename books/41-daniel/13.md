# Daniel 13

## The Two Elders

**1** There was a man living in Babylon whose name was Joakim.

**2** He took a wife named Susanna, the daughter of Hilkiah, a very beautiful woman who feared the Lord.

**3** Her parents were righteous, and they had taught their daughter according to the law of Moses.

**4** Joakim was very rich, and he had a garden next to his house. The Jews used to come to him, because he was the most honored of them all.

**5** That year two elders from the people were appointed judges, men of the kind the Master spoke of: "Lawlessness came out of Babylon, from elders who were judges, who were thought to govern the people."

**6** These men spent their time at Joakim's house, and all who had cases to be judged came to them there.

**7** When the people left at midday, Susanna would go into her husband's garden and walk there.

**8** The two elders watched her every day as she went in and walked about, and they began to desire her.

**9** They perverted their minds and turned their eyes away, so as not to look to heaven or remember righteous judgments.

**10** Both were overwhelmed with desire for her, but they did not tell each other of their torment,

**11** for they were ashamed to tell of their desire to lie with her.

**12** Day after day they watched eagerly to see her.

**13** They said to each other, "Let us go home, for it is time for the midday meal." So they went out and parted from each other.

**14** But each turned back, and they met at the same place. When they questioned each other about the reason, they confessed their desire, and then together they agreed on a time when they could find her alone.

## In the Garden

**15** And it came to pass, while they were watching for a suitable day, that she went in as she had on the days before, with only two maids, and she wanted to bathe in the garden, for it was hot.

**16** No one was there except the two elders, who had hidden themselves and were watching her.

**17** She said to the maids, "Bring me oil and ointments, and shut the garden doors so that I can bathe."

**18** They did as she said: they shut the garden doors and went out by the side doors to bring what they had been told to bring, and they did not see the elders, because they were hidden.

**19** As soon as the maids had gone out, the two elders got up and ran to her

**20** and said, "The garden doors are shut, and no one sees us. We desire you; so give in to us and lie with us.

**21** If you refuse, we will testify against you that a young man was with you, and that this is why you sent your maids away."

**22** Susanna groaned and said, "I am hemmed in on every side. If I do this, it is death for me; and if I do not, I will not escape your hands.

**23** It is better for me not to do it and fall into your hands than to sin before the Lord."

**24** Then Susanna cried out with a loud voice, and the two elders shouted against her.

**25** One of them ran and opened the garden doors.

**26** When the people in the house heard the shouting in the garden, they rushed in by the side door to see what had happened to her.

**27** When the elders told their story, the servants were deeply ashamed, for nothing like this had ever been said about Susanna.

## The Trial

**28** And it came to pass the next day, when the people gathered at the house of her husband Joakim, that the two elders came, full of their lawless plan to have Susanna put to death. They said before the people,

**29** "Send for Susanna daughter of Hilkiah, the wife of Joakim." So they sent for her.

**30** She came with her parents, her children and all her relatives.

**31** Susanna was a woman of great refinement and beautiful to look at.

**32** The lawless men ordered her to be unveiled, for she was veiled, so that they might feast their eyes on her beauty.

**33** Those who were with her and all who saw her were weeping.

**34** Then the two elders stood up among the people and laid their hands on her head.

**35** Weeping, she looked up toward heaven, for her heart trusted in the Lord.

**36** The elders said, "While we were walking alone in the garden, this woman came in with two maids, shut the garden doors and sent the maids away.

**37** Then a young man who had been hidden came to her and lay with her.

**38** We were in a corner of the garden, and when we saw this lawlessness we ran to them.

**39** We saw them lying together, but we could not hold the man, because he was stronger than we were, and he opened the doors and got away.

**40** But we seized this woman and asked her who the young man was,

**41** and she would not tell us. To these things we testify." The assembly believed them, since they were elders of the people and judges, and they condemned her to death.

**42** Then Susanna cried out with a loud voice and said, "Eternal God, who knows what is hidden, who knows all things before they come to be,

**43** You know that they have testified falsely against me. Now I am to die, though I have done none of the things these men have wickedly charged against me."

**44** The Lord heard her voice.

## Daniel Judges the Judges

**45** As she was being led away to be put to death, God stirred up the holy spirit of a young boy named Daniel,

**46** and he cried out with a loud voice, "I am innocent of this woman's blood!"

**47** All the people turned to him and said, "What is this you have said?"

**48** Standing among them, he said, "Are you such fools, sons of Israel? Without examining or learning the facts, you have condemned a daughter of Israel?

**49** Go back to the court, for these men have testified falsely against her."

**50** So all the people hurried back. The elders said to him, "Come, sit among us and tell us, for God has given you the standing of an elder."

**51** Daniel said to them, "Separate them far from each other, and I will examine them."

**52** When they had been separated from each other, he called one of them and said to him, "You who have grown old in wicked days, your sins have now come home, the sins you committed before,

**53** pronouncing unjust judgments, condemning the innocent and letting the guilty go free, though the Lord said, 'Do not kill the innocent and the righteous.'

**54** So, if you really saw her, tell me: under what tree did you see them being intimate with each other?" He answered, "Under a mastic tree."

**55** Daniel said, "Right! You have lied against your own head, for the messenger of God has already received the sentence from God and will split you in two."

**56** He had him put aside and ordered the other to be brought. He said to him, "Offspring of Canaan and not of Judah, beauty has deceived you, and desire has perverted your heart.

**57** This is how you have been treating the daughters of Israel, and they were afraid and gave in to you; but a daughter of Judah would not endure your lawlessness.

**58** So tell me: under what tree did you catch them being intimate with each other?" He answered, "Under an evergreen oak."

**59** Daniel said to him, "Right! You too have lied against your own head, for the messenger of God is waiting with his sword to saw you in two, to destroy you both."

**60** Then the whole assembly cried out with a loud voice and blessed God, who saves those who hope in Him.

**61** They rose up against the two elders, for Daniel had convicted them out of their own mouths of being false witnesses, and they did to them as they had wickedly planned to do to their neighbor,

**62** acting according to the law of Moses, and they put them to death. So innocent blood was spared that day.

**63** Hilkiah and his wife praised God for their daughter, with Joakim her husband and all her relatives, because nothing shameful was found in her.

**64** From that day onward Daniel was great in the eyes of the people.

## Notes

- **v1 the story of Susanna** — there is no Hebrew or Aramaic for chapters 13 and 14; this canon counts the two Greek stories as Daniel 13 and 14. The text here is Theodotion's Greek, the version quoted in the notes on chapters 1–12 and the one the English verse numbers follow. The Old Greek tells the story in sixty verses, more briefly and in other words.
- **v2 "Susanna"** — *Sousanna*, the Hebrew *shoshannah*, **lily**: *Like a lily among thorns, so is my darling among the young women* (Song of Songs 2:2).
- **v4 "a garden"** — *paradeisos*, the Greek word for the garden God planted *in Eden* (Genesis 2:8). Every scene until the trial takes place there.
- **v5 "Lawlessness came out of Babylon, from elders who were judges"** — no prophet in the Bible says these words. Jeremiah writes of two men in Babylon, *Ahab son of Kolaiah and Zedekiah son of Maaseiah*, who *committed adultery with their neighbors' wives* and whom the king of Babylon *roasted in the fire* (Jeremiah 29:21–23).
- **vv22–23 "I am hemmed in on every side… fall into your hands"** — *Stena moi pantothen*, David's words in the Greek of 2 Samuel 24:14, when he chose: *Let us fall into the hand of the LORD… but let me not fall into the hand of man*. Susanna chooses the other way: the hands of men rather than sin before the Lord.
- **v45 "the holy spirit of a young boy"** — *to pneuma to hagion paidariou*, **the spirit, the holy one, of a boy**. *To pneuma to hagion* is the phrase the New Testament uses for the Holy Spirit; here it is followed by *of a young boy*, which makes it the boy's own spirit. It can also be read as the Holy Spirit that God stirred up in the boy.
- **v46 "I am innocent of this woman's blood!"** — *Athōos egō apo tou haimatos tautēs*, the words Pilate used when he washed his hands: *I am innocent of this man's blood* (Matthew 27:24).
- **v52 "You who have grown old in wicked days"** — *pepalaiōmene hēmerōn kakōn*. In the Greek of 7:9 the judge on the throne is *palaios hēmerōn*, **the Ancient of Days**; the elder who judged falsely is *one grown old in evil days*.
- **vv54–59 "a mastic tree… split you in two… an evergreen oak… saw you in two"** — wordplay in the Greek: *schinos*, **mastic**, and *schisei*, **he will split**; *prinos*, **oak**, and *prisai*, **to saw**. Each elder names the tree of his own sentence.
- **vv34, 53, 61–62 the law of Moses** — the witnesses lay their hands on the head of the accused, as the law has *all who heard lay their hands on his head* (Leviticus 24:14). Daniel quotes *Do not kill the innocent and the righteous* (Exodus 23:7). The false witnesses receive what they planned, as the law says, *you shall do to him as he planned to do to his brother* (Deuteronomy 19:19), in the same Greek words.
