# Daniel 11

## Persia and Greece

**1** "As for me, in the first year of Darius the Mede, I stood up to strengthen him and be a fortress for him.

**2** Now I will tell you the truth. Three more kings will arise in Persia, and the fourth will be far richer than all of them; and when he has grown strong through his riches, he will stir up everyone against the kingdom of Greece.

**3** Then a mighty king will arise, who will rule with great dominion and do as he pleases.

**4** But as soon as he has arisen, his kingdom will be broken and divided toward the four winds of heaven, but not to his descendants, nor with the dominion he ruled with; for his kingdom will be uprooted and given to others besides these.

## The South and the North

**5** Then the king of the south will grow strong, but one of his princes will grow stronger than he and will rule, and his dominion will be a great dominion.

**6** At the end of some years they will make an alliance, and the daughter of the king of the south will come to the king of the north to make an agreement. But she will not keep the strength of her arm, and he and his arm will not stand; she will be given up, with those who brought her, her father, and the one who supported her in those times.

**7** But a branch from her roots will arise in his place. He will come against the army and enter the fortress of the king of the north, and he will deal with them and prevail.

**8** He will also carry off to Egypt as plunder their gods, with their cast images and their precious vessels of silver and gold; and for some years he will keep away from the king of the north.

**9** Then he will enter the kingdom of the king of the south and return to his own land.

**10** His sons will stir themselves up and gather a multitude of great forces, and one of them will surely come and overflow and pass through; then he will wage war again, up to his fortress.

**11** Then the king of the south, enraged, will go out and fight with him, with the king of the north. He will raise a great multitude, but the multitude will be given into his hand.

**12** When the multitude is carried off, his heart will be lifted up; he will bring down tens of thousands, but he will not prevail.

**13** For the king of the north will again raise a multitude, greater than the first, and at the end of the times, after some years, he will surely come with a great army and much equipment.

**14** In those times many will rise against the king of the south, and the violent men of your own people will lift themselves up to fulfill the vision, but they will stumble.

**15** Then the king of the north will come and throw up a siege mound and take a fortified city, and the forces of the south will not stand, not even his choice troops; there will be no strength to stand.

**16** He who comes against him will do as he pleases, and no one will stand before him; he will stand in the beautiful land, with destruction in his hand.

**17** He will set his face to come with the strength of his whole kingdom, bringing terms of agreement, and he will carry them out; he will give him a daughter in marriage, to destroy it; but it will not stand or be his.

**18** Then he will turn his face to the coastlands and capture many; but a commander will put an end to his insolence, and more than that, he will turn his insolence back on him.

**19** Then he will turn his face back toward the fortresses of his own land, but he will stumble and fall and not be found.

**20** Then in his place one will arise who sends an exactor through the glory of the kingdom; but within a few days he will be broken, though not in anger or in battle.

## The Contemptible King

**21** In his place will arise a contemptible man, on whom royal majesty has not been conferred. He will come in while they are at ease and seize the kingdom by smooth words.

**22** Overwhelming forces will be swept away before him and broken, and the prince of the covenant as well.

**23** After an alliance is made with him he will act deceitfully; he will rise and become strong with a small nation.

**24** While they are at ease he will enter the richest parts of the province and do what neither his fathers nor his fathers' fathers did, scattering plunder, spoil and goods among them. He will devise plans against fortresses, but only for a time.

**25** He will stir up his strength and his courage against the king of the south with a great army, and the king of the south will wage war with a very great and mighty army; but he will not stand, for plans will be devised against him.

**26** Those who eat his royal food will break him; his army will be swept away, and many will fall slain.

**27** The two kings, their hearts set on evil, will speak lies at one table; but it will not succeed, for the end is still to come at the appointed time.

**28** He will return to his land with great wealth, but his heart will be set against the holy covenant; he will act, and return to his land.

**29** At the appointed time he will come again into the south, but this time it will not be as it was before.

**30** Ships of Kittim will come against him, and he will lose heart and turn back. He will rage against the holy covenant and take action; he will turn back and pay heed to those who forsake the holy covenant.

**31** Forces from him will rise up and profane the sanctuary, the fortress, and take away the continual offering, and set up the loathsome thing that desolates.

**32** Those who act wickedly against the covenant he will corrupt with smooth words; but the people who know their God will stand firm and take action.

**33** The wise among the people will give understanding to the many, though for some days they will stumble by sword and flame, by captivity and plunder.

**34** When they stumble, they will receive a little help, and many will join them with smooth words.

**35** Some of the wise will stumble, to refine, purify and make them white, until the time of the end, for it is still to come at the appointed time.

## The King Who Exalts Himself

**36** The king will do as he pleases. He will exalt himself and magnify himself above every god, and against the God of gods he will speak astonishing things. He will prosper until the wrath is complete, for what has been decreed will be done.

**37** He will pay no heed to the gods of his fathers, or to the one desired by women; he will pay no heed to any god, for he will magnify himself above all.

**38** Instead, he will honor the god of fortresses in its place; a god his fathers did not know he will honor with gold and silver, with precious stones and precious things.

**39** He will deal with the strongest fortresses with the help of a foreign god. Those who acknowledge him he will load with honor; he will make them rulers over the many and divide the land for a price.

**40** At the time of the end the king of the south will charge at him, and the king of the north will storm against him with chariots and horsemen and many ships. He will enter the lands, overflow them and pass through.

**41** He will enter the beautiful land, and many will stumble; but these will escape from his hand: Edom, Moab and the chief of the sons of Ammon.

**42** He will stretch out his hand against the lands, and the land of Egypt will not escape.

**43** He will rule over the hidden treasures of gold and silver and all the precious things of Egypt, and the Libyans and the Cushites will follow in his steps.

**44** But reports from the east and the north will alarm him, and he will go out in great fury to destroy many and destroy them completely.

**45** He will pitch his royal tents between the seas and the beautiful holy mountain; yet he will come to his end, and no one will help him.

## Notes

- **v1 "in the first year of Darius the Mede"** — the man in linen is still speaking, from 10:21. *Him* is most likely Michael, the last one named: the speaker stood by Michael then, as Michael stands by him now. It could also be Darius. The Greek has *in the first year of Cyrus*.
- **vv2–45 the kings** — after Persia and Greece (vv2–4), not one king in the chapter is named. They are *the king of the south* and *the king of the north*, and the only places named are Egypt, Kittim, Edom, Moab, Ammon, Libya and Cush. *Ma'oz*, **fortress**, runs through the chapter, from the speaker's own help in the first year of Darius (v1) to *the god of fortresses* (v38).
- **v7 "a branch from her roots"** — *netser shorasheha*, the words of Isaiah's king from Jesse's line, *a branch from his roots will bear fruit* (Isaiah 11:1), here used of a southern king's heir.
- **v22 "the prince of the covenant"** — *negid berit*. *Nagid*, **prince**, is the word of 9:25–26, *an anointed one, a prince* and *the prince who is to come*. Who the prince of the covenant is, the chapter does not say.
- **v26 "Those who eat his royal food"** — *okhlei fat-bago*, the *pat-bag* that Daniel would not eat at Nebuchadnezzar's court (1:5, 8). The king is broken by the men who eat at his table.
- **v30 "Ships of Kittim"** — Balaam's last oracle: *Ships shall come from the coast of Kittim, and they shall afflict Asshur* (Numbers 24:24), *tsim mi-yad Kittim*; here *tsiyyim Kittim*.
- **v31 "the loathsome thing that desolates"** — *ha-shiqquts meshomem*, with the continual offering taken away, as in 8:11–13 and 9:27, and again in 12:11. The Greek has *an abomination made desolate*; Jesus quotes it as *the abomination of desolation* (Matthew 24:15).
- **vv33–35 "The wise among the people… to refine, purify and make them white"** — *maskilei am*, who *give understanding to the many*. They return at the end of the book, where *those who are wise will shine* and many will be *purified, made white and refined* (12:3, 10).
- **vv36–39 "against the God of gods… the one desired by women… the god of fortresses"** — the king speaks as the little horn did, *words against the Most High* (7:25), and stands against *the Prince of princes* (8:25). *Chemdat nashim*, **the desire of women**, is obscure: a god whom women love, or the love of women itself. *Eloah ma'uzzim*, **the god of fortresses**, is left untranslated in the Greek, *Maōzin*.
- **v45 "he will come to his end, and no one will help him"** — he dies as the horn of 8:25 was broken, *not by any hand*; no battle is described. The faithful were given *a little help* (v34); he is given none.
