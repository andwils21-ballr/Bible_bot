# Tobit 1

## Tobit and His Tribe

**1** The book of the words of Tobit son of Tobiel, son of Ananiel, son of Aduel, son of Gabael, of the descendants of Asiel, of the tribe of Naphtali,

**2** who was taken captive in the days of Shalmaneser king of the Assyrians from Thisbe, which is south of Kedesh of Naphtali in Galilee, above Asher.

**3** I, Tobit, walked in the ways of truth and righteousness all the days of my life, and I did many acts of charity for my brothers and my nation who had gone with me into captivity to the land of the Assyrians, to Nineveh.

**4** When I was in my own country, in the land of Israel, while I was still young, the whole tribe of Naphtali my father fell away from the house of Jerusalem, which had been chosen out of all the tribes of Israel for all the tribes to sacrifice there. There the temple, the dwelling of the Most High, was consecrated and built for all generations forever.

**5** All the tribes that fell away together sacrificed to Baal the heifer, and so did the house of Naphtali my father.

**6** I alone went often to Jerusalem for the festivals, as it is written for all Israel by an everlasting decree, taking the firstfruits and the tithes of the crops and the first shearings,

**7** and I gave them to the priests, the sons of Aaron, for the altar. The tenth of all my produce I gave to the sons of Levi who served at Jerusalem. The second tenth I sold, and I went and spent the money in Jerusalem each year.

**8** The third I gave to those to whom it was due, as Deborah my father's mother had commanded me, for I had been left an orphan by my father.

**9** When I became a man, I married Anna, a woman of our own family, and by her I had a son, Tobias.

## In Nineveh

**10** When we were carried away captive to Nineveh, all my brothers and those of my race ate the food of the nations,

**11** but I kept myself from eating it,

**12** because I remembered God with all my soul.

**13** The Most High gave me favor and good standing before Shalmaneser, and I became his buyer of supplies.

**14** I used to go into Media, and once at Rages in Media I left ten talents of silver in trust with Gabael, the brother of Gabrias.

**15** When Shalmaneser died, Sennacherib his son reigned in his place. His roads became unsafe, and I could no longer go into Media.

**16** In the days of Shalmaneser I did many acts of charity for my brothers.

**17** I gave my bread to the hungry and clothing to the naked, and if I saw any of my people dead and thrown out behind the wall of Nineveh, I buried him.

**18** If King Sennacherib killed any who came fleeing from Judea, I buried them in secret, for in his anger he killed many. When the king looked for the bodies, they could not be found.

**19** Then one of the men of Nineveh went and informed the king about me, that I was burying them, and I hid. When I learned that I was being sought to be put to death, I was afraid and ran away.

**20** All my property was seized, and nothing was left to me except Anna my wife and Tobias my son.

**21** But not fifty days passed before his two sons killed him and fled to the mountains of Ararat. Esarhaddon his son reigned in his place, and he put Ahikar son of Anael, my brother's son, over all the accounts of his kingdom and over all its administration.

**22** Ahikar spoke for me, and I came back to Nineveh. Ahikar had been cupbearer, keeper of the signet, administrator and treasurer, and Esarhaddon appointed him second to himself. He was my nephew.

## Notes

- **v1 "The book of the words of Tobit"** — rendered from the Greek, the shorter of its two forms (Swete's main text); the longer Greek is cited where it differs in substance. The names follow the English forms readers know: Greek *Enemessar* is **Shalmaneser** (2 Kings 17:3), *Sacherdonos* is **Esarhaddon**, and *Achiacharus* is **Ahikar**.
- **v3 "acts of charity"** — *eleēmosynai*, from *eleos*, **mercy**: mercy shown in what is given. It is the book's word, said of Tobit at the start and taught to his son at the end.
- **v5 "sacrificed to Baal the heifer"** — the Greek puts the feminine article before *Baal*. The longer Greek says plainly *to the calf that Jeroboam king of Israel made in Dan* (1 Kings 12:28–30).
- **vv6–8 "the firstfruits and the tithes… the second tenth… the third"** — three tithes: the Levites' tenth (Numbers 18:21); a second, turned into money and spent in Jerusalem (Deuteronomy 14:22–26); and a third for *the guest, the orphan and the widow* (14:28–29). Tobit, an orphan himself, gives the third.
- **vv10–12 "I kept myself from eating it"** — as Daniel would in Babylon: *Daniel resolved that he would not defile himself with the king's food* (Daniel 1:8).
- **vv15, 18 "Sennacherib"** — the Greek held here reads *Acherel*, a name found nowhere else. The longer Greek has *Sennacherim*, and v21 tells his end as Kings tells Sennacherib's: his two sons killed him and fled to the land of Ararat, and *Esarhaddon his son reigned in his place* (2 Kings 19:37).
- **vv17–18 "I buried him"** — the act the whole story turns on: Tobit's burying of the dead costs him his goods here and his sight in the next chapter.
- **vv21–22 "Ahikar… my nephew"** — the highest official of the Assyrian court is family, and the reason Tobit can come home.
