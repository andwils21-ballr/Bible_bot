# Tobit 2

## The Feast of Weeks

**1** When I had come home and Anna my wife and Tobias my son had been given back to me, at the festival of Pentecost, which is the holy festival of seven weeks, a good dinner was prepared for me, and I reclined to eat.

**2** I saw the many dishes, and I said to my son, "Go and bring whatever poor man you find among our brothers who remembers the Lord. I will wait for you."

**3** He came back and said, "Father, one of our people has been strangled and thrown out in the marketplace."

**4** Before I had tasted anything, I sprang up and carried him into a room until the sun went down.

**5** Then I came back, washed myself and ate my bread in sorrow.

**6** I remembered the prophecy of Amos, how he said, "Your festivals shall be turned into mourning, and all your merrymaking into lamentation."

**7** I wept. When the sun had gone down, I went and dug a grave and buried him.

**8** My neighbors laughed at me and said, "He is no longer afraid of being put to death for this. He ran away, and here he is burying the dead again."

## The Sparrows

**9** That same night, after I had buried him, I came back, and because I was unclean I slept by the wall of the courtyard, with my face uncovered.

**10** I did not know that there were sparrows in the wall. My eyes were open, and the sparrows dropped warm droppings into my eyes, and white films formed on my eyes. I went to physicians, but they did not help me. Ahikar took care of me until he went to Elymais.

## Anna and the Goat

**11** My wife Anna earned money at women's work,

**12** and she sent it to the owners, and they paid her wages and also gave her a young goat.

**13** When she came back to me, the goat began to bleat. I said to her, "Where did this kid come from? It was not stolen, was it? Give it back to its owners, for it is not right to eat anything stolen."

**14** She said, "It was given to me as a gift, besides my wages." But I did not believe her, and I told her to give it back to its owners, and I flushed with anger at her. Then she answered me, "Where are your acts of charity and your righteous deeds? See, everything about you is plain to see."

## Notes

- **v1 "the festival of Pentecost, which is the holy festival of seven weeks"** — *Pentecost*, **the fiftieth**, the Greek name for the Festival of Weeks, kept *seven weeks* after the harvest began (Deuteronomy 16:9–10).
- **v2 "whatever poor man you find… who remembers the Lord"** — Tobit's charity at his own table: the feast is for a guest who is poor and faithful.
- **v6 "Your festivals shall be turned into mourning"** — Amos 8:10, *I will turn your feasts into mourning, and all your songs into lamentation*. Tobit's feast is turned exactly so.
- **v9 "because I was unclean"** — whoever touches a dead body *is unclean seven days* (Numbers 19:11), so he does not go into the house.
- **v10 "white films formed on my eyes"** — *leukōmata*, **white spots**, a clouding of the eye. The longer Greek says the droppings *brought on* the white films. Tobit's blindness comes from his burying of the dead, and the physicians cannot help.
- **v14 "Where are your acts of charity and your righteous deeds?"** — Anna turns his own words against him (1:3). The reproach of a wife to a righteous man struck down recalls Job's: *Do you still hold fast your integrity?* (Job 2:9).
