# Judith 4

## Fear in Judea

**1** The sons of Israel who lived in Judea heard all that Holofernes, the commander in chief of Nebuchadnezzar king of the Assyrians, had done to the nations, and how he had plundered all their temples and given them up to destruction.

**2** They were terrified of him, and they were alarmed for Jerusalem and for the temple of the Lord their God.

**3** For they had only lately come up from the captivity, and all the people of Judea had only just been gathered together, and the vessels, the altar and the house had been consecrated after their profanation.

## The Passes Held

**4** So they sent word to every region of Samaria, and to Kona, Beth-horon, Belmain and Jericho, and to Choba, Aesora and the valley of Salem,

**5** and they occupied all the tops of the high mountains, fortified the villages on them and stored up food in preparation for war, for their fields had just been harvested.

**6** Joakim the high priest, who was in Jerusalem in those days, wrote to those who lived in Bethulia and Betomesthaim, which faces Esdraelon opposite the plain near Dothan,

**7** telling them to hold the passes into the hill country, since through them was the way into Judea. It was easy to stop any who tried to come up, because the approach was narrow, wide enough for two men at most.

**8** The sons of Israel did as Joakim the high priest and the council of the whole people of Israel, sitting in Jerusalem, had commanded them.

## The Cry to God

**9** Every man of Israel cried out to God with great fervor, and they humbled themselves with great fervor,

**10** they and their wives and their little children and their cattle. Every guest, hired worker and purchased slave put sackcloth around his waist.

**11** All the men, women and children of Israel who lived in Jerusalem fell down before the temple, put ashes on their heads and spread out their sackcloth before the Lord.

**12** They even draped the altar in sackcloth, and they cried out with one accord and fervently to the God of Israel, not to give up their little children to be seized, or their wives to be carried off, or the cities of their inheritance to be destroyed, or the sanctuary to be profaned and mocked, a thing for the nations to gloat over.

**13** The Lord heard their voice and looked on their distress. The people fasted many days in all Judea and in Jerusalem before the sanctuary of the Lord Almighty.

**14** Joakim the high priest and all the priests who stood before the Lord and ministered to the Lord, with sackcloth around their waists, offered the continual burnt offering and the vows and the freewill offerings of the people,

**15** with ashes on their turbans, and they cried out to the Lord with all their might to look with favor on the whole house of Israel.

## Notes

- **v3 "they had only lately come up from the captivity… the house had been consecrated after their profanation"** — the book's Israel has returned and rebuilt, as in Ezra and Nehemiah, and now fears for the house just made holy again.
- **v7 "wide enough for two men at most"** — the whole land's defense rests on narrow passes held by one town, Bethulia, where the story will stay.
- **vv9–10 "they humbled themselves… they and their wives and their little children and their cattle"** — as Nineveh fasted at Jonah's word: *let man and beast be covered with sackcloth* (Jonah 3:8).
- **v10 "Every guest, hired worker and purchased slave"** — *paroikos*, the Greek for *ger*, **the guest** of the law, joins the fast with Israel.
- **v13 "the Lord Almighty"** — *Kyrios Pantokratōr*, **Lord, ruler of all**, the Greek for *the LORD of hosts*. The answer to a king who calls himself *lord of the whole earth* (2:5).
