# Judith 1

## The War against Arphaxad

**1** In the twelfth year of the reign of Nebuchadnezzar, who reigned over the Assyrians in the great city of Nineveh, in the days of Arphaxad, who reigned over the Medes in Ecbatana,

**2** this Arphaxad built walls around Ecbatana of hewn stones three cubits wide and six cubits long. He made the walls seventy cubits high and fifty cubits wide.

**3** At its gates he set towers a hundred cubits high, and he laid their foundations sixty cubits wide.

**4** He made its gates, gates that rose to a height of seventy cubits and were forty cubits wide, for the marching out of his mighty forces and the drawing up of his foot soldiers.

**5** In those days King Nebuchadnezzar made war against King Arphaxad in the great plain, which is in the region of Ragau.

**6** There came to meet him all who lived in the hill country and all who lived along the Euphrates, the Tigris and the Hydaspes, and in the plain Arioch king of the Elymaeans. Many nations joined the ranks of the sons of Cheleoul.

## The Nations Refuse

**7** Then Nebuchadnezzar king of the Assyrians sent to all who lived in Persia and to all who lived in the west, to those who lived in Cilicia and Damascus, Lebanon and Antilebanon, and to all who lived along the seacoast,

**8** and to those among the nations of Carmel and Gilead, and to Upper Galilee and the great plain of Esdraelon,

**9** and to all who were in Samaria and its towns, and beyond the Jordan as far as Jerusalem, Bethany, Chelous and Kadesh, and the river of Egypt, Tahpanhes, Raamses and all the land of Goshen,

**10** until one comes beyond Tanis and Memphis, and to all who lived in Egypt as far as the borders of Ethiopia.

**11** But all who lived in the whole land made light of the summons of Nebuchadnezzar king of the Assyrians and did not join him in the war. They were not afraid of him, but looked on him as a man like themselves, and they sent his messengers back empty-handed and in disgrace.

**12** Nebuchadnezzar was furious with all this land, and he swore by his throne and his kingdom that he would take vengeance on all the territory of Cilicia, Damascus and Syria, and would kill with his sword all who lived in the land of Moab, the sons of Ammon, all Judea and all who were in Egypt, as far as the borders of the two seas.

## The Defeat of Arphaxad

**13** In the seventeenth year he drew up his forces against King Arphaxad and prevailed in the battle. He overthrew all the army of Arphaxad, all his cavalry and all his chariots,

**14** and became master of his cities. He came as far as Ecbatana, took its towers, plundered its streets and turned its beauty into shame.

**15** He captured Arphaxad in the mountains of Ragau, struck him through with his hunting spears and destroyed him once and for all.

**16** Then he returned with them, he and his army, for a hundred and twenty days.

## Notes

- **v1 "Nebuchadnezzar, who reigned over the Assyrians in the great city of Nineveh"** — Kings and Chronicles know Nebuchadnezzar as *king of Babylon* (2 Kings 24:1), and Nineveh as the city whose fall Tobias lived to hear of (Tobit 14:15). The book sets its story in a world where the great enemies of Israel are joined into one king.
- **v1 "Arphaxad, who reigned over the Medes"** — the name of a son of Shem (Genesis 10:22). He is known nowhere else as a king.
- **vv2–4 "seventy cubits high and fifty cubits wide… towers a hundred cubits high"** — the walls of the city that will fall in v14. The more the numbers grow, the greater the king who takes it.
- **v6 "Arioch king of the Elymaeans"** — a name from the war of the four kings, *Arioch king of Ellasar* (Genesis 14:1).
- **v11 "looked on him as a man like themselves"** — *hōs anēr isos*, **as an equal man**. The book is about who is God and who is a man; the nations do not yet fear him.
- **v12 "he swore by his throne and his kingdom"** — the king swears by himself. Its counterpart is his title in the next chapter, *the lord of the whole earth* (2:5).
