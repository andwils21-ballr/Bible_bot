# Judith 10

## Judith Makes Herself Ready

**1** And it came to pass, when she had stopped crying out to the God of Israel and had finished all these words,

**2** that she rose from where she had fallen, called her maid, and went down into the house where she used to stay on the sabbaths and her festivals.

**3** She took off the sackcloth she had been wearing and put off the clothes of her widowhood. She bathed her body with water and anointed herself with rich perfume, arranged her hair and put a headband on it, and put on the festive clothes she used to wear while her husband Manasseh was alive.

**4** She put sandals on her feet, and put on her anklets, bracelets, rings, earrings and all her jewelry. She made herself very beautiful, to catch the eye of any man who might see her.

**5** She gave her maid a skin of wine and a flask of oil, and filled a bag with roasted grain, a cake of dried figs and clean bread. She wrapped up all her vessels and gave them to her to carry.

**6** They went out to the gate of the city of Bethulia and found Uzziah standing there with the elders of the city, Chabris and Charmis.

**7** When they saw her, her face changed and her clothing altered, they were amazed at her beauty, and they said to her,

**8** "May the God of our fathers grant you favor and fulfill your plans, to the glory of the sons of Israel and the exaltation of Jerusalem." She worshiped God,

**9** and said to them, "Order the gate of the city to be opened for me, and I will go out to accomplish the things you spoke with me about." So they ordered the young men to open it for her, as she had said.

**10** They did so, and Judith went out, she and her maid with her. The men of the city watched her until she had gone down the mountain and crossed the valley, and they could see her no longer.

## Into the Assyrian Lines

**11** They went straight on through the valley, and an Assyrian outpost met her.

**12** They took her and asked, "Whose are you? Where do you come from, and where are you going?" She said, "I am a daughter of the Hebrews, and I am fleeing from them, because they are about to be given to you to be devoured.

**13** I am on my way to the presence of Holofernes, the commander in chief of your army, to give him a true report. I will show him a way by which he can go and take all the hill country without losing one of his men, not one body or one living breath."

**14** When the men heard her words and looked at her face, it seemed to them wonderfully beautiful, and they said to her,

**15** "You have saved your life by hurrying down to the presence of our lord. Now go to his tent; some of us will escort you until they hand you over to him.

**16** When you stand before him, do not be afraid in your heart, but tell him what you have told us, and he will treat you well."

**17** They chose a hundred of their men to accompany her and her maid, and they brought them to the tent of Holofernes.

**18** There was a commotion in the whole camp, for her arrival was reported from tent to tent. They came and stood around her as she waited outside the tent of Holofernes, until they told him about her.

**19** They marveled at her beauty and admired the sons of Israel because of her, and each said to his neighbor, "Who can despise this people, who have women like this among them? It is not wise to leave a single man of them alive, for if they are let go, they will be able to outwit the whole world."

## Before Holofernes

**20** Then those who slept near Holofernes and all his servants came out and brought her into the tent.

**21** Holofernes was resting on his bed under a canopy woven of purple and gold, with emeralds and precious stones.

**22** When they told him about her, he came out to the front of the tent, with silver lamps carried before him.

**23** When Judith came before him and his servants, they all marveled at the beauty of her face. She fell on her face and bowed down to him, and his servants raised her up.

## Notes

- **vv3–4 "She took off the sackcloth… put on the festive clothes"** — the widow of three years (8:4–5) dresses as a bride. *To catch the eye of any man who might see her*: the book says plainly what the dress is for.
- **v5 "clean bread"** — *artōn katharōn*, food she may eat under the law. She takes her own into the camp and will not eat Holofernes' food (12:1–2).
- **v12 "I am a daughter of the Hebrews"** — as Jonah answered the sailors, *I am a Hebrew, and I fear the LORD, the God of heaven* (Jonah 1:9).
- **v13 "without losing one of his men, not one body or one living breath"** — true in a way Holofernes will not hear: when Judith's plan is done, it is the Assyrians who flee (15:1–3).
- **v19 "Who can despise this people, who have women like this among them?"** — the enemy's soldiers say it of her beauty. The book means it of more.
- **v21 "under a canopy woven of purple and gold"** — *kōnōpion*, a net hung against mosquitoes, the word that gives English *canopy*. It will come down with him (13:9).
