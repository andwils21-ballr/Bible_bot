# Judith 12

## Three Days in the Camp

**1** Then he ordered them to bring her in where his silver dinnerware was kept, and he commanded that a table be set for her from his own delicacies and that she drink from his own wine.

**2** But Judith said, "I will not eat of these, so that it will not be an offense, but I will be supplied from the things I have brought with me."

**3** Holofernes said to her, "If what you have with you runs out, where will we get more like it to give you? For none of your people is here with us."

**4** Judith said to him, "As your soul lives, my lord, your servant will not use up what I have with me before the Lord carries out by my hand what He has planned."

**5** Then Holofernes' servants brought her into the tent, and she slept until midnight. She rose toward the morning watch

**6** and sent word to Holofernes, saying, "Let my lord now give orders to allow your servant to go out and pray."

**7** Holofernes commanded his bodyguards not to stop her. So she stayed in the camp three days, and each night she went out into the valley of Bethulia and bathed at the spring in the camp.

**8** When she came up, she prayed to the Lord God of Israel to direct her way for the raising up of the sons of her people.

**9** Then she came back purified and stayed in the tent until she took her food toward evening.

## The Invitation

**10** And it came to pass on the fourth day that Holofernes held a drinking feast for his own servants only, and did not invite any of the officers.

**11** He said to Bagoas the eunuch, who was in charge of all his affairs, "Go now and persuade the Hebrew woman who is in your care to come to us and eat and drink with us.

**12** It would be a disgrace for us to let such a woman go without enjoying her company. If we do not draw her to us, she will laugh at us."

**13** So Bagoas went out from the presence of Holofernes, went in to her and said, "Let this lovely maidservant not hesitate to come to my lord, to be honored in his presence, and to drink wine with us in gladness, and to become today like one of the daughters of the Assyrians who serve in the house of Nebuchadnezzar."

**14** Judith said to him, "Who am I to refuse my lord? Whatever is pleasing in his eyes I will hurry to do, and it will be a joy to me until the day of my death."

**15** So she got up and dressed herself in her garments and all her finery. Her maid went ahead and spread on the ground before Holofernes the fleeces she had received from Bagoas for her daily use, to recline on while she ate.

## The Feast

**16** Judith came in and lay down, and Holofernes' heart was carried away by her and his soul was stirred. He was overcome with desire to lie with her, for he had been watching for a chance to seduce her from the day he first saw her.

**17** Holofernes said to her, "Drink now, and be merry with us."

**18** Judith said, "I will drink now, my lord, for my life means more to me today than in all the days since I was born."

**19** Then she took and ate and drank before him what her maid had prepared.

**20** Holofernes was delighted with her, and he drank a great deal of wine, more than he had ever drunk in a single day since he was born.

## Notes

- **vv1–2 "I will not eat of these, so that it will not be an offense"** — *skandalon*, a stumbling block. Like Daniel at the king's table (Daniel 1:8), and like Tobit in Nineveh (Tobit 1:10–12), she keeps the food laws in the enemy's house, and the bread she brought (10:5) is enough.
- **v4 "As your soul lives, my lord… before the Lord carries out by my hand what He has planned"** — again two lords in one sentence; the plan she speaks of is not Holofernes'.
- **v7 "each night she went out into the valley… and bathed at the spring"** — the nightly going out that Holofernes allowed (11:17) is the way out she will need on the last night (13:10).
- **v14 "it will be a joy to me until the day of my death"** — true as she means it; the day of death in view is Holofernes'.
- **v18 "my life means more to me today than in all the days since I was born"** — *emegalynthē to zēn mou*, **my life has been made great**. The banquet is the day her life is staked on.
- **v20 "more than he had ever drunk in a single day since he was born"** — Judith speaks of *all the days since I was born*; the narrator answers with Holofernes' own.
