# 1 Samuel 11

## Nahash

**1** Nahash the Ammonite went up and camped against Jabesh-gilead. All the men of Jabesh said to Nahash, "Make a covenant with us, and we will serve you."

**2** But Nahash the Ammonite said to them, "On this condition I will make a covenant with you: that I gouge out every right eye of yours, and so bring disgrace on all Israel."

**3** The elders of Jabesh said to him, "Give us seven days, so that we may send messengers through all the territory of Israel. Then, if there is no one to save us, we will come out to you."

**4** When the messengers came to Gibeah of Saul, they told the news in the hearing of the people, and all the people lifted up their voices and wept.

## Saul Rises

**5** Just then Saul was coming behind the oxen from the field. Saul said, "What is wrong with the people, that they are weeping?" So they told him the words of the men of Jabesh.

**6** The Spirit of God rushed on Saul when he heard these words, and his anger burned hot.

**7** He took a pair of oxen and cut them in pieces and sent them throughout all the territory of Israel by the hand of the messengers, saying, "Whoever does not come out after Saul and after Samuel, so shall it be done to his oxen!" The dread of the LORD fell on the people, and they came out as one man.

**8** He mustered them at Bezek. The sons of Israel were three hundred thousand, and the men of Judah thirty thousand.

**9** They said to the messengers who had come, "Say this to the men of Jabesh-gilead: 'Tomorrow, by the time the sun is hot, you shall have deliverance.'" When the messengers came and told the men of Jabesh, they rejoiced.

**10** So the men of Jabesh said, "Tomorrow we will come out to you, and you may do to us whatever seems good in your eyes."

**11** And it came to pass the next day that Saul put the people in three companies. They came into the middle of the camp in the morning watch and struck down the Ammonites until the heat of the day. Those who survived were scattered, so that no two of them were left together.

## The Kingdom Renewed

**12** Then the people said to Samuel, "Who is it that said, 'Shall Saul reign over us?' Give us the men, so that we may put them to death."

**13** But Saul said, "No one shall be put to death this day, for today the LORD has worked deliverance in Israel."

**14** Samuel said to the people, "Come, let us go to Gilgal and renew the kingdom there."

**15** So all the people went to Gilgal, and there they made Saul king before the LORD in Gilgal. There they offered peace offerings before the LORD, and there Saul and all the men of Israel rejoiced greatly.

## Notes

- **v1 "Nahash the Ammonite… Jabesh-gilead"** — *Nachash* is **serpent**. Jabesh-gilead is the town that sent no one to Mizpah and was destroyed by Israel to find wives for Benjamin (Judges 21:8–14); Saul is a Benjaminite, and his first act as king is to save it. It will also be the town that buries him (31:11–13). The Greek opens the verse with *And it came to pass about a month later*, reading the last words of 10:27 (*but he kept silent*, *ke-macharish*) as *about a month*, *ke-mechodesh*: the letters differ by one.
- **v2 "gouge out every right eye… disgrace on all Israel"** — Nahash says why: the mutilation is meant as a *disgrace*, *cherpah*, for all Israel, not only Jabesh. The town's surrender is to be a sign of Israel's helplessness.
- **v5 "Saul was coming behind the oxen from the field"** — the anointed king is still plowing. The *oxen* he walks behind are the ones he will cut up in v7.
- **v6 "The Spirit of God rushed on Saul… his anger burned hot"** — *va-titslach*, as in 10:6, 10:10, and as on Samson (Judges 14:6). This time the Spirit comes with anger, and the anger goes into a rescue.
- **v7 "cut them in pieces and sent them throughout all the territory of Israel"** — the words of Judges 19:29, where the Levite cut up his secondary wife and *sent her throughout all the territory of Israel*. That summons began a war against Benjamin; this one, from a Benjaminite, rescues the town that paid for that war. *As one man*, as the tribes came out in Judges 20:1.
- **v8 "The sons of Israel… and the men of Judah"** — Judah is counted apart from the rest of Israel, the first time the book does so. The Greek has *six hundred thousand* and *seventy thousand*.
- **v10 "Tomorrow we will come out to you"** — the men of Jabesh tell Nahash what he wants to hear, and it is true in a way he does not expect: they come out with Saul's army.
- **v11 "three companies… in the morning watch"** — Gideon's three companies (Judges 7:16), and the hour when the LORD looked down on the camp of Egypt and threw it into panic (Exodus 14:24).
- **vv12–13 "No one shall be put to death this day, for today the LORD has worked deliverance"** — the men who despised Saul (10:27) are spared by him, and he credits the rescue to the LORD. The Greek of v12 has *Who is it that said, "Saul shall not reign over us"?*
- **vv14–15 "renew the kingdom… made Saul king before the LORD in Gilgal"** — the third step in Saul's making, after the private anointing (10:1) and the lot at Mizpah (10:24). Gilgal is where Israel first camped in the land (Joshua 4:19). The Greek has *Samuel anointed Saul king there*.
