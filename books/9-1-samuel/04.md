# 1 Samuel 4

## Aphek

**1** The word of Samuel came to all Israel. Israel went out to battle against the Philistines. They camped at Ebenezer, and the Philistines camped at Aphek.

**2** The Philistines drew up in line against Israel, and when the battle spread, Israel was struck down before the Philistines, who killed about four thousand men on the field of battle.

**3** When the people came to the camp, the elders of Israel said, "Why has the LORD struck us down today before the Philistines? Let us bring the ark of the covenant of the LORD here from Shiloh, so that it may come among us and save us from the hand of our enemies."

**4** So the people sent to Shiloh and brought from there the ark of the covenant of the LORD of hosts, who is enthroned on the cherubim. The two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, were there with the ark of the covenant of God.

**5** And it came to pass, when the ark of the covenant of the LORD came into the camp, that all Israel gave a great shout, and the earth rang.

**6** When the Philistines heard the sound of the shout, they said, "What is this great shouting in the camp of the Hebrews?" And they learned that the ark of the LORD had come into the camp.

**7** The Philistines were afraid, for they said, "A god has come into the camp." They said, "Woe to us! Nothing like this has happened before.

**8** Woe to us! Who will deliver us from the hand of these mighty gods? These are the gods who struck the Egyptians with every kind of plague in the wilderness.

**9** Take courage and be men, Philistines, or you will serve the Hebrews as they have served you. Be men and fight!"

**10** So the Philistines fought, and Israel was struck down, and they fled, each to his tent. The slaughter was very great: thirty thousand foot soldiers of Israel fell.

**11** The ark of God was taken, and the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, died.

## The News at Shiloh

**12** A man of Benjamin ran from the battle line and came to Shiloh the same day, with his clothes torn and earth on his head.

**13** When he came, Eli was sitting on his seat beside the road, watching, for his heart trembled for the ark of God. When the man came to tell it in the town, the whole town cried out.

**14** When Eli heard the sound of the outcry, he said, "What is this uproar?" The man hurried and came and told Eli.

**15** Eli was ninety-eight years old; his eyes were fixed, and he could not see.

**16** The man said to Eli, "I am the one who came from the battle line; I fled from the battle line today." He said, "How did it go, my son?"

**17** The one who brought the news answered, "Israel has fled before the Philistines, and there has also been a great slaughter among the people. Your two sons also, Hophni and Phinehas, are dead, and the ark of God has been taken."

**18** And it came to pass, when he mentioned the ark of God, that Eli fell backward from his seat beside the gate, and his neck was broken and he died, for the man was old and heavy. He had judged Israel forty years.

## Ichabod

**19** His daughter-in-law, the wife of Phinehas, was pregnant and about to give birth. When she heard the news that the ark of God was taken and that her father-in-law and her husband were dead, she bowed down and gave birth, for her pains came upon her.

**20** As she was dying, the women standing by her said, "Do not be afraid, for you have borne a son." But she did not answer or pay attention.

**21** She named the boy Ichabod, saying, "The glory has gone into exile from Israel," because the ark of God had been taken, and because of her father-in-law and her husband.

**22** She said, "The glory has gone into exile from Israel, for the ark of God has been taken."

## Notes

- **v1 "Ebenezer"** — **stone of help**. The place gets its name only in 7:12, when Samuel sets up a stone there after a victory; here it is named in advance, at the site of a defeat. The Greek opens the verse with *And it came to pass in those days that the Philistines gathered for war against Israel*.
- **v3 "so that it may come among us and save us"** — the Hebrew verbs can be read *it* or *He*. The elders speak as though the ark were the LORD, and as though bringing it would oblige Him. The LORD Himself says nothing in the chapter.
- **v4 "the LORD of hosts, who is enthroned on the cherubim"** — *yoshev ha-keruvim*, the first time the title is given. The cherubim are the two on the cover of the ark, *from between the two cherubim* where the LORD said He would speak (Exodus 25:22).
- **v6 "the Hebrews"** — *ivrim*, the name the Egyptians used for Israel (Exodus 1:15–16), and the Philistines' name for them here. Israel rarely calls itself this.
- **vv7–8 "A god has come… these mighty gods… with every kind of plague in the wilderness"** — the Philistines know the Exodus story, but in their own terms: *Elohim* as many gods, and the plagues moved from Egypt to the wilderness. They are afraid for the right reason and tell the story wrong.
- **v13 "his heart trembled for the ark of God"** — *chared*, the word of the fearful men sent home from Gideon's army (Judges 7:3). Eli fears for the ark before he fears for his sons. The written text has *yakh*, the reading *yad*, **beside**.
- **v15 "ninety-eight years old"** — the Greek has *ninety*, and in v18 it has *twenty* years where the Hebrew has *forty*. The text follows the Hebrew.
- **v18 "old and heavy"** — *kaved*, **heavy**, from the root of *kavod*, **honor, glory**. The man of God warned Eli that he had given weight to his sons above the LORD (2:29–30).
- **vv21–22 "Ichabod… The glory has gone into exile"** — *I-khavod*, **where is the glory?** or **no glory**. *Galah*, **has gone into exile**, is the word for a people carried off from its land. The glory, *ha-kavod*, has gone with the ark, and the dying mother names her son for the loss of it, not for the son. The Greek name is *Ouai barchaboth*, **woe, Barchaboth**.
- **v22** — the mother's last words repeat v21 without the husband and the father-in-law: only the ark.
