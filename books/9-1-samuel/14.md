# 1 Samuel 14

## Jonathan and His Armor-bearer

**1** And it came to pass one day that Jonathan son of Saul said to the young man who carried his armor, "Come, let us cross over to the Philistine garrison on the other side." But he did not tell his father.

**2** Saul was staying on the outskirts of Gibeah under the pomegranate tree at Migron, and the people with him were about six hundred men.

**3** Ahijah son of Ahitub, the brother of Ichabod son of Phinehas son of Eli, the priest of the LORD at Shiloh, was wearing an ephod. The people did not know that Jonathan had gone.

**4** Between the passes by which Jonathan sought to cross over to the Philistine garrison there was a rocky crag on one side and a rocky crag on the other side. The name of the one was Bozez, and the name of the other Seneh.

**5** The one crag rose on the north in front of Michmash, and the other on the south in front of Geba.

**6** Jonathan said to the young man who carried his armor, "Come, let us cross over to the garrison of these uncircumcised. Perhaps the LORD will act for us, for nothing restrains the LORD from saving by many or by few."

**7** His armor-bearer said to him, "Do all that is in your heart. Go ahead; I am with you, as your heart is."

**8** Jonathan said, "We will cross over to the men and show ourselves to them.

**9** If they say to us, 'Wait until we come to you,' then we will stand where we are and not go up to them.

**10** But if they say, 'Come up to us,' then we will go up, for the LORD has given them into our hand. This will be the sign for us."

**11** So the two of them showed themselves to the Philistine garrison, and the Philistines said, "See, Hebrews are coming out of the holes where they have hidden themselves."

**12** The men of the garrison called to Jonathan and his armor-bearer, "Come up to us, and we will show you something!" Jonathan said to his armor-bearer, "Come up after me, for the LORD has given them into the hand of Israel."

**13** Jonathan climbed up on his hands and feet, with his armor-bearer behind him. They fell before Jonathan, and his armor-bearer killed them behind him.

**14** That first slaughter, which Jonathan and his armor-bearer made, was about twenty men, within about half a furrow's length in an acre of field.

**15** There was trembling in the camp, in the field and among all the people. The garrison and the raiders also trembled. The earth quaked, and it became a trembling from God.

## The LORD Saved Israel

**16** Saul's watchmen in Gibeah of Benjamin looked, and the multitude was melting away, surging here and there.

**17** Saul said to the people who were with him, "Count now and see who has gone from us." When they counted, Jonathan and his armor-bearer were not there.

**18** Saul said to Ahijah, "Bring the ephod here," for he carried the ephod on that day before Israel.

**19** While Saul was talking to the priest, the tumult in the camp of the Philistines went on growing louder. So Saul said to the priest, "Withdraw your hand."

**20** Saul and all the people who were with him rallied and came to the battle, and every man's sword was against his fellow, in very great confusion.

**21** The Hebrews who had been with the Philistines before, who had gone up with them into the camp from all around, also turned to be with Israel, who were with Saul and Jonathan.

**22** All the men of Israel who had hidden themselves in the hill country of Ephraim heard that the Philistines had fled, and they too pursued them closely in the battle.

**23** So the LORD saved Israel that day, and the battle passed beyond Beth-aven.

## Saul's Oath

**24** The men of Israel were hard pressed that day, for Saul had put the people under an oath, saying, "Cursed be the man who eats food until evening, until I have taken vengeance on my enemies." So none of the people tasted food.

**25** All the people of the land came into the forest, and there was honey on the ground.

**26** When the people came into the forest, the honey was flowing, but no one put his hand to his mouth, for the people feared the oath.

**27** But Jonathan had not heard when his father put the people under the oath. He put out the end of the staff that was in his hand, dipped it in the honeycomb and brought his hand back to his mouth, and his eyes brightened.

**28** Then one of the people said, "Your father strictly put the people under oath, saying, 'Cursed be the man who eats food today.'" The people were faint.

**29** Jonathan said, "My father has brought trouble on the land. See how my eyes have brightened because I tasted a little of this honey.

**30** How much more, if the people had eaten freely today from the spoil of their enemies that they found! For now the slaughter among the Philistines has not been great."

**31** They struck down the Philistines that day from Michmash to Aijalon, and the people were very faint.

**32** The people pounced on the spoil and took sheep and cattle and calves and slaughtered them on the ground, and the people ate them with the blood.

**33** They told Saul, "See, the people are sinning against the LORD by eating with the blood." He said, "You have dealt faithlessly. Roll a great stone to me today."

**34** Saul said, "Scatter among the people and say to them, 'Each of you bring me his ox or his sheep, and slaughter them here and eat, and do not sin against the LORD by eating with the blood.'" So all the people brought each his ox with him that night and slaughtered them there.

**35** Saul built an altar to the LORD. It was the first altar he built to the LORD.

## Jonathan Taken by Lot

**36** Saul said, "Let us go down after the Philistines by night and plunder them until the morning light, and let us not leave a man of them." They said, "Do whatever seems good in your eyes." But the priest said, "Let us draw near to God here."

**37** Saul asked God, "Shall I go down after the Philistines? Will You give them into the hand of Israel?" But He did not answer him that day.

**38** Saul said, "Come here, all you leaders of the people, and find out and see how this sin has come about today.

**39** For as the LORD lives, who saves Israel, even if it is in Jonathan my son, he shall die." But not one of all the people answered him.

**40** He said to all Israel, "You be on one side, and I and Jonathan my son will be on the other side." The people said to Saul, "Do what seems good in your eyes."

**41** Then Saul said, "O LORD God of Israel, why have You not answered Your servant today? Is the guilt in me or in Jonathan my son? O LORD God of Israel, give Urim. But if it is in Your people Israel, give Thummim." Jonathan and Saul were taken, and the people went free.

**42** Saul said, "Cast the lot between me and Jonathan my son. Whomever the LORD takes shall die." The people said to Saul, "This shall not be." But Saul prevailed over the people, and they cast the lot between him and Jonathan his son, and Jonathan was taken.

**43** Saul said to Jonathan, "Tell me what you have done." Jonathan told him, "I tasted a little honey with the end of the staff that was in my hand. Here I am; I must die."

**44** Saul said, "May God do so, and more also; you shall die, Jonathan."

**45** But the people said to Saul, "Shall Jonathan die, who has worked this great deliverance in Israel? Far from it! As the LORD lives, not one hair of his head shall fall to the ground, for he has worked with God this day." So the people ransomed Jonathan, and he did not die.

**46** Then Saul went up from pursuing the Philistines, and the Philistines went to their own place.

## Saul's Wars and Household

**47** When Saul had taken the kingship over Israel, he fought against all his enemies on every side: against Moab, against the sons of Ammon, against Edom, against the kings of Zobah and against the Philistines. Wherever he turned, he punished them.

**48** He acted valiantly and struck Amalek, and delivered Israel from the hand of those who plundered them.

**49** The sons of Saul were Jonathan, Ishvi and Malchi-shua. The names of his two daughters were these: the name of the firstborn was Merab, and the name of the younger Michal.

**50** The name of Saul's wife was Ahinoam daughter of Ahimaaz. The name of the commander of his army was Abner son of Ner, Saul's uncle.

**51** Kish was the father of Saul, and Ner the father of Abner was the son of Abiel.

**52** The war against the Philistines was hard all the days of Saul. Whenever Saul saw any mighty man or any man of valor, he took him into his service.

## Notes

- **v3 "Ahijah son of Ahitub, the brother of Ichabod son of Phinehas son of Eli"** — the house of Eli, condemned in 2:30–36, still has a priest, and he stands beside Saul wearing the ephod. Ichabod was born on the day the ark was taken (4:19–21).
- **v6 "nothing restrains the LORD from saving by many or by few"** — Gideon's army was cut down because it was too many, *or Israel will boast against Me* (Judges 7:2). Jonathan goes with one man. His *perhaps* leaves the outcome to the LORD, and the sign of vv9–10 lets the Philistines' own words decide.
- **v14 "within about half a furrow's length in an acre of field"** — *ke-vachatsi ma'anah tsemed sadeh*, **as in half a furrow of a yoke of field**. *Tsemed* is a yoke of oxen, and as a measure the land a yoke plows, as in *ten yokes of vineyard* (Isaiah 5:10). The twenty fell in a small space. The Greek has *with darts and with stones of the field*.
- **v15 "it became a trembling from God"** — *chardat Elohim*. In 13:7 Israel followed Saul *trembling*, *charedu*; now the trembling is in the Philistine camp, the ground shakes with it, and it is sent by God.
- **v18 "Bring the ephod here… for he carried the ephod on that day before Israel"** — these words are in the Greek. The Hebrew has *Bring the ark of God here, for the ark of God was on that day, and the sons of Israel*, a sentence without an end. The ark was at Kiriath-jearim in the house of Abinadab (7:1–2), where David finds it (2 Samuel 6:3). Ahijah is wearing the ephod (v3), and *withdraw your hand* (v19) stops the priest in the middle of asking by it. The text follows the Greek.
- **vv24–29 "Saul had put the people under an oath… My father has brought trouble on the land"** — *akhar*, the word Joshua spoke to Achan, *Why have you brought trouble on us? The LORD will bring trouble on you today* (Joshua 7:25). Achan broke the LORD's ban; Saul laid an oath of his own, and his son broke it without knowing. The Greek of v24 opens with *Saul committed a great error that day*. In v27 the written text has *his eyes saw*, and the reading is *his eyes brightened*, which Jonathan repeats in v29, *oru einai*.
- **vv32–35 "the people ate them with the blood… You have dealt faithlessly"** — *al ha-dam*, the words of the law, *You shall not eat anything with the blood in it* (Leviticus 19:26). For *begadtem*, **you have dealt faithlessly**, the Greek has *In Gittaim*. The altar of v35 is Saul's first.
- **vv41–42 "give Urim… give Thummim… Whomever the LORD takes shall die"** — these words are in the Greek, which names the two lots *dēloi*, **clear ones**, its word for Urim again in 28:6, and *hosiotēs*, **holiness**. The Hebrew of v41 has only *Saul said to the LORD God of Israel, "Give perfect,"* *tamim*, from the root of *Thummim*; *LORD God of Israel* stands twice in the Greek, and the Hebrew has passed from the first to the second. In v42 the Hebrew passes likewise from *between me and Jonathan my son* to *Jonathan was taken*, without the people's protest. The second half of the prayer is compressed even in the Greek, *and if it says this, give to Your people Israel, give Thummim*. The text follows the Greek.
- **v45 "he has worked with God this day… the people ransomed Jonathan"** — *im Elohim*, **with God**. The Greek has *the people of God*, reading the same letters, *am*, as **people**. *Va-yifdu*, **ransomed**, is the word for redeeming a firstborn son (Exodus 13:13); the Greek has *the people prayed for Jonathan*.
- **v47 "When Saul had taken the kingship… Wherever he turned, he punished them"** — *lakhad*, the verb of the lot: Saul *was taken* at Mizpah (10:21), and Jonathan *was taken* in v42. *Yarshia'*, **he declared guilty, punished**; the Greek has *he was saved*.
