# 1 Samuel 13

## Jonathan Strikes the Garrison

**1** Saul was *[number lost]* years old when he began to reign, and he reigned two years over Israel.

**2** Saul chose for himself three thousand men of Israel. Two thousand were with Saul at Michmash and in the hill country of Bethel, and a thousand were with Jonathan at Gibeah of Benjamin. The rest of the people he sent away, each to his tent.

**3** Jonathan struck down the Philistine garrison at Geba, and the Philistines heard of it. Saul blew the horn throughout all the land, saying, "Let the Hebrews hear!"

**4** All Israel heard it said, "Saul has struck down the Philistine garrison, and Israel has also become a stench to the Philistines." The people were called out to follow Saul to Gilgal.

**5** The Philistines gathered to fight against Israel: thirty thousand chariots and six thousand horsemen, and people as many as the sand on the seashore. They came up and camped at Michmash, east of Beth-aven.

**6** When the men of Israel saw that they were in a strait, for the people were hard pressed, the people hid themselves in caves and in thickets, among the rocks, in strongholds and in cisterns.

**7** Some Hebrews crossed the Jordan to the land of Gad and Gilead. But Saul was still at Gilgal, and all the people followed him trembling.

## Saul Offers the Burnt Offering

**8** He waited seven days, to the appointed time that Samuel had set, but Samuel did not come to Gilgal, and the people were scattering from him.

**9** So Saul said, "Bring me the burnt offering and the peace offerings." Then he offered the burnt offering.

**10** As soon as he had finished offering the burnt offering, Samuel came. Saul went out to meet him and to greet him.

**11** Samuel said, "What have you done?" Saul said, "When I saw that the people were scattering from me, and that you did not come at the appointed days, and that the Philistines were gathering at Michmash,

**12** I said, 'Now the Philistines will come down against me at Gilgal, and I have not sought the LORD's favor.' So I forced myself and offered the burnt offering."

**13** Samuel said to Saul, "You have acted foolishly. You have not kept the command of the LORD your God that He commanded you. For now the LORD would have established your kingdom over Israel forever.

**14** But now your kingdom shall not stand. The LORD has sought for Himself a man after His own heart, and the LORD has appointed him leader over His people, because you have not kept what the LORD commanded you."

**15** Then Samuel arose and went from Gilgal on his way. The rest of the people went up after Saul to meet the fighting men, and they came from Gilgal to Gibeah of Benjamin. Saul counted the people who were present with him, about six hundred men.

## No Smith in Israel

**16** Saul and Jonathan his son and the people who were present with them stayed at Geba of Benjamin, while the Philistines camped at Michmash.

**17** The raiders came out of the camp of the Philistines in three companies. One company turned toward the road to Ophrah, to the land of Shual;

**18** another company turned toward the road to Beth-horon; and another company turned toward the road of the border that looks down on the Valley of Zeboim, toward the wilderness.

**19** No smith was to be found in all the land of Israel, for the Philistines said, "In case the Hebrews make a sword or a spear."

**20** So all Israel went down to the Philistines, each to sharpen his plowshare, his mattock, his axe and his sickle.

**21** The charge was a pim for the plowshares and for the mattocks, and for the three-pronged forks and for the axes, and for setting the goads.

**22** So on the day of battle neither sword nor spear was found in the hand of any of the people who were with Saul and Jonathan; only Saul and Jonathan his son had them.

**23** The Philistine garrison went out to the pass of Michmash.

## Notes

- **v1 "Saul was [number lost] years old when he began to reign"** — the Hebrew reads *ben shanah Sha'ul be-molkho*, **a son of a year was Saul when he reigned**. This is the formula for a king's age at his accession, as in *David was thirty years old when he began to reign* (2 Samuel 5:4), and the number that belongs before *year* is missing from the text. The Greek does not have the verse. *Two years* is what the Hebrew has for the length of the reign; Paul gives Saul *forty years* (Acts 13:21).
- **v3 "Let the Hebrews hear!"** — Saul calls Israel by the name the Philistines use for them (4:6, 4:9, and v19). The Greek has *The slaves have revolted*; *ha-ivrim*, **the Hebrews**, and *ha-avadim*, **the slaves**, differ by one letter.
- **v4 "Israel has also become a stench to the Philistines"** — *niv'ash*, the word of Jacob's rebuke to Simeon and Levi after Shechem, *to make me stink among those who dwell in the land* (Genesis 34:30). The report gives Saul the credit for Jonathan's blow (v3).
- **vv6–7 "hid themselves in caves… Some Hebrews crossed the Jordan"** — *ve-ivrim avru*: the name *Hebrews* and the verb *crossed* share their letters. The Greek has *those who crossed over crossed*. The people who were called out in v4 hide or flee.
- **v8 "to the appointed time that Samuel had set"** — the Hebrew has no verb, *the appointed time that Samuel*; *had set* is supplied. The appointment is the one Samuel gave at the anointing: *Seven days you shall wait, until I come to you and show you what you are to do* (10:8). Saul waits the seven days, and Samuel comes as the offering ends.
- **vv12–13 "I have not sought the LORD's favor… So I forced myself… You have acted foolishly"** — *u-fenei YHWH lo chilliti*, **the face of the LORD I have not entreated**, the phrase for asking for favor. *Va-etappaq* is the verb of Joseph who *held himself in* before his brothers (Genesis 43:31, 45:1): Saul says he acted against his own will. Samuel's answer is *niskalta*. David uses the same word of himself after the census, *I have acted very foolishly* (2 Samuel 24:10), and Saul will say *I have played the fool* to David (26:21).
- **v14 "a man after His own heart… leader over His people"** — *ish ki-levavo*. Paul uses the words of David: *I have found David son of Jesse, a man after my heart* (Acts 13:22). *Nagid*, **leader**, is the word the LORD used of Saul (9:16, 10:1); it is now given to another.
- **v15 "went from Gilgal on his way. The rest of the people went up after Saul… from Gilgal to Gibeah of Benjamin"** — these words are in the Greek. The Hebrew has *Samuel arose and went up from Gilgal to Gibeah of Benjamin*, sending Samuel to Saul's town; *from Gilgal* stands twice in the Greek, and the Hebrew has passed from the first to the second. The text follows the Greek.
- **v17 "The raiders"** — *ha-mashchit*, **the destroyer**, the word of the Passover night, *He will not let the destroyer come into your houses* (Exodus 12:23). Here it is the Philistine raiding force.
- **vv19–21 "No smith… a pim"** — the Philistines keep the working of metal to themselves, and Israel pays them to sharpen its farm tools. The last tool in v20 is *machareshah*, nearly the same word as the first, *macharesheth*, **plowshare**; the Greek has *sickle*, and the text follows it. *Pim*, *petsirah*, **the charge**, and *qilleshon*, rendered **three-pronged forks** from *shelosh qilleshon*, **three of a qilleshon**, all occur only here in the Bible. *Pim* is the price, and the verse does not say its value. The Greek has *three shekels to the tooth*.
