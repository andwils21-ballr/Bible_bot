# 1 Samuel 5

## Dagon

**1** The Philistines had taken the ark of God, and they brought it from Ebenezer to Ashdod.

**2** The Philistines took the ark of God and brought it into the house of Dagon and set it up beside Dagon.

**3** When the people of Ashdod rose early the next day, Dagon had fallen on his face to the ground before the ark of the LORD. So they took Dagon and put him back in his place.

**4** But when they rose early the next morning, Dagon had fallen on his face to the ground before the ark of the LORD, and the head of Dagon and both the palms of his hands lay cut off on the threshold. Only the trunk of Dagon was left to him.

**5** That is why the priests of Dagon and all who come into the house of Dagon do not tread on the threshold of Dagon in Ashdod to this day.

## The Hand of the LORD

**6** The hand of the LORD was heavy on the people of Ashdod. He devastated them and struck them with tumors, Ashdod and its territory, and mice sprang up in the middle of its land.

**7** When the men of Ashdod saw how it was, they said, "The ark of the God of Israel must not stay with us, for His hand is hard on us and on Dagon our god."

**8** So they sent and gathered all the lords of the Philistines to them and said, "What shall we do with the ark of the God of Israel?" They said, "Let the ark of the God of Israel be moved around to Gath." So they moved the ark of the God of Israel there.

**9** After they had moved it, the hand of the LORD was against the city, with a very great confusion. He struck the men of the city, both small and great, and tumors broke out on them.

**10** So they sent the ark of God to Ekron. When the ark of God came to Ekron, the Ekronites cried out, "They have moved the ark of the God of Israel around to us, to kill us and our people!"

**11** They sent and gathered all the lords of the Philistines and said, "Send away the ark of the God of Israel, and let it return to its own place, so that it does not kill us and our people." For there was a deadly confusion throughout the whole city; the hand of God was very heavy there.

**12** The men who did not die were struck with tumors, and the cry of the city went up to heaven.

## Notes

- **vv3–4 "Dagon had fallen on his face"** — the posture of worship: the god of the Philistines lies face down before the ark, *le-fanav artsah*, as a worshipper would. The second morning he is broken, head and hands cut off, and *only the trunk* is left. *Dagon* was the god of the Philistines, and it was at a sacrifice to him in Gaza that Samson died (Judges 16:23–30).
- **v4 "Only the trunk of Dagon was left to him"** — the Hebrew has only *Dagon was left*, **what was Dagon**: the body without head or hands. The Greek has *only the back of Dagon was left*.
- **v5 "do not tread on the threshold"** — the fallen god's head and hands lay on the threshold, and the custom of stepping over it is explained by the story. *To this day* marks the narrator's own time.
- **vv6, 7, 11 "The hand of the LORD was heavy"** — *va-tikhbad yad YHWH*, from *kaved*, **heavy**, the word of Eli's death (4:18) and of Ichabod's lost *glory* (4:21). The glory that went into exile from Israel is heavy on the Philistines.
- **vv6, 9, 12 "tumors"** — the written text has *ofalim*, **swellings, mounds**; the reading tradition has *techorim*, a word of the same sense. The same pair stands in Deuteronomy 28:27, *the boils of Egypt… tumors*, one of the curses on Israel, and it falls here on Israel's enemy.
- **v6 "and mice sprang up in the middle of its land"** — this clause is in the Greek, not the Hebrew. In the Hebrew the golden mice of 6:4–5 appear with no plague of mice told beforehand, though 6:5 speaks of *your mice that ravage the land*. The Greek keeps the detail the context calls for, and the text follows it (as also at 6:1).
- **v7 "His hand is hard on us and on Dagon our god"** — the Philistines draw the conclusion the Exodus story drew: the LORD's judgment falls *against all the gods* of the land as well as its people (Exodus 12:12).
- **vv8–10 Ashdod, Gath, Ekron** — three of the five Philistine cities (6:17). Each passes the ark on to the next, and the last cries out before it arrives.
- **v9 "a very great confusion"** — *mehumah*, what the LORD brings on an enemy in battle: *throw them into great confusion*, *mehumah gedolah* (Deuteronomy 7:23). Here there is no battle, only the ark.
- **v12 "the cry of the city went up to heaven"** — *shav'at ha-ir*, the cry for help. Israel cried out in Egypt, and the cry *went up to God* (Exodus 2:23). Now the cry is the Philistines'.
