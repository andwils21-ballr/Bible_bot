# 1 Samuel 27

## Ziklag

**1** David said in his heart, "Now I shall perish one day by the hand of Saul. There is nothing better for me than to escape to the land of the Philistines. Then Saul will give up seeking me any longer in all the territory of Israel, and I shall escape from his hand."

**2** So David arose and crossed over, he and the six hundred men with him, to Achish son of Maoch, king of Gath.

**3** David stayed with Achish at Gath, he and his men, each with his household, and David with his two wives, Ahinoam of Jezreel, and Abigail of Carmel, Nabal's widow.

**4** When Saul was told that David had fled to Gath, he no longer sought him.

**5** David said to Achish, "If I have found favor in your eyes, let a place be given me in one of the country towns, so that I may live there. Why should your servant live in the royal city with you?"

**6** So that day Achish gave him Ziklag. That is why Ziklag has belonged to the kings of Judah to this day.

**7** The time that David lived in the country of the Philistines was a year and four months.

## The Raids

**8** David and his men went up and raided the Geshurites, the Gizrites and the Amalekites, for these were the inhabitants of the land from of old, as you go to Shur and as far as the land of Egypt.

**9** David struck the land and left neither man nor woman alive, and he took sheep, cattle, donkeys, camels and clothing, and came back to Achish.

**10** When Achish asked, "Where have you raided today?" David would say, "Against the Negev of Judah," or "Against the Negev of the Jerahmeelites," or "Against the Negev of the Kenites."

**11** David left neither man nor woman alive to bring to Gath, thinking, "In case they tell about us, saying, 'This is what David did.'" This was his practice all the time he lived in the country of the Philistines.

**12** Achish trusted David, thinking, "He has made himself utterly hateful to his people Israel, and he will be my servant forever."

## Notes

- **v1 "Now I shall perish one day by the hand of Saul"** — *espeh*, the verb David used of Saul in the last chapter, *he will go down into battle and perish* (26:10). After two sparings and two promises from Saul, David despairs of the promises and leaves the land.
- **v2 "Achish son of Maoch, king of Gath"** — the king before whom David played the madman (21:10–15). He comes back with six hundred men, not alone; the Greek has *four hundred*.
- **v6 "Ziklag has belonged to the kings of Judah to this day"** — Ziklag was given to Judah in Joshua's allotment and then to Simeon (Joshua 15:31, 19:5); here a Philistine king gives it to David. *The kings of Judah* is written from a time when Judah had kings of its own.
- **v7 "a year and four months"** — *yamim ve-arba'ah chodashim*, **days and four months**. *Yamim*, **days**, can mean a full year, as in *the yearly sacrifice*, *zevach ha-yamim* (1:21). The Greek has *four months*.
- **vv8–9 "the Geshurites, the Gizrites and the Amalekites… left neither man nor woman alive"** — the Geshurites are among the peoples of *the land that remains* in Joshua's day (Joshua 13:2). David does to the Amalekites what Saul was commanded to do and did not (15:3, 15:9), though he keeps the livestock.
- **vv10–11 "Against the Negev of Judah… In case they tell about us"** — a lie to his Philistine lord. He names his own tribe and the Kenites, whom Saul had warned away from Amalek (15:6). None are left alive to contradict him.
- **v12 "He has made himself utterly hateful to his people Israel"** — *hav'esh hiv'ish*, **he has made himself stink**, the word of 13:4, when *Israel has also become a stench to the Philistines*. *My servant forever*, *eved olam*, are the words of the law for a slave who chooses to stay with his master: *he shall be your slave forever* (Deuteronomy 15:17).
