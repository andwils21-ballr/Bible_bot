# 1 Samuel 12

## "Whose Ox Have I Taken?"

**1** Samuel said to all Israel, "I have listened to your voice in all that you have said to me, and have made a king over you.

**2** Now the king walks before you. I am old and gray, and my sons are with you. I have walked before you from my youth until this day.

**3** Here I am. Testify against me before the LORD and before His anointed: Whose ox have I taken? Whose donkey have I taken? Whom have I cheated? Whom have I oppressed? From whose hand have I taken a bribe to blind my eyes with it? I will return it to you."

**4** They said, "You have not cheated us or oppressed us, and you have not taken anything from any man's hand."

**5** He said to them, "The LORD is witness against you, and His anointed is witness this day, that you have not found anything in my hand." They said, "He is witness."

## The LORD's Righteous Acts

**6** Samuel said to the people, "It is the LORD who appointed Moses and Aaron and brought your fathers up from the land of Egypt.

**7** Now stand still, so that I may plead with you before the LORD concerning all the righteous acts of the LORD that He did for you and for your fathers.

**8** When Jacob went into Egypt, your fathers cried out to the LORD, and the LORD sent Moses and Aaron, who brought your fathers out of Egypt and settled them in this place.

**9** But they forgot the LORD their God, and He sold them into the hand of Sisera, commander of the army of Hazor, and into the hand of the Philistines, and into the hand of the king of Moab, and they fought against them.

**10** Then they cried out to the LORD and said, 'We have sinned, because we have forsaken the LORD and served the Baals and the Ashtaroth. Now deliver us from the hand of our enemies, and we will serve You.'

**11** The LORD sent Jerubbaal and Bedan and Jephthah and Samuel, and delivered you from the hand of your enemies all around, and you lived in safety.

**12** But when you saw that Nahash king of the sons of Ammon came against you, you said to me, 'No, a king shall reign over us,' when the LORD your God was your king.

**13** Now here is the king you have chosen, whom you have asked for. The LORD has set a king over you.

**14** If you will fear the LORD and serve Him and listen to His voice and not rebel against the command of the LORD, and both you and the king who reigns over you follow the LORD your God, it will be well.

**15** But if you will not listen to the voice of the LORD, but rebel against the command of the LORD, then the hand of the LORD will be against you, as it was against your fathers.

## Thunder at Harvest

**16** Now stand still and see this great thing that the LORD is about to do before your eyes.

**17** Is it not wheat harvest today? I will call to the LORD, and He will send thunder and rain. Then you will know and see that your wickedness is great, which you have done in the eyes of the LORD by asking for yourselves a king."

**18** So Samuel called to the LORD, and the LORD sent thunder and rain that day, and all the people greatly feared the LORD and Samuel.

**19** All the people said to Samuel, "Pray for your servants to the LORD your God, so that we may not die, for we have added to all our sins this evil, to ask for ourselves a king."

**20** Samuel said to the people, "Do not be afraid. You have done all this evil; only do not turn aside from following the LORD, but serve the LORD with all your heart.

**21** Do not turn aside after empty things that cannot profit or deliver, for they are empty.

**22** For the LORD will not forsake His people, for His great name's sake, because it has pleased the LORD to make you a people for Himself.

**23** As for me, far be it from me that I should sin against the LORD by ceasing to pray for you. I will instruct you in the good and the right way.

**24** Only fear the LORD and serve Him faithfully with all your heart, for consider what great things He has done for you.

**25** But if you still do evil, both you and your king shall be swept away."

## Notes

- **v3 "Whose ox have I taken? Whose donkey have I taken?"** — Moses said the same when his leadership was challenged: *I have not taken one donkey from them* (Numbers 16:15). *A bribe to blind my eyes* is the law of Exodus 23:8, *a bribe blinds the clear-sighted*. Samuel's sons took bribes (8:3); he asks the people to find one in his hand. The Greek adds *and a sandal* to the bribe.
- **vv3, 5 "His anointed"** — *meshicho*, said now of Saul, the first king to whom Hannah's word *the horn of His anointed* (2:10) is applied.
- **v6 "It is the LORD who appointed Moses and Aaron"** — the Hebrew sentence has no verb, only *The LORD who made Moses and Aaron*; *it is* is supplied. The Greek has *The Lord is witness, who made Moses and Aaron*, carrying on the *witness* of v5.
- **v7 "the righteous acts of the LORD"** — *tsidqot YHWH*, the words of the Song of Deborah, *there they recount the righteous acts of the LORD* (Judges 5:11). Samuel pleads the case like a lawyer, *ishafetah*, and his evidence is the history of Judges.
- **v11 "Jerubbaal and Bedan and Jephthah and Samuel"** — *Bedan* is named nowhere else as a deliverer. The Greek has *Barak*, the partner of Deborah in the Sisera story that v9 has just recalled. Samuel also names himself as the last deliverer.
- **v12 "when the LORD your God was your king"** — chapter 8 gave the elders' reason as Samuel's age and his sons; here Samuel names the Ammonite threat of chapter 11 as the reason. Both are given, and neither excuses the request.
- **v15 "as it was against your fathers"** — the Hebrew has *against you and against your fathers*. The Greek has *against you and against your king*, which matches v14 (*both you and the king*) and v25 (*both you and your king*).
- **v17 "Is it not wheat harvest today?… thunder and rain"** — rain at the wheat harvest, in early summer, was out of season. *Thunder* is *qolot*, **voices**, the word for the thunder at Sinai (Exodus 19:16).
- **v21 "empty things… for they are empty"** — *tohu*, the word of Genesis 1:2, *the earth was wild and waste*. The gods Israel might turn to are called the stuff before creation.
- **v23 "far be it from me that I should sin against the LORD by ceasing to pray for you"** — Samuel counts not praying for the people as a sin. The prophet is the one who prays for them, as he did at Mizpah (7:5, 7:8–9).
