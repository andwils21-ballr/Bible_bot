# 1 Samuel 19

## Jonathan Speaks for David

**1** Saul told Jonathan his son and all his servants to kill David. But Jonathan, Saul's son, delighted greatly in David.

**2** Jonathan told David, "Saul my father is seeking to kill you. So now, please, be on your guard in the morning; stay in a secret place and hide.

**3** I will go out and stand beside my father in the field where you are, and I will speak about you to my father. If I see anything, I will tell you."

**4** Jonathan spoke well of David to Saul his father. He said to him, "Let the king not sin against his servant David, for he has not sinned against you, and his deeds have been very good for you.

**5** He took his life in his hand and struck down the Philistine, and the LORD worked a great deliverance for all Israel. You saw it and rejoiced. Why then would you sin against innocent blood by killing David without cause?"

**6** Saul listened to the voice of Jonathan, and Saul swore, "As the LORD lives, he shall not be put to death."

**7** Jonathan called David and told him all these words. Then Jonathan brought David to Saul, and he was in his presence as before.

## Michal Saves David

**8** There was war again. David went out and fought the Philistines and struck them with a great slaughter, and they fled before him.

**9** Then an evil spirit from the LORD came on Saul as he sat in his house with his spear in his hand, while David was playing with his hand.

**10** Saul tried to pin David to the wall with the spear, but David slipped away from Saul, and Saul drove the spear into the wall. David fled and escaped that night.

**11** Saul sent messengers to David's house to watch him and to kill him in the morning. But Michal, David's wife, told him, "If you do not save your life tonight, tomorrow you will be put to death."

**12** So Michal let David down through the window, and he went and fled and escaped.

**13** Michal took the household gods and laid them in the bed, put a net of goats' hair at the head, and covered it with the garment.

**14** When Saul sent messengers to take David, she said, "He is sick."

**15** Then Saul sent the messengers to see David, saying, "Bring him up to me in the bed, so that I may kill him."

**16** When the messengers came in, there were the household gods in the bed, with the net of goats' hair at the head.

**17** Saul said to Michal, "Why have you deceived me like this and let my enemy go, so that he has escaped?" Michal answered Saul, "He said to me, 'Let me go. Why should I kill you?'"

## Saul Among the Prophets

**18** David fled and escaped, and came to Samuel at Ramah and told him all that Saul had done to him. He and Samuel went and stayed at Naioth.

**19** Saul was told, "David is at Naioth in Ramah."

**20** Saul sent messengers to take David. When they saw the company of the prophets prophesying, with Samuel standing as leader over them, the Spirit of God came on Saul's messengers, and they also prophesied.

**21** When Saul was told, he sent other messengers, and they also prophesied. Saul sent messengers a third time, and they also prophesied.

**22** Then he himself went to Ramah and came to the great cistern that is in Secu. He asked, "Where are Samuel and David?" Someone said, "They are at Naioth in Ramah."

**23** So he went there, to Naioth in Ramah, and the Spirit of God came on him too, and he went along prophesying until he came to Naioth in Ramah.

**24** He too stripped off his clothes, and he too prophesied before Samuel, and he lay naked all that day and all that night. That is why they say, "Is Saul also among the prophets?"

## Notes

- **v1 "Jonathan… delighted greatly in David"** — *chafets*, the word Saul's servants were told to use to lure David, *the king delights in you* (18:22). The king's delight was a trap; his son's is real.
- **v5 "He took his life in his hand… without cause"** — *sam nafsho be-khappo*, as Jephthah said, *I took my life in my hands* (Judges 12:3). *Chinnam*, **for nothing, without cause**: Jonathan lays the case before his father like a judge, and Saul yields to it with an oath (v6).
- **vv9–10 "an evil spirit from the LORD… Saul drove the spear into the wall"** — in 18:11 David evaded the spear twice; now Saul throws it again. The oath of v6 lasts until the next victory over the Philistines (v8).
- **v12 "Michal let David down through the window"** — the rescue of the spies at Jericho: *she let them down by a rope through the window* (Joshua 2:15). The king's daughter takes the part Rahab took against her own king.
- **vv13–17 "Michal took the household gods… Why have you deceived me like this?"** — *terafim*, the household gods Rachel stole from her father Laban and hid by sitting on them, telling him she could not rise (Genesis 31:34–35). Michal too hides the husband her father hunts, with household gods, and answers her father with a lie. *Kevir*, **net** or **quilt** of goats' hair, occurs only in vv13 and 16. The Greek has *the liver of goats*.
- **vv18–23 "Naioth in Ramah"** — *Nayot* is named only in this chapter and in 20:1. The Greek has *Auath*.
- **v20 "the company of the prophets"** — *lahaqat*, which occurs only here. The Greek has *the assembly*; *qehillah*, **assembly**, has the same letters as *lahaqah* in another order.
- **vv20–23 "the Spirit of God came on Saul's messengers… on him too"** — three sets of messengers, then the king himself, are stopped by the Spirit before they can reach David. At his anointing the Spirit made Saul *another man* (10:6); now it comes on him while he hunts David.
- **v24 "He too stripped off his clothes… before Samuel"** — *va-yifshat*, the verb of Jonathan, who *stripped off the robe* and gave it to David (18:4). *Samuel did not see Saul again until the day of his death* (15:35) speaks of Samuel going to Saul; here Saul comes before Samuel.
- **v24 "Is Saul also among the prophets?"** — the proverb's second origin. At 10:11–12 it began with Saul newly anointed and prophesying among the prophets; here it is said of a king lying naked on the ground.
