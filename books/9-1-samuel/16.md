# 1 Samuel 16

## Samuel at Bethlehem

**1** The LORD said to Samuel, "How long will you mourn over Saul, when I have rejected him from reigning over Israel? Fill your horn with oil and go. I will send you to Jesse the Bethlehemite, for I have seen among his sons a king for Myself."

**2** Samuel said, "How can I go? Saul will hear of it and kill me." The LORD said, "Take a heifer with you and say, 'I have come to sacrifice to the LORD.'

**3** Invite Jesse to the sacrifice, and I will let you know what you shall do. You shall anoint for Me the one I name to you."

**4** Samuel did what the LORD said and came to Bethlehem. The elders of the town came trembling to meet him and said, "Do you come in peace?"

**5** He said, "In peace. I have come to sacrifice to the LORD. Consecrate yourselves and come with me to the sacrifice." He consecrated Jesse and his sons and invited them to the sacrifice.

**6** And it came to pass, when they came, that he saw Eliab and said, "Surely the LORD's anointed stands before Him."

**7** But the LORD said to Samuel, "Do not look at his appearance or at the height of his stature, because I have rejected him. For it is not as a man sees: a man looks at what is before the eyes, but the LORD looks at the heart."

**8** Then Jesse called Abinadab and made him pass before Samuel. He said, "The LORD has not chosen this one either."

**9** Then Jesse made Shammah pass by. He said, "The LORD has not chosen this one either."

**10** Jesse made seven of his sons pass before Samuel, and Samuel said to Jesse, "The LORD has not chosen these."

**11** Samuel said to Jesse, "Are these all the young men?" He said, "There is still the youngest, and he is tending the sheep." Samuel said to Jesse, "Send and bring him, for we will not sit down to eat until he comes here."

**12** So he sent and brought him in. He was ruddy, with beautiful eyes, and good to look at. The LORD said, "Arise, anoint him, for this is the one."

**13** Samuel took the horn of oil and anointed him among his brothers, and the Spirit of the LORD rushed on David from that day forward. Then Samuel arose and went to Ramah.

## David in Saul's Service

**14** The Spirit of the LORD departed from Saul, and an evil spirit from the LORD terrified him.

**15** Saul's servants said to him, "An evil spirit from God is terrifying you.

**16** Let our lord command your servants who are before you to look for a man who knows how to play the lyre. And it shall come to pass, when the evil spirit from God is on you, that he will play with his hand, and you will be well."

**17** Saul said to his servants, "Find me a man who plays well, and bring him to me."

**18** One of the young men answered, "I have seen a son of Jesse the Bethlehemite who knows how to play, a man of worth and a man of war, wise in speech and handsome, and the LORD is with him."

**19** So Saul sent messengers to Jesse and said, "Send me David your son, who is with the sheep."

**20** Jesse took a donkey loaded with bread, a skin of wine and a young goat, and sent them by David his son to Saul.

**21** David came to Saul and entered his service. Saul loved him greatly, and he became his armor-bearer.

**22** Saul sent to Jesse, saying, "Let David remain in my service, for he has found favor in my eyes."

**23** And it would come to pass, whenever the spirit from God was on Saul, that David would take the lyre and play with his hand, and Saul would find relief and be well, and the evil spirit would depart from him.

## Notes

- **v1 "I have seen among his sons a king for Myself"** — *ra'iti… li melekh*. *Ra'ah*, **to see**, is also **to see to, provide**, as in *God will see to the lamb* (Genesis 22:8). The chapter turns on the verb: Samuel *saw* Eliab (v6), *a man sees… the LORD looks* (v7), *find me*, literally *see for me* (v17), and *I have seen a son of Jesse* (v18). The first king was the people's request (8:5); this one the LORD has seen for Himself.
- **v4 "The elders of the town came trembling… Do you come in peace?"** — *va-yecherdu*, the trembling of 14:15. A visit from the prophet who had just hewn Agag to pieces (15:33) is not assumed to be peaceful.
- **v7 "the height of his stature… a man looks at what is before the eyes, but the LORD looks at the heart"** — Saul was chosen *taller than any of the people* (9:2, 10:23); Eliab is rejected for the same look. The Hebrew is *man sees to the eyes, and the LORD sees to the heart*. The first half is compressed, *for not what a man sees*; the Greek supplies *God sees*.
- **v11 "the youngest… tending the sheep"** — *ha-qatan*, **the small one**, as Saul was *small in your own eyes* (15:17). *Ro'eh*, **shepherding**, is the word of the LORD's charge to David when he becomes king: *You shall shepherd My people Israel* (2 Samuel 5:2).
- **v12 "He was ruddy"** — *admoni*, the word used of Esau at his birth, *the first came out ruddy* (Genesis 25:25). *With beautiful eyes, and good to look at*: after v7, the narrator still notes the look of the chosen one.
- **vv13–14 "the Spirit of the LORD rushed on David… The Spirit of the LORD departed from Saul"** — *va-titslach*, the word of the Spirit on Saul (10:6, 10:10, 11:6). On David it comes *from that day forward*, and in the next verse it leaves Saul.
- **v14 "an evil spirit from the LORD terrified him"** — as *God sent an evil spirit between Abimelech and the lords of Shechem* (Judges 9:23). *Bi'atattu*, **terrified him**; the Greek has *choked him*. In v23 the Hebrew calls it only *the spirit of God*, and names it *the evil spirit* at the end of the verse; the Greek has *evil spirit* in both places.
- **v18 "a man of worth and a man of war, wise in speech"** — *gibbor chayil*, as Kish is described (9:1). The servant's picture of David goes past what a musician needs, and ends with the words the story will keep repeating: *the LORD is with him* (18:12, 18:14, 18:28).
- **v20 "a donkey loaded with bread"** — *chamor lechem*, **a donkey of bread**. The Greek has *a homer of bread*, *chomer*, the same letters read as a measure.
- **vv21, 23 "Saul loved him greatly… Saul would find relief"** — Saul is the first to love David; Jonathan (18:1, 18:3), all Israel and Judah (18:16) and Michal (18:20) follow. *Ravach*, **to find relief, have room to breathe**, is built on the same letters as *ruach*, **spirit, breath**: when the spirit comes on Saul, the music gives him breath.
