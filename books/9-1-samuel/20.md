# 1 Samuel 20

## David and Jonathan

**1** David fled from Naioth in Ramah and came and said to Jonathan, "What have I done? What is my guilt, and what is my sin against your father, that he is seeking my life?"

**2** He said to him, "Far from it! You shall not die. My father does nothing, great or small, without telling me. Why should my father hide this from me? It is not so."

**3** But David swore again and said, "Your father knows well that I have found favor in your eyes, and he thinks, 'Let Jonathan not know this, or he will be grieved.' But truly, as the LORD lives and as your soul lives, there is only a step between me and death."

**4** Jonathan said to David, "Whatever your soul says, I will do for you."

**5** David said to Jonathan, "Tomorrow is the new moon, and I ought to sit with the king to eat. Let me go, so that I may hide in the field until the evening of the third day.

**6** If your father misses me at all, say, 'David earnestly asked leave of me to run to Bethlehem, his town, for there is a yearly sacrifice there for the whole clan.'

**7** If he says, 'Good,' your servant will be safe. But if he burns with anger, know that he has resolved on evil.

**8** Show loyal love to your servant, for you have brought your servant into a covenant of the LORD with you. But if there is guilt in me, kill me yourself. Why should you bring me to your father?"

**9** Jonathan said, "Far be it from you! If I knew for certain that my father had resolved that evil should come on you, would I not tell you?"

**10** David said to Jonathan, "Who will tell me if your father answers you harshly?"

**11** Jonathan said to David, "Come, let us go out into the field." So the two of them went out into the field.

## The Covenant Renewed

**12** Jonathan said to David, "The LORD, the God of Israel, is witness: when I have sounded out my father about this time tomorrow, or the third day, if he is well disposed toward David, will I not then send to you and tell you?

**13** May the LORD do so to Jonathan, and more also, if my father means to do you evil and I do not tell you and send you away, so that you may go in peace. May the LORD be with you, as He has been with my father.

**14** If I am still alive, will you not show me the loyal love of the LORD, so that I do not die?

**15** Do not cut off your loyal love from my house forever, not even when the LORD cuts off every one of David's enemies from the face of the earth."

**16** So Jonathan made a covenant with the house of David: "May the LORD require it at the hand of David's enemies."

**17** Jonathan made David swear again by his love for him, for he loved him as he loved his own soul.

**18** Jonathan said to him, "Tomorrow is the new moon, and you will be missed, because your seat will be empty.

**19** On the third day go down quickly to the place where you hid on the day of the affair, and stay beside the stone Ezel.

**20** I will shoot three arrows to one side of it, as though I were shooting at a mark.

**21** Then I will send the boy: 'Go, find the arrows.' If I say to the boy, 'The arrows are on this side of you; take them,' then come, for it is peace for you and nothing is wrong, as the LORD lives.

**22** But if I say to the young man, 'The arrows are beyond you,' go, for the LORD has sent you away.

**23** As for the word that you and I have spoken, the LORD is between you and me forever."

## The New Moon Meal

**24** So David hid in the field. When the new moon came, the king sat down to the meal to eat.

**25** The king sat on his seat as at other times, on the seat by the wall. Jonathan stood, and Abner sat by Saul's side, but David's place was empty.

**26** Saul said nothing that day, for he thought, "Something has happened to him. He is not clean; surely he is not clean."

**27** And it came to pass on the day after the new moon, the second day, that David's place was empty. Saul said to Jonathan his son, "Why has the son of Jesse not come to the meal, either yesterday or today?"

**28** Jonathan answered Saul, "David earnestly asked leave of me to go to Bethlehem.

**29** He said, 'Please let me go, for our clan has a sacrifice in the town, and my brother has commanded me to be there. So now, if I have found favor in your eyes, let me slip away and see my brothers.' That is why he has not come to the king's table."

**30** Saul's anger burned against Jonathan, and he said to him, "You son of a perverse, rebellious woman! Do I not know that you have chosen the son of Jesse, to your own shame and to the shame of your mother's nakedness?

**31** For as long as the son of Jesse lives on the earth, neither you nor your kingdom will be established. So now send and bring him to me, for he deserves to die."

**32** Jonathan answered Saul his father, "Why should he be put to death? What has he done?"

**33** Saul hurled the spear at him to strike him, and Jonathan knew that his father had resolved to kill David.

**34** Jonathan rose from the table in fierce anger and ate no food on the second day of the month, for he was grieved for David, because his father had disgraced him.

## The Arrows

**35** And it came to pass in the morning that Jonathan went out into the field to the meeting with David, and a little boy was with him.

**36** He said to his boy, "Run, find the arrows I am shooting." As the boy ran, he shot an arrow beyond him.

**37** When the boy came to the place of the arrow that Jonathan had shot, Jonathan called after the boy, "Is not the arrow beyond you?"

**38** Jonathan called after the boy, "Hurry! Be quick! Do not stop!" So Jonathan's boy gathered up the arrows and came to his master.

**39** The boy knew nothing; only Jonathan and David knew the matter.

**40** Jonathan gave his weapons to his boy and said to him, "Go, carry them to the town."

**41** When the boy had gone, David rose from beside the south side, fell on his face to the ground and bowed down three times. They kissed one another and wept with one another, until David wept the more.

**42** Jonathan said to David, "Go in peace, for we have both sworn in the name of the LORD, saying, 'The LORD shall be between me and you, and between my seed and your seed, forever.'" Then David rose and went, and Jonathan went into the town.

## Notes

- **vv5, 18, 24 "the new moon"** — *chodesh*, the first day of the month, marked by its own burnt offerings (Numbers 28:11) and by trumpets *at the beginnings of your months* (Numbers 10:10). The king's household ate together at it, and an empty seat was noticed.
- **v8 "Show loyal love to your servant, for you have brought your servant into a covenant of the LORD"** — *chesed*, the loyalty owed inside a covenant. The covenant is the one Jonathan made in 18:3; David calls it *a covenant of the LORD*, made before Him.
- **v13 "May the LORD be with you, as He has been with my father"** — *the LORD is with him* is the story's word for David (16:18, 18:12, 18:14). The king's son says it to David as a blessing, and speaks of the LORD's presence with Saul as past.
- **vv14–15 "show me the loyal love of the LORD… Do not cut off your loyal love from my house"** — *karat*, **cut off**, runs through the verses: *do not cut off your loyal love… when the LORD cuts off David's enemies*, and Jonathan *cut*, made, a covenant (v16). David keeps it when he is king: *Is there still anyone left of the house of Saul, so that I may show him loyal love for Jonathan's sake?* (2 Samuel 9:1), and Jonathan's son *shall eat bread at my table always* (2 Samuel 9:7), the table David is missing here.
- **v18 "you will be missed, because your seat will be empty"** — *ve-nifqadta ki yippaqed moshavekha*: the same verb, *paqad*, **to be looked for**, and so **to be missed** or **empty**, is used of David and of his seat.
- **v26 "He is not clean; surely he is not clean"** — the new-moon meal was a sacrifice (v29), and *the person who eats flesh from the peace offering… while he is unclean* is cut off (Leviticus 7:20). Saul supposes an accident has made David unable to come.
- **vv27, 30, 31 "the son of Jesse"** — Saul does not say David's name in the chapter. Three times he calls him *the son of Jesse*.
- **v31 "he deserves to die"** — *ben mavet*, **a son of death**. David will say it of the rich man in Nathan's story: *As the LORD lives, the man who did this deserves to die* (2 Samuel 12:5).
- **v33 "Saul hurled the spear at him"** — *va-yatel*, the verb of the spear at David (18:11). Now Saul throws it at his own son, and Jonathan has his answer to *Why should he be put to death?* (v32).
- **v42 "Then David rose and went, and Jonathan went into the town"** — in the Hebrew these words are 21:1, and the rest of chapter 21 runs one verse ahead of the English. The Greek has them as 20:43.
