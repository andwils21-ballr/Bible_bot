# 1 Enoch 9

**1** Then Michael and Uriel and Raphael and Gabriel looked down from heaven and saw much blood being shed on the earth, and all the lawlessness being done on the earth.

**2** They said to one another, "The sound of their crying — the wasted earth cries out as far as the gates of heaven."

**3** "Now to you, holy ones of heaven, the souls of men bring their case, saying, 'Bring our judgment before the Most High.'"

**4** They said to the Lord, "You are Lord of lords and God of gods and King of kings. The throne of Your glory is for all the generations of eternity, and Your name is holy and glorious for all the generations of eternity, blessed and glorious.

**5** You made all things, and power over all is with You, and everything is uncovered and open before You, and You see all things, and there is nothing that can be hidden from You.

**6** See then what Azazel has done — how he taught all unrighteousness on the earth, and revealed the eternal secrets that are kept in heaven, which men were striving to learn.

**7** Shemihazah, to whom You gave authority to rule over those who are with him.

**8** They went in to the daughters of the men of the earth and slept with them and defiled themselves, and revealed to them all these sins.

**9** The women bore giants, and by them the whole earth has been filled with blood and unrighteousness.

**10** Now — the souls of those who have died cry out and bring their case as far as the gates of heaven, and their groaning has gone up and cannot stop, because of the lawless deeds done on the earth.

**11** You know all things before they come to pass, and You see this and their deeds, and You say nothing to us. What are we to do with them about this?"

## Notes

- **v1 the four names** — the **Greek** gives Michael, Uriel, Raphael and Gabriel, and this rendering follows it. The **Ge'ez** has Michael, Gabriel, **Suriel** and Uriel. The two lists agree on three names and differ on the fourth, and Suriel and Raphael are not variants of one name — they are different angels. The set of four archangels is not fixed even inside this one book.
- **v2 "the wasted earth cries out"** — the cry in chapter 8 went up from men being destroyed. Here it is the earth making the noise. The book keeps giving the ground a voice, and it has done so since 7:6.
- **v3 "bring their case"** — the vocabulary through this chapter is legal: a case brought, a judgment requested, a charge laid. The four are being asked to act as advocates, and what they do in vv4–11 is file a brief.
- **v4 the doxology, and what the Greek puts after it** — the Greek witness adds a sentence the Ge'ez does not have: *Then the Most High commanded the holy archangels, and they bound their leaders and cast them into the abyss until the judgment.* In the Greek the sentence of judgment lands **here**, in the middle of the appeal, before the angels have finished speaking. In the Ge'ez nothing happens until chapter 10. That is a difference in the shape of the story, not in a word.
- **v6 "the eternal secrets that are kept in heaven"** — the charge against Azazel is not that he invented anything. It is that what he handed over was already real, and already above. The crime is disclosure.
- **v7 "to whom You gave authority"** — the angels point out, carefully, that the ringleader holds his rank by appointment. It is the most pointed sentence in the brief and the shortest.
- **v11 "You say nothing to us"** — the Ge'ez ends the appeal there. The **Greek** is sharper: *You see these things and **permit them**, yet you do not tell us what we ought to do.* One witness has the angels complaining that God is silent. The other has them saying He is allowing it.
- **v11 "What ought we to do to them about this?"** — the chapter ends on a question from heaven, unanswered, and the four who ask it are the ones who will be given the answer one verse later. The whole appeal is built so that the reader hears the case before the sentence.
