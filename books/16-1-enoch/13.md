# 1 Enoch 13

**1** And Enoch went and said to Azazel, "There will be no peace for you. A great judgment has gone out against you, to bind you.

**2** And there will be no relief or mercy or petition for you, because of the wrong you taught, and because of all the works of blasphemy and violence and sin that you showed to the sons of men."

**3** Then I went and spoke to all of them together, and they were all afraid, and fear and trembling seized them.

**4** And they asked me to write a record of petition for them, so that they might obtain forgiveness, and that I might carry the record of their petition up before the Lord of heaven —

**5** because from then on they could not speak, nor lift up their eyes to heaven, for shame at the sins for which they were condemned.

**6** And then I wrote the record of their petition and their supplication, concerning their spirits and each of their deeds, and concerning what they asked — that there might be forgiveness for them, and forbearance.

**7** And I went and sat down by the waters of Dan, in the land of Dan, which is south-west of Hermon, and I read out the record of their petition until I fell asleep.

**8** And here — a dream came to me, and visions fell upon me, and I saw a vision of wrath, and a voice came saying: speak to the sons of heaven and rebuke them.

**9** And when I woke, I came to them, and they were all sitting gathered together, mourning at Ubelseyael, which is between Lebanon and Senesher, with their faces covered.

**10** And I spoke before them all the visions I had seen in my sleep, and I began to speak words of righteousness, and to rebuke the Watchers of heaven.

## Notes

- **v1 "There will be no peace for you"** — the refrain, delivered face to face and to one named angel. It has been said to human sinners (5:4) and to the whole company of Watchers (12:5). Chapter 16 will close the section by repeating it one last time.
- **v4 "they asked me to write a record of petition"** — the extraordinary reversal of the book. The fallen angels cannot approach God, so they hire a man as their lawyer. Enoch drafts the appeal, reads it aloud, and carries it up. Chapter 15 will tell him exactly how backwards this is.
- **v5 "they could not speak, nor lift up their eyes"** — the reason given is not that they are forbidden. It is shame. Beings who left heaven cannot now look at it.
- **v7 "by the waters of Dan"** — a real place: the springs at the foot of Mount Hermon that feed the Jordan, in the territory of the tribe of Dan. The book has put the Watchers on Hermon since 6:6, and it keeps its geography consistent. The Ge'ez says *south-west* of Hermon; the Greek says *to the south of Hermon on the west*.
- **v7 "I read out the record... until I fell asleep"** — he does not pray it. He reads the document aloud, by water, at the mountain where the oath was sworn, and falls asleep over it. The answer comes as a dream.
- **v8 "and a voice came saying"** — the **Greek** has the voice; the Ge'ez has only the vision of wrath and Enoch's own understanding that he should speak. One witness has him commissioned out loud, the other has him work it out.
- **v9 "mourning at Ubelseyael"** — the Ge'ez and the Greek give different forms of both place names: **Ubelseyael** against *Ebelsata*, and **Senesher** against *Senisel*. Neither place is known from anywhere else. What both agree on is the picture: two hundred angels sitting on the ground between Lebanon and another range, faces covered, waiting for a man to come back with an answer.
