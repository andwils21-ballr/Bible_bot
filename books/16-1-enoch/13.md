# 1 Enoch 13

**1** Enoch went and said to Azazel, "There will be no peace for you. A great judgment has gone out against you, to bind you.

**2** There will be no relief or mercy or petition for you, because of the wrong you taught, and because of all the works of blasphemy and violence and sin that you showed to the sons of men."

**3** Then I went and spoke to all of them together, and they were all afraid, and fear and trembling seized them.

**4** They asked me to write a record of petition for them, so that they might obtain forgiveness, and that I might carry the record of their petition up before the Lord of heaven —

**5** because from then on they could not speak, nor lift up their eyes to heaven, for shame at the sins for which they were condemned.

**6** Then I wrote the record of their petition and their supplication, concerning their spirits and each of their deeds, and concerning what they asked — that there might be forgiveness for them, and forbearance.

**7** I went and sat down by the waters of Dan, in the land of Dan, which is south-west of Hermon, and I read out the record of their petition until I fell asleep.

**8** A dream came to me, and visions fell upon me, and I saw a vision of wrath, and a voice came saying: speak to the sons of heaven and rebuke them.

**9** When I woke, I came to them, and they were all sitting gathered together, mourning at Ubelseyael, which is between Lebanon and Senesher, with their faces covered.

**10** I spoke before them all the visions I had seen in my sleep, and I began to speak words of righteousness, and to rebuke the Watchers of heaven.

## Notes

- **v4 "they asked me to write a record of petition"** — the extraordinary reversal of the book. The fallen angels cannot approach God, so they hire a man as their lawyer. Enoch drafts the appeal, reads it aloud, and carries it up. Chapter 15 will tell him exactly how backwards this is.
