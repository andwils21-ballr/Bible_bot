# 1 Enoch 32

**1** After these fragrances, looking north over the mountains, I saw seven mountains full of choice nard and fragrant trees and pepper.

**2** From there I went over the summits of those mountains, far to the east, and I passed over the Red Sea and went far from it, and passed above the angel Zotiel.

## The Garden of Righteousness

**3** I came into the garden of righteousness, and beyond those trees I saw many trees, great ones, growing there. Their fragrance was good, and they were great and good, and their beauty was abundant and glorious. There too was the tree of wisdom: those who eat from it learn great wisdom.

**4** It is like the carob tree, and its fruit is like a very beautiful cluster of grapes, and the fragrance of that tree spreads and reaches far.

**5** I said, "How beautiful this tree is, and how good and delightful to look at!"

**6** The holy angel Raphael, who was with me, answered me and said, "This is the tree of wisdom that your aged father and your ancient mother, who were before you, ate from. They learned wisdom, and their eyes were opened, and they knew that they were naked, and they were driven out of the garden."

## Notes

- **v2 "the Red Sea"** — Ge'ez *bāḥra ʾErtrā*, Greek *erythra thalassa*, the Erythraean Sea: in Greek writers the name covers the Red Sea and the waters east of it. Enoch crosses it going east, toward the garden *in Eden, in the east* of Genesis 2:8. *Zotiel* is named only here: an angel in the Ge'ez, a bare name in the Greek.
- **v3 "the tree of wisdom"** — the **Greek** first singles out *two especially, very great*, then the tree of wisdom, *of whose fruit the holy ones eat*. The Ge'ez has no *holy ones*: anyone who eats learns wisdom.
- **v4 "like the carob tree"** — the Ge'ez. The **Greek** is twice as long here: *like a fir in height, its leaves like the carob, its fruit like clusters of the vine*. The Ge'ez keeps only the carob and the grapes. No witness calls it an apple, a fig or any tree named in Genesis.
- **v5 "How beautiful this tree is, and how good and delightful to look at"** — Enoch's words are close to what Eve saw in Genesis 3:6, *that the tree was good for food, and that it was a delight to the eyes*. He stands where she stood and says what she saw.
- **v6 "your aged father and your ancient mother"** — Adam and Eve, unnamed. The Ge'ez continues the story in the words of Genesis 3:7 and 3:23–24: *their eyes were opened, and they knew that they were naked*, and they were driven out. The **Greek** breaks off after *your father ate*: the Greek manuscript of this part of the book ends here, and from 33 to 36 the Ge'ez is the only witness.
