# 1 Enoch 78

**1** These are the names of the sun: the first Oryares, and the second Tomases.

**2** The moon has four names: the first name is Asonya, the second Ebla, the third Benase, and the fourth Erae.

**3** These are the two great lights. Their circle is like the circle of heaven, and the measures of the two are equal.

**4** In the circle of the sun there are seven parts of light that are added to it beyond the moon, and by measure it is put in until the seventh part of the sun passes.

**5** They set and enter the gates of the west, go around by the north, and come out through the gates of the east on the face of heaven.

**6** When the moon rises, it appears in heaven with half a seventh part of light in it, and on the fourteenth day it completes all its light.

**7** Fifteen parts of light are put into it, until on the fifteenth day its light is complete, for the sign of the year, and it becomes fifteen parts; and the moon grows by half a seventh at a time.

**8** In its waning, on the first day it decreases to fourteen parts of its light, on the second it decreases to thirteen parts, on the third to twelve, on the fourth to eleven, on the fifth to ten, on the sixth to nine, on the seventh to eight, on the eighth to seven, on the ninth to six, on the tenth to five, on the eleventh to four, on the twelfth to three, on the thirteenth to two, on the fourteenth to half a seventh, and on the fifteenth day all the light that remains is gone.

**9** In appointed months the moon has twenty-nine days, and at times twenty-eight.

**10** Uriel the angel showed me another law: when light is put into the moon, and from which side it is put in from the sun.

**11** All the time the moon is going on, it takes its light facing the sun, and in fifteen days its light is complete in heaven; and when it is all aglow, its light is complete in heaven.

**12** The first day is called the Rising, because on that day the light rises on it.

**13** It becomes full exactly on the day the sun goes down in the west. From the east it rises at night, and the moon shines all night until the sun rises opposite it, and the moon is seen opposite the sun.

**14** From the side where the light of the moon comes out, from there it wanes again until all the light is gone and the days of the month pass, and its circle stays empty, without light.

**15** Three months it makes of thirty days each, for its time, and three months it makes of twenty-nine days each, in which it makes its waning, in the first period and in the first gate, in one hundred and seventy-seven days.

**16** In the time of its going out it appears for three months of thirty days each, and for three months of twenty-nine days each.

**17** At night it appears like a man, for twenty days at a time, and by day like the sky, for there is nothing else in it except its light.

## Notes

- **vv1–2 the names of the sun and the moon** — given as the EOTC spells them. Charles has *Orjârês* and *Tômâs* for the sun, *Asônjâ, Eblâ, Benâsê* and *Erâe* for the moon.
- **v3 "the measures of the two are equal"** — as at 72:37.
- **v7 "Fifteen parts of light"** — the EOTC has *šalastu təḫəmsəta*, whose sense is uncertain; the verse counts the moon's light to its fullness on the fifteenth day, and Charles has *fifteen parts*, which the English follows (see the choice on numbers in the 72 report).
- **vv6–8 the waxing and waning** — the EOTC's count of the wane runs fourteen, thirteen, twelve and on down by one part a day to half a seventh on the fourteenth day, with nothing left on the fifteenth.
- **v12 "the Rising"** — *śarq*, the name of the first day of the month; Charles gives *the new moon*.
- **v15 "one hundred and seventy-seven days"** — three months of thirty days and three of twenty-nine: 90 + 87 = 177.
- **v17 "like a man, for twenty days at a time"** — the sense is uncertain; *days* is supplied, as Charles supplies it.
- **Source text** — Ge'ez: Beta Masaheft, Universität Hamburg (text of the EOTC printed Bible), CC BY-SA 4.0. Greek (scraps of vv1, 8): Online Critical Pseudepigrapha, CC BY 4.0. English translation by this project.
