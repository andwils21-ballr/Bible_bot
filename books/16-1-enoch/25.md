# 1 Enoch 25

**1** He said to me, "Enoch, why do you ask me about the fragrance of this tree, and why do you want to know the truth?"

**2** Then I answered him, "I want to know about everything, but especially about this tree."

**3** He answered me, "This high mountain you saw, whose summit is like the throne of God, is His throne, where the Holy and Great One, the Lord of glory, the Eternal King, will sit when He comes down to visit the earth in goodness.

**4** As for this fragrant tree, no flesh has authority to touch it until the great judgment, when He takes vengeance on all and brings everything to its end forever. Then it will be given to the righteous and the holy.

**5** Its fruit will be life for the chosen. It will be transplanted toward the north, into the holy place, beside the house of God, the Eternal King.

**6** Then they will rejoice greatly and be glad, and they will go into the holy place. Its fragrance will be in their bones, and they will live a longer life on earth than your fathers lived, and in their days no torment or plague or affliction will touch them."

**7** Then I blessed the God of glory, the Eternal King, who has prepared such things for righteous men, and created them, and promised to give them.

## Notes

- **v3 the mountain is the throne** — chapter 24 said the seventh mountain was *like the seat of a throne*. Here the comparison is dropped: it **is** His throne, and the answer to a question about a tree turns out to be about the mountain it stands on.
- **v4 "no flesh has authority to touch it"** — **Genesis 3:22–24** ends with the man put out of the garden *lest he put out his hand and take also from the tree of life, and eat, and live forever*, with cherubim set to guard the way. This book picks the tree up again at the other end of history: still untouchable, still guarded, but now with a date on it.
- **v4 "then it will be given to the righteous"** — the difference between this and Genesis is one word: **until**. Genesis bars the tree; this bars it *until the great judgment*. The prohibition becomes a delay.
- **v5 "it will be transplanted... beside the house of God"** — the tree does not stay on its mountain. It is moved, and the place it is moved to is the temple. What was lost from a garden comes back into a building.
- **v6 "rejoice greatly"** — the source doubles the verb with its own noun, *rejoice with rejoicing*, the intensifier Hebrew and Ge'ez both use and English does not. It is the same construction as *dying you shall die* at Genesis 2:17. What it marks is force, not repetition.
- **v6 "its fragrance will be in their bones"** — the strangest promise in the chapter and the most physical. Not eaten, not worn: in the bones. The whole description of the tree began with its smell (24:4) and ends with the smell inside the people.
- **v6 "a longer life than your fathers lived"** — Enoch's fathers are the men of Genesis 5, who lived eight and nine hundred years. The promise is not immortality; it is more than nine hundred years, which is a strange and very concrete thing to promise.
- **v3 what the Greek and the Ge'ez each say** — the Greek has the Eternal King sitting on the throne *when he comes down to visit the earth in goodness*. The Ge'ez is broken at the same point and repeats itself. Where a sentence is damaged in one witness and clean in the other, this rendering follows the clean one.
