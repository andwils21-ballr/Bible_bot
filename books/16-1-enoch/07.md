# 1 Enoch 7

**1** All the others with them took wives for themselves, each choosing one for himself, and they began to go in to them and to defile themselves with them. They taught them sorceries and spells, and the cutting of roots, and showed them plants.

**2** They became pregnant and bore great giants, whose height was three thousand cubits.

**3** These consumed all the labor of men, until men could no longer sustain them.

**4** The giants turned against them, to devour mankind.

**5** They began to sin against the birds and the beasts and the creeping things and the fish, and to devour one another's flesh, and to drink the blood from it.

**6** Then the earth brought accusation against the lawless ones.

## Notes

- **v1 "they taught them sorceries and spells, and the cutting of roots"** — the marriages are one sentence; the teaching gets the rest of the verse, and it is what the next chapters return to. The corruption in this book is not chiefly sexual. It is **knowledge handed down that was not meant to come down.**
- **v2 "three thousand cubits"** — about forty-five hundred feet, taller than any mountain in Israel. The number is in the Ge'ez and the Greek alike. Whatever it is, it is not an attempt at a plausible giant.
- **vv3–4 "consumed all the labor of men... then the giants turned against them"** — the order matters. First they eat what people produce; only when there is nothing left do they eat the people. The famine comes before the violence, and the text presents that as the natural sequence.
- **v5 "the birds and the beasts and the creeping things and the fish"** — the categories of the fifth and sixth days of creation, run through as a list of victims. The giants work backward through what was made.
- **v5 "and to drink the blood from it"** — the one prohibition Genesis lays on all humanity after the flood is exactly this: *flesh with its life, its blood, you shall not eat* (Genesis 9:4). The giants are doing beforehand the single thing the new world will be forbidden.
- **v6 "the earth brought accusation"** — the earth is the plaintiff, not the victim. It is the same legal picture as Genesis 4:10, where Abel's blood **cries out** to God from the ground. In this book the ground itself files the complaint, and chapters 8 and 9 are the case being heard.
- **On the chapter division** — the **Aramaic** fragment begins this chapter with a line the Ge'ez and Greek place at the end of chapter 6: *these are the chiefs and the chiefs of the tens*. The break between the two chapters sits in a different place in the oldest witness.
- **v6 in the Aramaic** — the fragment ends with a *vacat*: a deliberate blank space left in the scroll. It is a scribe's paragraph mark, still visible on the leather, and it falls exactly where the narrative turns from what the Watchers did to what heaven does about it.
