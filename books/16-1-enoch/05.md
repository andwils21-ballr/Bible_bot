# 1 Enoch 5

**1** Observe how the trees cover themselves with green leaves, and how all their fruit is for honor and glory. Think carefully about all His works, and understand that the living God made them so, and that He lives for all the ages.

**2** And all His works stand before Him, going on from year to year in the same way, and all His works carry out their tasks for Him and do not change — everything happens just as it was commanded.

**3** And see how the sea and the rivers do the same, and do not change their works from His word.

**4** But you have not held fast, and you have not done according to His commandments. You have turned away, and you have spoken great and hard words with your unclean mouth against His greatness. You have spoken against Him in your lies, you hard-hearted — there is no peace for you.

**5** And because of this you will curse your days, and the years of your life will be destroyed.

**6** Then your names will become an everlasting curse to all the righteous, and by you all who curse will curse, and all the sinners and the wicked will swear by you.

**7** And for the chosen there will be light and favor and peace, and they will inherit the earth. But for you, the wicked, there will be a curse.

**8** Then light and favor will be given to the chosen, and they will inherit the earth. Then wisdom will be given to all the chosen, and they will all live, and they will never sin again — not through unfaithfulness and not through pride. In the enlightened man there will be light, and in the understanding man, insight, and they will not go wrong.

**9** And they will not sin all the days of their life, and they will not die in burning anger, but they will complete the number of the days of their life. And their life will grow in peace, and the years of their joy will be multiplied in gladness and in everlasting peace, through all the days of their life.

## Notes

- **v1 "for honor and glory"** — in the Greek, and, broken, in the **Aramaic** (*for maj]estic splen[dor*). The point of the whole nature sequence turns on it: the fruit is not for eating, it is for honor. Obedience is being described as a kind of beauty.
- **v2 "everything happens just as it was commanded"** — the Greek and the **Aramaic** agree on how this sentence ends; the Aramaic independently has *all of them perform his word*.
- **v4 "you have spoken great and hard words with your unclean mouth"** — the charge, and the reason for everything that follows. The Greek and the **Aramaic** carry it almost phrase for phrase: *But you have changed your works [... and have spo]ken against him great and harsh words with the mouth of your impurity; [... hearts,] there will be no peace for you.* Where two witnesses this far apart in age and language line up this closely, the wording behind both is likely near the original.
- **v4 "there is no peace for you"** — a refrain in this book, and it lands on the accused rather than on their circumstances. Nothing is said about what will happen to them. It is said about what they are.
- **v6 "all the sinners and the wicked will swear by you"** — a name used in an oath is a name people trust. The curse is that these names stay in use — as the standard of what a wrecked life looks like.
- **v8 "wisdom will be given to all the chosen"** — the most hopeful sentence in the first five chapters, and it survives in the Greek: wisdom, life, the end of sin, light in the enlightened man, insight in the understanding one.
- **v9 "they will complete the number of the days of their life"** — the promise is not immortality. It is that the count runs out on time. Against chapter 6, where two hundred beings come down and ruin exactly this, it is the argument of the book stated in advance.
