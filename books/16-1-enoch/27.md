# 1 Enoch 27

**1** Then I said, "Why is this land blessed and all full of trees, but this ravine between them accursed?"

**2** Then Raphael answered me, one of the holy angels who was with me, "This accursed valley is for those who are accursed forever. Here will be gathered all who speak with their mouth an improper word against the Lord, and say hard things about His glory. Here they will be gathered, and here will be their place.

**3** And in the last times, in the days of the true judgment, in the presence of the righteous forever — here the godless will bless the Lord of glory, the Eternal King.

**4** In the days of their judgment they will bless Him for the mercy He has allotted them."

**5** Then I blessed the Lord of glory, and I proclaimed His glory and praised Him greatly.

## Notes

- **The valley is Gehenna, and this is where it becomes that.** The ravine west and south of Jerusalem is the **Valley of Hinnom**, *gei ben Hinnom*. It appears twelve times in the Hebrew Bible, and what happens there is child sacrifice: Ahaz *burned his sons in the fire* there (2 Chronicles 28:3), Manasseh did the same (33:6), and Josiah *defiled the Topheth which is in the valley of the sons of Hinnom, so that no one might make his son or daughter pass through the fire* (2 Kings 23:10). **Nowhere in the Hebrew Bible is it a place of judgment after death.** It is a real valley with a real history.
- **And in the Gospels it is the fire of judgment.** Greek transliterates *gei Hinnom* as **γέεννα**, *geenna* — the word in Matthew, Mark and Luke that English Bibles print as **hell**. Between the Hebrew Bible and the New Testament, a rubbish-strewn valley with a memory of burning children became the name of the place where judgment falls. **This chapter is that turn, in writing.** A book Jude quotes puts the gathering of the accursed in exactly that ravine.
- **v2 "who speak with their mouth an improper word against the Lord"** — the offence named is speech. Not idolatry and not murder: hard words about His glory. It is the same charge laid against the wicked in 5:4 — *you have spoken great and hard words with your unclean mouth against His greatness* — and the book is consistent about what it thinks the worst sin is.
- **v3 "here the godless will bless the Lord"** — that is the **Greek**. The Ge'ez says *here the **merciful** will bless the Lord*. It is the difference between the damned being made to praise God and the righteous praising Him over them, and the two witnesses land on opposite parties. The Greek is the harder reading and the one this rendering follows; v4, where *they* bless Him for mercy allotted to *them*, reads more naturally of the condemned.
- **v1 the question is about landscape** — Enoch does not ask why people are punished. He asks why one valley is cursed when the land around it is full of trees. The book's answers keep arriving as geography.
