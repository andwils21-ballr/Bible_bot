# 1 Enoch 60

**1** In the five hundredth year, in the seventh month, on the fourteenth of the month, in the life of Enoch. In that parable I saw how the heaven of heavens shook violently, and the host of the Most High and the angels, thousands of thousands and ten thousand times ten thousand, were thrown into great turmoil.

**2** Then I saw the Head of Days sitting on the throne of His glory, and the angels and the righteous standing around Him.

**3** A great trembling seized me, and fear took hold of me. My loins gave way and went slack, my whole body melted, and I fell on my face.

**4** Holy Michael sent another holy angel, one of the holy angels, and he raised me up. When he had raised me up, my spirit returned, for I had not been able to endure the sight of that host, its turmoil, and the shaking of heaven.

**5** Holy Michael said to me, "Why are you troubled by a vision like this? Until today the day of His mercy has lasted, and He has been merciful and slow to anger toward those who live on the dry land.

**6** When the day of power and punishment and judgment comes, which the Lord of Spirits has prepared for those who bow down to the righteous judgment, and for those who deny the righteous judgment, and for those who take His name in vain, that day has been prepared: for the chosen a covenant, and for the sinners an inquiry."

**7** On that day two monsters will be separated: a female monster named Leviathan, to live in the depths of the sea above the springs of the waters,

**8** and the male, named Behemoth, who holds with his breast an unseen wilderness named Dendayn, east of the garden where the righteous and the chosen live, where my forefather was taken, the seventh from Adam, the first of the men whom the Lord of Spirits made.

**9** I asked that other angel to show me the might of those monsters, how they were separated on one day and thrown, one into the depths of the sea and one onto the dry land of the wilderness.

**10** He said to me, "You, son of man, want to know here what is hidden."

**11** The other angel who went with me spoke to me, and he showed me what is hidden: what is first and what is last in heaven, in the height, and on the dry land, in the depth, and at the ends of heaven and at the foundation of heaven,

**12** and in the storehouses of the winds: how the spirits are divided, and how they are weighed, and how the springs and the winds are counted by the power of the spirit, and the power of the light of the moon, and how it is a power of righteousness, and the divisions of the stars by their names, and how each is divided;

**13** and the thunders by the places where they fall, and how each is divided, and the lightning, how it flashes, and their host, that they may obey at once.

**14** For the thunder has resting places; they are given to its voice with patience. Thunder and lightning are not separated, and neither goes alone: the two go together by their spirit and are not separated.

**15** For when the lightning flashes, the thunder gives its voice, and the spirit rests at its proper time and divides evenly between them. For the storehouse of their times is like the sand, and each of them in its time is held with a bridle and turned back by the power of the spirit, and so it is driven on across the many regions of the earth.

**16** The spirit of the sea is male, and by the power of the sea's strength he turns it back with a bridle, and so it is driven on and scattered among all the mountains of the earth.

**17** The spirit of the frost is its own angel, and the spirit of the hail is a good angel.

**18** The angel of the snow has departed because of his wickedness, and the snow has a spirit of its own. What rises from it is like smoke, and its name is Dadak.

**19** The spirit of the mist is not joined with them in their storehouses; it has a storehouse of its own, for its course is in glory, in light and in darkness, in winter and in summer. Its storehouse is light, and the spirit is its angel.

**20** The spirit of the dew has its dwelling at the ends of heaven and is joined to the storehouses of the rain. Its course is in winter and in summer, and its clouds and the clouds of the mist are joined, and one gives to the other.

**21** When the spirit of the rain moves out of its storehouse, the angels come and open the storehouse and lead it out. When it is scattered over all the dry land, it joins with all the water on the dry land, and whenever it joins with the water on the dry land —

**22** for the waters are for those who live on the dry land, for the food of the dry land comes from the Most High who is in heaven. For this reason the rain has a measure, and the angels take charge of it.

**23** All these I saw as far as the garden of the righteous.

**24** The angel of peace who was with me said to me, "These two monsters, prepared according to the greatness of the Lord, will be fed, so that the punishment of the Lord may not be in vain. Children will be killed with their mothers, and sons with their fathers.

**25** When the punishment of the Lord of Spirits rests on them, so that it may not come on them in vain, afterward the judgment will take place in His mercy and His patience."

## Notes

- **v1 "In the five hundredth year… in the life of Enoch"** — the date does not fit Enoch, who lived three hundred and sixty-five years (Genesis 5:23). Genesis gives the five hundredth year to Noah: *Noah was five hundred years old* (Genesis 5:32). In v8 the one speaking calls Enoch *my forefather, the seventh from Adam*, so in this chapter the vision is Noah's.
- **vv3–4 "I fell on my face… he raised me up"** — Daniel before Gabriel: *I was terrified and fell on my face… and he touched me and set me upright* (Daniel 8:17–18).
- **v5 "merciful and slow to anger"** — *maḥāri wa-rəḥuqa maʿāt*, the words used of Michael himself at 40:9, *the merciful and slow to anger, holy Michael*. Here Michael says them of God, as the LORD proclaims Himself at Sinai: *God merciful and gracious, slow to anger* (Exodus 34:6).
- **v6 "for those who bow down to the righteous judgment"** — so both Ge'ez witnesses. Charles adds a *not*, *those who worship not*. As it stands, the day is prepared for all three groups, and the verse ends by saying how it falls on each: *for the chosen a covenant, and for the sinners an inquiry*. *Take His name in vain* is *yənaśśəʾu səmo bak*, **lift up His name in vain**, the commandment's own picture: *You shall not lift up the name of the LORD your God for emptiness* (Exodus 20:7).
- **vv7–8 "Leviathan… Behemoth"** — the two great beasts of Job, *Behemoth, which I made along with you* (Job 40:15) and Leviathan: *Can you draw out Leviathan with a hook?* (Job 41:1). Isaiah sets Leviathan's end *on that day*, when the LORD will punish *Leviathan the fleeing serpent… and kill the dragon that is in the sea* (Isaiah 27:1). Here Leviathan is female and Behemoth male, one given the sea and the other the dry land. The wilderness is *Dēndāyn* in the EOTC, *Dandayn* in Knibb's Ge'ez.
- **v8 "where my forefather was taken, the seventh from Adam"** — *Enoch walked with God. He was not, for God took him* (Genesis 5:24). Jude calls him by the same title when he quotes this book: *Enoch, the seventh from Adam, prophesied* (Jude 14).
- **v10 "son of man"** — *walda sabʾ*, the words of the title *the Son of Man* (46:2). Here they are said to the man seeing the vision, as Gabriel says to Daniel, *Understand, son of man* (Daniel 8:17).
- **v12 "how the spirits are divided"** — *manāfəst*, the word in *the Lord of Spirits*. In vv12–21 the spirits are the powers of the weather: the spirit of the sea, of frost, hail, snow, mist, dew and rain.
- **vv12, 22 "how they are weighed… the rain has a measure"** — Job 28:25–26: God gave *the wind its weight* and measured out *the waters by measure*, *when He made a decree for the rain and a way for the thunderbolt*.
- **v14 "the thunder has resting places"** — *məʿrāfāt*, the word for the *resting places* of the righteous at 45:3. The sense of the whole clause is uncertain; Charles marks it as corrupt.
- **v16 "he turns it back with a bridle"** — the sea held in, as in the LORD's words to Job: *who shut in the sea with doors… and said, Thus far you shall come, and no farther* (Job 38:8, 11).
- **v18 "The angel of the snow has departed because of his wickedness"** — the EOTC, *malʾaka ḥamadā ḫadaga ba-ʾənta ʾəkaya ziʾahu*. *ʾƏkay* is **wickedness**; its adjective is the word in *every wicked deed* (10:16). Knibb's Ge'ez has *the spirit of the snow* and *ḫayla ziʾahu*, **his strength**; both English renderings follow it. In the EOTC the snow's angel stands against the *good angel* of the hail in v17.
- **v24 "so that the punishment of the Lord may not be in vain"** — neither Ge'ez witness has *not* in this verse. It stands in v25: *so that it may not come on them in vain*.
