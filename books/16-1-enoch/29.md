# 1 Enoch 29

**1** From there I went to another place, beyond the desert, and drew near to the east of that mountain.

**2** There I saw trees of judgment, giving off a strong fragrance of frankincense and myrrh, and the trees were not like one another.

---

## Notes

- **v1 "beyond the desert"** — the Ge'ez, *emna madbara*, where *madbar* is the word for desert. The **Greek** again has a place name, **Babdera**, as it had *Mandobara* at 28:1. The two names in the Greek and the one word in the Ge'ez look like the same thing seen two ways: a place called by a name, or a name taken as a description.
- **v2 "trees of judgment"** — both witnesses have it: Ge'ez *ʿəḍawa kʷənnane*, Greek *dendra kriseōs*. Neither explains why fragrant trees would be called trees of *judgment*. At 25:4 the fragrant tree on the mountain may not be touched *until the great judgment*, and these trees stand on the road east to the garden (32:3). What the phrase means is not stated anywhere in the book.
- **v2 "the trees were not like one another"** — the Ge'ez. The **Greek** says the opposite kind of thing: *their trees were like nut trees*. The Ge'ez *ʾiyətmāsalu*, **they did not resemble**, and the Greek comparison cannot both stand, and nothing in the chapter decides between them.
