# 1 Enoch 12

**1** And before all these things Enoch was taken, and no one among men knew where he was taken, or where he was, or what had become of him.

**2** And his whole dwelling was with the Watchers and with the holy ones in his days.

**3** And I, Enoch, was blessing the Lord of majesty and King of the ages. And here — the Watchers called me, Enoch the scribe, and said to me:

**4** "Enoch, scribe of righteousness, go and declare to the Watchers of heaven — who have left the high heaven and the eternal holy sanctuary, and have defiled themselves with women, and have done as the sons of men do, and have taken wives for themselves, and have worked great ruin on the earth:

**5** 'There will be no peace for you, nor forgiveness of sin.

**6** And concerning your sons in whom you rejoice — you will see the slaughter of your beloved ones, and you will lament over the destruction of your sons, and you will make supplication forever, but there will be neither mercy nor peace for you.'"

## Notes

- **v1 "Enoch was taken, and no one knew where"** — **Genesis 5:24** says it in six Hebrew words: *and Enoch walked with God, and he was not, for God took him.* Genesis says it and moves on. This book takes that sentence as its license: a man removed from the world, unaccounted for, is available to be told things. Everything in these 108 chapters stands on one verse that explains nothing.
- **v1 "before all these things"** — the book has just narrated the flood, the binding of Azazel and the renewal of the earth. Now it goes back. The chapter is a hinge: what follows is the part of the story Enoch himself is in.
- **v2 "his dwelling was with the Watchers and with the holy ones"** — the same word, **Watchers**, that names the ones who fell. Not all of them fell. Enoch lives among the wakeful ones, which is why the guilty can reach him and why he can carry a message in either direction.
- **v3 "Enoch the scribe"** — the title is the point. He is not a prophet here, and not a priest. He is the one who takes it down, and in v4 the fuller form is *scribe of righteousness*. In a book about knowledge that was written down and should not have been, the hero's job is writing.
- **v4 "who have left the high heaven and the eternal holy sanctuary"** — the Greek word is *sanctuary*, and it makes heaven a temple and the Watchers its priests. What they have done, in that picture, is walk off their station. **Jude 6** puts it the same way: *angels who did not keep their own domain but left their own dwelling.*
- **v4 "have done as the sons of men do"** — the charge is not that they did something monstrous. It is that they did something ordinary. They married and had children, and for them that was the transgression.
- **v5 "There will be no peace for you"** — the refrain, and here it is delivered to the Watchers' faces by a man. In 5:4 the same sentence was aimed at human sinners. The book uses one verdict for both.
- **vv5–6 the sentence Enoch carries** — no peace, no forgiveness, and then the specific grief: they will watch their own sons killed, and their pleading will not stop and will not work. Chapter 10 ordered the giants to destroy each other; this is what that order looks like from the fathers' side.
- **v3 "the Watchers called me"** — worth noticing who initiates. The condemned angels summon the human. In the next chapters they will ask him to write their petition and carry it up for them, and he does. The scribe becomes their advocate.
