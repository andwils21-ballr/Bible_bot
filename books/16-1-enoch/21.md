# 1 Enoch 21

**1** I went on as far as the place where nothing was made.

**2** There I saw a terrible thing: no heaven above, and no earth founded beneath — but an unformed and terrible place.

**3** There I saw seven of the stars of heaven, bound and cast into it together, like great mountains and burning with fire.

**4** Then I said, "For what sin are they bound, and for what reason have they been cast here?"

**5** Then Uriel answered me, one of the holy angels who was with me and led me, and he said, "Enoch, why do you ask, and why are you eager for the truth, and why do you search?"

**6** "These are among the stars of heaven that transgressed the command of the Lord, and they are bound here until ten thousand ages are completed — the number of the days of their sin."

**7** From there I went to another place, more terrible than this. I saw a terrible thing: a great fire there, burning and blazing, and the place had a cleft reaching down to the abyss, full of great pillars of fire falling. Neither its extent nor its size could I see, nor could I guess at it.

**8** Then I said, "How terrible this place is, and how painful to look at!"

**9** Then Uriel answered me, one of the holy angels who was with me, and said, "Enoch, why are you so afraid and trembling?" I answered, "Because of this terrible place, and because of the sight of this suffering."

**10** He said to me, "This place is the prison of the angels, and here they will be held forever."

## Notes

- **The chapter repeats chapter 18.** Verses 1–6 describe the same desolate place and the same seven bound stars as 18:12–16, and vv7–10 revisit the chasm of 18:11. The book tours the same two sites twice, with the same angel explaining them. Whether that is a deliberate second pass or two versions of one journey preserved side by side, the doubling is in every witness.
- **v1 "the place where nothing was made"** — the Greek word is **ἀκατασκεύαστος**, *akataskeuastos*, **unbuilt**, not constructed. And that is the Septuagint's word for the state of the earth in **Genesis 1:2**: *the earth was invisible and **unformed***, rendering Hebrew *tohu*. The prison at the end of the world is described with the vocabulary of the world before creation — a patch that the six days never reached.
- **v2 "no heaven above, and no earth founded beneath"** — the same verse says it plainly. Genesis 1 separates the waters and founds the earth; this place has had neither done to it.
- **v5 "why do you ask... why do you search?"** — the angel's reply to a perfectly reasonable question is to ask why the question was asked. He answers it anyway in the next verse. It is the only place in the journey where curiosity is treated as something to be remarked on.
- **v6 "ten thousand ages"** — the Ge'ez. The Greek has **ten thousand years**, matching what it said at 18:16. The Ge'ez at 18:16 said *the year of mystery*. Across two tellings of one sentence the two traditions give three different spans.
- **v10 "the prison of the angels"** — the second place is for the Watchers, the first for the stars. The book keeps them apart: beings that fell for women, and lights that were late. Both are held in the unmade part of the world.
