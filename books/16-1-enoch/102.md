# 1 Enoch 102

**1** In those days, if He brings a terrible fire on you, where will you flee, and where will you be saved? When He sends out His voice against you, will you not be shaken and afraid?

**2** All the lights will be shaken and troubled with great fear, and all the earth will be shaken with great fear, and will tremble and quake.

**3** All the angels will carry out their commands and will seek to hide from the presence of the Great Glory, and the children of the earth will tremble and be troubled. You sinners are cursed forever, and you will have no peace.

**4** Do not fear, you souls of the righteous, and be hopeful, you who have died in righteousness.

**5** Do not grieve that your soul has gone down into great distress, and groaning and sorrow, and into Sheol in grief, and that your body did not receive in your life what your goodness deserved. It was only that the days you lived in were days of sinners, days of curse and punishment.

**6** When you die, the sinners say over you, "As we die, the righteous die, and what gain did they have from their deeds?

**7** Look: like us they died, in grief and in darkness, and what more do they have than we? From now on we are equal.

**8** What will they receive, and what will they see forever? For look, they too have died, and from now on they will never see light."

**9** I tell you, you sinners: it is enough for you to eat and drink, to rob and sin and strip men naked, and to gain wealth and see good days.

**10** Have you seen the righteous, how their end is peace, because no violence was found in them until the day of their death?

**11** "Yet they perished and became as though they had never been, and their souls went down into Sheol in distress."

## Notes

- **v1 "When He sends out His voice against you"** — *qālo*, **His voice**, so the Greek; Charles has *His Word*. The psalm: *He uttered His voice, the earth melted* (Psalm 46:6).
- **v3 "the Great Glory"** — the title of 14:20, *The Great Glory sat on it*. Here even the angels seek to hide from it.
- **vv4–5 "Do not grieve that your soul has gone down… into Sheol"** — the Epistle answers the question the righteous ask at their death: why did they suffer in this life, and go down to Sheol like everyone else? The answer comes in 103:3–4.
- **v5 "the days you lived in were days of sinners"** — the Ge'ez reads *in the day in which you were sinners, and in the day of curse and punishment*. The Greek has *since the days in which you lived were days of sinners and of the cursed upon the earth*, and the English follows that sense: the righteous did not suffer for their own sins. Charles gives *But wait for the day of the judgement of sinners*.
- **vv6–8 "As we die, the righteous die"** — the sinners' argument is Ecclesiastes' complaint: *one fate comes to the righteous and to the wicked* (Ecclesiastes 9:2); *how the wise man dies, just like the fool!* (Ecclesiastes 2:16). The Greek has *The pious have died according to fate*.
- **v9 "it is enough for you to eat and drink"** — in the Greek the sinners say it themselves: *Let us plunder and sin and rob and acquire wealth and see good days!* Isaiah: *Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die* (Isaiah 22:13).
- **v10 "how their end is peace"** — the psalm: *Watch the blameless man, and look at the upright, for the end of that man is peace* (Psalm 37:37). The Greek turns the verse on the sinners: *See, then, you who justify yourselves, what kind of end was theirs*.
- **v11 "Yet they perished and became as though they had never been"** — the Ge'ez does not say who speaks. Charles sets the verse in quotation marks as the sinners' answer to v10, and the English follows him; the Greek says it of those who justify themselves. Enoch replies in chapter 103.
