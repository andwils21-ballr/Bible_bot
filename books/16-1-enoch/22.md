# 1 Enoch 22

**1** From there I went to another place, and he showed me in the west a great and high mountain of hard rock.

**2** There were four hollow places in it, deep and wide and very smooth — three of them dark and one bright, and a spring of water in the middle of it. I said, "How smooth these hollows are, and how deep and dark to look at."

**3** Then Raphael answered, one of the holy angels who was with me, and said to me, "These hollow places are for the spirits of the souls of the dead to be gathered into. For this they were made, that all the souls of the sons of men should be gathered here.

**4** These places were made to hold them until the day of their judgment, and until their appointed time — and the appointed time is long, until the great judgment comes upon them."

**5** I saw the spirits of the sons of men who were dead, and their voice went out as far as heaven and cried out.

**6** Then I asked Raphael, the angel who was with me, "Whose spirit is this, whose voice goes out like this and cries out as far as heaven?"

**7** He answered me, "This is the spirit that came out from Abel, whom Cain his brother killed. He brings his case against him until his seed is destroyed from the face of the earth, and his seed is wiped out from among the seed of men."

## Why Are They Separated?

**8** Then I asked about all the hollow places, "Why is one separated from another?"

**9** He answered me, "These three were made so that the spirits of the dead might be separated. In this way the spirits of the righteous are set apart — this is where the spring of bright water is.

**10** In this way it was made for sinners when they die and are buried in the earth, and judgment was not carried out on them in their lifetime.

**11** Here their spirits are set apart, in this great pain, until the great day of judgment — of scourges and torments of the accursed, forever. There is the repayment of their spirits, and there He will bind them forever.

**12** In this way it was set apart for the spirits of those who bring their case, who make it known that they were destroyed, when they were killed in the days of the sinners.

**13** In this way it was made for the spirits of men who will not be righteous but sinners, complete in transgression, and they will be partners with the lawless. But their spirits will not be killed on the day of judgment, nor will they be raised from here."

**14** Then I blessed the Lord of glory and said, "Blessed are You, Lord of glory and righteousness, who rule over all forever."

## Notes

- **This is the oldest description of the afterlife having rooms.** The Hebrew Bible has *Sheol*, and Sheol is one place where everyone goes. Here the dead are sorted on arrival, held in separate compartments, and the sorting happens **before** the judgment rather than after it. Everything later literature assumes about a waiting-place with divisions starts in this chapter.
- **v2 "four hollow places... three of them dark and one bright"** — and this is where the Greek earns its keep. The **Ge'ez** says only that there were four beautiful hollow places, deep and dark. The Greek adds the detail that makes the chapter work: **three dark, one bright, with a spring in it**. Without that line, v9's *these three were made* contradicts the four of v2. With it, the arithmetic is plain: three compartments for the dead who are being held, and one lit chamber with water for the righteous.
- **v5 "their voice went out as far as heaven and cried out"** — the dead are not silent and they are not asleep. What they do in the waiting place is make noise, and the noise reaches heaven.
- **v7 "the spirit that came out from Abel"** — **Genesis 4:10**: *the voice of your brother's **bloods** is crying out to Me from the ground.* Genesis has the blood crying once, at the moment of the murder. This book has Abel **still crying**, thousands of years later, in a room in a mountain, and says he will not stop until Cain's line is extinct. It takes one verse of Genesis and makes it a permanent condition.
- **v7 "he brings his case against him"** — the legal vocabulary again, as in chapters 7 and 9. The earth brought a case; Abel is bringing one. In this book the dead have standing.
- **v9 "the spring of bright water"** — the one furnished detail in any of the four rooms. Light and water for the righteous; the rest are described only by what happens in them.
- **v13 "their spirits will not be killed on the day of judgment, nor will they be raised from here"** — the hardest sentence in the chapter, and the two witnesses differ on the reason. The Greek supplies one the Ge'ez lacks: *because those who were afflicted here are punished less than they*. Either way the fate described is neither destruction nor resurrection. It is being left where they are, permanently, and the book offers it as a third outcome.
- **v14 "Then I blessed the Lord"** — he has just been shown the sorting of every human being who has ever died, including a murdered man still shouting. His response is a blessing. The book does not explain that either.
