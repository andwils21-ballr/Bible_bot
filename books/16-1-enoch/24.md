# 1 Enoch 24

**1** And from there I went to another place of the earth, and he showed me a mountain of fire that burned day and night.

**2** And I went toward it and saw seven magnificent mountains, each different from the others, with precious and beautiful stones, all of them splendid and glorious in appearance and beautiful in form — three toward the east, set one upon another, and three toward the south, one upon another, and deep and rugged ravines, none of them running into another.

**3** And the seventh mountain was in the middle of these, and it rose above them, like the seat of a throne, and fragrant trees surrounded it.

**4** And among them was a tree such as I had never smelled — no other among them or any other tree was like it. It had a fragrance beyond all spices, and its leaves and its blossom and its wood never wither, forever. And its fruit is beautiful, and its fruit is like the clusters of a date palm.

**5** And then I said, "Look at this beautiful tree — beautiful to look at, and lovely are its leaves, and its fruit is a delight to the eye."

**6** Then Michael answered me, one of the holy and honored angels who was with me, and who was set over them.

## Notes

- **v2 "three toward the east... three toward the south"** — that is the Greek. The Ge'ez has *three toward the **north***. A compass point, and the two witnesses disagree. The same seven mountains appeared back at 18:6, where both witnesses said **south** — so here the Ge'ez has drifted from its own earlier reading.
- **vv2–3 the seventh in the middle** — six mountains in two rows of three, and a seventh standing higher between them. That is the shape of a **menorah**: three branches, three branches, and a central shaft. The text does not say so; it just gives the arrangement and then compares the middle one to a throne.
- **v4 "a tree such as I had never smelled"** — the first thing said about it is its smell, and the second is that nothing is like it. The book is describing the tree of life and will not name it; that comes in the next chapter. Here it is only a tree nobody may touch, which is what it was in Genesis 3:22.
- **v4 "its leaves and its blossom and its wood never wither"** — every part of it is permanent. The one thing about it that changes is the fruit, and the fruit is the only part of it anyone will ever be given.
- **v4 "like the clusters of a date palm"** — the one ordinary comparison in the description. After *beyond all spices* and *never wither*, the fruit is said to look like dates. The book reaches for something a reader has actually eaten.
- **v6 "Michael... who was set over them"** — each place on this journey has its own angelic guide: Uriel for the stars and the prison (21:5), Raphael for the dead (22:3), Raguel for the fire (23:4), and now Michael, who ranks above the others, for the tree. The book assigns its most senior angel to the one thing it will not describe plainly.
