# 1 Enoch 1

**1** The word of blessing of Enoch, with which he blessed the chosen and the righteous, who will be there on the day of distress, when all the evil and the wicked are removed.

**2** And Enoch answered and said — a righteous man, whose eyes were opened by God, and he saw:

**3** Concerning the chosen I speak, and concerning them I took up my parable — the Great Holy One will come out from His dwelling,

**4** and the God of the age will tread upon Mount Sinai, and appear in the strength of His power from heaven.

**5** And they will fear. And the Watchers will believe, and they will sing hidden things in all the ends of the earth, and all the ends of the earth will shake.

**6** And the high mountains will be shaken and will fall and be broken apart, and the high hills will be brought low, and they will melt like honeycomb before the flame.

**7** And the earth will be split with a great chasm, and everything on the earth will be destroyed, and there will be judgment on all.

**8** And with the righteous He will make peace, and over the chosen there will be protection and peace, and He will help them.

**9** And here — He came with ten thousands of holy ones, to carry out judgment on them, and to destroy the wicked, and to bring a charge against all flesh for everything that sinners and the wicked have done and committed against Him.

## Notes

- **On this book, and a warning about one of its witnesses** — 1 Enoch does not survive whole in the language it was written in. The complete text exists only in **Ge'ez** (classical Ethiopic); behind it stand fragments of **Greek**, and behind those, fragments of the **Aramaic** original from Qumran. This rendering follows the fullest witness at each point and the notes say which one that is. **One caution applies throughout:** the Ge'ez transcription in this repository is incomplete. Two thirds of its verses break off mid-sentence — they are cut short in our copy, not in the Ethiopic tradition. So where a note below reports what the Ge'ez *says*, that is evidence. Nothing here argues from what the Ge'ez appears to leave out, because our copy is not in a position to tell us.
- **v1 "when all the evil and the wicked are removed"** — the Ge'ez says the evil and the wicked. The Greek says *all the **enemies*** — a political word where the Ge'ez has a moral one. The Greek also closes the verse with four words that are not in our Ge'ez line: *and the righteous will be saved*.
- **v1 "the chosen and the righteous"** — the Ge'ez joins them with *and*, making two groups. The Greek has no *and*: *chosen righteous*, one group described twice. Much of this book turns on who the chosen are, and the two witnesses differ in the opening sentence about whether that is one category or two.
- **v3–4 "the Great Holy One will come out from His dwelling"** — this half-line is in the **Aramaic** (*the Gr[eat] Holy One will go forth from*) and in the Greek. Our Ge'ez line breaks off just before it.
- **v4 "in the strength of His power"** — from the **Aramaic**: *he will appear in the [might] of [his] streng[th]*. The Ge'ez word at this point is different — *ba-te'yentu*, **in His camp** or **host**, an army rather than a display of force. Three independent readings of the Ge'ez were checked against this: two read it as *camp*, one as *power*, and the one that read *power* gave the Aramaic's word rather than the Ethiopic's. The Greek breaks off here and cannot settle it.
- **v5 "the Watchers"** — the Greek has *all will be afraid, and the **Watchers** will believe, and they will sing hidden things in all the ends of the earth*, and the Aramaic independently confirms the ending: *[al]l the end[s of the earth will tremble]*. The Watchers — *egrēgoroi*, the sleepless ones — are the subject of chapters 6 through 16 and the reason the book exists. This is their first appearance.
- **v6 "melt like honeycomb before the flame"** — the image is in the Ge'ez; the Greek breaks off before it. English Enochs traditionally print *like **wax***, which is the smoother phrase, but the Ge'ez word is honeycomb, and three independent readings of it agree.
- **v6 "shaken... fall... broken apart"** — the Greek gives the mountains three verbs where the Ge'ez gives one. The single Ge'ez verb is built on a root used for being startled or frightened, so it may carry more than physical shaking, but it is regularly used of things trembling and this verse alone does not settle it.
- **v7 "the earth will be split with a great chasm"** — the Greek has the earth **split**. The Ge'ez verb at this point is *wa-tesattam*, **and it will be submerged** — the world ends by water, not by cracking open. This is a genuine difference between the witnesses, and it survives the caution above, because it is a word the Ge'ez has rather than one it lacks. It is also the first thing a fluent paraphrase destroys: of three independent readings of this word, two glossed it *submerged* and then one of those two silently printed *rent asunder* in its English.
- **v8 "with the righteous He will make peace"** — the Greek says **with**; the Ge'ez says **upon**. And where the Ge'ez says *righteous* twice, the Greek distinguishes: peace with the righteous, protection and peace upon the **chosen**. If those are two groups, this verse promises them different things.
- **v9 "And here — He came"** — this is the verse the New Testament quotes. **Jude 14–15** attributes it to *Enoch, the seventh from Adam*, and quotes it closely: *Behold, the Lord came with his holy myriads, to execute judgment against all and to convict all the ungodly.* Jude's last two words are *sinners, ungodly* — the same pair in the same order that ends this verse in the Ge'ez. Jude uses a past tense, as does the surviving Latin scrap (*Ecce venit*), while the Greek witness here has the present, *he comes*. The Ge'ez verb is the perfect form, grammatically past, but Semitic prophecy regularly states a future event in the perfect as something already done, so the form does not settle the tense.
