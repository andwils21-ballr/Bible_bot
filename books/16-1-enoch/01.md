# 1 Enoch 1

**1** The word of blessing of Enoch, with which he blessed the chosen and the righteous, who will be there on the day of distress, when all the evil and the wicked are removed.

**2** And Enoch answered and said — a righteous man, whose eyes were opened by God, and he saw:

**3** Concerning the chosen I speak, and concerning them I took up my parable — the Great Holy One will come out from His dwelling,

**4** and the God of the age will tread upon Mount Sinai, and appear in the strength of His power from heaven.

**5** And they will fear. And the Watchers will believe, and they will sing hidden things in all the ends of the earth, and all the ends of the earth will shake.

**6** And the high mountains will be shaken and will fall and be broken apart, and the high hills will be brought low, and they will melt like honeycomb before the flame.

**7** And the earth will be split with a great chasm, and everything on the earth will be destroyed, and there will be judgment on all.

**8** And with the righteous He will make peace, and over the chosen there will be protection and peace, and He will help them.

**9** And here — He came with ten thousands of holy ones, to carry out judgment on them, and to destroy the wicked, and to bring a charge against all flesh for everything that sinners and the wicked have done and committed against Him.

## Notes

- **On this book and how it is rendered** — 1 Enoch does not survive whole in the language it was written in. The complete text exists only in **Ge'ez** (classical Ethiopic), a translation of a translation. Behind it stand fragments of **Greek**, and behind those, fragments of the **Aramaic** original found at Qumran among the Dead Sea Scrolls. This rendering follows the fullest witness at each point and the notes say which one that is. Where a witness is missing a line the others have, that is reported rather than smoothed over.
- **v1 "when all the evil and the wicked are removed"** — the Ge'ez says the evil and the wicked. The Greek says *all the **enemies*** — a political word, not a moral one. The Greek also closes the verse with four words the Ge'ez does not have at all: *and the righteous will be saved*.
- **v1 "the chosen and the righteous"** — the Ge'ez joins them with *and*, making two groups. The Greek has no *and*: *chosen righteous*, one group described twice. The whole book turns on who the chosen are, and the two witnesses disagree in the opening sentence about whether that is one category or two.
- **v3–4 "the Great Holy One will come out from His dwelling"** — this half-line is not in the Ge'ez, which runs straight from *the Holy One who goes forth* into *and the God of the age*. It is in the **Aramaic**: *the Gr[eat] Holy One will go forth from* — and in the Greek. The Qumran fragment is the oldest witness on the page and it has a line the complete Ethiopic text lost.
- **v4 "in the strength of His power"** — again from the **Aramaic**: *he will appear in the [might] of [his] streng[th]*. The Ge'ez instead says He appears *in His camp* — an army rather than a display of force. The Greek is broken off at exactly this point and cannot settle it.
- **v5 "the Watchers"** — the entire Ge'ez verse is two words: **and they will fear**. The Greek has *all will be afraid, and the **Watchers** will believe, and they will sing hidden things in all the ends of the earth.* The Aramaic independently confirms the ending: *[al]l the end[s of the earth will tremble]*. The Watchers — *egrēgoroi* in Greek, the sleepless ones — are the subject of chapters 6 through 16 and the reason the book exists. The complete Ethiopic text drops them from their first appearance.
- **v6 "melt like honeycomb before the flame"** — the image is in the Ge'ez only; the Greek breaks off before it. The Greek instead gives the mountains three verbs where the Ge'ez gives one, and they are physical — *shaken*, *fall*, *dissolved* — while the Ge'ez verb is closer to terror than to geology.
- **v7 "the earth will be split with a great chasm"** — the Ge'ez has two words here: **and it will sink**. The Greek has the whole verse: the earth splits, everything on it perishes, judgment comes on all. Two witnesses, two different ends of the world — one drowns, one cracks open.
- **v8 "with the righteous He will make peace"** — the Greek says **with**; the Ge'ez says **upon**. And where the Ge'ez says *righteous* twice, the Greek distinguishes: peace with the righteous, protection and peace upon the **chosen**. If those are two groups, this verse promises them different things.
- **v9 "And here — He came"** — this is the verse the New Testament quotes. **Jude 14–15** attributes it to *Enoch, the seventh from Adam*, and quotes it closely: *Behold, the Lord **came** with his holy myriads, to execute judgment against all and to convict all the ungodly.* Jude's last two words are *sinners, ungodly* — the same pair in the same order that ends this verse in the Ge'ez. Jude also uses the past tense, *came*, matching the Ge'ez and the surviving Latin scrap (*Ecce venit*), while the Greek witness here has the present, *he comes*. A New Testament letter quotes this book as prophecy, and the tense it quotes is the one the Ethiopic preserved.
