# 1 Enoch 1

**1** The word of blessing of Enoch, with which he blessed the chosen and the righteous who will be there on the day of distress, when all the evil and the wicked are removed.

**2** Enoch answered and said — a righteous man, whose eyes were opened by God, who saw a vision of the Holy One in heaven, which the angels showed me. I heard everything from them, and I understood what I saw; and not for this generation, but for generations far off, that are still to come.

**3** Concerning the chosen I spoke, and I took up my parable concerning them: the Holy One will come out, and the Great One, from His dwelling,

**4** the eternal God. From there He will tread upon Mount Sinai, and appear with His host, and appear in the strength of His power, from heaven.

**5** They will all fear, and the Watchers will shake, and fear and great trembling will seize them, to the ends of the earth.

**6** The high mountains will be shaken, and the high hills will be brought low, and they will melt like honeycomb before the flame.

**7** The earth will be submerged, and everything on the earth will perish, and there will be judgment on all —

**8** and on all the righteous. But for the righteous He will make peace, and He will guard the chosen, and mercy will be on them. They will all belong to God, and it will go well with them, and they will be blessed, and the light of God will shine on them.

**9** And here — He came with ten thousands of holy ones, to carry out judgment on them, and to destroy the wicked, and to bring a charge against all flesh for everything that sinners and the wicked have done and committed against Him.

## Notes

- **On this book and how it is rendered** — 1 Enoch does not survive whole in the language it was written in. The complete text exists only in **Ge'ez** (classical Ethiopic); behind it stand fragments of **Greek**, and behind those, fragments of the **Aramaic** original found at Qumran. This rendering follows the fullest witness at each point and the notes say which one that is. Where the witnesses disagree about a word, the disagreement is the interesting part and is reported rather than smoothed away.
- **v1 "when all the evil and the wicked are removed"** — the Ge'ez has the evil and the wicked, a moral pair. The Greek has *all the **enemies***, a political word. The Greek then adds four words the Ge'ez does not: *and the righteous will be saved*.
- **v1 "the chosen and the righteous"** — the Ge'ez joins them with *and*, making two groups. The Greek has no *and*: *chosen righteous*, one group described twice. Much of this book turns on who the chosen are, and the witnesses differ in the opening sentence about whether that is one category or two.
- **v2 "not for this generation, but for generations far off"** — Enoch says at the outset that the book is not addressed to his own contemporaries. Whatever else it is, it is written as a document meant to be found later.
- **v4 "appear with His host, and appear in the strength of His power"** — the Ge'ez says both, in that order, using the same verb twice. The **Aramaic** from Qumran preserves the second half — *he will appear in the [might] of [his] streng[th]* — and the Greek breaks off before either. An army and a display of force are two different pictures, and the Ethiopic keeps both.
- **v5 "the Watchers"** — *təguhān* in the Ge'ez, *egrēgoroi* in the Greek: the wakeful or sleepless ones. This is their first appearance, and they are the subject of chapters 6 through 16 and the reason the book exists. The Greek adds a line here the Ge'ez does not have — *and they will sing hidden things in all the ends of the earth* — where the Ge'ez has fear and trembling instead of singing.
- **v6 "melt like honeycomb before the flame"** — the Ge'ez words are *maʿāra gərā*, **honey of the comb**. Printed English Enochs say *like **wax***, which is smoother and is what beeswax renders down to, but it is not the word. Three independent readings of the Ge'ez were checked on this and all three gave honeycomb.
- **v7 "the earth will be submerged"** — the Ge'ez verb is *wa-tesattam*, to be sunk or flooded. The **Greek** has something else entirely: *the earth will be split with a deep chasm*. Two witnesses, two ends of the world — one drowns, one cracks open. This is the sharpest single-word disagreement in the chapter, and it is also the first casualty of a fluent translation: of three independent readings of this word, two gave *submerged* and then one of those two printed *rent asunder* in its English anyway.
- **v8 "they will all belong to God, and it will go well with them"** — the Ge'ez carries the verse well past where the Greek stops, through prospering, blessing, and the light of God shining on them. The Greek instead distinguishes two groups the Ge'ez does not: peace **with** the righteous, protection and peace **upon** the chosen.
- **v9 "And here — He came"** — the verse the New Testament quotes. **Jude 14–15** attributes it to *Enoch, the seventh from Adam*, and quotes it closely: *Behold, the Lord came with his holy myriads, to execute judgment against all and to convict all the ungodly.* Jude's last two words are *sinners, ungodly* — the same pair in the same order that ends this verse in the Ge'ez. Jude uses a past tense, as does the surviving Latin scrap (*Ecce venit*), while the Greek witness here has the present, *he comes*. The Ge'ez verb is the perfect form, grammatically past, but Semitic prophecy regularly states a future event in the perfect as something already done, so the form does not settle the tense.
