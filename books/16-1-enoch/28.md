# 1 Enoch 28

**1** From there I went toward the east, into the middle of the mountain range of the desert, and I saw a wilderness, alone and empty, yet full of trees.

**2** They were from this seed, and water gushed out over it from above, and a torrent appeared, running abundantly toward the north-west, and from every side water and dew rose up.

---

## Notes

- **The verses** — the EOTC Ge'ez divides this chapter into two verses. Knibb's Ge'ez, the Greek and Charles's English divide the same words into three, and other English editions number it 28:1–3.
- **v1 "the mountain range of the desert"** — the Ge'ez. The **Greek** gives a place name instead: **Mandobara**, which is not known from anywhere else and which the Ethiopic does not have. It may be a name the Greek preserved and the Ge'ez turned into a description, or a description the Greek misread as a name.
- **v1 "a wilderness, alone and empty, yet full of trees"** — the verse sets the two against each other on purpose. The word for the place is desert; what is actually in it is water and growth. The journey east is beginning to arrive somewhere, and chapters 29 to 32 will keep describing fragrant trees until they reach the garden of righteousness.
- **v2 "from this seed"** — *this* seed, as though the reader has already been shown it. Nothing in the preceding chapters identifies it. Both witnesses have the phrase and neither explains it.
