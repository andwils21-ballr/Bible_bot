# 1 Enoch 18

**1** And I saw the storehouses of all the winds, and I saw that with them He ordered the whole creation and the foundations of the earth. And I saw the cornerstone of the earth.

**2** And I saw the four winds that bear the earth and the firmament of heaven.

**3** And I saw how the winds stretch out the height of heaven, and they stand between heaven and earth. They are the pillars of heaven.

**4** And I saw the winds that turn the heaven, that bring the disk of the sun and all the stars to their setting.

**5** And I saw the winds upon the earth bearing the clouds, and I saw the paths of the angels. I saw at the edge of the earth the firmament of heaven above.

**6** And I passed on toward the south, and it burns day and night, where the seven mountains are of precious stone — three facing east and three facing south.

**7** And those toward the east were of colored stone, and one was of pearl, and one of healing stone. And those toward the south were of red stone.

**8** And the middle one reached up to heaven, like the throne of God, of alabaster stone, and the summit of the throne was of sapphire.

**9** And I saw a burning fire. And beyond these mountains

**10** is a place at the end of the great earth. There the heavens come to an end.

**11** And I saw a great chasm among the pillars of heavenly fire, and I saw pillars of fire descending among them, without measure, neither toward the height nor toward the depth.

**12** And beyond that chasm I saw a place where there was no firmament of heaven above it, and no foundation of earth beneath it, and no water on it, and no birds — but it was a desolate and terrible place.

**13** I saw there seven stars, like great burning mountains. And when I asked about them,

**14** the angel said, "This is the place of the end of heaven and earth. It has become a prison for the stars of heaven and for the host of heaven.

**15** And the stars that roll over the fire — these are the ones that transgressed the command of the Lord at the beginning of their rising, because they did not come out at their appointed times."

**16** And He was angry with them, and bound them until the time when their sin is completed.

## Notes

- **v1 "the storehouses of all the winds"** — the same word used in 11:1 for the *storehouses of blessing*. The book's picture of heaven is a warehouse with rooms: blessing in one, wind in another, and later snow, hail and dew in their own. Nothing in the weather is loose.
- **v1 "the cornerstone of the earth"** — the building term. Job 38:6 asks who laid *the cornerstone* of the earth and gets no answer. Enoch says he saw it.
- **vv2–3 "the four winds that bear the earth... they are the pillars of heaven"** — the winds are not weather here; they are structural. They hold the earth up, hold the sky apart from it, and turn the sky at v4. This is a working model, and the moving part is air.
- **v8 "like the throne of God, of alabaster... the summit of sapphire"** — the middle mountain of seven, reaching to heaven, described as a throne. **Exodus 24:10** has the elders see under God's feet *something like a pavement of sapphire*, and Ezekiel 1:26 puts a throne of sapphire above the firmament. The stone is consistent across three books.
- **v10 "there the heavens come to an end"** — that is the Greek. The Ge'ez says something else entirely: *there the waters gather together*. One witness ends the sky at this point; the other pools the sea here.
- **vv12–16 the prison for stars** — **and this is the book's strangest judgment.** Seven stars are locked up, and the charge in v15 is that *they did not come out at their appointed times*. Not rebellion, not pride. **Lateness.** They broke schedule.
- **v15 against chapter 2** — the opening of the book told the reader to *observe the lights in heaven, how they do not change their paths* (2:1), and built its whole case against humanity on the contrast. Chapter 18 shows what happens to the lights that do change. The argument turns out to have teeth on both sides.
- **v13 who asks the question** — in the Greek, Enoch asks about the seven stars. In the Ge'ez it is the reverse: *a spirit questioned me*. A small clause, and the two traditions put the interrogation in opposite directions.
- **v16 "until the time when their sin is completed"** — the Ge'ez adds *in the year of mystery*; the Greek says **ten thousand years**. One tradition gives the sentence a number and the other makes its length the point of not knowing.
