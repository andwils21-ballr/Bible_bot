# 1 Enoch 2

**1** Observe all the work in heaven, how the lights in heaven do not change their paths, how each one rises and sets, each appointed in its season, and they do not overstep their command.

**2** Look at the earth, and think carefully about the work done on it, from the beginning to the end — how no work of God changes as it appears.

**3** Look at the summer and the winter, how the whole earth is filled with water, and cloud and dew and rain settle on it.

---

## Notes

- **v1 "Observe"** — the first word of a sequence running to the end of chapter 5. Four times the reader is told to look at something in nature — the stars, the earth, the trees, the seasons — and each time the point is that they keep to their orders. The argument being built is that people do not.
- **v1 "they do not overstep their command"** — where the Ge'ez says the lights do not transgress what they were told, the **Greek** says they *appear at their **festivals***. That is not decorative: the whole of 1 Enoch 72–82 is a solar calendar argued against the lunar one, and the feast days are set by the lights. The Greek makes the calendar argument here; the Ge'ez makes an argument about obedience.
- **v3 "the signs of summer and of winter"** — the Ge'ez and the Greek both have simply *summer and winter*. The **Aramaic** from Qumran has *the **signs** of* each. A sign is something meant to be read. The oldest witness makes the seasons a text; the later two make them weather.
- **v2 "as it comes into view"** — the **Aramaic** has a word neither of the others does: *observe **its** works* — the earth's own works, rather than God's works upon it.
