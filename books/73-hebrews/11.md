# Hebrews 11

## What Faith Is

**1** Now faith is the assurance of things hoped for, the proof of things not seen.

**2** For by it the people of old received God's approval.

**3** By faith we understand that the ages were put in order by the word of God, so that what is seen was not made out of things that appear.

## From Abel to Abraham

**4** By faith Abel offered to God a better sacrifice than Cain, and through it he was approved as righteous, God bearing witness over his gifts; and through it, though he died, he still speaks.

**5** By faith Enoch was taken up so that he did not see death, and he was not found, because God had taken him. For before he was taken up he was approved as having pleased God.

**6** Without faith it is impossible to please Him, for whoever draws near to God must believe that He exists and that He rewards those who seek Him.

**7** By faith Noah, when he was warned by God about things not yet seen, in reverent fear built an ark to save his household. Through it he condemned the world and became an heir of the righteousness that comes by faith.

**8** By faith Abraham obeyed when he was called to go out to a place he was to receive as an inheritance; and he went out, not knowing where he was going.

**9** By faith he lived as a guest in the land of promise, as in a foreign land, living in tents with Isaac and Jacob, heirs with him of the same promise.

**10** For he was looking forward to the city that has foundations, whose designer and builder is God.

**11** By faith, with Sarah herself, he received power to father a child, even past the age for it, since he considered Him faithful who had promised.

**12** So from one man, and him as good as dead, were born descendants as many as the stars of heaven and as countless as the sand by the shore of the sea.

## Seeking a Homeland

**13** These all died in faith, not having received the promises, but having seen them and greeted them from afar, and having confessed that they were strangers and exiles on the earth.

**14** For people who speak this way make it plain that they are seeking a homeland.

**15** If they had been thinking of the country they had left, they would have had opportunity to return.

**16** But as it is, they desire a better country, that is, a heavenly one. That is why God is not ashamed to be called their God, for He has prepared a city for them.

## From Abraham to Moses

**17** By faith Abraham, when he was tested, offered up Isaac; he who had received the promises was offering up his only son,

**18** of whom it was said, "Through Isaac your seed shall be called."

**19** He reckoned that God was able even to raise him from the dead; and from there, in a figure, he did receive him back.

**20** By faith Isaac blessed Jacob and Esau, even concerning things to come.

**21** By faith Jacob, when he was dying, blessed each of the sons of Joseph, and bowed in worship over the top of his staff.

**22** By faith Joseph, at the end of his life, made mention of the exodus of the sons of Israel and gave instructions about his bones.

**23** By faith Moses, when he was born, was hidden for three months by his parents, because they saw that the child was beautiful, and they were not afraid of the king's edict.

**24** By faith Moses, when he had grown up, refused to be called the son of Pharaoh's daughter,

**25** choosing rather to be mistreated with the people of God than to enjoy the passing pleasures of sin,

**26** considering the reproach of the Christ greater wealth than the treasures of Egypt, for he was looking to the reward.

**27** By faith he left Egypt, not fearing the anger of the king, for he endured as seeing Him who is invisible.

**28** By faith he kept the Passover and the sprinkling of the blood, so that the destroyer of the firstborn would not touch them.

## The Rest of the Witnesses

**29** By faith they crossed the Red Sea as on dry land, but the Egyptians, when they attempted it, were swallowed up.

**30** By faith the walls of Jericho fell, after they had been encircled for seven days.

**31** By faith Rahab the prostitute did not perish with those who were disobedient, because she had welcomed the spies in peace.

**32** What more shall I say? Time would fail me to tell of Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, of David and Samuel and the prophets,

**33** who through faith conquered kingdoms, carried out justice, obtained promises, shut the mouths of lions,

**34** quenched the power of fire, escaped the edge of the sword, were made strong out of weakness, became mighty in war, put foreign armies to flight.

**35** Women received back their dead by resurrection. Others were tortured, refusing to accept release, so that they might obtain a better resurrection.

**36** Others suffered mocking and flogging, and even chains and prison.

**37** They were stoned, they were sawn in two, they were killed with the sword. They went about in sheepskins and goatskins, destitute, afflicted, mistreated,

**38** people of whom the world was not worthy, wandering in deserts and mountains, and in caves and holes in the ground.

**39** All these, though approved through their faith, did not receive what was promised,

**40** since God had foreseen something better for us, so that they would not be made perfect apart from us.

## Notes

- **v1 "the assurance of things hoped for"** — *hypostasis*, literally **what stands under**. This book has used it twice already: of God's own *being*, of which the Son is the exact imprint (1:3), and of the *confidence* we hold firm to the end (3:14). So faith may be the **assurance** of things hoped for, held in the believer, or their **substance**, the reality they already have before they are seen.
- **v5 "he was approved as having pleased God"** — Genesis 5:24 in the Greek, *Enoch pleased God, and he was not found, because God took him*. The Hebrew reads *Enoch walked with God. He was not, for God took him*. Hebrews quotes the Greek words and builds v6 on them: *without faith it is impossible to please Him*.
- **v11 "with Sarah herself, he received power to father a child"** — *autē Sarra*, as the edition prints it, **with Sarah herself**; the same words without the mark of the dative are **Sarah herself**. *Dynamin eis katabolēn spermatos*, **power for laying down seed**, is said of a father, and the sentence goes on: *from one man, and him as good as dead* (v12). So Abraham receives power, with Sarah. Read with *Sarah* as the subject, the verse is the faith of the barren wife (Genesis 11:30) who laughed at the promise (Genesis 18:12).
- **v21 "bowed in worship over the top of his staff"** — Genesis 47:31 in the Greek, *at the top of his staff*. The Hebrew reads *at the head of the bed*. The two words are written with the same letters, *mittah*, **bed**, and *matteh*, **staff**; the Greek read the second.
