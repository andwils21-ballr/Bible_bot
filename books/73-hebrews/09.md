# Hebrews 9

## The Earthly Tent

**1** Now even the first covenant had regulations for worship and a sanctuary of this world.

**2** For a tent was prepared, the first one, in which were the lampstand and the table and the bread of the Presence; it is called the Holy Place.

**3** Behind the second curtain was a tent called the Holy of Holies,

**4** which had a golden altar of incense and the ark of the covenant, covered on all sides with gold. In the ark were a golden jar holding the manna, and Aaron's staff that budded, and the tablets of the covenant.

**5** Above it were the cherubim of glory, overshadowing the atonement cover. Of these things we cannot now speak in detail.

**6** With these things prepared in this way, the priests go continually into the first tent to carry out their services,

**7** but into the second only the high priest goes, once a year, and not without blood, which he offers for himself and for the sins the people have committed unknowingly.

**8** By this the Holy Spirit makes plain that the way into the holy places has not yet been revealed while the first tent is still standing.

**9** This is a figure for the present time. Under it, gifts and sacrifices are offered that cannot make the worshiper perfect in conscience,

**10** since they deal only with food and drink and various washings, regulations for the flesh imposed until the time of setting things right.

## The Blood of Christ

**11** But when Christ came as high priest of the good things that have come, through the greater and more perfect tent not made with hands, that is, not of this creation,

**12** He entered once for all into the holy places, not by the blood of goats and calves, but by His own blood, having obtained eternal redemption.

**13** For if the blood of goats and bulls, and the ashes of a heifer sprinkled on those who have been defiled, make them holy for the cleansing of the flesh,

**14** how much more will the blood of Christ, who through the eternal Spirit offered Himself without defect to God, cleanse our conscience from dead works to serve the living God!

**15** That is why He is the mediator of a new covenant: since a death has taken place to redeem them from the transgressions committed under the first covenant, those who are called may receive the promised eternal inheritance.

**16** For where there is a will, the death of the one who made it must be established.

**17** A will takes effect only at death; it never has force while the one who made it is alive.

**18** So not even the first covenant was put into effect without blood.

**19** When every commandment of the Law had been spoken by Moses to all the people, he took the blood of the calves, with water and scarlet wool and hyssop, and sprinkled both the book itself and all the people,

**20** saying, "This is the blood of the covenant that God commanded for you."

**21** In the same way he sprinkled with the blood both the tent and all the vessels used in worship.

**22** Under the Law almost everything is cleansed with blood, and without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness.

## Once for All

**23** So it was necessary for the copies of the things in the heavens to be cleansed with these, but the heavenly things themselves with better sacrifices than these.

**24** For Christ did not enter holy places made with hands, copies of the true ones, but heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God on our behalf.

**25** Nor was it to offer Himself again and again, as the high priest enters the holy places year by year with blood not his own,

**26** for then He would have had to suffer again and again since the foundation of the world. But as it is, He has appeared once, at the end of the ages, to put away sin by the sacrifice of Himself.

**27** Just as it is appointed for people to die once, and after that comes judgment,

**28** so Christ, having been offered once to bear the sins of many, will appear a second time, not to deal with sin, but for the salvation of those who are eagerly waiting for Him.

## Notes

- **v4 "a golden altar of incense"** — *chrysoun thymiatērion*. In the Greek Old Testament the word is used only for a **censer**: Uzziah's in the temple (2 Chronicles 26:19), the elders' in Ezekiel's vision (Ezekiel 8:11). The golden altar of incense of Exodus stood before the curtain, *thysiastērion thymiamatos* there (Exodus 30:1, 6). So the verse can mean the altar, the one golden object of incense the tent had, set with the inner room it served, or a golden censer kept inside it.
- **vv16–17 "where there is a will"** — *diathēkē*, the word that is **covenant** in v15 and v18. In everyday Greek it was a person's **will**, which takes effect only when its maker dies. The writer moves from one sense to the other without a new word: the new covenant is also an inheritance (v15), and it came into force by a death. Read as *covenant* throughout, vv16–17 speak of the death of the victim by which a covenant was ratified, the blood of v18.
- **v20 "This is the blood of the covenant"** — Exodus 24:8, *Here is the blood of the covenant that the LORD has cut with you*. Hebrews has *touto*, **this is**, the words of Jesus at the supper: *This is My blood of the covenant, which is poured out for many* (Mark 14:24).
- **v28 "offered once to bear the sins of many"** — Isaiah 53:12, *yet he carried the sin of many*, in the Greek *hamartias pollōn anēnegken*, the verb and words Hebrews uses.
