# Jeremiah 46

**1** The word of the LORD that came to Jeremiah the prophet concerning the nations.

**2** Concerning Egypt: about the army of Pharaoh Neco king of Egypt, which was by the river Euphrates at Carchemish, and which Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon struck down in the fourth year of Jehoiakim son of Josiah, king of Judah.

**3** Prepare buckler and shield,  
and advance to battle!

**4** Harness the horses;  
mount, you horsemen!  
Take your stand with your helmets on;  
polish the spears,  
put on your coats of mail!

**5** What do I see?  
They are dismayed and falling back;  
their warriors are beaten down;  
they flee headlong  
and do not look back —  
terror on every side!  
declares the LORD.

**6** The swift cannot flee,  
nor the warrior escape;  
in the north, by the river Euphrates,  
they have stumbled and fallen.

**7** Who is this that rises like the Nile,  
like rivers whose waters surge?

**8** Egypt rises like the Nile,  
like rivers whose waters surge.  
He said, "I will rise and cover the earth;  
I will destroy the city and those who live in it."

**9** Go up, you horses!  
Drive madly, you chariots!  
Let the warriors go out:  
Cush and Put, who handle the shield,  
and the Ludim, who handle and bend the bow.

**10** That day belongs to the Lord GOD of hosts,  
a day of vengeance, to avenge Himself on His foes.  
The sword will devour and be sated  
and drink its fill of their blood;  
for the Lord GOD of hosts has a sacrifice  
in the land of the north, by the river Euphrates.

**11** Go up to Gilead and take balm,  
virgin daughter of Egypt!  
In vain you have heaped up remedies;  
there is no new flesh for you.

**12** The nations have heard of your shame,  
and your cry fills the earth;  
for warrior has stumbled against warrior;  
they have fallen, both of them together.

## Nebuchadnezzar Comes Against Egypt

**13** The word that the LORD spoke to Jeremiah the prophet about the coming of Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon to strike the land of Egypt:

**14** Declare it in Egypt, proclaim it in Migdol,  
proclaim it in Memphis and in Tahpanhes;  
say, "Take your stand and be ready,  
for the sword has devoured those around you."

**15** Why are your mighty ones swept away?  
They did not stand,  
for the LORD thrust them down.

**16** He made many stumble;  
they fell, each against his neighbor,  
and they said, "Get up, let us go back to our own people  
and to the land of our birth,  
away from the oppressing sword."

**17** There they cried,  
"Pharaoh king of Egypt is a noise;  
he has let the appointed time pass by."

**18** As I live, declares the King,  
whose name is the LORD of hosts,  
like Tabor among the mountains,  
and like Carmel by the sea, he will come.

**19** Pack your baggage for exile,  
you who live there, daughter of Egypt!  
For Memphis will become a desolation,  
laid waste and without inhabitant.

**20** Egypt is a lovely heifer;  
a gadfly from the north has come, has come.

**21** Even her hired soldiers in her midst  
are like fattened calves;  
for they too have turned and fled together;  
they did not stand,  
for the day of their calamity has come upon them,  
the time of their punishment.

**22** She makes a sound like a snake slipping away,  
for they march in force  
and come against her with axes,  
like those who cut wood.

**23** They will cut down her forest, declares the LORD,  
though it cannot be searched out;  
for they are more than the locusts,  
and there is no counting them.

**24** The daughter of Egypt is put to shame;  
she is given into the hand of a people from the north.

**25** The LORD of hosts, the God of Israel, says: I am about to punish Amon of No, and Pharaoh, and Egypt with her gods and her kings, Pharaoh and those who trust in him.

**26** I will give them into the hand of those who seek their life, into the hand of Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon and the hand of his servants. Afterward it will be inhabited as in the days of old, declares the LORD.

## Jacob My Servant

**27** But you, do not fear, Jacob My servant,  
and do not be dismayed, Israel;  
for I am about to save you from far away,  
and your seed from the land of their captivity.  
Jacob will return and have quiet and ease,  
with no one to make him afraid.

**28** Do not fear, Jacob My servant, declares the LORD,  
for I am with you.  
I will make a full end of all the nations  
to which I have driven you,  
but of you I will not make a full end.  
I will correct you with justice,  
and I will not leave you unpunished.

## Notes

- **vv1–2 "concerning the nations… Carchemish… in the fourth year of Jehoiakim"** — the oracles against the nations run from here to the end of chapter 51. The Greek sets them after 25:13 and in another order, with Elam first and Egypt second. Carchemish was the battle of the year of the first scroll (25:1; 36:1); after it, *the king of Egypt did not come out of his land again, for the king of Babylon had taken all that belonged to the king of Egypt, from the Brook of Egypt to the river Euphrates* (2 Kings 24:7).
- **v5 "terror on every side!"** — *magor missaviv*, Jeremiah's cry over Judah (6:25) and the name he gave Pashhur, *Terror on Every Side* (20:3), now heard in Pharaoh's army.
- **vv7–8 "Who is this that rises like the Nile… I will rise and cover the earth"** — Egypt boasts in the image of its own river in flood. At 47:2 the flood comes the other way: *waters are rising from the north*.
- **v9 "Cush and Put… and the Ludim"** — Egypt's own kin in the table of nations: *The sons of Ham: Cush and Egypt and Put and Canaan*, and *Egypt fathered the Ludim* (Genesis 10:6, 13). The Greek has *Ethiopians and Libyans… and Lydians*.
- **v10 "a day of vengeance… the Lord GOD of hosts has a sacrifice"** — the words of Isaiah's oracle against Edom, *For the LORD has a sacrifice in Bozrah* (Isaiah 34:6). The battle is His altar.
- **v11 "Go up to Gilead and take balm… there is no new flesh for you"** — the questions once asked over Judah, *Is there no balm in Gilead?* (8:22), and the sentence once given to Zion, *no new flesh for you* (30:13), turned on Egypt.
- **v15 "Why are your mighty ones swept away? They did not stand, for the LORD thrust them down"** — *abbirekha*, "your mighty ones," a plural; the verbs that follow are singular, *he did not stand… thrust him down*. *Abbir* is also a bull, as in *strong bulls of Bashan* (Psalm 22:12). The Greek has *Why has Apis fled from you? Your chosen calf did not stand*, reading the letters of *nischaf*, "swept away," as two words, *nas*, "has fled," and a name. The chapter's other animals agree with a bull: Egypt is a heifer (v20) and her soldiers fattened calves (v21).
- **v17 "Pharaoh king of Egypt is a noise; he has let the appointed time pass by"** — *sha'on he'evir ha-mo'ed*, three words that sound like a name: Noise, Who Let the Time Go By. The Greek reads *shem*, "name," for *sham*, "there," and leaves the three words in Hebrew: *Call the name of Pharaoh Neco king of Egypt Saon-esbei-emoed*.
- **v25 "Amon of No"** — Amon, the god of the Egyptian city No, which Nahum calls by both names together, *No-amon* (Nahum 3:8).
- **vv27–28 "But you, do not fear, Jacob My servant"** — the words of 30:10–11, with small changes: here *to which I have driven you*, there *among which I have scattered you*. The Greek has these two verses here and not at 30:10–11. The Greek has nothing of v26.
