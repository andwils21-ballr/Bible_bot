# Jeremiah 38

**1** Shephatiah son of Mattan, Gedaliah son of Pashhur, Jucal son of Shelemiah and Pashhur son of Malchiah heard the words that Jeremiah was speaking to all the people:

**2** This is what the LORD says: Whoever stays in this city will die by the sword, by famine and by pestilence; but whoever goes out to the Chaldeans will live, and his life will be his as plunder, and he will live.

**3** This is what the LORD says: This city will surely be given into the hand of the army of the king of Babylon, and he will take it.

**4** Then the princes said to the king, "Let this man be put to death, for he is weakening the hands of the men of war who are left in this city, and the hands of all the people, by speaking such words to them. This man is not seeking peace for this people, but harm."

**5** King Zedekiah said, "He is in your hands, for the king can do nothing against you."

**6** So they took Jeremiah and threw him into the cistern of Malchiah the king's son, which was in the court of the guard, letting Jeremiah down with ropes. There was no water in the cistern, only mud, and Jeremiah sank in the mud.

**7** Ebed-melech the Cushite, a eunuch who was in the king's house, heard that they had put Jeremiah into the cistern, while the king was sitting in the Benjamin Gate.

**8** Ebed-melech went out from the king's house and spoke to the king, saying,

**9** "My lord the king, these men have done evil in all that they have done to Jeremiah the prophet, whom they have thrown into the cistern; he will die where he is because of the famine, for there is no more bread in the city."

**10** Then the king commanded Ebed-melech the Cushite, "Take thirty men from here with you, and bring Jeremiah the prophet up out of the cistern before he dies."

**11** So Ebed-melech took the men with him and went into the king's house, to a place under the treasury, and took from there worn-out rags and worn-out clothes, and let them down to Jeremiah in the cistern with ropes.

**12** Ebed-melech the Cushite said to Jeremiah, "Put these worn-out rags and clothes under your armpits, under the ropes." Jeremiah did so.

**13** Then they pulled Jeremiah up with the ropes and brought him up out of the cistern; and Jeremiah stayed in the court of the guard.

## At the Third Entrance

**14** King Zedekiah sent and had Jeremiah the prophet brought to him at the third entrance of the house of the LORD. The king said to Jeremiah, "I am going to ask you something; hide nothing from me."

**15** Jeremiah said to Zedekiah, "If I tell you, will you not surely put me to death? If I give you advice, you will not listen to me."

**16** King Zedekiah swore secretly to Jeremiah, "As the LORD lives, who made this life for us, I will not put you to death or give you into the hand of these men who are seeking your life."

**17** Then Jeremiah said to Zedekiah, "This is what the LORD, the God of hosts, the God of Israel, says: If you will indeed go out to the princes of the king of Babylon, then your life will be spared, and this city will not be burned with fire, and you will live, you and your house.

**18** But if you do not go out to the princes of the king of Babylon, this city will be given into the hand of the Chaldeans, and they will burn it with fire, and you will not escape from their hand."

**19** King Zedekiah said to Jeremiah, "I am anxious about the Jews who have gone over to the Chaldeans, in case they give me into their hand and they abuse me."

**20** Jeremiah said, "They will not give you over. Obey, please, the voice of the LORD in what I am telling you, and it will go well with you, and your life will be spared.

**21** But if you refuse to go out, this is the word that the LORD has shown me:

**22** All the women who are left in the house of the king of Judah are being brought out to the princes of the king of Babylon, and they are saying:  
'Your friends have misled you  
and prevailed over you;  
your feet are sunk in the mire;  
they have turned back.'

**23** All your wives and your sons they will bring out to the Chaldeans, and you will not escape from their hand, but will be captured by the hand of the king of Babylon; and you will burn this city with fire."

**24** Then Zedekiah said to Jeremiah, "Let no one know of these words, and you will not die.

**25** If the princes hear that I have spoken with you, and they come to you and say to you, 'Tell us, please, what you said to the king; hide nothing from us, and we will not put you to death; and what did the king say to you?'

**26** then you shall say to them, 'I was bringing my plea before the king, that he would not send me back to Jonathan's house to die there.'"

**27** All the princes came to Jeremiah and asked him, and he told them all these words, as the king had commanded. So they said no more to him, for the conversation had not been overheard.

**28** So Jeremiah stayed in the court of the guard until the day Jerusalem was taken. And it came to pass, when Jerusalem was taken —

## Notes

- **v1 "Jucal son of Shelemiah and Pashhur son of Malchiah"** — Jucal is written *Yehukhal* at 37:3, where Zedekiah sent him to ask for prayer; Pashhur son of Malchiah was sent to inquire of the LORD before (21:1). Both now want the prophet dead (v4). The Greek has three names, without Pashhur son of Malchiah.
- **v4 "he is weakening the hands of the men of war… This man is not seeking peace for this people, but harm"** — what his letter to the exiles had said, turned around: *Seek the peace of the city* (29:7), and the LORD's *plans for peace and not for harm* (29:11).
- **v6 "There was no water in the cistern, only mud"** — *bor*, as when Joseph's brothers *threw him into the pit. The pit was empty; there was no water in it* (Genesis 37:24).
- **vv7–13 "Ebed-melech the Cushite, a eunuch who was in the king's house"** — *Ebed-melech* means "servant of the king." The Greek calls him *the Ethiopian*, its word for the Cushite of 13:23. The king was sitting in the Benjamin Gate, where Jeremiah had been seized (37:13). The princes put the prophet into the mud, and a Cushite of the king's house brings him out; his reward is told at 39:15–18.
- **v9 "he will die where he is"** — *va-yamat*, "and he died": the Hebrew tells it as good as done. In the Greek, Ebed-melech says to the king, *You have done evil in what you have done, to kill this man*.
- **vv17, 20 "your life will be spared… you will live, you and your house"** — *chayah*, "live," the word of the choice set before the people, *the way of life and the way of death* (21:8), now set before the king.
- **v19 "they abuse me"** — *hit'allelu bi*, Saul's fear on his last day: *these uncircumcised will come and run me through and abuse me* (1 Samuel 31:4). Zedekiah fears his own people who have gone over more than the Chaldeans.
- **vv22–23 "Your friends have misled you and prevailed over you; your feet are sunk in the mire… and you will burn this city with fire"** — *anshei shelomekha*, "the men of your peace," and *yakhol*, "prevail," are the words of the prophet's complaint: *All my friends watch for my fall… and we can prevail against him* (20:10). *Tava*, "sink," is the verb of v6: the king who let Jeremiah sink in the mud of the cistern will be left with his own feet in the mire. The Hebrew lays the burning of the city on the king: *tisrof*, "you will burn." The Greek has *this city will be burned*.
- **vv25–27 "I was bringing my plea before the king… for the conversation had not been overheard"** — the plea he had in fact made (37:20). The Greek ends v27 with *for the word of the Lord had not been heard*.
- **v28 "And it came to pass, when Jerusalem was taken —"** — the sentence is finished at 39:3, after the dates of 39:1–2: *all the princes of the king of Babylon came and sat in the Middle Gate*. *Ve-hayah* tells what happened, as at 37:11. The Greek has only *until the time when Jerusalem was taken*.
