# Jeremiah 41

**1** And it came to pass in the seventh month that Ishmael son of Nethaniah, son of Elishama, of the royal seed and one of the king's chief officers, came with ten men to Gedaliah son of Ahikam at Mizpah; and they ate bread together there at Mizpah.

**2** Then Ishmael son of Nethaniah and the ten men who were with him rose up and struck down with the sword Gedaliah son of Ahikam, son of Shaphan, whom the king of Babylon had appointed over the land, and killed him.

**3** Ishmael also struck down all the Jews who were with Gedaliah at Mizpah, and the Chaldeans who were found there, the men of war.

**4** And it came to pass on the day after Gedaliah was killed, before anyone knew of it,

**5** that eighty men came from Shechem, from Shiloh and from Samaria, with their beards shaved, their clothes torn and their bodies gashed, carrying grain offerings and frankincense to bring to the house of the LORD.

**6** Ishmael son of Nethaniah went out from Mizpah to meet them, weeping as he went. When he met them, he said to them, "Come to Gedaliah son of Ahikam."

**7** When they had entered the city, Ishmael son of Nethaniah and the men who were with him slaughtered them and threw them into the cistern.

**8** But ten men were found among them who said to Ishmael, "Do not kill us, for we have stores hidden in the field: wheat, barley, oil and honey." So he held back and did not kill them with their brothers.

**9** The cistern into which Ishmael threw all the corpses of the men he had struck down because of Gedaliah was the one King Asa had made for fear of Baasha king of Israel; Ishmael son of Nethaniah filled it with the slain.

**10** Then Ishmael took captive all the rest of the people who were at Mizpah, the king's daughters and all the people who remained at Mizpah, whom Nebuzaradan the chief of the slaughterers had committed to Gedaliah son of Ahikam. Ishmael son of Nethaniah took them captive and set out to cross over to the Ammonites.

## Johanan Brings Back the Captives

**11** When Johanan son of Kareah and all the commanders of the forces who were with him heard of all the evil that Ishmael son of Nethaniah had done,

**12** they took all their men and went to fight against Ishmael son of Nethaniah, and they found him by the great waters that are in Gibeon.

**13** When all the people who were with Ishmael saw Johanan son of Kareah and all the commanders of the forces who were with him, they were glad.

**14** All the people whom Ishmael had carried away captive from Mizpah turned around and came back, and went over to Johanan son of Kareah.

**15** But Ishmael son of Nethaniah escaped from Johanan with eight men and went to the Ammonites.

**16** Then Johanan son of Kareah and all the commanders of the forces who were with him took all the rest of the people whom he had won back from Ishmael son of Nethaniah, taken from Mizpah after Ishmael had struck down Gedaliah son of Ahikam: the fighting men, the women, the children and the eunuchs whom he had brought back from Gibeon.

**17** They went and stayed at Geruth Chimham, which is near Bethlehem, on their way into Egypt,

**18** because of the Chaldeans; for they were afraid of them, because Ishmael son of Nethaniah had struck down Gedaliah son of Ahikam, whom the king of Babylon had appointed over the land.

## Notes

- **vv1–2 "of the royal seed… they ate bread together there… and killed him"** — Kings tells it in one verse: *Ishmael son of Nethaniah, son of Elishama, of the royal seed, came with ten men, and they struck down Gedaliah, and he died* (2 Kings 25:25). Here they first eat together, the betrayal of the psalm: *Even the man of my peace, in whom I trusted, who ate my bread, has lifted up his heel against me* (Psalm 41:9). The warning Gedaliah called a lie (40:14–16) came true. The Greek has no *one of the king's chief officers*.
- **v5 "eighty men… from Shiloh… their bodies gashed, carrying grain offerings and frankincense to bring to the house of the LORD"** — mourners from the old northern kingdom, bringing offerings to a house that has been burned. Gashing for the dead is forbidden in the law, *You shall not gash yourselves* (Deuteronomy 14:1), and in this book it is a mourning custom (16:6). Shiloh is *My place that was in Shiloh* (7:12), the ruined sanctuary; the Greek has *Salem*. The offerings are those of 17:26, *grain offerings and frankincense… to the house of the LORD*.
- **v6 "weeping as he went"** — Ishmael weeps with the mourners to lead them in. The Greek gives the weeping to the pilgrims: *they went along weeping*.
- **vv7–9 "threw them into the cistern… the one King Asa had made for fear of Baasha"** — Asa built Mizpah with the stones Baasha had been building with at Ramah (1 Kings 15:22); Kings does not mention the cistern. *Bor*, "cistern," is the word of the cistern Jeremiah was let down into (38:6).
- **v9 "the men he had struck down because of Gedaliah"** — *be-yad Gedalyahu*, "by the hand of Gedaliah," which cannot mean that Gedaliah struck them, since he was dead the day before (v4). Read as *because of*, it joins the pilgrims' deaths to his: they were killed while his murder was still hidden. The Greek has *it is a great cistern* in place of these words.
- **v12 "the great waters that are in Gibeon"** — the pool of Gibeon, where David's servants and Abner's met and fought (2 Samuel 2:13–16).
- **v17 "Geruth Chimham, which is near Bethlehem"** — *Geruth* is "the lodging place," of the root *gur*, "live as a guest," the word for what they mean to do in Egypt (42:15, 17). *Chimham* (the read form) is the name of Barzillai's son, whom David took with him to Jerusalem (2 Samuel 19:37–40).
