# Jeremiah 24

**1** The LORD showed me two baskets of figs set in front of the temple of the LORD. This was after Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had carried into exile from Jerusalem Jeconiah son of Jehoiakim, king of Judah, with the princes of Judah, the craftsmen and the smiths, and had brought them to Babylon.

**2** One basket had very good figs, like first-ripe figs; the other basket had very bad figs, so bad that they could not be eaten.

**3** The LORD said to me, "What do you see, Jeremiah?" I said, "Figs: the good figs very good, and the bad figs very bad, so bad that they cannot be eaten."

**4** Then the word of the LORD came to me, saying,

**5** "This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: Like these good figs, so I will regard as good the exiles of Judah, whom I have sent away from this place to the land of the Chaldeans.

**6** I will set My eyes on them for good, and I will bring them back to this land. I will build them up and not overthrow them; I will plant them and not uproot them.

**7** I will give them a heart to know Me, that I am the LORD; they will be My people, and I will be their God, for they will return to Me with their whole heart.

**8** But like the bad figs, so bad that they cannot be eaten — for this is what the LORD says — so I will give up Zedekiah king of Judah, his princes, the remnant of Jerusalem who remain in this land, and those who live in the land of Egypt.

**9** I will make them a horror and a disaster to all the kingdoms of the earth, a reproach, a proverb, a byword and a curse in all the places where I will drive them.

**10** I will send the sword, famine and pestilence among them, until they are consumed from the land that I gave to them and to their fathers."

## Notes

- **v1 "Jeconiah son of Jehoiakim… the craftsmen and the smiths"** — the Coniah of 22:24–30, now in Babylon. Kings tells the same exile: *all the craftsmen and the smiths. None remained except the poorest people of the land* (2 Kings 24:14). Zedekiah, his uncle, now reigns in Jerusalem (2 Kings 24:17), and the vision is about the two halves of the people: those taken and those left.
- **v2 "like first-ripe figs"** — *bikkurot*, the first figs of the year, the best. Hosea: *Like the first-ripe fruit on the fig tree I saw your fathers* (Hosea 9:10); Isaiah: *like a first-ripe fig before the summer: whoever sees it swallows it as soon as it is in his hand* (Isaiah 28:4).
- **v3 "What do you see, Jeremiah?"** — the question of the almond branch and the boiling pot (1:11, 13). The book's first visions told of judgment coming; this one sorts the people after it has begun.
- **vv5–6 "I will set My eyes on them for good… I will build them up and not overthrow them; I will plant them and not uproot them"** — the reverse of 21:10, *I have set My face against this city for harm and not for good*, and of Amos 9:4, *I will set My eyes on them for harm and not for good*. The four verbs are Jeremiah's call, *to uproot and to pull down, to destroy and to overthrow, to build and to plant* (1:10): the building and planting fall to the exiles.
- **v7 "I will give them a heart to know Me"** — Moses said at the end of the wilderness years, *Yet the LORD has not given you a heart to know, or eyes to see, or ears to hear, to this day* (Deuteronomy 29:4). What that generation was not given, the exiles will be. *They will be My people, and I will be their God* is the covenant's own sentence (11:4).
- **vv8–10 "the bad figs… a horror… a reproach, a proverb, a byword and a curse"** — the curses of Deuteronomy: *you will be a horror to all the kingdoms of the earth* (28:25); *a horror, a proverb and a byword among all the peoples where the LORD drives you* (28:37). Those left in Jerusalem said of the exiles, *Keep far from the LORD; to us this land is given as a possession* (Ezekiel 11:15). The vision says the opposite: the ones taken away are the good figs.
