# Jeremiah 52

**1** Zedekiah was twenty-one years old when he began to reign, and he reigned eleven years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Hamutal daughter of Jeremiah of Libnah.

**2** He did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD, according to all that Jehoiakim had done.

**3** For because of the anger of the LORD it came to this in Jerusalem and Judah, until He cast them out from His presence. Zedekiah rebelled against the king of Babylon.

**4** And it came to pass in the ninth year of his reign, in the tenth month, on the tenth day of the month, that Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came, he and all his army, against Jerusalem. They encamped against it and built a siege wall all around it.

**5** So the city was under siege until the eleventh year of King Zedekiah.

**6** In the fourth month, on the ninth day of the month, the famine was severe in the city, and there was no bread for the people of the land.

**7** Then the city was breached, and all the men of war fled and went out of the city by night by the way of the gate between the two walls, by the king's garden, while the Chaldeans were all around the city. They went by the way of the Arabah.

**8** But the army of the Chaldeans pursued the king and overtook Zedekiah in the plains of Jericho, and all his army was scattered from him.

**9** They seized the king and brought him up to the king of Babylon at Riblah in the land of Hamath, and he passed sentence on him.

**10** The king of Babylon slaughtered the sons of Zedekiah before his eyes; he also slaughtered all the princes of Judah at Riblah.

**11** He put out the eyes of Zedekiah and bound him in bronze fetters, and the king of Babylon brought him to Babylon and put him in the house of custody until the day of his death.

## The Burning of the House of the LORD

**12** In the fifth month, on the tenth day of the month — that was the nineteenth year of King Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon — Nebuzaradan the chief of the slaughterers, who stood before the king of Babylon, came to Jerusalem.

**13** He burned the house of the LORD, the king's house and all the houses of Jerusalem; every great house he burned with fire.

**14** All the army of the Chaldeans who were with the chief of the slaughterers broke down all the walls around Jerusalem.

**15** Some of the poorest of the people, the rest of the people who were left in the city, the deserters who had gone over to the king of Babylon, and the rest of the craftsmen, Nebuzaradan the chief of the slaughterers carried into exile.

**16** But Nebuzaradan the chief of the slaughterers left some of the poorest of the land to be vinedressers and plowmen.

**17** The pillars of bronze that belonged to the house of the LORD, the stands and the bronze Sea that were in the house of the LORD, the Chaldeans broke in pieces and carried all the bronze to Babylon.

**18** They took the pots, the shovels, the snuffers, the basins, the dishes and all the vessels of bronze used in the service.

**19** The cups, the firepans, the basins, the pots, the lampstands, the dishes and the bowls — what was of gold, in gold, and what was of silver, in silver — the chief of the slaughterers took.

**20** The two pillars, the one Sea and the twelve bronze oxen that were under the stands, which King Solomon had made for the house of the LORD — the bronze of all these vessels was beyond weighing.

**21** As for the pillars, the height of the one pillar was eighteen cubits, and a line of twelve cubits measured its circumference; it was four fingers thick, and hollow.

**22** On it was a capital of bronze. The height of the one capital was five cubits, with a network and pomegranates all around the capital, all of bronze; and the second pillar had the same, with pomegranates.

**23** There were ninety-six pomegranates on the sides; all the pomegranates on the network all around were a hundred.

**24** The chief of the slaughterers took Seraiah the chief priest and Zephaniah the second priest and the three keepers of the threshold.

**25** From the city he took one eunuch who was set over the men of war, and seven men of those who saw the king's face who were found in the city, the secretary of the commander of the army, who mustered the people of the land, and sixty men of the people of the land who were found in the city.

**26** Nebuzaradan the chief of the slaughterers took them and brought them to the king of Babylon at Riblah.

**27** The king of Babylon struck them down and put them to death at Riblah in the land of Hamath. So Judah was carried into exile from its land.

## The Exiles Counted

**28** This is the people whom Nebuchadnezzar carried into exile: in the seventh year, three thousand and twenty-three Jews;

**29** in the eighteenth year of Nebuchadnezzar, from Jerusalem, eight hundred and thirty-two persons;

**30** in the twenty-third year of Nebuchadnezzar, Nebuzaradan the chief of the slaughterers carried into exile seven hundred and forty-five Jews. All the persons were four thousand six hundred.

## Jehoiachin Released

**31** And it came to pass in the thirty-seventh year of the exile of Jehoiachin king of Judah, in the twelfth month, on the twenty-fifth day of the month, that Evil-merodach king of Babylon, in the year that he began to reign, lifted the head of Jehoiachin king of Judah and brought him out of prison.

**32** He spoke kindly to him and set his throne above the thrones of the kings who were with him in Babylon.

**33** So Jehoiachin put off his prison garments, and he ate bread before the king continually all the days of his life.

**34** For his allowance, a continual allowance was given him by the king of Babylon, a portion each day, until the day of his death, all the days of his life.

## Notes

- **vv1–34 "Zedekiah was twenty-one years old…"** — after *Here end the words of Jeremiah* (51:64), the book closes with the history as Kings tells it (2 Kings 24:18–25:30), and where the Hebrew is the same, so is the English. It leaves out Gedaliah's governorship, which chapters 40–41 have told at length, and adds the count of the exiles (vv28–30). The Greek has nothing of vv2–3, 15 or 28–30.
- **v12 "on the tenth day of the month… who stood before the king of Babylon"** — Kings has *the seventh day* and *a servant of the king of Babylon* (2 Kings 25:8). *To stand before* is the language of service (35:19).
- **v15 "Some of the poorest of the people… the rest of the craftsmen"** — Kings has neither the first group nor *craftsmen*: its line ends *the rest of the multitude*, *he-hamon* (2 Kings 25:11), where Jeremiah has *ha-amon*, the word for Wisdom as *a master workman* (Proverbs 8:30). The next verse leaves *some of the poorest of the land* behind.
- **v20 "the twelve bronze oxen that were under the stands"** — Kings leaves the oxen out (2 Kings 25:16). Solomon's Sea *stood on twelve oxen* (1 Kings 7:25), and the Greek has *under the Sea*; but the stands too were decorated with oxen (1 Kings 7:29), and long before, Ahaz *took down the Sea from the bronze oxen that were under it* (2 Kings 16:17).
- **vv21–22 "a line of twelve cubits… five cubits"** — the measures of 1 Kings 7:15–16. Kings at the fall gives the capital *three cubits* (2 Kings 25:17); this chapter agrees with the account of the building.
- **v25 "seven men of those who saw the king's face"** — Kings has *five men* (2 Kings 25:19).
- **vv28–30 "in the seventh year, three thousand and twenty-three Jews… four thousand six hundred"** — a count found only here. Kings dates the first exile to Nebuchadnezzar's *eighth year* and gives *ten thousand exiles* (2 Kings 24:12, 14); the numbers and the years here are different.
- **vv31–34 "lifted the head of Jehoiachin… a portion each day, until the day of his death"** — Kings has *the twenty-seventh day*; here it is the twenty-fifth. The book that began with *to uproot and to pull down… to build and to plant* (1:10) ends with Judah's king alive in exile, eating at the table of Babylon.
