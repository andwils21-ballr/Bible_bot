# Deuteronomy 15

## The Release of Debts

**1** At the end of every seven years you shall make a release.

**2** This is the manner of the release: every creditor shall release what he has lent to his neighbor. He shall not press his neighbor, his brother, for payment, because the LORD's release has been proclaimed.

**3** From the foreigner you may press for payment, but whatever of yours is with your brother, your hand shall release.

**4** Yet there will be no needy among you — for the LORD will surely bless you in the land that the LORD your God is giving you as an inheritance to possess —

**5** if only you listen, listen to the voice of the LORD your God, to be careful to do all this commandment that I command you today.

**6** For the LORD your God will bless you, as He promised you. You will lend to many nations, but you will not borrow; you will rule over many nations, but they will not rule over you.

## Open Your Hand

**7** If there is among you a needy man, one of your brothers, in any of your towns in your land that the LORD your God is giving you, you shall not harden your heart or shut your hand against your needy brother.

**8** Rather, you shall open, open your hand to him, and lend, lend him enough for his need, whatever he lacks.

**9** Guard yourself so that there is not a worthless thought in your heart, saying, 'The seventh year, the year of release, is near,' and your eye is evil toward your needy brother and you give him nothing. He will cry to the LORD against you, and it will be sin in you.

**10** You shall give, give to him, and your heart shall not be evil when you give to him, because for this thing the LORD your God will bless you in all your work and in all that you put your hand to.

**11** For the needy will never cease from the land. That is why I command you, saying, 'You shall open, open your hand to your brother, to your poor and to your needy in your land.'

## Freeing a Hebrew Slave

**12** If your brother, a Hebrew man or a Hebrew woman, is sold to you, he shall serve you six years, and in the seventh year you shall send him away from you free.

**13** When you send him away from you free, you shall not send him away empty-handed.

**14** You shall load, load him from your flock, from your threshing floor and from your winepress. As the LORD your God has blessed you, you shall give to him.

**15** Remember that you were a slave in the land of Egypt and the LORD your God redeemed you. That is why I command you this thing today.

**16** And it shall come to pass, if he says to you, 'I will not go out from you,' because he loves you and your household, since it is good for him with you,

**17** then you shall take an awl and put it through his ear into the door, and he shall be your slave forever. To your female slave also you shall do the same.

**18** It shall not seem hard in your eyes when you send him away from you free, for he has served you six years, worth twice the wages of a hired man. The LORD your God will bless you in all that you do.

## The Firstborn of the Herd and Flock

**19** Every firstborn male that is born in your herd and in your flock you shall consecrate to the LORD your God. You shall not work with the firstborn of your ox, and you shall not shear the firstborn of your flock.

**20** Before the LORD your God you shall eat it, year by year, in the place that the LORD will choose, you and your household.

**21** But if it has a blemish, if it is lame or blind, any bad blemish, you shall not sacrifice it to the LORD your God.

**22** Within your towns you may eat it, the unclean and the clean alike, as the gazelle and the deer.

**23** Only its blood you shall not eat; pour it out on the ground like water.

## Notes

- **vv1–2 "a release"** — *shemittah*, from *shamat*, **to let drop**: the verb of Exodus 23:11, where the land in the seventh year is left alone: *The seventh you shall let go*. Here it is debts that are dropped. *The LORD's release* makes God the one who proclaims it.
- **vv4 and 11 "no needy among you… the needy will never cease from the land"** — not a contradiction but two sides of one law: poverty need not exist if the command is kept (vv4–5), and since it will not be kept perfectly, the hand must stay open (v11). Jesus echoes v11 in Matthew 26:11: *you always have the poor with you*.
- **v7 "harden your heart… shut your hand"** — *te'ammets*, **make strong**, and *tiqpots*, **close tight**, like a fist. The body shows what the heart has decided.
- **vv8, 10, 11, 14 "open, open… lend, lend… give, give… load, load"** — the verbs are doubled each time, the Hebrew way of pressing hard.
- **v9 "a worthless thought"** — *davar… veliyya'al*, the word of *worthless men* in 13:14. The creditor who refuses a loan because the release is near is doing the same kind of thing as the man who leads a town astray.
- **v12 "a Hebrew man or a Hebrew woman"** — Exodus 21:2–11 treats the female slave by a separate law; Deuteronomy frees her on the same terms as the man (v17).
- **v13 "not… empty-handed"** — *reqam*, the word of Exodus 3:21, where Israel is promised it will not leave Egypt empty-handed. The freed slave leaves as Israel left.
- **v17 "put it through his ear into the door"** — Exodus 21:6 brings him *to the door or to the doorpost* to pierce the ear. Deuteronomy says nothing of bringing him *to God*.
- **v18 "worth twice the wages of a hired man"** — *mishneh sekhar sakhir*: the master has had six years of work at half what a hired worker would have cost him.
- **v21 "a blemish"** — *mum*, as in Leviticus 22:20.
