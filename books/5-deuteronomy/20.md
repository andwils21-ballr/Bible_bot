# Deuteronomy 20

## Do Not Be Afraid

**1** When you go out to battle against your enemies and see horses and chariots, a people greater than you, you shall not be afraid of them, for the LORD your God is with you, who brought you up from the land of Egypt.

**2** And it shall come to pass, when you draw near to the battle, the priest shall come forward and speak to the people.

**3** He shall say to them, 'Hear, Israel: you are drawing near today to battle against your enemies. Do not let your heart be faint. Do not be afraid, do not panic, and do not tremble before them,

**4** for the LORD your God is the one who goes with you, to fight for you against your enemies, to save you.'

## Who Goes Home

**5** The officials shall speak to the people, saying, 'Who is the man who has built a new house and has not dedicated it? Let him go and return to his house, or he may die in the battle and another man dedicate it.

**6** Who is the man who has planted a vineyard and has not yet used its fruit? Let him go and return to his house, or he may die in the battle and another man use its fruit.

**7** Who is the man who is engaged to a woman and has not taken her? Let him go and return to his house, or he may die in the battle and another man take her.'

**8** The officials shall speak further to the people and say, 'Who is the man who is afraid and faint of heart? Let him go and return to his house, so that he does not make his brothers' hearts melt like his own.'

**9** And it shall come to pass, when the officials have finished speaking to the people, they shall appoint commanders of the armies at the head of the people.

## Cities Far and Near

**10** When you draw near to a city to fight against it, you shall call out to it for peace.

**11** And it shall come to pass, if it answers you with peace and opens to you, all the people found in it shall be forced labor for you and shall serve you.

**12** But if it does not make peace with you and makes war against you, you shall besiege it.

**13** When the LORD your God gives it into your hand, you shall strike all its males with the sword.

**14** Only the women, the little ones, the animals and all that is in the city, all its spoil, you shall take as plunder for yourself, and you shall eat the spoil of your enemies that the LORD your God has given you.

**15** So you shall do to all the cities that are very far from you, which are not of the cities of these nations here.

**16** But of the cities of these peoples that the LORD your God is giving you as an inheritance, you shall not let anything that breathes live.

**17** You shall devote them to destruction — the Hittite and the Amorite, the Canaanite and the Perizzite, the Hivite and the Jebusite — as the LORD your God has commanded you,

**18** so that they do not teach you to do according to all their detestable things that they have done for their gods, and so sin against the LORD your God.

## The Trees of the Field

**19** When you besiege a city for many days, fighting against it to take it, you shall not destroy its trees by swinging an axe against them. You may eat from them, but you shall not cut them down. Is the tree of the field a man, that it should go into the siege before you?

**20** Only the trees that you know are not trees for food you may destroy and cut down, and build siege works against the city that is making war with you, until it falls.

## Notes

- **v1 "horses and chariots"** — what Egypt had at the Sea of Reeds (Exodus 14). *Who brought you up from the land of Egypt* is the reason not to fear them.
- **v2 "the priest shall come forward"** — the first voice before a battle is not a commander's but a priest's, and his message is *Hear, Israel*, the opening of 6:4.
- **vv5–7 house, vineyard, bride** — three beginnings not yet enjoyed. A man is sent home rather than die and leave the first use to another. The newly married man appears again in 24:5.
- **v6 "has not yet used its fruit"** — *lo chillelo*, **has not made it common**. Leviticus 19:23–25 kept the fruit of a new tree from ordinary eating until the fifth year; the verb here is the release of the fruit for ordinary eating.
- **v8 "afraid and faint of heart"** — fear spreads: *so that he does not make his brothers' hearts melt*, the word the spies used in 1:28.
- **vv10–18 two kinds of cities** — a city far away is first offered peace. The cities of the seven nations are *devoted to destruction* (7:2), and the reason given is worship: *so that they do not teach you*.
- **v19 "Is the tree of the field a man"** — *ki ha-adam ets ha-sadeh*. The fruit tree cannot run from the army and is not the enemy. The same words can be read as a statement, *for the tree of the field is man's life*, and older translations divided on it.
