# Deuteronomy 26

## The Basket of Firstfruits

**1** And it shall come to pass, when you come into the land that the LORD your God is giving you as an inheritance, and possess it and live in it,

**2** you shall take some of the first of all the fruit of the ground that you bring in from your land that the LORD your God is giving you, and put it in a basket, and go to the place that the LORD your God will choose to make His name dwell there.

**3** You shall come to the priest who is in office in those days and say to him, 'I declare today to the LORD your God that I have come into the land that the LORD swore to our fathers to give us.'

**4** The priest shall take the basket from your hand and set it down before the altar of the LORD your God.

**5** Then you shall answer and say before the LORD your God: 'A wandering Aramean was my father. He went down to Egypt and lived there as a guest, few in number, and there he became a nation, great, mighty and numerous.

**6** The Egyptians treated us badly and afflicted us and laid hard service on us.

**7** We cried to the LORD, the God of our fathers, and the LORD heard our voice and saw our affliction, our toil and our crushing.

**8** The LORD brought us out of Egypt with a strong hand and an outstretched arm, with great terror, and with signs and wonders.

**9** He brought us to this place and gave us this land, a land flowing with milk and honey.

**10** And now I have brought the first of the fruit of the ground that You, LORD, have given me.' You shall set it down before the LORD your God and bow down before the LORD your God.

**11** You shall rejoice in all the good that the LORD your God has given you and your household, you and the Levite and the guest who is among you.

## The Third-Year Tithe

**12** When you have finished tithing all the tithe of your yield in the third year, the year of the tithe, and have given it to the Levite, the guest, the orphan and the widow, so that they may eat within your towns and be satisfied,

**13** you shall say before the LORD your God: 'I have removed the holy portion from my house, and I have also given it to the Levite and to the guest, to the orphan and to the widow, according to all Your commandment that You commanded me. I have not transgressed any of Your commandments, and I have not forgotten.

**14** I have not eaten of it in my mourning, I have not removed any of it while unclean, and I have not given any of it to the dead. I have listened to the voice of the LORD my God; I have done according to all that You commanded me.

**15** Look down from Your holy dwelling, from heaven, and bless Your people Israel and the ground that You have given us, as You swore to our fathers, a land flowing with milk and honey.'

## You Have Declared, and He Has Declared

**16** This day the LORD your God commands you to do these statutes and rules. You shall be careful to do them with all your heart and with all your soul.

**17** You have declared today that the LORD is your God, and that you will walk in His ways and keep His statutes and His commandments and His rules, and listen to His voice.

**18** The LORD has declared today that you are a people for His own treasure, as He promised you, and that you are to keep all His commandments,

**19** and that He will set you high above all the nations that He has made, for praise and for a name and for glory, and that you shall be a holy people to the LORD your God, as He has spoken.

## Notes

- **v5 "A wandering Aramean was my father"** — *arammi oved avi*, three words. *Oved* is **perishing, lost, straying**, the verb of *anything your brother loses* in 22:3. The father is Jacob, who served Laban *the Aramean* (Genesis 31:20) and went down to Egypt seventy souls strong (10:22). The worshipper says *my father*, and then *us*: the story is told in the first person.
- **vv5–9 the confession** — Israel's whole history before the land in five verses, from a lost man to a land of milk and honey, spoken over a basket of fruit. Nothing about Sinai is in it; the confession is about the gift of the land.
- **vv6–7 "afflicted us… our affliction… our crushing"** — *va-ye'annunu*, the verb of Exodus 1:11, *to afflict them with their burdens*; *'onyenu*, as in Exodus 3:7, *I have seen the affliction of My people*; *lachatsenu*, the *crushing* of Exodus 3:9.
- **v10 "the first of the fruit of the ground that You, LORD, have given me"** — the speaker turns from telling God's deeds to addressing God directly.
- **vv13–14 the declaration of the tithe** — *I have removed*, *bi'arti*, the verb of *purge the evil*: the holy portion is cleared out of the house as completely as evil is from Israel. *Given any of it to the dead* refers to offerings to the dead, or food for mourners (compare *eaten of it in my mourning*).
- **v15 "Look down from Your holy dwelling"** — *hashqifah*, the verb of Genesis 18:16, where the men *looked down over the face of Sodom* before its judgment. Here the look that is asked for is blessing.
- **vv17–18 "You have declared… The LORD has declared"** — *he'emarta* and *he'emirkha*, a form of *amar*, **say**, found only in these two verses. Each side has the other say what it will be: Israel says the LORD is its God, and the LORD says Israel is His people. It is the covenant in its simplest form.
- **v19 "a holy people"** — the promise of Exodus 19:6, *a kingdom of priests and a holy nation*.
