# Deuteronomy 24

## A Certificate of Divorce

**1** When a man takes a wife and marries her, and it comes to pass that she finds no favor in his eyes because he has found something indecent in her, and he writes her a certificate of divorce, puts it in her hand and sends her out of his house,

**2** and she leaves his house and goes and becomes another man's wife,

**3** and the second man hates her and writes her a certificate of divorce, puts it in her hand and sends her out of his house, or the second man who took her as his wife dies,

**4** then her first husband, who sent her away, may not take her again to be his wife after she has been made unclean, for that is detestable before the LORD. You shall not bring sin on the land that the LORD your God is giving you as an inheritance.

**5** When a man takes a new wife, he shall not go out with the army, and no duty shall be laid on him. He shall be free at home one year and make the wife he has taken happy.

## Pledges and Persons

**6** No one shall take a mill or an upper millstone in pledge, for he would be taking a life in pledge.

**7** If a man is found stealing a person from among his brothers, the sons of Israel, and treats him as a slave or sells him, that thief shall die. So you shall purge the evil from among you.

**8** Take care in a mark of blight to be very careful to do according to all that the Levitical priests teach you. As I commanded them, so you shall be careful to do.

**9** Remember what the LORD your God did to Miriam on the way when you came out of Egypt.

**10** When you make your neighbor a loan of any kind, you shall not go into his house to take his pledge.

**11** You shall stand outside, and the man to whom you are lending shall bring the pledge out to you.

**12** If he is a poor man, you shall not sleep in his pledge.

**13** You shall surely return the pledge to him when the sun goes down, so that he may sleep in his cloak and bless you. It will be righteousness for you before the LORD your God.

## Wages, Justice, Gleanings

**14** You shall not exploit a hired man who is poor and needy, whether one of your brothers or one of your guests who are in your land inside your gates.

**15** You shall give him his wages on his day, before the sun goes down on it, for he is poor and sets his heart on it. Otherwise he will cry to the LORD against you, and it will be sin in you.

**16** Fathers shall not be put to death for sons, and sons shall not be put to death for fathers; each shall be put to death for his own sin.

**17** You shall not bend the judgment of the guest or the orphan, and you shall not take a widow's garment in pledge.

**18** Remember that you were a slave in Egypt and the LORD your God redeemed you from there. That is why I command you to do this thing.

**19** When you reap your harvest in your field and forget a sheaf in the field, you shall not go back to get it. It shall be for the guest, the orphan and the widow, so that the LORD your God may bless you in all the work of your hands.

**20** When you beat your olive trees, you shall not go over the branches after you. It shall be for the guest, the orphan and the widow.

**21** When you gather the grapes of your vineyard, you shall not glean after you. It shall be for the guest, the orphan and the widow.

**22** Remember that you were a slave in the land of Egypt. That is why I command you to do this thing.

## Notes

- **v1 "something indecent"** — *ervat davar*, the phrase of 23:15, *anything indecent* in the camp. What it covers here was argued over: the Mishnah (Gittin 9:10) records that the school of Shammai read it as unfaithfulness and the school of Hillel as almost anything. Matthew 19:7 has the Pharisees ask, *Why then did Moses command us to give a certificate of divorce?*
- **vv1–4 one law, not a command to divorce** — the whole passage is a single case, and its only ruling is v4: the first husband may not take her back. The certificate protects the woman: it proves she is free to marry again.
- **v5 "make the wife he has taken happy"** — *ve-simmach et ishto*. The year at home is for her joy, and 20:7 already sent the engaged man home from battle.
- **v6 "a mill or an upper millstone"** — the household's means of making bread each day. To take it in pledge is to take *a life*, *nefesh*.
- **v7 "stealing a person"** — *gonev nefesh*, the law of Exodus 21:16, *Whoever steals a man and sells him… shall be put to death*.
- **vv8–9 "a mark of blight… Miriam"** — *nega ha-tsara'at*, the priests' teaching of Leviticus 13–14. Miriam is the example: *Miriam was blighted, white as snow* (Numbers 12:10), after she spoke against Moses.
- **vv10–13 the pledge** — the lender may not enter the borrower's house to choose his pledge. Exodus 22:26–27 already required a cloak taken in pledge to be returned *before the sun goes down… In what shall he sleep?*
- **vv14–15 the hired man's wages** — Leviticus 19:13: *The wages of a hired man shall not stay with you overnight until morning.* *He sets his heart on it*: he has nothing else that day.
- **v16 "each shall be put to death for his own sin"** — 2 Kings 14:6 quotes this verse: King Amaziah spared the sons of his father's murderers.
- **vv19–21 sheaf, olives, grapes** — Leviticus 19:9–10 left the edges and gleanings *for the poor and for the guest*. Deuteronomy adds the forgotten sheaf, and names *the guest, the orphan and the widow* three times.
