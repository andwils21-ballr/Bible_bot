# Deuteronomy 8

## Remember the Way

**1** All the commandment that I command you today you shall be careful to do, so that you may live and multiply, and go in and possess the land that the LORD swore to your fathers.

**2** Remember all the way that the LORD your God has led you these forty years in the wilderness, to humble you, to test you, to know what was in your heart — whether you would keep His commandments or not.

**3** He humbled you and let you hunger and fed you with manna, which you did not know and your fathers did not know, to make you know that a person does not live on bread alone, but on everything that comes out of the mouth of the LORD.

**4** Your clothing did not wear out on you, and your foot did not swell, these forty years.

**5** Know in your heart that as a man disciplines his son, the LORD your God disciplines you.

**6** Keep the commandments of the LORD your God, to walk in His ways and to fear Him.

## A Good Land

**7** For the LORD your God is bringing you into a good land, a land of streams of water, of springs and deeps coming out in the valley and on the hill,

**8** a land of wheat and barley, of vine and fig and pomegranate, a land of olive oil and honey,

**9** a land where you will eat bread without scarcity, where you will lack nothing, a land whose stones are iron and out of whose hills you will dig copper.

**10** You will eat and be satisfied, and you shall bless the LORD your God for the good land that He has given you.

## Do Not Forget

**11** Guard yourself so that you do not forget the LORD your God by not keeping His commandments and His rules and His statutes that I command you today —

**12** so that, when you have eaten and are satisfied, and have built good houses and live in them,

**13** and your herds and your flocks multiply, and silver and gold multiply for you, and all that you have multiplies,

**14** your heart is not lifted up and you forget the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of slaves,

**15** who led you through the great and terrible wilderness, with its fiery serpents and scorpions, a thirsty ground where there was no water; who brought water out for you from the flint rock;

**16** who fed you in the wilderness with manna that your fathers did not know, to humble you and to test you, to do you good in the end —

**17** and you say in your heart, 'My power and the might of my hand have made me this wealth.'

**18** Remember the LORD your God, for it is He who gives you power to make wealth, so that He may confirm His covenant that He swore to your fathers, as it is this day.

**19** And it shall come to pass, if you forget, forget the LORD your God and go after other gods and serve them and bow down to them, I testify against you today that you will perish, perish.

**20** Like the nations that the LORD is destroying before you, so you will perish, because you would not listen to the voice of the LORD your God.

## Notes

- **v2 "to humble you, to test you"** — *'annotekha*, from *'anah*, the verb used of Egypt's hard labor in Exodus 1:11. The wilderness pressed on Israel too, but for a purpose: *to know what was in your heart*.
- **v3 "does not live on bread alone"** — the manna was bread no one had known, and the lesson was hunger first, then food. Jesus answers the tempter with this verse in Matthew 4:4, after forty days without food in the wilderness.
- **v4 "Your clothing did not wear out"** — the same claim is made again in 29:4, where the sandals are added.
- **v5 "as a man disciplines his son"** — *yasar*, **to discipline, to instruct**: the wilderness years were a father's training, not a sentence.
- **vv7–8 the land's seven kinds** — wheat, barley, vine, fig, pomegranate, olive, and *devash*, **honey**, which here stands in a list of plants and so may mean date syrup as well as bees' honey.
- **v9 "whose stones are iron… you will dig copper"** — *nechoshet* is the same word as *bronze* in the tabernacle; the ore is copper, the alloy is bronze.
- **v15 "fiery serpents"** — *nachash saraph*, the snakes of Numbers 21:6. *Saraph* is from the verb **to burn**, perhaps from the burning of the bite.
- **v17 "My power and the might of my hand"** — *kochi ve-otsem yadi*. The danger this chapter names is not hunger in the wilderness but plenty in the land (vv12–14).
- **vv19–20 "you will perish, perish… Like the nations"** — *avod tovedun*, the verb doubled. The nations are being destroyed for the same reason Israel would be; there is no separate rule for the chosen people.
