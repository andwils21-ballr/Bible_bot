# Deuteronomy 16

## Passover and Unleavened Bread

**1** Keep the month of Aviv, and make the Passover to the LORD your God, for in the month of Aviv the LORD your God brought you out of Egypt by night.

**2** You shall sacrifice the Passover to the LORD your God, from the flock and the herd, in the place that the LORD will choose to make His name dwell there.

**3** You shall not eat anything leavened with it. Seven days you shall eat unleavened bread with it, the bread of affliction — for in haste you came out of the land of Egypt — so that you may remember the day you came out of the land of Egypt all the days of your life.

**4** No leaven shall be seen with you in all your territory for seven days, and none of the meat that you sacrifice in the evening on the first day shall remain overnight until morning.

**5** You may not sacrifice the Passover within any of your towns that the LORD your God is giving you,

**6** but at the place that the LORD your God will choose to make His name dwell there, there you shall sacrifice the Passover, in the evening, as the sun goes down, at the time you came out of Egypt.

**7** You shall cook it and eat it in the place that the LORD your God will choose, and in the morning you shall turn and go to your tents.

**8** Six days you shall eat unleavened bread, and on the seventh day there shall be a closing assembly to the LORD your God; you shall do no work.

## The Festival of Weeks

**9** You shall count seven weeks for yourself. From the time the sickle is first put to the standing grain you shall begin to count seven weeks.

**10** Then you shall keep the Festival of Weeks to the LORD your God, with a freewill offering from your hand, which you shall give as the LORD your God blesses you.

**11** You shall rejoice before the LORD your God, you and your son and your daughter, your male slave and your female slave, the Levite who is inside your gates, and the guest, the orphan and the widow who are among you, in the place that the LORD your God will choose to make His name dwell there.

**12** Remember that you were a slave in Egypt, and be careful to do these statutes.

## The Festival of Booths

**13** You shall keep the Festival of Booths seven days, when you gather in from your threshing floor and from your winepress.

**14** You shall rejoice in your festival, you and your son and your daughter, your male slave and your female slave, the Levite and the guest, the orphan and the widow who are inside your gates.

**15** Seven days you shall keep the festival to the LORD your God in the place that the LORD will choose, for the LORD your God will bless you in all your yield and in all the work of your hands, and you shall be nothing but joyful.

**16** Three times in the year all your males shall appear before the face of the LORD your God in the place that He will choose: at the Festival of Unleavened Bread, at the Festival of Weeks, and at the Festival of Booths. They shall not appear before the face of the LORD empty-handed,

**17** each with the gift of his hand, according to the blessing of the LORD your God that He has given you.

## Judges and Officials

**18** Judges and officials you shall appoint for yourself in all your towns that the LORD your God is giving you, tribe by tribe, and they shall judge the people with righteous judgment.

**19** You shall not bend judgment. You shall not show partiality, and you shall not take a bribe, for a bribe blinds the eyes of the wise and twists the words of the righteous.

**20** Justice, justice you shall pursue, so that you may live and possess the land that the LORD your God is giving you.

**21** You shall not plant for yourself a sacred pole, any tree, beside the altar of the LORD your God that you make for yourself.

**22** You shall not set up for yourself a standing stone, which the LORD your God hates.

## Notes

- **v1 "by night"** — Exodus 12:31 has Pharaoh call for Moses *at night*, and Numbers 33:3 has Israel set out *on the day after the Passover*. The night is when the release was won.
- **v2 "from the flock and the herd"** — Exodus 12:5 takes the Passover animal *from the sheep or from the goats*; Deuteronomy allows cattle for the Passover sacrifice and moves it from the household to the chosen place (vv5–6).
- **v3 "the bread of affliction"** — *lechem 'oni*, from the same *'oni* as *the affliction of My people* in Exodus 3:7. Unleavened bread is poor people's bread, and bread made in a hurry.
- **v7 "You shall cook it"** — *u-vishshalta*, the verb Exodus 12:9 used when it forbade the lamb *boiled at all in water*. Here it may mean simply *cook*. 2 Chronicles 35:13 puts the two together: *they cooked the Passover in the fire*.
- **v8 "a closing assembly"** — *atseret*, as at the end of Booths in Leviticus 23:36.
- **v9 "seven weeks"** — counted from the first cutting of the grain, not from a fixed date as in Leviticus 23:15. Leviticus 23:16 counts *fifty days*; from the Greek word for *fiftieth* comes the festival's later name, *Pentecost*.
- **vv11, 14 the guest, the orphan and the widow** — the festivals are not for the landowner's household alone. Those who own no land are named each time.
- **v19 "a bribe blinds the eyes of the wise"** — Exodus 23:8 has *the clear-sighted*, *piqchim*; Deuteronomy has *chakhamim*, **the wise**. The warning holds even for the best judge.
- **v20 "Justice, justice you shall pursue"** — *tsedeq tsedeq tirdof*, the noun doubled for emphasis.
- **vv21–22 sacred pole and standing stone** — the objects that 7:5 and 12:3 ordered torn down among the nations are not to be set up beside God's own altar either.
