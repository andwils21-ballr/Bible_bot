# Deuteronomy 25

## Forty Blows

**1** If there is a dispute between men and they come to judgment, and the judges judge them, declaring the righteous one righteous and the wicked one wicked,

**2** and it comes to pass, if the wicked one deserves to be beaten, the judge shall make him lie down and have him beaten in his presence with the number of blows his wickedness deserves.

**3** Forty blows he may give him, and no more, or if he goes on to beat him with more blows than these, your brother would be degraded in your eyes.

**4** You shall not muzzle an ox while it is threshing.

## The Brother's Duty

**5** If brothers live together and one of them dies and has no son, the wife of the dead man shall not marry outside the family to a stranger. Her husband's brother shall go in to her and take her as his wife and do the brother's duty by her.

**6** And it shall come to pass that the firstborn whom she bears shall carry on the name of his dead brother, so that his name is not wiped out of Israel.

**7** But if the man does not wish to take his brother's wife, then his brother's wife shall go up to the gate, to the elders, and say, 'My husband's brother refuses to raise up a name for his brother in Israel; he will not do the brother's duty by me.'

**8** The elders of his town shall call him and speak to him. If he stands firm and says, 'I do not wish to take her,'

**9** then his brother's wife shall come up to him in the sight of the elders, pull his sandal off his foot, and spit in his face. She shall answer and say, 'So shall it be done to the man who will not build up his brother's house.'

**10** His name shall be called in Israel, 'The house of him whose sandal was pulled off.'

## Various Laws

**11** When men fight together, a man and his brother, and the wife of one comes near to rescue her husband from the hand of the one striking him, and puts out her hand and seizes him by his private parts,

**12** you shall cut off her hand. Your eye shall not pity.

**13** You shall not have in your bag two kinds of weights, a large and a small.

**14** You shall not have in your house two kinds of ephah, a large and a small.

**15** A full and honest weight you shall have; a full and honest ephah you shall have, so that your days may be long on the ground that the LORD your God is giving you.

**16** For everyone who does these things, everyone who does injustice, is detestable to the LORD your God.

## Remember Amalek

**17** Remember what Amalek did to you on the way when you came out of Egypt,

**18** how he met you on the way and cut off at your rear all who were lagging behind you, when you were faint and weary, and he did not fear God.

**19** And it shall come to pass, when the LORD your God has given you rest from all your enemies around you, in the land that the LORD your God is giving you as an inheritance to possess, you shall blot out the memory of Amalek from under the heavens. Do not forget.

## Notes

- **vv1–3 forty blows** — the limit protects the guilty man: *your brother would be degraded*. He is still *your brother* while he is being punished. Later practice stopped at thirty-nine to keep from going over; Paul says in 2 Corinthians 11:24, *five times I received forty less one*.
- **v4 "You shall not muzzle an ox while it is threshing"** — the animal working the grain may eat from it. Paul quotes the verse in 1 Corinthians 9:9 for the right of those who work in the gospel to be supported.
- **vv5–10 "do the brother's duty"** — *yabbem*, the word Judah uses to Onan in Genesis 38:8, *do the brother's duty by her, and raise up seed for your brother*. The Sadducees put this law to Jesus in Matthew 22:24.
- **v6 "carry on the name of his dead brother"** — *yaqum al shem achiv*, **stand on his brother's name**. The child counts as the dead man's.
- **vv9–10 the sandal and the spit** — the refusal is made public, and the man's household carries the name. Ruth 4:7 describes a sandal given over in a similar exchange of rights.
- **vv11–12 the wife's hand** — *mevushav*, **his private parts**, from *bosh*, **shame**. The penalty is fixed and not to be softened: *Your eye shall not pity*, as for the murderer (19:13) and the false witness (19:21).
- **vv13–15 "two kinds of weights"** — *even va-even*, **a stone and a stone**: one to buy with and one to sell with. Leviticus 19:36: *You shall have honest scales, honest weights, an honest ephah*.
- **vv17–19 Amalek** — Exodus 17:8–16 tells the battle at Rephidim; Deuteronomy adds how Amalek fought: from behind, against the stragglers, *when you were faint and weary*. The command repeats God's own words in Exodus 17:14, *I will utterly blot out the memory of Amalek from under the heavens*, and ends with two words: *lo tishkach*, **Do not forget**.
