# Esther 1

## The King's Feasts

**1** And it came to pass in the days of Ahasuerus, the Ahasuerus who reigned from India to Cush over a hundred and twenty-seven provinces,

**2** in those days, when King Ahasuerus sat on the throne of his kingdom in Susa the citadel,

**3** in the third year of his reign he gave a feast for all his officials and servants. The army of Persia and Media, the nobles and the officials of the provinces were before him,

**4** while he showed the riches of his royal glory and the splendor and pomp of his greatness for many days, a hundred and eighty days.

**5** When these days were completed, the king gave a feast of seven days for all the people who were present in Susa the citadel, from the greatest to the least, in the court of the garden of the king's palace.

**6** There were hangings of white cotton and blue, fastened with cords of fine linen and purple to silver rings and pillars of marble, and couches of gold and silver on a pavement of porphyry, marble, mother-of-pearl and colored stone.

**7** Drink was served in vessels of gold, each vessel different from the others, and the royal wine was plentiful, as befitted the king's bounty.

**8** The drinking was by the rule: no one was compelled. For so the king had ordered every officer of his house, to do as each man wished.

**9** Queen Vashti also gave a feast for the women in the royal house that belonged to King Ahasuerus.

## Vashti's Refusal

**10** On the seventh day, when the king's heart was merry with wine, he told Mehuman, Biztha, Harbona, Bigtha and Abagtha, Zethar and Carkas, the seven eunuchs who served before King Ahasuerus,

**11** to bring Queen Vashti before the king wearing the royal crown, to show the peoples and the officials her beauty, for she was lovely to look at.

**12** But Queen Vashti refused to come at the king's word sent by the eunuchs. The king was very angry, and his wrath burned within him.

**13** Then the king said to the wise men who knew the times, for this was the king's way before all who knew law and judgment,

**14** those next to him being Carshena, Shethar, Admatha, Tarshish, Meres, Marsena and Memucan, the seven officials of Persia and Media who saw the king's face and sat first in the kingdom:

**15** "According to the law, what is to be done to Queen Vashti, because she has not done the command of King Ahasuerus sent by the eunuchs?"

## The Decree for Every House

**16** Memucan said before the king and the officials, "Queen Vashti has done wrong not against the king alone, but against all the officials and all the peoples in all the provinces of King Ahasuerus.

**17** For the queen's deed will go out to all the women, making their husbands contemptible in their eyes, when they say, 'King Ahasuerus ordered Queen Vashti brought before him, and she did not come.'

**18** This very day the noble ladies of Persia and Media who have heard of the queen's deed will say the same to all the king's officials, and there will be contempt and wrath in plenty.

**19** If it pleases the king, let a royal word go out from him, and let it be written among the laws of Persia and Media so that it may not pass away: that Vashti is never again to come before King Ahasuerus. Let the king give her royal position to another who is better than she.

**20** When the king's decree that he makes is heard throughout all his kingdom, vast as it is, all the women will give honor to their husbands, from the greatest to the least."

**21** The advice pleased the king and the officials, and the king did as Memucan said.

**22** He sent letters to all the king's provinces, to each province in its own script and to each people in its own language, that every man should be master in his own house and speak the language of his people.

## Notes

- **The text of Esther** — rendered from the Hebrew, with Swete's Greek as witness. The Greek tells the story at greater length: it calls the king Artaxerxes, opens with a dream of Mordecai before this verse, and carries six passages that the Hebrew does not have. The English numbering gathers those at the end of the book, as chapters 11–16; the dream is 11:2–12:6. The Hebrew of Esther does not name God.
