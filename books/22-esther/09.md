# Esther 9

## The Thirteenth of Adar

**1** In the twelfth month, the month of Adar, on the thirteenth day of it, when the king's word and his law were about to be carried out, on the very day when the enemies of the Jews hoped to gain power over them, it was turned the other way: the Jews gained power over those who hated them.

**2** The Jews gathered in their cities in all the provinces of King Ahasuerus to lay a hand on those who sought their harm, and no one could stand before them, for the fear of them had fallen on all the peoples.

**3** All the officials of the provinces, the satraps, the governors and those who did the king's work helped the Jews, for the fear of Mordecai had fallen on them.

**4** For Mordecai was great in the king's house, and his fame went out through all the provinces, for the man Mordecai grew greater and greater.

**5** The Jews struck all their enemies with the sword, killing and destroying them, and did as they pleased to those who hated them.

**6** In Susa the citadel the Jews killed and destroyed five hundred men,

**7** and Parshandatha, Dalphon, Aspatha,

**8** Poratha, Adalia, Aridatha,

**9** Parmashta, Arisai, Aridai and Vaizatha,

**10** the ten sons of Haman son of Hammedatha, the enemy of the Jews, they killed. But they did not lay their hands on the plunder.

## A Second Day in Susa

**11** That day the number of those killed in Susa the citadel was brought before the king.

**12** The king said to Queen Esther, "In Susa the citadel the Jews have killed and destroyed five hundred men and the ten sons of Haman. What have they done in the rest of the king's provinces? Now what is your wish? It shall be granted you. What further request do you have? It shall be done."

**13** Esther said, "If it pleases the king, let the Jews who are in Susa be allowed tomorrow also to do according to today's law, and let the ten sons of Haman be hanged on the pole."

**14** So the king commanded that this be done. A law was given out in Susa, and they hanged the ten sons of Haman.

**15** The Jews who were in Susa gathered also on the fourteenth day of the month of Adar and killed three hundred men in Susa, but they did not lay their hands on the plunder.

**16** The rest of the Jews who were in the king's provinces gathered and stood for their lives, and had rest from their enemies, and killed seventy-five thousand of those who hated them, but they did not lay their hands on the plunder.

**17** This was on the thirteenth day of the month of Adar, and on the fourteenth day they rested and made it a day of feasting and gladness.

**18** But the Jews who were in Susa gathered on the thirteenth day and on the fourteenth, and on the fifteenth day they rested and made it a day of feasting and gladness.

**19** That is why the Jews of the villages, who live in the unwalled towns, keep the fourteenth day of the month of Adar as a day of gladness and feasting, a holiday, and a day for sending portions to one another.

## Mordecai's Letter

**20** Mordecai wrote these things down and sent letters to all the Jews who were in all the provinces of King Ahasuerus, near and far,

**21** to bind them to keep the fourteenth day of the month of Adar and the fifteenth day of it, every year,

**22** as the days on which the Jews had rest from their enemies, and the month that was turned for them from sorrow to gladness and from mourning to a holiday, that they should make them days of feasting and gladness, of sending portions to one another and gifts to the poor.

**23** So the Jews took on what they had begun to do, and what Mordecai had written to them.

**24** For Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite, the enemy of all the Jews, had plotted against the Jews to destroy them, and had cast pur, that is, the lot, to crush them and to destroy them.

**25** But when it came before the king, he ordered by letter that the evil plot Haman had devised against the Jews should return on his own head, and that he and his sons should be hanged on the pole.

**26** Therefore they called these days Purim, after the name pur. Therefore, because of all the words of this letter, and what they had seen of this matter and what had come upon them,

**27** the Jews bound themselves and took it on themselves, on their offspring and on all who joined them, without fail, to keep these two days as it was written and at their appointed time, every year,

**28** and that these days should be remembered and kept in every generation, in every family, every province and every city, and that these days of Purim should not pass away from among the Jews, nor the memory of them cease from their offspring.

## Esther's Letter

**29** Then Queen Esther daughter of Abihail, and Mordecai the Jew, wrote with full authority to confirm this second letter of Purim.

**30** He sent letters to all the Jews, to the hundred and twenty-seven provinces of the kingdom of Ahasuerus, words of peace and truth,

**31** to confirm these days of Purim at their appointed times, as Mordecai the Jew and Queen Esther had bound on them, and as they had bound on themselves and on their offspring, with the matters of the fasts and their cry.

**32** The command of Esther confirmed these matters of Purim, and it was written in the book.

## Notes

- **v1 "it was turned the other way"** — *ve-nahafokh hu*. The same verb in v22: *the month that was turned for them from sorrow to gladness*.
- **vv7–9 the ten sons of Haman** — Haman boasted of *the number of his sons* (5:11).
- **vv10, 15, 16 "they did not lay their hands on the plunder"** — said three times. The king's letter allowed it (8:11), as Haman's had (3:13). When Saul fought Agag's people, Samuel asked him, *Why did you swoop on the plunder?* (1 Samuel 15:19).
- **v19 "the Jews of the villages"** — the read form, *ha-perazim*. What is written, *ha-perozim*, is another spelling of the same word. The Greek adds that those who live in the cities keep the fifteenth.
- **v22 "gifts to the poor"** — *mattanot la-evyonim*, added to the feasting and the portions of v19.
- **v25 "But when it came before the king"** — *u-ve-vo'ah*, **when she came** or **when it came**: Esther, or the matter. The Greek has *when he went in to the king*, of Haman.
- **v26 "Purim, after the name pur"** — the lot cast before Haman (3:7).
- **v27 "the Jews bound themselves and took it on themselves"** — *qiyyemu ve-qibbelu*, the read form. What is written is *ve-qibbel*, **and he took on**.
- **vv27–28 "without fail… should not pass away"** — *ve-lo ya'avor*, *lo ya'avru*, the words of the law of Persia and Media that may not pass away (1:19).
- **v31 "the matters of the fasts and their cry"** — the fasting and the crying out of 4:1–3 and 4:16.
