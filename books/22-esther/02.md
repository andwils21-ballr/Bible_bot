# Esther 2

## The Search for a Queen

**1** After these things, when the wrath of King Ahasuerus had died down, he remembered Vashti and what she had done and what had been decreed against her.

**2** Then the king's young men who served him said, "Let young virgins, lovely to look at, be sought for the king.

**3** Let the king appoint officers in all the provinces of his kingdom, and let them gather every young virgin lovely to look at to Susa the citadel, to the house of the women, into the charge of Hegai the king's eunuch, keeper of the women, and let their cosmetics be given them.

**4** The young woman who pleases the king, let her be queen in place of Vashti." This pleased the king, and he did so.

## Mordecai and Esther

**5** There was a Jewish man in Susa the citadel whose name was Mordecai son of Jair, son of Shimei, son of Kish, a Benjaminite,

**6** who had been carried into exile from Jerusalem with the exiles carried away with Jeconiah king of Judah, whom Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had carried into exile.

**7** He was bringing up Hadassah, that is Esther, the daughter of his uncle, for she had neither father nor mother. The young woman was beautiful in form and lovely to look at, and when her father and mother died, Mordecai took her as his own daughter.

**8** When the king's word and his decree were proclaimed and many young women were gathered to Susa the citadel into the charge of Hegai, Esther also was taken to the king's house, into the charge of Hegai, keeper of the women.

**9** The young woman pleased him and won his kindness. He quickly gave her her cosmetics and her portions, and the seven young women chosen to be given her from the king's house, and he moved her and her young women to the best place in the house of the women.

**10** Esther had not made known her people or her kindred, for Mordecai had charged her not to make it known.

**11** Every day Mordecai walked back and forth in front of the court of the house of the women, to learn how Esther was and what was being done with her.

## The Turn of Each Young Woman

**12** When each young woman's turn came to go in to King Ahasuerus, after twelve months under the rule for the women, for so the days of their beautifying were completed, six months with oil of myrrh and six months with spices and the cosmetics of the women,

**13** this was how the young woman went in to the king: whatever she asked for was given her to take with her from the house of the women to the king's house.

**14** In the evening she went in, and in the morning she returned to the second house of the women, into the charge of Shaashgaz the king's eunuch, keeper of the secondary wives. She did not go in to the king again unless the king delighted in her and she was called by name.

**15** When the turn came for Esther daughter of Abihail, the uncle of Mordecai, who had taken her as his own daughter, to go in to the king, she asked for nothing except what Hegai the king's eunuch, keeper of the women, advised. Esther won favor in the eyes of all who saw her.

## Esther Made Queen

**16** Esther was taken to King Ahasuerus, into his royal house, in the tenth month, the month of Tebeth, in the seventh year of his reign.

**17** The king loved Esther more than all the women, and she won favor and kindness before him more than all the virgins. He set the royal crown on her head and made her queen in place of Vashti.

**18** Then the king gave a great feast for all his officials and servants, Esther's feast. He granted a release to the provinces and gave gifts, as befitted the king's bounty.

## Mordecai Uncovers a Plot

**19** When the virgins were gathered a second time, Mordecai was sitting in the king's gate.

**20** Esther had not made known her kindred or her people, as Mordecai had charged her, for Esther did what Mordecai said, as she had when she was being brought up by him.

**21** In those days, while Mordecai was sitting in the king's gate, Bigthan and Teresh, two of the king's eunuchs who guarded the threshold, became angry and sought to lay a hand on King Ahasuerus.

**22** The matter became known to Mordecai, and he told Queen Esther, and Esther told the king in Mordecai's name.

**23** The matter was looked into and found to be so, and the two of them were hanged on a pole. It was written in the book of the chronicles before the king.

## Notes

- **v5 "Mordecai son of Jair, son of Shimei, son of Kish, a Benjaminite"** — *ish yemini*. Kish is the name of Saul's father, *a Benjaminite* (1 Samuel 9:1), and Shimei the name of the man *of the family of the house of Saul* who cursed David as he fled (2 Samuel 16:5). The next chapter names Mordecai's enemy *the Agagite* (3:1).
- **v6 "carried into exile… the exiles carried away… had carried into exile"** — *galah* four times in one verse. The exile with Jeconiah is the one told in 2 Kings 24:12–16.
- **v7 "Hadassah, that is Esther"** — *hadassah*, from *hadas*, **myrtle**: *instead of the brier shall come up the myrtle* (Isaiah 55:13). The Greek says that when her parents died Mordecai *brought her up for himself as a wife*.
- **vv9, 17 "won his kindness… won favor and kindness before him"** — *va-tissa chesed*, *va-tissa chen va-chesed*. Of Joseph in the round house: *the LORD… extended kindness to him, and gave him favor in the eyes of the keeper* (Genesis 39:21).
- **vv10, 20 "Esther had not made known her people or her kindred"** — said twice, the second time with the reason: she did what Mordecai said. She makes it known at the second feast (7:4).
- **v14 "keeper of the secondary wives"** — *ha-pilagshim*. A woman who had gone in to the king went to the second house, not back to the first.
- **v16 "in the tenth month… in the seventh year of his reign"** — four years after the feast of 1:3. The Greek has *the twelfth month, which is Adar*.
- **v23 "hanged on a pole"** — *va-yittalu… al etz*, **on a tree** or **on wood**, the word of the law: *if you hang him on a tree, his body shall not remain all night on the tree* (Deuteronomy 21:22–23). Haman has a pole made for Mordecai (5:14), and is hanged on it himself (7:10).
- **v23 "It was written in the book of the chronicles before the king"** — the record that is read to the king on a sleepless night (6:1–2).
