# Esther 8

## Mordecai Given Haman's Place

**1** That day King Ahasuerus gave Queen Esther the house of Haman, the enemy of the Jews, and Mordecai came before the king, for Esther had told what he was to her.

**2** The king took off his signet ring, which he had taken from Haman, and gave it to Mordecai. Esther set Mordecai over the house of Haman.

## Esther Pleads Again

**3** Then Esther spoke again before the king. She fell at his feet and wept and pleaded with him to avert the evil of Haman the Agagite and the plot he had devised against the Jews.

**4** The king held out the golden scepter to Esther, and Esther rose and stood before the king.

**5** She said, "If it pleases the king, and if I have found favor before him, and the thing seems right before the king, and I am pleasing in his eyes, let it be written to revoke the letters devised by Haman son of Hammedatha the Agagite, which he wrote to destroy the Jews who are in all the king's provinces.

**6** For how can I bear to see the evil that will come on my people? How can I bear to see the destruction of my kindred?"

**7** Then King Ahasuerus said to Queen Esther and to Mordecai the Jew, "I have given Esther the house of Haman, and they have hanged him on the pole, because he laid a hand on the Jews.

**8** As for you, write concerning the Jews as seems good to you, in the king's name, and seal it with the king's signet ring, for a writing written in the king's name and sealed with the king's signet ring cannot be revoked."

## The Letters Go Out

**9** The king's scribes were summoned at that time, in the third month, the month of Sivan, on the twenty-third day of it. It was written just as Mordecai commanded, to the Jews, and to the satraps, the governors and the officials of the provinces from India to Cush, a hundred and twenty-seven provinces, to each province in its own script and to each people in its own language, and to the Jews in their own script and their own language.

**10** He wrote in the name of King Ahasuerus and sealed it with the king's signet ring, and sent letters by couriers on horseback, riding swift horses of the royal service, bred from the royal stud.

**11** In them the king allowed the Jews who were in every city to gather and to stand for their lives, to destroy, to kill and to annihilate any armed force of any people or province that might attack them, children and women, and to plunder their goods,

**12** on one day in all the provinces of King Ahasuerus, the thirteenth of the twelfth month, the month of Adar.

**13** A copy of the writing was to be given out as law in every province, published to all the peoples, so that the Jews would be ready on that day to take vengeance on their enemies.

**14** The couriers, riding the swift royal horses, went out urgently and in haste at the king's word, and the law was given out in Susa the citadel.

## Light for the Jews

**15** Then Mordecai went out from the presence of the king in royal robes of blue and white, with a great golden crown and a mantle of fine linen and purple, and the city of Susa shouted and rejoiced.

**16** For the Jews there was light and gladness, joy and honor.

**17** In every province and in every city, wherever the king's word and his law reached, there was gladness and joy for the Jews, a feast and a holiday. Many of the peoples of the land became Jews, for the fear of the Jews had fallen on them.

## Notes

- **vv1, 7, 13 "the Jews"** — the read form, *ha-Yehudim*. What is written each time is the fuller spelling *ha-Yehudiyyim*. In v13 *ready* is also the read form, *atidim*; what is written, *atudim*, has the same sense.
- **v2 "his signet ring, which he had taken from Haman"** — the ring the king gave Haman to seal the first letter (3:10).
- **v5 "let it be written to revoke the letters"** — *le-hashiv*, **to turn back**. The king answers that a letter sealed with his ring cannot be turned back (v8; 1:19), so a second letter is written instead of the first being withdrawn.
- **v9 "in the third month, the month of Sivan, on the twenty-third day"** — seventy days after Haman's letters were written (3:12).
- **v10 "riding swift horses of the royal service, bred from the royal stud"** — *rokhvei ha-rekhesh ha-achashteranim benei ha-rammakhim*. *Rekhesh* is the swift horse of 1 Kings 4:28 and Micah 1:13; *achashteranim* (here and v14) and *rammakhim* occur nowhere else, and their exact sense is uncertain.
- **v11 "to destroy, to kill and to annihilate… children and women, and to plunder their goods"** — word for word the terms of Haman's letter (3:13), now given to the Jews. After v12 the Greek gives the text of this second letter, which the English numbering places at 16:1–24.
- **v15 "royal robes of blue and white"** — *tekhelet va-chur*, the colors of the hangings at the king's feast (1:6). When Haman's letters went out, *the city of Susa was thrown into confusion* (3:15).
- **v17 "became Jews"** — *mityahadim*, a word found only here. The Greek has *were circumcised and lived as Jews*.
