# Esther 10

## Mordecai Second to the King

**1** King Ahasuerus laid a tribute on the land and on the coastlands of the sea.

**2** All the acts of his power and might, and the full account of the greatness of Mordecai, to which the king raised him, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Media and Persia?

**3** For Mordecai the Jew was second to King Ahasuerus, great among the Jews and well liked by the multitude of his brothers. He sought the good of his people and spoke peace to all his offspring.

---

## Notes

- **v1 "King Ahasuerus"** — the read form, *Achashverosh*. What is written here is the shorter *Achashrosh*.
- **v2 "are they not written in the book of the chronicles"** — the closing words of Kings for the kings of Israel and Judah (1 Kings 14:19, 29), here for the kings of Media and Persia. The book in which Mordecai's deed was first written (2:23; 6:1) now holds his greatness.
- **v3 "second to King Ahasuerus"** — *mishneh la-melekh*. Of Joseph in Egypt: Pharaoh had him ride *in the second chariot that he had*, *mirkevet ha-mishneh* (Genesis 41:43).
- **v3 "He sought the good of his people"** — *doresh tov le-ammo*, the last words of the Hebrew book.
- **v3, and the Greek after it** — the Greek goes on. Mordecai says, *These things have come from God*, and explains his dream from the beginning of the Greek book: the two dragons are he and Haman, the little spring that became a river is Esther. A closing note tells how the letter of Purim was brought to Egypt. The English numbering places this at 10:4–11:1.
