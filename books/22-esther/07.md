# Esther 7

## Esther's Request

**1** So the king and Haman came in to drink with Queen Esther.

**2** On the second day also, as they drank wine at the feast, the king said to Esther, "What is your wish, Queen Esther? It shall be granted you. What is your request? Even to half of the kingdom, it shall be done."

**3** Then Queen Esther answered, "If I have found favor in your eyes, O king, and if it pleases the king, let my life be given me at my wish, and my people at my request.

**4** For we have been sold, I and my people, to be destroyed, to be killed and to be annihilated. If we had only been sold as slaves, men and women, I would have kept silent, for such distress would not be worth troubling the king."

**5** Then King Ahasuerus said to Queen Esther, "Who is he, and where is he, who has dared to do this?"

**6** Esther said, "An adversary and an enemy, this wicked Haman!" Then Haman was terrified before the king and the queen.

## The Pole Fifty Cubits High

**7** The king rose in his wrath from the wine feast and went into the palace garden, but Haman stayed to beg Queen Esther for his life, for he saw that harm had been determined against him by the king.

**8** When the king returned from the palace garden to the house of the wine feast, Haman was falling on the couch where Esther was. The king said, "Will he even assault the queen with me in the house?" As the word left the king's mouth, they covered Haman's face.

**9** Then Harbona, one of the eunuchs before the king, said, "The pole also, which Haman made for Mordecai, whose word was for the good of the king, is standing at Haman's house, fifty cubits high." The king said, "Hang him on it."

**10** So they hanged Haman on the pole that he had prepared for Mordecai. Then the king's wrath died down.

## Notes

- **v3 "let my life be given me at my wish, and my people at my request"** — the king's two words, *wish* and *request* (v2; 5:6), answered with two gifts: herself and her people.
- **v4 "to be destroyed, to be killed and to be annihilated"** — the three words of Haman's letter (3:13). *We have been sold*: Haman had offered silver for them (3:9).
- **v4 "for such distress would not be worth troubling the king"** — *ki ein ha-tsar shoveh be-nezeq ha-melekh*. *Tsar* is **distress** or **adversary**, and the clause can also be read *the enemy could not make up the king's loss*. The Greek has *for the slanderer is not worthy of the king's court*.
- **v6 "An adversary and an enemy"** — *ish tsar ve-oyev*, the *tsar* of v4, and the root of Haman's title, *tsorer ha-Yehudim*, **the enemy of the Jews** (3:10).
- **v8 "Will he even assault the queen"** — *ha-gam li-khbosh et ha-malkah*. *Kavash* is the verb of *subdue* in *fill the earth and subdue it* (Genesis 1:28).
- **v8 "they covered Haman's face"** — *u-fenei Haman chafu*. He went home from the first honor with his head covered (6:12).
- **v9 "Harbona, one of the eunuchs"** — one of the seven sent to bring Vashti (1:10).
- **v10 "the king's wrath died down"** — *ve-chamat ha-melekh shakhakhah*, the words of 2:1, after Vashti.
