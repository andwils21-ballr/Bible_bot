# Joel 2

## The LORD's Army

**1** Blow the horn in Zion;  
sound the alarm on My holy mountain!  
Let all the inhabitants of the land tremble,  
for the day of the LORD is coming;  
it is near —

**2** a day of darkness and gloom,  
a day of cloud and thick darkness!  
Like dawn spread out over the mountains  
comes a people great and mighty;  
there has never been the like of it from of old,  
nor will there be again after it,  
through the years of generation after generation.

**3** Before it fire devours,  
and behind it a flame burns;  
the land before it is like the garden of Eden,  
and behind it a desolate wilderness,  
and no one escapes it.

**4** Its appearance is like the appearance of horses,  
and like horsemen, so they run.

**5** As with the noise of chariots  
they skip on the tops of the mountains,  
like the noise of a flame of fire  
devouring stubble,  
like a mighty people  
drawn up for battle.

**6** Before it peoples writhe;  
all faces grow pale.

**7** Like warriors they run;  
like men of war they climb the wall;  
they march each on his way,  
and they do not swerve from their paths.

**8** They do not jostle one another;  
each marches in his track;  
they plunge through the weapons  
and are not cut off.

**9** They leap on the city;  
they run on the wall;  
they climb into the houses;  
they enter through the windows like a thief.

**10** Before it the earth trembles,  
the heavens shake;  
the sun and the moon grow dark,  
and the stars withdraw their shining.

**11** The LORD utters His voice  
before His army,  
for His camp is very great;  
for mighty is the one who does His word.  
For the day of the LORD is great  
and very fearful;  
who can endure it?

## Return to Me

**12** Yet even now, declares the LORD,  
return to Me with all your heart,  
with fasting, with weeping and with lamenting.

**13** Tear your hearts and not your garments,  
and return to the LORD your God;  
for He is gracious and merciful,  
slow to anger and abundant in loyal love,  
and He relents of the evil.

**14** Who knows? He may turn and relent,  
and leave a blessing behind Him,  
a grain offering and a drink offering  
for the LORD your God.

**15** Blow the horn in Zion;  
consecrate a fast;  
proclaim a solemn assembly;

**16** gather the people;  
consecrate the assembly;  
bring together the elders;  
gather the children  
and those who nurse at the breast;  
let the bridegroom go out of his room,  
and the bride out of her chamber.

**17** Between the porch and the altar  
let the priests, the ministers of the LORD, weep,  
and let them say,  
"Spare Your people, LORD,  
and do not give Your inheritance to scorn,  
for the nations to rule over them.  
Why should they say among the peoples,  
'Where is their God?'"

## The LORD Answers

**18** Then the LORD was jealous for His land  
and had pity on His people.

**19** The LORD answered and said to His people:  
I am about to send you  
the grain, the new wine and the oil,  
and you will be satisfied with it;  
and I will no longer make you  
a scorn among the nations.

**20** The northerner I will remove far from you,  
and drive him into a land dry and desolate,  
his front into the eastern sea  
and his rear into the western sea;  
his stench will go up,  
and his foul smell will rise,  
for he has done great things.

**21** Do not fear, O ground;  
rejoice and be glad,  
for the LORD has done great things!

**22** Do not fear, you beasts of the field,  
for the pastures of the wilderness are green;  
for the tree bears its fruit;  
the fig tree and the vine  
give their full strength.

**23** You sons of Zion,  
rejoice and be glad in the LORD your God,  
for He has given you the early rain for righteousness;  
He has brought down rain for you,  
the early rain and the late rain, at the first.

**24** The threshing floors will be full of grain,  
and the vats will overflow with new wine and oil.

**25** I will repay you for the years  
that the locust has eaten,  
the young locust, the grasshopper and the cutting locust,  
My great army,  
which I sent among you.

**26** You will surely eat and be satisfied,  
and praise the name of the LORD your God,  
who has dealt wondrously with you;  
and My people will never be put to shame.

**27** You will know that I am among Israel,  
and that I am the LORD your God,  
and there is no other;  
and My people will never be put to shame.

## I Will Pour Out My Spirit

**28** And it shall come to pass afterward  
that I will pour out My Spirit on all flesh;  
your sons and your daughters will prophesy,  
your old men will dream dreams,  
and your young men will see visions.

**29** Even on the male servants and the female servants  
in those days I will pour out My Spirit.

**30** I will give wonders  
in the heavens and on the earth:  
blood and fire and columns of smoke.

**31** The sun will be turned to darkness,  
and the moon to blood,  
before the great and fearful day of the LORD comes.

**32** And it shall come to pass  
that everyone who calls on the name of the LORD will escape;  
for on Mount Zion and in Jerusalem  
there will be those who escape,  
as the LORD has said,  
and among the survivors  
those whom the LORD calls.

## Notes

- **vv1–3 "Blow the horn in Zion… a day of darkness and gloom… no one escapes it"** — the alarm of Hosea, *Blow the horn in Gibeah… Sound the alarm* (Hosea 5:8), now on Zion itself. The day is Amos's *darkness, and not light, gloom with no brightness in it* (Amos 5:20), and the cloud of Sinai, *darkness, cloud and thick darkness* (Deuteronomy 4:11). *There has never been the like of it* is said of the locusts of Egypt, *Before them there had never been locusts like them, and after them there will not be* (Exodus 10:14). The garden before and the wilderness behind is the promise run backward, *This land that was desolate has become like the garden of Eden* (Ezekiel 36:35). *Peletah*, **escape**, is what the locusts of Egypt ate up, *what is left of what escaped* (Exodus 10:5), and the word comes back in v32.
- **vv4–9 "like horsemen, so they run… all faces grow pale… they enter through the windows like a thief"** — the army may be the locusts of chapter 1 seen as soldiers, or soldiers seen as locusts; the chapter names it only *His army* (v11) and, in v25, *My great army*, with the four locust names. *Kibbetsu pa'rur*, **grow pale**, is uncertain, word for word **gather pa'rur**, perhaps a glow; the phrase is found again only in Nahum, of Nineveh. The Greek has *every face like a scorched pot*: *parur*, **pot**, has nearly the same letters, the word of *boiled it in pots* (Numbers 11:8). *Yashoqqu*, **they leap**, is the verb of *as locusts leap, men leap on it* (Isaiah 33:4). Houses full of them were the word to Pharaoh, *Your houses will be full* (Exodus 10:6).
- **vv10–11 "the earth trembles, the heavens shake… The LORD utters His voice before His army… who can endure it?"** — the lights fail as on Babylon's day, *the sun will be dark at its rising, and the moon will not shine its light* (Isaiah 13:10). *Natan qolo*, **utters His voice**, is the LORD's roar from Zion in Amos 1:2. *Machanehu*, **His camp**, is the word of Jacob's *This is God's camp* (Genesis 32:2), and *the one who does His word* is said of His messengers, *mighty in strength, who do His word* (Psalm 103:20). *Mi yekhilennu*, **who can endure it**, as in *the nations cannot endure His indignation* (Jeremiah 10:10).
- **vv12–14 "Yet even now… return to Me… Tear your hearts and not your garments… Who knows?"** — *shuvu adai*, **return to Me**, answers Amos's refrain, *yet you did not return to Me, declares the LORD* (Amos 4:6), said of the locust too (Amos 4:9); *with all your heart* is the law's, *when you return to the LORD your God with all your heart* (Deuteronomy 30:10). To tear the garments is the outward sign, as Ahab *tore his clothes* (1 Kings 21:27). The name is the LORD's own from Sinai, *God merciful and gracious, slow to anger, and abundant in loyal love* (Exodus 34:6), with *gracious* put first, and Jonah says it again in the same order (Jonah 4:2). *He relents of the evil*, as *the LORD relented of the evil* at the calf (Exodus 32:14). *Who knows?* is David's word as he fasted, *Who knows? The LORD may be gracious to me* (2 Samuel 12:22). The blessing left behind is the grain offering and drink offering that were cut off (1:9, 13).
- **vv15–17 "let the bridegroom go out of his room… Between the porch and the altar… Where is their God?"** — no one is excused: not the infants, not the bridegroom, whom the law sends home for a year, *he shall not go out with the army* (Deuteronomy 24:5). *Chuppah*, the bride's **chamber**, is the word of *like a bridegroom from his chamber* (Psalm 19:5). *Between the porch and the altar* is where Ezekiel saw twenty-five men bow to the sun (Ezekiel 8:16); here the priests weep there. *Li-mshol bam goyim*, **for the nations to rule over them**, can also be read **for the nations to make a proverb of them**, from *mashal*, the *proverb* of *a horror, a proverb and a byword* (Deuteronomy 28:37); the Greek has *to rule over them*. The last line is word for word the psalm's but for *among the peoples*: *Why should the nations say, "Where is their God?"* (Psalm 79:10).
- **vv18–20 "Then the LORD was jealous for His land… The northerner I will remove far from you… for he has done great things"** — *va-yeqanne*, **was jealous**, is the LORD of the commandment, *a jealous God* (Exodus 20:5), jealous now for His land. *Hineni sholeach*, **I am about to send**, gives back the grain, new wine and oil of 1:10. *Ha-tsefoni*, **the northerner**, is the enemy out of the north of Jeremiah, *Out of the north disaster will break loose* (Jeremiah 1:14). *The eastern sea* (Ezekiel 47:18) and *the western sea* (Deuteronomy 11:24) are the land's two borders, east and west. The stench is the plague of frogs, *the land stank* (Exodus 8:14). *Ki higdil la'asot*, **for he has done great things**, ends v20 of the enemy and v21 of the LORD: the pride of the one is answered by the deeds of the other, *The LORD has done great things for us* (Psalm 126:3).
- **vv21–24 "Do not fear, O ground… the pastures of the wilderness are green… the early rain for righteousness"** — chapter 1 is undone in its own words: the ground that mourned (1:10), the beasts that longed for God (1:20), the pastures the fire devoured (1:19), the fig tree and the vine (1:7, 12). *Cheilam*, **their full strength**, is *chayil*, the word for *His army* (v11): the vine gives its strength where the army took it. *Ha-moreh li-tsdaqah* is **the early rain for righteousness**, as *moreh* is the early rain in Psalm 84:6 and in this verse's next line; *moreh* is also **teacher**, *your eyes will see your Teacher* (Isaiah 30:20), and the verb is Hosea's *until He comes and rains righteousness on you* (Hosea 10:12). The Greek has *the food for righteousness*. *The early rain and the late rain* are the law's promise (Deuteronomy 11:14); *ba-rishon*, **at the first**, is in the first month, or as before, and the Greek has *as before*.
- **vv25–27 "I will repay you for the years… My great army… My people will never be put to shame"** — *ve-shillamti*, **I will repay**, is the verb of Hosea's *the days of repayment* (Hosea 9:7), now turned to Israel's good. The four of 1:4 come back in another order, and are named the LORD's own *great army, which I sent among you*. *Akhol ve-savoa*, **surely eat and be satisfied**, is the law's *You will eat and be satisfied* (Deuteronomy 8:10), where the curse had said *you will eat and not be satisfied* (Leviticus 26:26). *Lo yevoshu*, **will never be put to shame**, twice, is *bosh*, the verb of the farmers' shame (1:11). *Be-qerev Yisra'el ani*, **I am among Israel**, answers the false boast of Micah's rulers, *Is not the LORD among us?* (Micah 3:11), with the LORD's own word; and *there is no other* is His word in Isaiah, *I am the LORD, and there is no other* (Isaiah 45:5).
- **vv28–29 "I will pour out My Spirit on all flesh… Even on the male servants and the female servants"** — in the Hebrew text vv28–32 are 3:1–5, and chapter 3 is chapter 4; the English numbering is followed. *Ve-hayah acharei khen*, **and it shall come to pass afterward**: after the rain, the Spirit. Moses had wished it, *If only all the LORD's people were prophets, and the LORD would put His Spirit on them!* (Numbers 11:29), and Ezekiel and Isaiah heard it promised, *when I have poured out My Spirit on the house of Israel* (Ezekiel 39:29), *I will pour My Spirit on your seed* (Isaiah 44:3). The Spirit falls on old and young, sons and daughters, and the lowest of the house, *male servants and female servants*, the pair of Genesis 12:16. The Greek has *of My Spirit* and *My* servants, and Peter follows it at Pentecost as far as *will escape* in v32, with *in the last days* for *afterward* (Acts 2:16–21).
- **vv30–32 "blood and fire and columns of smoke… everyone who calls on the name of the LORD will escape"** — *mofetim*, **wonders**, are the *signs and wonders* the LORD gave against Egypt (Deuteronomy 6:22), and the smoke is Sinai's, *Its smoke went up like the smoke of a kiln* (Exodus 19:18); *columns of smoke* is the phrase of Song of Songs 3:6. The sun and moon darkened in v10 are now turned to darkness and to blood, and *the great and fearful day* is v11's. *Calling on the name of the LORD* is as old as Seth's son, *Then it was begun, calling on the name of the LORD* (Genesis 4:26). *Yimmalet*, **will escape**, and *peletah*, **those who escape**: none escaped the army (v3), and some will escape the day, as Isaiah heard, *out of Jerusalem shall go a remnant, and survivors out of Mount Zion* (Isaiah 37:32). *Yiqra*, **calls on**, and *qore*, **calls**: those who call on the LORD are those the LORD calls. The Greek has *those who bring good news, whom the Lord has called*.
