# Joel 1

## What the Locust Left

**1** The word of the LORD that came to Joel son of Pethuel.

**2** Hear this, you elders;  
give ear, all inhabitants of the land!  
Has such a thing happened in your days,  
or in the days of your fathers?

**3** Tell your children of it,  
and let your children tell their children,  
and their children another generation.

**4** What the cutting locust left,  
the locust has eaten;  
what the locust left,  
the young locust has eaten;  
and what the young locust left,  
the grasshopper has eaten.

## Wake Up and Weep

**5** Wake up, you drunkards, and weep;  
wail, all you drinkers of wine,  
over the sweet wine,  
for it is cut off from your mouth.

**6** For a nation has come up against my land,  
mighty and without number;  
its teeth are the teeth of a lion,  
and it has the jaws of a lioness.

**7** It has made my vine a waste  
and my fig tree splinters;  
it has utterly stripped it bare and thrown it down;  
its tendrils have turned white.

**8** Lament like a virgin girded with sackcloth  
for the husband of her youth.

**9** Grain offering and drink offering are cut off  
from the house of the LORD;  
the priests mourn,  
the ministers of the LORD.

**10** The field is devastated,  
the ground mourns;  
for the grain is devastated,  
the new wine dries up,  
the oil wastes away.

**11** Be ashamed, you farmers;  
wail, you vinedressers,  
over the wheat and over the barley,  
for the harvest of the field has perished.

**12** The vine dries up,  
and the fig tree wastes away;  
pomegranate, palm too, and apple tree,  
all the trees of the field are dried up;  
for joy has dried up  
from the sons of men.

## Proclaim a Solemn Assembly

**13** Gird yourselves and lament, you priests;  
wail, you ministers of the altar;  
come, spend the night in sackcloth,  
you ministers of my God;  
for grain offering and drink offering are withheld  
from the house of your God.

**14** Consecrate a fast;  
proclaim a solemn assembly;  
gather the elders,  
all the inhabitants of the land,  
to the house of the LORD your God,  
and cry out to the LORD.

## The Day of the LORD Is Near

**15** Alas for the day!  
For the day of the LORD is near,  
and it will come like destruction from the Almighty.

**16** Is not the food cut off  
before our eyes,  
joy and gladness  
from the house of our God?

**17** The seeds shrivel under their clods;  
the storehouses are desolate;  
the granaries are torn down,  
for the grain has dried up.

**18** How the beasts groan!  
The herds of cattle wander in confusion,  
because they have no pasture;  
even the flocks of sheep bear the guilt.

**19** To You, LORD, I call,  
for fire has devoured the pastures of the wilderness,  
and flame has burned up all the trees of the field.

**20** Even the beasts of the field long for You,  
for the streams of water are dried up,  
and fire has devoured the pastures of the wilderness.

## Notes

- **v1 "The word of the LORD that came to Joel son of Pethuel"** — Joel names no king and no year, where Hosea and Amos name both (Hosea 1:1; Amos 1:1). *Yo'el*, **Joel**, is also the name of Samuel's firstborn (1 Samuel 8:2). For Pethuel the Greek has *Bathouel*, the Greek spelling of Bethuel, Rebekah's father (Genesis 24:15).
- **vv2–3 "Has such a thing happened in your days… Tell your children of it"** — the words of the locusts of Egypt: *such as your fathers and your fathers' fathers have not seen* (Exodus 10:6), and *Before them there had never been locusts like them* (Exodus 10:14). The telling down the generations is the charge given with that plague, *so that you may tell in the ears of your son and your son's son what I did in Egypt* (Exodus 10:2): there the plague fell on Egypt; here it falls on the land of the house of the LORD (v9).
- **v4 "the cutting locust… the locust… the young locust… the grasshopper"** — four names: *gazam*, *arbeh*, *yeleq*, *chasil*. *Arbeh*, **locust**, is the locust of Egypt (Exodus 10:14); *gazam* is the one that in Amos *devoured your many gardens and vineyards* (Amos 4:9); *yeleq* is in *the locusts came, young locusts without number* (Psalm 105:34); and *chasil* in *locust or grasshopper* (1 Kings 8:37), from the verb of *for the locust will consume it* (Deuteronomy 28:38). *Yeter*, **what… left**, is the word of the locusts of Egypt, which *will eat what is left of what escaped* (Exodus 10:5). Whether they are four kinds or four waves, the verse does not say; the LORD names all four again in 2:25. The Greek has *caterpillar, locust, wingless locust, blight*.
- **vv5–7 "Wake up, you drunkards… a nation has come up against my land… its teeth are the teeth of a lion"** — the drunkards are called first, to weep for the wine. The locusts are called *goy*, **a nation**, an army *without number*, with the teeth of Judah's lion in Jacob's blessing, *like a lion, and like a lioness* (Genesis 49:9); *metalle'ot*, **jaws**, is the word of *I broke the jaws of the unjust* (Job 29:17). The vine and the fig tree are the sign of peace, *each man under his vine and under his fig tree* (Micah 4:4), and what Hosea foretold, *I will lay waste her vines and her fig trees* (Hosea 2:12). *Chasof chasafah*, **utterly stripped it bare**, and the *sarigim*, **tendrils**, as on the vine of the cupbearer's dream (Genesis 40:10), are left white.
- **v8 "Lament like a virgin girded with sackcloth for the husband of her youth"** — *eli*, **lament**, is said to a woman, the land; its letters spell *elai*, **to me**, and the Greek has *Lament to me*. A *betulah*, **virgin**, mourning *the husband of her youth* may be a young woman mourning the man she was promised to, for the law calls a betrothed virgin *his neighbor's wife* (Deuteronomy 22:24).
- **vv9, 13 "Grain offering and drink offering are cut off… spend the night in sackcloth"** — with no grain and no wine, the daily offering stops: *a drink offering of a quarter of a hin of wine, for the one lamb*, morning and evening (Exodus 29:40–41). *Meshartei YHWH*, **the ministers of the LORD**, are the priests at the altar. They are to lie the night in sackcloth, as Ahab *lay in sackcloth* when he heard the word against him (1 Kings 21:27).
- **vv10–12 "the new wine dries up… Be ashamed, you farmers… joy has dried up"** — the grain, new wine and oil are the blessing of the law, *your grain and your new wine and your oil* (Deuteronomy 7:13), which Israel did not know were the LORD's gift, *She did not know that it was I who gave her the grain, the new wine and the oil* (Hosea 2:8). *Hovish*, said five times in this chapter (vv10, 11, 12 twice, 17), is a form of two verbs, *yavesh*, **dry up**, and *bosh*, **be ashamed**: the wine dries up, the farmers are put to shame, and joy does both. *Shuddad sadeh*, **the field is devastated**, the two words nearly alike. *Umlal*, **wastes away**, is Hosea's *all who live in it waste away* (Hosea 4:3).
- **v14 "Consecrate a fast; proclaim a solemn assembly"** — *qir'u atsarah* are the words of Jehu's trap, *Proclaim a solemn assembly for Baal* (2 Kings 10:20); here it is for the LORD, and the whole land is called in with the elders. The call is given again in 2:15–16.
- **v15 "Alas for the day! For the day of the LORD is near"** — *ahah la-yom*, as Ezekiel was told to cry over Egypt, *Wail, 'Alas for the day!' For the day is near, the day of the LORD is near* (Ezekiel 30:2–3). The rest is word for word Isaiah's against Babylon, *Wail, for the day of the LORD is near; it will come like destruction from the Almighty* (Isaiah 13:6), and the English follows his. *Shod mi-Shaddai*, **destruction from the Almighty**, plays on the name of *God Almighty* (Genesis 17:1). Amos had warned, *Woe to you who long for the day of the LORD!* (Amos 5:18).
- **vv17–20 "The seeds shrivel… even the flocks of sheep bear the guilt… the beasts of the field long for You"** — v17 is obscure: *avshu*, **shrivel**, *megrefot*, **clods**, and *mammegurot*, **granaries**, are found only here; the Greek has *the heifers leaped at their mangers*. *Navokhu*, **wander in confusion**, is what Pharaoh said of Israel in the wilderness (Exodus 14:3). *Ne'shamu*, **bear the guilt**, is the verb of *now they must bear their guilt* (Hosea 10:2): the flocks suffer for the guilt of men. The Greek has *were destroyed*, as if from *nashammu*, **are desolate** (v17), nearly the same letters. *Ta'arog*, **long for**, is the deer of *As a deer longs for streams of water, so my soul longs for You, God* (Psalm 42:1), with the same *afiqei mayim*, **streams of water**: the beasts pray as the prophet prays (v19).
