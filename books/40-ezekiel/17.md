# Ezekiel 17

**1** The word of the LORD came to me, saying,

**2** "Son of man, put a riddle and speak a parable to the house of Israel,

**3** and say: This is what the Lord GOD says:  
A great eagle with great wings and long pinions,  
full of feathers of many colors,  
came to Lebanon  
and took the top of the cedar.

**4** He plucked off the topmost of its young shoots  
and carried it to a land of trade;  
he set it in a city of merchants.

**5** Then he took some of the seed of the land  
and put it in a field for seed;  
he set it like a willow  
beside many waters.

**6** It sprouted and became a spreading vine, low in height,  
its branches turned toward him  
and its roots under him;  
so it became a vine,  
and it put out branches and sent out shoots.

**7** There was another great eagle,  
with great wings and many feathers;  
and this vine bent its roots toward him  
and sent out its branches toward him,  
from the beds where it was planted,  
for him to water it.

**8** It had been planted in a good field, beside many waters,  
to put out branches and bear fruit  
and become a noble vine.

**9** Say: This is what the Lord GOD says:  
Will it thrive?  
Will he not pull up its roots  
and strip off its fruit, so that it withers,  
all its fresh sprouting leaves withered?  
It will take no strong arm or many people  
to lift it up by its roots.

**10** It is planted; will it thrive?  
Will it not wither completely when the east wind strikes it,  
wither on the beds where it sprouted?"

**11** The word of the LORD came to me, saying,

**12** "Say now to the rebellious house: Do you not know what these things mean? Say: The king of Babylon came to Jerusalem and took its king and its princes and brought them to himself in Babylon.

**13** He took one of the royal line and made a covenant with him, putting him under an oath; and he took away the leading men of the land,

**14** so that the kingdom would be brought low and not lift itself up, but keep his covenant and stand by it.

**15** But he rebelled against him by sending his envoys to Egypt to be given horses and a large army. Will he thrive? Will the one who does such things escape? Can he break a covenant and escape?

**16** As I live, declares the Lord GOD, in the place of the king who made him king, whose oath he despised and whose covenant he broke, there in Babylon, with him, he shall die.

**17** Pharaoh with his great army and large company will not help him in the war, when ramps are thrown up and siege walls built to cut off many lives.

**18** He despised the oath by breaking the covenant; he gave his hand, and yet did all these things. He will not escape.

**19** Therefore this is what the Lord GOD says: As I live, My oath that he despised and My covenant that he broke, I will bring it on his own head.

**20** I will spread My net over him, and he will be caught in My snare; I will bring him to Babylon and enter into judgment with him there for the treachery he committed against Me.

**21** All the fugitives of all his troops will fall by the sword, and those who are left will be scattered to every wind; and you will know that I, the LORD, have spoken.

**22** This is what the Lord GOD says:  
I Myself will take from the top of the lofty cedar and set it out;  
from the topmost of its young shoots I will pluck off a tender one,  
and I Myself will plant it on a high and lofty mountain.

**23** On the high mountain of Israel I will plant it,  
and it will put out branches and bear fruit  
and become a noble cedar.  
Under it will live every bird of every wing;  
in the shade of its branches they will live.

**24** All the trees of the field will know  
that I am the LORD.  
I bring low the high tree,  
I make high the low tree,  
I dry up the green tree,  
and I make the dry tree flourish.  
I, the LORD, have spoken, and I will do it."

## Notes

- **v2 "put a riddle and speak a parable"** — *chud chidah u-meshol mashal*, each verb with its own noun: the riddle of vv3–10, and the meaning in vv12–21.
- **vv3–4, 12 "A great eagle… came to Lebanon and took the top of the cedar… The king of Babylon came to Jerusalem and took its king"** — the eagle is Nebuchadnezzar, and the cedar's top is the king he carried off with *the leading men of the land*, as Kings tells it: *He carried Jehoiachin into exile to Babylon* (2 Kings 24:15). *A land of trade*, *eretz kena'an*, is Babylon, as at 16:29.
- **vv5–6, 13–14 "some of the seed of the land… a spreading vine, low in height… He took one of the royal line and made a covenant with him"** — Zedekiah, set on the throne under oath, a vine meant to stay low and turned toward the eagle. *Like a willow*, *tsaftsafah*, is found only here.
- **vv7, 15 "another great eagle… sending his envoys to Egypt to be given horses"** — Pharaoh. The vine turns its roots toward the second eagle, and the king breaks his oath to seek Egypt's help.
- **vv16, 19–20 "in the place of the king who made him king… there in Babylon… he shall die… My oath that he despised"** — Chronicles says Zedekiah *rebelled against King Nebuchadnezzar, who had made him swear by God* (2 Chronicles 36:13); the oath was sworn in God's name, so God calls it *My oath* and *My covenant*. What happened is in Jeremiah: the king of Babylon *put out the eyes of Zedekiah… and put him in the house of custody until the day of his death* (Jeremiah 52:11).
- **vv22–23 "I Myself will take from the top of the lofty cedar… On the high mountain of Israel I will plant it"** — the riddle turned: what the eagle did, God now does, *I Myself*, and the shoot He plants becomes a cedar where *every bird of every wing* lives.
- **v24 "I bring low the high tree, I make high the low tree"** — the song of Hannah has the same reversal in other words (1 Samuel 2:7–8): what is high and green is not safe, and what is low and dry is not lost.
