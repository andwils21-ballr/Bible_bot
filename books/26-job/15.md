# Job 15

**1** Eliphaz the Temanite answered and said:

## Your Own Mouth Condemns You

**2** Should a wise man answer with windy knowledge,  
and fill his belly with the east wind?

**3** Should he argue with talk that does no good,  
or with words that are of no use?

**4** But you are doing away with the fear of God  
and cutting short devotion before God.

**5** For your iniquity teaches your mouth,  
and you choose the tongue of the crafty.

**6** Your own mouth condemns you, and not I;  
your own lips testify against you.

**7** Were you the first man to be born?  
Were you brought forth before the hills?

**8** Have you listened in the council of God?  
Do you keep wisdom to yourself?

**9** What do you know that we do not know?  
What do you understand that is not with us?

**10** Both the gray-haired and the aged are among us,  
older than your father.

**11** Are the comforts of God too small for you,  
or the word spoken gently with you?

**12** Why does your heart carry you away,  
and why do your eyes flash,

**13** that you turn your spirit against God  
and bring such words out of your mouth?

**14** What is man, that he can be pure,  
or one born of a woman, that he can be righteous?

**15** He puts no trust in His holy ones,  
and the heavens are not clean in His sight;

**16** how much less one who is detestable and corrupt,  
a man who drinks injustice like water!

## The Fate of the Wicked

**17** I will show you; hear me,  
and what I have seen I will declare,

**18** what wise men have told,  
and did not hide from their fathers,

**19** to whom alone the land was given,  
and no stranger passed among them.

**20** The wicked man writhes in pain all his days,  
all the number of years laid up for the ruthless.

**21** A sound of terrors is in his ears;  
in time of peace the destroyer will come on him.

**22** He does not believe that he will return out of darkness,  
and he is marked out for the sword.

**23** He wanders about for bread: "Where is it?"  
He knows that a day of darkness is ready at his hand.

**24** Distress and anguish terrify him;  
they overpower him, like a king ready for the attack.

**25** For he has stretched out his hand against God  
and plays the mighty man against the Almighty,

**26** running at Him with neck outstretched,  
behind the thick bosses of his shields.

**27** For he has covered his face with his fat  
and put layers of fat on his loins,

**28** and has lived in ruined cities,  
in houses no one should live in,  
marked out to become heaps of rubble.

**29** He will not be rich, and his wealth will not last,  
and his gains will not bend down to the earth.

**30** He will not escape from darkness;  
the flame will dry up his shoots,  
and he will depart by the breath of His mouth.

**31** Let him not trust in emptiness, deceiving himself,  
for emptiness will be his reward.

**32** It will be paid in full before his day,  
and his branch will not be green.

**33** He will shake off his unripe grapes like the vine,  
and cast off his blossom like the olive tree.

**34** For the company of the godless is barren,  
and fire consumes the tents of bribery.

**35** They conceive trouble and give birth to iniquity,  
and their womb prepares deceit.

## Notes

- **v2 "the east wind"** — the hot desert wind, *qadim*; Job's words are hot air (8:2).
- **v4 "cutting short devotion before God"** — *sichah*, **meditation**, **complaint**: by his talk Job undermines the reverence that others owe to God.
- **v7 "Were you the first man to be born? Were you brought forth before the hills?"** — words of Wisdom in Proverbs 8:25, *before the hills, I was brought forth*. Eliphaz mocks Job as though he claimed to be older than creation.
- **v8 "the council of God"** — *sod Eloah*, the heavenly council of 1:6, where *the LORD's secret counsel* is spoken (Jeremiah 23:18).
- **v11 "the comforts of God"** — Eliphaz counts his own speeches as God's comfort, *spoken gently*; compare 4:2.
- **vv14–16** — Eliphaz repeats the night voice of 4:17–19, now with Job's own words of 14:1, **born of a woman**. **Detestable** is *nit'av*, from the root of *to'evah*.
- **v19 "no stranger passed among them"** — the wisdom Eliphaz offers is old and pure, untouched by foreign teaching.
- **v24 "like a king ready for the attack"** — *kidor*, a word found only here, perhaps **onset**, **battle**.
- **v29 "his gains will not bend down to the earth"** — *minlam*, a word found only here; perhaps his crops or his possessions, too thin to bow under their own weight.
- **v33 "like the vine… like the olive tree"** — a vine dropping its grapes before they ripen, an olive shedding its blossom: a life that bears nothing.
