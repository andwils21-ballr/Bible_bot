# Job 39

## The Mountain Goats and the Wild Donkey

**1** Do you know when the mountain goats give birth?  
Do you watch over the does in labor?

**2** Can you count the months they fulfill,  
and do you know the time they give birth,

**3** when they crouch and bring forth their young  
and are delivered of their pains?

**4** Their young grow strong; they grow up in the open;  
they go out and do not come back to them.

**5** Who has let the wild donkey go free?  
Who has loosed the bonds of the onager,

**6** to whom I gave the desert for a home  
and the salt land for his dwelling?

**7** He laughs at the uproar of the city;  
he does not hear the shouts of the driver.

**8** He ranges the mountains as his pasture  
and searches after every green thing.

## The Wild Ox

**9** Is the wild ox willing to serve you?  
Will he spend the night at your manger?

**10** Can you bind the wild ox in the furrow with a rope,  
or will he harrow the valleys after you?

**11** Will you trust him because his strength is great,  
and leave your labor to him?

**12** Will you rely on him to bring back your grain  
and gather it to your threshing floor?

## The Ostrich

**13** The wings of the ostrich flap with joy,  
but are they the wings and plumage of the stork?

**14** For she leaves her eggs on the ground  
and lets them be warmed in the dust,

**15** forgetting that a foot may crush them  
and the wild beast trample them.

**16** She treats her young harshly, as if they were not hers;  
her labor is in vain, yet she has no fear,

**17** because God has made her forget wisdom  
and given her no share in understanding.

**18** When she rouses herself to run,  
she laughs at the horse and its rider.

## The Horse

**19** Do you give the horse his might?  
Do you clothe his neck with a mane?

**20** Do you make him leap like the locust?  
The majesty of his snorting is terrible.

**21** He paws in the valley and rejoices in his strength;  
he goes out to meet the weapons.

**22** He laughs at fear and is not dismayed;  
he does not turn back from the sword.

**23** On him the quiver rattles,  
the flashing spear and the javelin.

**24** With trembling and rage he swallows the ground;  
he cannot stand still at the sound of the horn.

**25** At every blast of the horn he says, "Aha!"  
He smells the battle from far off,  
the thunder of the captains, and the shouting.

## The Hawk and the Eagle

**26** Is it by your understanding that the hawk soars  
and spreads his wings toward the south?

**27** Is it at your command that the eagle mounts up  
and makes his nest on high?

**28** On the rock he dwells and spends the night,  
on the crag of the rock and the stronghold.

**29** From there he spies out his food;  
his eyes see it from far away.

**30** His young ones gulp down blood,  
and where the slain are, there he is.

## Notes

- **The animals** — God's questions turn to the wild creatures that live beyond man's reach and use: the goat and the doe, the wild donkey, the wild ox, the ostrich, the war horse, the hawk and the eagle. None of them serves man, and God cares for each.
- **v5 "the onager"** — *arod*, the swift wild ass of the Syrian desert, a second word for the wild donkey, *pere*, of the first line (6:5; 11:12; 24:5).
- **v9 "the wild ox"** — *re'em*, the great wild bull, the aurochs (Numbers 23:22; Deuteronomy 33:17); the old English *unicorn* came from the Greek *monokeros*.
- **v13 "the ostrich… the stork"** — *renanim*, **the cries**, a name for the ostrich found only here. *Chasidah*, **the stork**, is from *chesed*, **loyal love**: the stork was known for caring for its young, and the ostrich for leaving its eggs.
- **v17 "God has made her forget wisdom"** — even a creature without wisdom has its place; God made her as she is, and she outruns the horse (v18).
- **vv19–25 the war horse** — the one domestic animal in the list, but untamed in courage: *the horse is made ready for the day of battle* (Proverbs 21:31).
- **v24 "the horn"** — *shofar*, the ram's horn trumpet of war (Joshua 6:5; Amos 3:6).
- **v26 "toward the south"** — the hawk's migration south in autumn (Jeremiah 8:7).
- **v30 "where the slain are, there he is"** — *nesher*, the eagle or vulture; *Wherever the corpse is, there the vultures will gather* (Matthew 24:28).
