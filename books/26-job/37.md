# Job 37

## God Thunders

**1** At this also my heart trembles  
and leaps from its place.

**2** Listen, listen to the thunder of His voice  
and the rumbling that goes out from His mouth.

**3** Under the whole heaven He lets it loose,  
and His lightning to the ends of the earth.

**4** After it His voice roars;  
He thunders with His majestic voice,  
and He does not hold back the lightnings when His voice is heard.

**5** God thunders wondrously with His voice;  
He does great things that we cannot understand.

**6** For to the snow He says, "Fall on the earth,"  
and to the shower of rain, the showers of His strength.

**7** He seals up the hand of every man,  
so that all men whom He made may know it.

**8** Then the beasts go into their lairs  
and stay in their dens.

**9** From its chamber comes the storm,  
and the cold from the scattering winds.

**10** By the breath of God ice is given,  
and the broad waters are frozen hard.

**11** He loads the thick cloud with moisture;  
the cloud scatters His lightning.

**12** It turns round and round by His guidance,  
to do all that He commands them  
over the face of the inhabited earth.

**13** Whether for a rod, or for His land,  
or for loyal love, He makes it happen.

## Consider the Wonders of God

**14** Listen to this, Job;  
stand still and consider the wonders of God.

**15** Do you know how God lays His command on them  
and makes the lightning of His cloud shine?

**16** Do you know the balancing of the clouds,  
the wonders of the One perfect in knowledge,

**17** you whose garments are hot  
when the earth lies still under the south wind?

**18** Can you, like Him, spread out the skies,  
hard as a mirror of cast metal?

**19** Teach us what we should say to Him;  
we cannot set our case in order because of darkness.

**20** Shall it be told Him that I would speak?  
Did any man ever ask to be swallowed up?

**21** Now no one can look on the light  
when it is bright in the skies,  
when the wind has passed and cleared them.

**22** Out of the north comes golden splendor;  
around God is awesome majesty.

**23** The Almighty, we cannot find Him;  
He is great in power;  
in justice and abundant righteousness He does not oppress.

**24** Therefore men fear Him;  
He does not regard any who are wise in their own heart.

## Notes

- **v2 "the thunder of His voice"** — *the voice of the LORD is over the waters; the God of glory thunders* (Psalm 29:3). Elihu seems to describe a storm gathering as he speaks; from it God will answer (38:1).
- **v6 "to the snow He says, 'Fall on the earth'"** — *Have you entered the storehouses of the snow?* God asks in 38:22.
- **v7 "He seals up the hand of every man"** — in the winter storms men cannot work in the fields, and must recognize God's work.
- **v9 "From its chamber"** — *ha-cheder*, the chamber of the south (9:9). **The scattering winds**, *mezarim*, bring the cold from the north.
- **v13 "for a rod, or for His land, or for loyal love"** — the rain may come as punishment, for the need of the land, or as *chesed*, **loyal love**.
- **vv14–18** — Elihu's questions anticipate God's: *Do you know…? Can you…?* (38:4–39:30).
- **v17 "the south wind"** — the hot wind from the desert, when the air is still and heavy (Luke 12:55).
- **v18 "hard as a mirror of cast metal"** — ancient mirrors were polished bronze; the sky as a solid dome (Genesis 1:6–8).
- **v22 "Out of the north comes golden splendor"** — *zahav*, **gold**, the brightness of the sky after the storm, or the glory of God coming from the north, His mountain (26:7; Ezekiel 1:4).
- **v24 "any who are wise in their own heart"** — Elihu ends with the fear of God, as the poem on wisdom did (28:28).
