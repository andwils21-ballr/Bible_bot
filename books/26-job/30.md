# Job 30

## Mocked by the Outcasts

**1** But now they laugh at me,  
men younger than I,  
whose fathers I would have disdained  
to set with the dogs of my flock.

**2** What use to me was the strength of their hands,  
men whose vigor was gone?

**3** Gaunt with want and hunger,  
they gnaw the dry ground,  
yesterday's waste and desolation;

**4** they pick saltwort among the bushes,  
and the roots of the broom tree are their food.

**5** They are driven out from among men;  
people shout after them as after a thief.

**6** In the gullies of the wadis they must live,  
in holes in the ground and in the rocks.

**7** Among the bushes they bray;  
under the nettles they huddle together.

**8** A senseless brood, a nameless brood,  
they were whipped out of the land.

**9** Now I have become their song;  
I am a byword to them.

**10** They abhor me; they keep far from me;  
they do not hold back from spitting in my face.

**11** Because He has loosed my cord and humbled me,  
they have cast off restraint before me.

**12** On my right the rabble rise;  
they push my feet away;  
they build up against me their ways of ruin.

**13** They break up my path;  
they help on my disaster;  
no one restrains them.

**14** As through a wide breach they come;  
among the crash they roll on.

**15** Terrors are turned on me;  
my honor is chased away as by the wind,  
and my safety has passed away like a cloud.

## You Have Turned Cruel to Me

**16** Now my soul is poured out within me;  
days of affliction have taken hold of me.

**17** At night He pierces my bones,  
and the pains that gnaw me take no rest.

**18** With great force my garment is disfigured;  
it binds me like the collar of my tunic.

**19** He has thrown me into the mud,  
and I have become like dust and ashes.

**20** I cry to You for help, and You do not answer me;  
I stand, and You only look at me.

**21** You have turned cruel to me;  
with the might of Your hand You attack me.

**22** You lift me up on the wind; You make me ride on it,  
and You toss me about in the roar of the storm.

**23** For I know that You will bring me to death,  
to the house appointed for all the living.

**24** Yet does not one in ruins stretch out his hand,  
and cry for help in his disaster?

**25** Did I not weep for the one whose day was hard?  
Was not my soul grieved for the needy?

**26** Yet when I hoped for good, evil came,  
and when I waited for light, darkness came.

**27** My insides are in turmoil and never still;  
days of affliction come to meet me.

**28** I go about darkened, but not by the sun;  
I stand up in the assembly and cry for help.

**29** I am a brother of jackals  
and a companion of ostriches.

**30** My skin turns black and peels from me,  
and my bones burn with heat.

**31** My lyre is turned to mourning,  
and my pipe to the voice of those who weep.

## Notes

- **v1 "But now"** — *ve-attah*, three times in the chapter (vv1, 9, 16), each time against the *then* of chapter 29: the elders who stood for him (29:8) are replaced by the young who laugh at him.
- **vv2–8 the outcasts** — men driven out of society, living like the wild donkeys of 24:5. **Saltwort** (*malluach*) and **broom roots** are the food of starvation. **A nameless brood**, *benei veli shem*, **sons of no name**: men of no family or standing.
- **v11 "He has loosed my cord"** — the written text has **His cord**, the read form, followed here, **my cord**: the string of Job's bow (29:20), or the cord of his tent (4:21).
- **v17 "At night He pierces my bones"** — the Hebrew has only **it pierces**; the subject may be the night itself, or God, or the disease.
- **v18 "my garment is disfigured"** — the Hebrew is hard; the Greek reads **He seized my garment**. The picture is of God seizing Job by the collar.
- **v19 "dust and ashes"** — *afar va-efer*, Abraham's words for himself before God (Genesis 18:27); Job will use them again in 42:6.
- **v22 "the roar of the storm"** — the read form *teshuwwah*, **crash**; the written form is *tushiyyah*, **success**, a different word.
- **v23 "the house appointed for all the living"** — Sheol, *the place where all the living must go* (Ecclesiastes 12:5).
- **v28 "darkened, but not by the sun"** — *qoder*, **dark**, the black clothing of a mourner, or his skin blackened by disease (v30), not by sunburn.
- **v29 "a brother of jackals and a companion of ostriches"** — animals of the desert ruins, known for their mournful cries (Micah 1:8).
