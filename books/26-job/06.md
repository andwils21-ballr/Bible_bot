# Job 6

**1** Job answered and said:

## Heavier than the Sand

**2** Oh that my vexation were weighed,  
and my calamity laid with it in the scales!

**3** For now it would be heavier than the sand of the seas;  
that is why my words have been rash.

**4** For the arrows of the Almighty are in me;  
my spirit drinks their poison;  
the terrors of God are arrayed against me.

**5** Does the wild donkey bray over its grass,  
or the ox low over its fodder?

**6** Can what is tasteless be eaten without salt,  
or is there any taste in the slime of the mallow?

**7** My soul refuses to touch them;  
they are like sickness in my food.

**8** Oh that my request would come,  
and that God would grant my hope,

**9** that God would be pleased to crush me,  
that He would let loose His hand and cut me off!

**10** Then I would still have this comfort,  
and would leap for joy in pain that does not spare,  
for I have not denied the words of the Holy One.

**11** What is my strength, that I should wait?  
What is my end, that I should be patient?

**12** Is my strength the strength of stones,  
or is my flesh bronze?

**13** Is it not that there is no help in me,  
and success has been driven from me?

## Treacherous Friends

**14** The one who is fainting should have kindness from his friend,  
even if he forsakes the fear of the Almighty.

**15** My brothers have been treacherous like a wadi,  
like the channels of wadis that pass away,

**16** which are dark with ice,  
where the snow hides itself.

**17** When they are scorched, they vanish;  
when it is hot, they are gone from their place.

**18** The caravans turn aside from their course;  
they go up into the waste and perish.

**19** The caravans of Tema look,  
the travelers of Sheba hope for them.

**20** They are ashamed because they trusted;  
they come there and are put to shame.

**21** For now you have become like that;  
you see a terror and are afraid.

**22** Have I said, "Give me something,"  
or, "Offer a bribe for me from your wealth,"

**23** or, "Deliver me from the hand of the enemy,"  
or, "Redeem me from the hand of the ruthless"?

**24** Teach me, and I will be silent;  
make me understand how I have gone astray.

**25** How forceful are upright words!  
But what does your reproof prove?

**26** Do you mean to reprove words,  
when the speech of a despairing man is wind?

**27** You would even cast lots over the fatherless,  
and bargain over your friend.

**28** But now be pleased to look at me;  
I will not lie to your face.

**29** Turn back, please; let there be no injustice;  
turn back, my righteousness is still at stake.

**30** Is there injustice on my tongue?  
Cannot my palate tell what is ruinous?

## Notes

- **v21 "you have become like that"** — the written text has *lo*, **not**: "you have become nothing"; the read form, followed here, has *lo*, **to it**: "you have become such," like the dry wadi.
