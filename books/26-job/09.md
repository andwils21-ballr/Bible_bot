# Job 9

**1** Job answered and said:

## The Power of God

**2** Truly I know that it is so;  
but how can a man be in the right with God?

**3** If one wished to contend with Him,  
he could not answer Him once in a thousand.

**4** He is wise in heart and mighty in strength;  
who has hardened himself against Him and come out whole?

**5** He moves mountains, and they do not know it,  
when He overturns them in His anger.

**6** He shakes the earth out of its place,  
and its pillars tremble.

**7** He speaks to the sun, and it does not rise;  
He seals up the stars.

**8** He alone stretches out the heavens  
and treads on the back of the sea.

**9** He made the Bear and Orion,  
the Pleiades and the chambers of the south.

**10** He does great things beyond searching out,  
wonders without number.

**11** If He passes by me, I do not see Him;  
He moves on, and I do not perceive Him.

**12** If He snatches away, who can turn Him back?  
Who will say to Him, "What are You doing?"

**13** God does not turn back His anger;  
beneath Him the helpers of Rahab bowed down.

## No Arbiter Between Us

**14** How then can I answer Him,  
choosing my words with Him?

**15** Though I am in the right, I cannot answer;  
I must plead for mercy with my judge.

**16** If I called and He answered me,  
I would not believe that He was listening to my voice.

**17** For He crushes me with a tempest  
and multiplies my wounds without cause.

**18** He will not let me get my breath,  
but fills me with bitter things.

**19** If it is a matter of strength, He is the mighty one!  
If it is a matter of justice, who will summon me?

**20** Though I am in the right, my own mouth would condemn me;  
though I am blameless, He would prove me crooked.

**21** I am blameless; I do not care about myself;  
I despise my life.

**22** It is all one; therefore I say,  
He destroys the blameless and the wicked.

**23** When the whip kills suddenly,  
He mocks at the despair of the innocent.

**24** The earth is given into the hand of the wicked;  
He covers the faces of its judges.  
If not He, then who is it?

**25** My days are swifter than a runner;  
they flee away; they see no good.

**26** They glide past like ships of reed,  
like an eagle swooping on its prey.

**27** If I say, "I will forget my complaint,  
I will put off my sad face and be cheerful,"

**28** I dread all my pains,  
for I know You will not hold me innocent.

**29** I am to be condemned;  
why then do I labor in vain?

**30** If I wash myself with snow  
and cleanse my hands with lye,

**31** even then You will plunge me into a pit,  
and my own clothes will abhor me.

**32** For He is not a man, as I am, that I might answer Him,  
that we should come to trial together.

**33** There is no arbiter between us,  
who might lay his hand on us both.

**34** Let Him take His rod away from me,  
and let not dread of Him terrify me.

**35** Then I would speak without fear of Him,  
for I am not so in myself.

## Notes

- **v2 "how can a man be in the right with God?"** — Job takes up the question of the night voice (4:17), but as a courtroom question: *tsadaq*, **be in the right**, **win the case**. The words of the law court run through the chapter: contend (v3), answer (vv14–16), judge (v15), summon (v19), condemn (v20), arbiter (v33).
- **v8 "treads on the back of the sea"** — *bamotei yam*, **the high places of the sea**, as one treads on a defeated enemy; *He walked on the sea* (Mark 6:48–49).
- **v9 "the Bear and Orion, the Pleiades"** — *Ash*, *Kesil* and *Kimah*, star groups named again in 38:31–32 and Amos 5:8; the identifications are traditional. **The chambers of the south** are the stars of the southern sky.
- **v10** — Job repeats Eliphaz's words of 5:9 almost exactly, but where Eliphaz praised God's power to help, Job fears it.
- **v13 "the helpers of Rahab"** — Rahab is the sea monster of chaos (26:12; Isaiah 51:9), not the Rahab of Jericho, who is spelled differently in Hebrew.
- **v17 "with a tempest"** — *bi-se'arah*; with other vowels, *bi-sa'arah*, **for a hair**, for a trifle. **Without cause** is *chinnam*, as in 1:9 and 2:3, though Job does not know it.
- **v19 "who will summon me?"** — the Hebrew has **me**; the Greek reads the line of God, *who will stand against His judgment?* Job may be quoting God's answer: *who will summon Me?*
- **v26 "ships of reed"** — light papyrus boats of the Nile (Isaiah 18:2).
- **v31 "into a pit"** — *shachat*, a muddy pit; even after washing in snow and lye he would come out filthier than his clothes could bear.
- **v33 "There is no arbiter between us"** — *mokhiach*, one who decides between two parties and can lay a hand on both; the Greek reads *Would that there were an arbiter between us*. Job's longing for a go-between returns in 16:19–21 and 19:25.
