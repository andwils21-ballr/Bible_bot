# Job 28

## The Miner Searches Out the Earth

**1** Surely there is a mine for silver,  
and a place for gold that they refine.

**2** Iron is taken from the earth,  
and copper is smelted from the stone.

**3** Man puts an end to darkness  
and searches out to the farthest limit  
the stone of gloom and deep shadow.

**4** He breaks open a shaft far from where people live,  
forgotten by every foot;  
they hang far from mankind and swing to and fro.

**5** The earth, out of it comes bread,  
but beneath, it is turned over as by fire.

**6** Its stones are the place of sapphire,  
and it holds the dust of gold.

**7** That path no bird of prey knows,  
and the falcon's eye has not seen it.

**8** The proud beasts have not trodden it;  
the lion has not passed over it.

**9** Man puts his hand to the flint  
and overturns mountains by the roots.

**10** He cuts out channels in the rocks,  
and his eye sees every precious thing.

**11** He dams up the streams so that they do not trickle,  
and what is hidden he brings out to light.

## Wisdom Cannot Be Bought

**12** But where shall wisdom be found,  
and where is the place of understanding?

**13** Man does not know its worth,  
and it is not found in the land of the living.

**14** The deep says, "It is not in me,"  
and the sea says, "It is not with me."

**15** It cannot be bought for gold,  
and silver cannot be weighed out as its price.

**16** It cannot be valued in the gold of Ophir,  
in precious onyx or sapphire.

**17** Gold and glass cannot equal it,  
nor can it be exchanged for vessels of fine gold.

**18** No mention will be made of coral or of crystal;  
the price of wisdom is above pearls.

**19** The topaz of Cush cannot equal it,  
nor can it be valued in pure gold.

## God Knows Its Place

**20** From where, then, does wisdom come,  
and where is the place of understanding?

**21** It is hidden from the eyes of all living  
and concealed from the birds of the heavens.

**22** Abaddon and Death say,  
"We have heard a rumor of it with our ears."

**23** God understands the way to it,  
and He knows its place.

**24** For He looks to the ends of the earth  
and sees everything under the heavens.

**25** When He gave the wind its weight  
and measured out the waters by measure,

**26** when He made a decree for the rain  
and a way for the thunderbolt,

**27** then He saw it and declared it;  
He established it, and searched it out.

**28** He said to man,  
"The fear of the Lord, that is wisdom,  
and to turn away from evil is understanding."

## Notes

- **The poem** — chapter 28 stands apart from the debate, a hymn on wisdom with a refrain (vv12, 20). It answers the whole argument so far: neither Job nor the friends have found wisdom by searching, for it is God's alone.
- **vv1–11 the miner** — man digs where no bird or beast has gone, into the dark under the mountains, and brings out silver, gold, iron, copper and precious stones. **They hang… and swing to and fro** (v4): miners let down on ropes into the shaft.
- **v6 "sapphire"** — *sappir*, probably lapis lazuli, the blue stone flecked with gold, which suits **the dust of gold** in the same verse.
- **v13 "Man does not know its worth"** — *erkah*, **its value**; the Greek reads *darkah*, **its way**, as in v23.
- **vv15–19** — every precious thing the miner digs out is named and found wanting: gold of Ophir, onyx, sapphire, glass (costly in the ancient world), coral, crystal, pearls, the topaz of Cush. *Wisdom is better than jewels* (Proverbs 8:11).
- **v22 "Abaddon and Death"** — even the world of the dead has only **heard a rumor** of wisdom (26:6).
- **vv25–27** — God fixed wisdom when He set the weight of the wind and the measure of the waters, at creation; *The LORD by wisdom founded the earth* (Proverbs 3:19; 8:22–31).
- **v28 "The fear of the Lord, that is wisdom"** — *yir'at Adonai*, the only place in Job where God is called *Adonai*, **the Lord**. *The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom* (Proverbs 9:10; Psalm 111:10). **To turn away from evil** is the word of 1:1 for Job himself: the man the poem describes is Job.
