# Job 35

**1** Elihu answered and said:

**2** Do you think this is just?  
You say, "My right is from God,"

**3** that you ask, "What use is it to you?  
What do I gain more than if I had sinned?"

**4** I will answer you,  
and your friends with you.

## Your Sin Cannot Touch Him

**5** Look at the heavens and see;  
gaze at the clouds, which are higher than you.

**6** If you have sinned, what do you do against Him?  
If your transgressions are many, what do you do to Him?

**7** If you are righteous, what do you give to Him,  
or what does He receive from your hand?

**8** Your wickedness touches a man like yourself,  
and your righteousness a son of man.

## Where Is God My Maker?

**9** Because of the many oppressions people cry out;  
they call for help because of the arm of the mighty.

**10** But no one says, "Where is God my Maker,  
who gives songs in the night,

**11** who teaches us more than the beasts of the earth  
and makes us wiser than the birds of the heavens?"

**12** There they cry out, but He does not answer,  
because of the pride of evil men.

**13** Surely God does not hear an empty cry,  
and the Almighty does not regard it.

**14** How much less when you say that you do not see Him,  
that the case is before Him, and you are waiting for Him!

**15** Now, because His anger does not punish,  
and He does not take much note of folly,

**16** Job opens his mouth in empty breath;  
he multiplies words without knowledge.

## Notes

- **v2 "My right is from God"** — *tsidqi me-El*, which can also mean **my righteousness is more than God's**. Elihu sees Job's claim as setting himself above God (32:2).
- **v3 "What do I gain more than if I had sinned?"** — Elihu's summary of Job's complaint that the righteous and the wicked fare alike (9:22; 21:7–15).
- **vv5–8 "Look at the heavens"** — Eliphaz's argument again (22:2–3, 12): God is too high to be harmed by sin or helped by righteousness; sin and righteousness touch other people.
- **v10 "who gives songs in the night"** — God's comfort in the dark; *at night His song is with me* (Psalm 42:8). The oppressed cry for relief but do not seek God Himself.
- **v11 "who teaches us more than the beasts"** — or **teaches us by the beasts**, as Job said in 12:7. **Birds of the heavens** is the same phrase.
- **v13 "an empty cry"** — *shav*, **emptiness**, a cry that seeks only relief, not God.
- **v14 "the case is before Him, and you are waiting for Him"** — Job's demand that God hear his case (13:18; 23:4); Elihu calls it impatience.
- **v15 "folly"** — *pash*, a word found only here; perhaps **transgression**, as the Greek reads it.
- **v16 "words without knowledge"** — the charge God will make in 38:2, *Who is this that darkens counsel by words without knowledge?*; there it is said of Job, or, as some read it, of Elihu.
