# Job 1

## The Man from Uz

**1** There was a man in the land of Uz whose name was Job. That man was blameless and upright; he feared God and turned away from evil.

**2** Seven sons and three daughters were born to him.

**3** His livestock was seven thousand sheep, three thousand camels, five hundred yoke of oxen and five hundred female donkeys, with a great many servants; that man was the greatest of all the people of the east.

**4** His sons would go and hold a feast, in the house of each one on his day, and they would send and invite their three sisters to eat and drink with them.

**5** When the days of the feast had gone their round, Job would send and consecrate them. He would rise early in the morning and offer burnt offerings by the number of them all, for Job said, "Perhaps my sons have sinned and cursed God in their hearts." This is what Job did all the days.

## The First Test

**6** And it came to pass one day that the sons of God came to present themselves before the LORD, and Satan also came among them.

**7** The LORD said to Satan, "Where have you come from?" Satan answered the LORD and said, "From roaming over the earth and from walking about on it."

**8** The LORD said to Satan, "Have you set your heart on My servant Job? For there is no one like him on the earth, a man blameless and upright, who fears God and turns away from evil."

**9** Satan answered the LORD and said, "Is it for nothing that Job fears God?

**10** Have You not put a hedge around him and around his house and around all that he has, on every side? You have blessed the work of his hands, and his livestock has spread out over the land.

**11** But stretch out Your hand now and touch all that he has, and he will surely curse You to Your face."

**12** The LORD said to Satan, "All that he has is in your hand; only do not stretch out your hand against him." Satan went out from the presence of the LORD.

**13** And it came to pass one day, as his sons and his daughters were eating and drinking wine in the house of their brother the firstborn,

**14** that a messenger came to Job and said, "The oxen were plowing and the donkeys grazing beside them,

**15** and Sheba fell on them and took them, and they struck the young men with the edge of the sword. I alone have escaped to tell you."

**16** While he was still speaking, another came and said, "The fire of God fell from heaven and burned up the sheep and the young men and consumed them. I alone have escaped to tell you."

**17** While he was still speaking, another came and said, "The Chaldeans formed three bands, raided the camels and took them, and they struck the young men with the edge of the sword. I alone have escaped to tell you."

**18** While he was still speaking, another came and said, "Your sons and your daughters were eating and drinking wine in the house of their brother the firstborn,

**19** and suddenly a great wind came from across the wilderness and struck the four corners of the house. It fell on the young people, and they are dead. I alone have escaped to tell you."

## Job's Answer

**20** Job rose, tore his robe and shaved his head; he fell to the ground and bowed down.

**21** He said,  
"Naked I came out of my mother's womb,  
and naked I will return there.  
The LORD gave, and the LORD has taken;  
blessed be the name of the LORD."

**22** In all this Job did not sin, nor did he charge God with wrong.

## Notes

- **The text of Job** — rendered from the Hebrew. The book is a story in prose (chapters 1–2 and 42:7–17) around a long debate in verse. The Greek translation of Job is shorter than the Hebrew, by about a sixth; where it differs in a way that matters, the note says so.
- **v1 "the land of Uz"** — east of Israel, toward Edom (Lamentations 4:21). **Blameless**, *tam*, is whole, of one piece; God repeats all four of the narrator's words about Job in v8 and again in 2:3.
- **v5 and v11 "cursed God… curse You"** — the Hebrew says *berakhu*, **blessed**. The scribes would not write "curse God," and put "bless" in its place; the sense is plain from the story. The same word returns at 2:5 and 2:9, and stands in its plain sense, "blessed," at 1:10 and 1:21.
- **v6 "the sons of God"** — the members of God's heavenly court, as in 38:7. **Satan** is *ha-satan*, with the article, **the accuser** or **the adversary**: a title in this book, as in Zechariah 3:1–2, not yet a name.
- **v7 "roaming over the earth"** — *mi-shut*, the verb of the eyes of the LORD, *which range through the whole earth* (Zechariah 4:10; 2 Chronicles 16:9).
- **v9 "Is it for nothing that Job fears God?"** — *ha-chinnam*, **for nothing**, **without cause**: the question of the whole book. God answers with the same word in 2:3, *you incited Me against him to destroy him without cause*.
- **vv15–19** — the losses alternate between raiders and fire or wind from heaven: **Sheba**, from Arabia (6:19); **the fire of God**, lightning, as in 1 Kings 18:38 and 2 Kings 1:12; **the Chaldeans**, the people of Babylonia, here still raiders of the desert. *Ha-ne'arim* is **the young men**, the servants, in vv15–17; in v19 the same word means Job's own children, **the young people**.
- **v21 "Naked I came out of my mother's womb"** — *As he came from his mother's womb he shall go again, naked as he came* (Ecclesiastes 5:15); *we brought nothing into the world, and we cannot take anything out* (1 Timothy 6:7). **There** is the earth, the mother of all (Psalm 139:15; Sirach 40:1).
- **v22 "nor did he charge God with wrong"** — *tiflah*, **what is unseemly**, **folly**: he did not ascribe anything unfitting to God.
