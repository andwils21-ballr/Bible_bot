# Job 22

**1** Eliphaz the Temanite answered and said:

## Is Not Your Evil Great?

**2** Can a man be of use to God?  
Surely the wise man is of use to himself.

**3** Is it any pleasure to the Almighty if you are in the right,  
or gain to Him if you make your ways blameless?

**4** Is it for your fear of Him that He reproves you  
and enters into judgment with you?

**5** Is not your evil great?  
There is no end to your iniquities.

**6** For you took pledges from your brothers for nothing  
and stripped the naked of their clothing.

**7** You gave no water to the weary to drink,  
and you withheld bread from the hungry.

**8** The man with power, the land was his,  
and the favored man lived in it.

**9** You sent widows away empty,  
and the arms of the fatherless were crushed.

**10** Therefore snares are all around you,  
and sudden terror overwhelms you,

**11** or darkness, so that you cannot see,  
and a flood of water covers you.

## What Does God Know?

**12** Is not God high in the heavens?  
See the highest of the stars, how high they are!

**13** But you say, "What does God know?  
Can He judge through the thick darkness?

**14** Thick clouds hide Him, so that He does not see,  
and He walks on the circle of the heavens."

**15** Will you keep to the old way  
that wicked men have trodden?

**16** They were snatched away before their time;  
their foundation was poured away by a river.

**17** They said to God, "Depart from us,"  
and, "What can the Almighty do to us?"

**18** Yet He filled their houses with good things;  
the counsel of the wicked is far from me.

**19** The righteous see it and are glad;  
the innocent laugh them to scorn:

**20** "Surely our adversaries are cut off,  
and what they left the fire has consumed."

## Return to the Almighty

**21** Come to terms with Him, and be at peace;  
by this, good will come to you.

**22** Receive instruction from His mouth,  
and lay up His words in your heart.

**23** If you return to the Almighty, you will be built up;  
if you remove injustice far from your tents,

**24** if you lay your gold in the dust,  
and the gold of Ophir among the stones of the stream bed,

**25** then the Almighty will be your gold  
and your silver in heaps.

**26** For then you will delight yourself in the Almighty  
and lift up your face to God.

**27** You will pray to Him, and He will hear you,  
and you will pay your vows.

**28** You will decide on a matter, and it will be established for you,  
and light will shine on your ways.

**29** When they are brought low, you will say, "Lift them up!"  
and He will save the one whose eyes are lowered.

**30** He will deliver even one who is not innocent,  
who will be delivered through the cleanness of your hands.

## Notes

- **vv2–3 "Can a man be of use to God?"** — Eliphaz's view: God gains nothing from a man's goodness, so He has no reason to test a righteous man. The prologue (1:8; 2:3) shows that God delights in Job.
- **v4 "Is it for your fear of Him?"** — the irony is complete: the reader knows that it is precisely for his fear of God that Job is being tested.
- **v6 "pledges… for nothing"** — taking a man's cloak as a pledge for a loan and keeping it overnight, which the law forbade (Exodus 22:26–27; Deuteronomy 24:10–13). **For nothing** is *chinnam* again (1:9; 2:3; 9:17). Eliphaz invents these sins; Job answers each in chapters 29 and 31.
- **v8 "the man with power, the land was his"** — the line seems to describe Job as the powerful man, *the man of arm*, who held the land for himself and his favorites.
- **vv13–14 "What does God know?"** — Eliphaz puts in Job's mouth the words of the wicked (Psalm 73:11; Isaiah 29:15). **The circle of the heavens** is the vault over the earth (Isaiah 40:22; Proverbs 8:27).
- **v16 "poured away by a river"** — the flood of Noah's day, or any sudden flood that washes away a house built on sand (Matthew 7:26–27).
- **v18** — Eliphaz repeats Job's words of 21:16 word for word, *the counsel of the wicked is far from me*, perhaps in mockery.
- **v24 "the gold of Ophir"** — the finest gold, from a land in Arabia or East Africa (1 Kings 9:28; Psalm 45:9). Job is to throw away his treasure; the Almighty will be his gold.
- **v29** — the Hebrew is hard. *Gevah*, read here as **lift up**, may also mean **pride**: *when they are brought low, you will say, "It was pride."*
- **v30 "He will deliver even one who is not innocent… through the cleanness of your hands"** — *i-naqi*, **not innocent**. Without knowing it, Eliphaz foretells his own case: in 42:7–9 God forgives the three friends through Job's prayer.
