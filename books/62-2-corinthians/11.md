# 2 Corinthians 11

## Jealous for You

**1** I wish you would put up with a little foolishness from me. Do put up with me!

**2** For I am jealous for you with the jealousy of God, since I betrothed you to one husband, to present you as a pure virgin to Christ.

**3** But I am afraid that, as the serpent deceived Eve by his cunning, your minds may be corrupted and led away from a sincere and pure devotion to Christ.

**4** For if someone comes and proclaims another Jesus than the one we proclaimed, or you receive a different spirit from the one you received, or a different gospel from the one you accepted, you put up with it readily enough.

**5** For I reckon that I am not in the least inferior to these super-apostles.

**6** Even if I am untrained in speaking, I am not untrained in knowledge; in every way we have made this plain to you in all things.

## False Apostles

**7** Did I commit a sin in humbling myself so that you might be exalted, because I preached the gospel of God to you free of charge?

**8** I robbed other churches by taking wages from them to serve you.

**9** When I was with you and was in need, I did not burden anyone, for the brothers who came from Macedonia supplied what I lacked. In every way I kept myself from being a burden to you, and I will go on doing so.

**10** As the truth of Christ is in me, this boast of mine will not be silenced in the regions of Achaia.

**11** Why? Because I do not love you? God knows that I do!

**12** What I am doing I will go on doing, to cut off the opportunity from those who want an opportunity to be found equal to us in what they boast about.

**13** For such men are false apostles, deceitful workers, disguising themselves as apostles of Christ.

**14** No wonder, for Satan himself disguises himself as an angel of light.

**15** So it is no great thing if his ministers also disguise themselves as ministers of righteousness. Their end will be what their deeds deserve.

## Boasting as a Fool

**16** I say again, let no one think me a fool. But if you do, then receive me even as a fool, so that I too may boast a little.

**17** What I am saying I am not saying as the Lord would, but as in foolishness, in this confident boasting.

**18** Since many boast according to the flesh, I too will boast.

**19** For you gladly put up with fools, being wise yourselves!

**20** For you put up with it if someone makes slaves of you, if someone devours you, if someone takes advantage of you, if someone exalts himself, if someone strikes you in the face.

**21** To my shame I must say that we were too weak for that! But whatever anyone dares to boast of — I am speaking in foolishness — I also dare to boast of.

**22** Are they Hebrews? So am I. Are they Israelites? So am I. Are they the seed of Abraham? So am I.

**23** Are they ministers of Christ? I am talking like a madman: I am more. I have had far more labors, far more imprisonments, beatings beyond measure, and often been near death.

**24** Five times I received from the Jews the forty lashes less one.

**25** Three times I was beaten with rods; once I was stoned; three times I was shipwrecked; a night and a day I spent in the open sea;

**26** on frequent journeys, in danger from rivers, danger from robbers, danger from my own people, danger from the Gentiles, danger in the city, danger in the wilderness, danger at sea, danger among false brothers;

**27** in labor and hardship, through many sleepless nights, in hunger and thirst, often fasting, in cold and nakedness.

**28** Besides everything else, there is the pressure on me every day of my anxiety for all the churches.

**29** Who is weak, and I am not weak? Who is made to stumble, and I do not burn?

**30** If I must boast, I will boast of the things that show my weakness.

**31** The God and Father of the Lord Jesus, He who is blessed forever, knows that I am not lying.

**32** In Damascus, the governor under King Aretas was guarding the city of the Damascenes to seize me,

**33** but I was let down in a basket through a window in the wall, and escaped his hands.

## Notes

- **vv2–3 "I am jealous for you with the jealousy of God… I betrothed you to one husband… as the serpent deceived Eve"** — the jealousy is a husband's. In the Law it is God's own: *the LORD, whose name is Jealous, is a jealous God*, said against going after other gods (Exodus 34:14). Paul, who arranged the marriage, guards the bride until she is presented to Christ. The danger he names is the first wife's, *The serpent deceived me* (Genesis 3:13), and his verb, *exēpatēsen*, is the Greek's *ēpatēsen* made stronger.
