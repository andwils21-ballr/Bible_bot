# 2 Corinthians 7

**1** Since we have these promises, beloved, let us cleanse ourselves from every defilement of body and spirit, bringing holiness to completion in the fear of God.

## Paul's Joy

**2** Make room for us in your hearts. We have wronged no one, we have ruined no one, we have taken advantage of no one.

**3** I do not say this to condemn you, for I said before that you are in our hearts, to die together and to live together.

**4** I speak to you with great boldness; I have great pride in you; I am filled with comfort; in all our trouble I am overflowing with joy.

**5** For even when we arrived in Macedonia, our bodies had no rest; we were troubled at every turn, fighting without and fears within.

**6** But God, who comforts the downcast, comforted us by the coming of Titus,

**7** and not only by his coming, but also by the comfort with which he was comforted by you, as he told us of your longing, your mourning, your zeal for me, so that I rejoiced still more.

## Sorrow That Leads to Repentance

**8** For even if I made you sorrowful with my letter, I do not regret it. Though I did regret it, for I see that the letter made you sorrowful, though only for a while,

**9** now I rejoice, not because you were made sorrowful, but because your sorrow led you to repentance. For you felt sorrow as God willed, so that you suffered no loss through us.

**10** For sorrow as God wills produces a repentance that leads to salvation and brings no regret, but the sorrow of the world produces death.

**11** See what earnestness this very thing, sorrow as God wills, has produced in you: what eagerness to clear yourselves, what indignation, what alarm, what longing, what zeal, what readiness to see justice done! In every way you have shown yourselves innocent in the matter.

**12** So although I wrote to you, it was not for the sake of the one who did the wrong, nor for the sake of the one who was wronged, but so that your earnestness for us might be made plain to you in the sight of God.

**13** For this reason we are comforted. Besides our own comfort, we rejoiced still more at the joy of Titus, because his spirit has been refreshed by all of you.

**14** For if I have boasted to him about you, I was not put to shame; but just as everything we said to you was true, so our boasting before Titus has proved true as well.

**15** His affection for you is all the greater as he remembers the obedience of you all, how you received him with fear and trembling.

**16** I rejoice that I have complete confidence in you.

## Notes

- **v6 "God, who comforts the downcast"** — Isaiah 49:13 in the Greek, *the LORD… has comforted the downcast of His people*, *tous tapeinous… parekalesen*, where the Hebrew has *will have compassion on His afflicted*. The comfort of chapter 1 comes back in a person: the coming of Titus.
- **vv8–10 "I do not regret it… a repentance that leads to salvation and brings no regret"** — two words. *Metamelomai*, **regret**, is the word of Judas, who *changed his mind* and brought back the silver (Matthew 27:3): sorrow over what has happened. *Metanoia*, **repentance**, is a change of mind and direction: sorrow that turns. Paul regretted the letter for a while; the repentance it produced is *ametamelētos*, a thing no one will ever regret.
