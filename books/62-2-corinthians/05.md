# 2 Corinthians 5

## The Earthly Tent

**1** For we know that if the earthly tent we live in is taken down, we have a building from God, a house not made with hands, eternal in the heavens.

**2** In this tent we groan, longing to put on our dwelling from heaven,

**3** if indeed, once we have put it on, we will not be found naked.

**4** For while we are still in this tent, we groan, weighed down, because we do not want to be unclothed but to be clothed over, so that what is mortal may be swallowed up by life.

**5** It is God who has prepared us for this very thing, and who has given us the Spirit as a pledge.

**6** So we are always confident, knowing that while we are at home in the body we are away from the Lord,

**7** for we walk by faith, not by sight.

**8** We are confident, I say, and we would rather be away from the body and at home with the Lord.

**9** So whether we are at home or away, we make it our aim to please Him.

**10** For all of us must appear before the judgment seat of Christ, so that each one may receive what is due for what he has done in the body, whether good or bad.

## The Love of Christ Compels Us

**11** Knowing, then, the fear of the Lord, we persuade others. What we are is plain to God, and I hope it is plain to your consciences too.

**12** We are not commending ourselves to you again, but giving you a reason to boast about us, so that you can answer those who boast in appearances and not in the heart.

**13** For if we are out of our minds, it is for God; if we are in our right mind, it is for you.

**14** For the love of Christ compels us, because we are convinced of this: one has died for all; therefore all have died.

**15** He died for all, so that those who live might no longer live for themselves but for Him who for their sake died and was raised.

## A New Creation

**16** So from now on we regard no one by human standards. Even though we once regarded Christ by human standards, we do not regard Him that way any longer.

**17** So if anyone is in Christ, there is a new creation: the old things have passed away; new things have come.

**18** All this is from God, who reconciled us to Himself through Christ and gave us the ministry of reconciliation:

**19** that in Christ God was reconciling the world to Himself, not reckoning their trespasses against them, and entrusting to us the message of reconciliation.

**20** So we are ambassadors for Christ, as though God were making His appeal through us. We implore you on behalf of Christ: be reconciled to God.

**21** He made Him who knew no sin to be sin for us, so that in Him we might become the righteousness of God.

## Notes

- **vv1–4 "the earthly tent… In this tent we groan, weighed down"** — the words of the Wisdom of Solomon, *a perishable body weighs down the soul, and this earthly tent burdens the mind* (Wisdom 9:15): *skēnos*, **tent**, is Wisdom's word, and Paul's *baroumenoi*, **weighed down**, is close kin to Wisdom's *barynei*. Wisdom left the soul burdened; Paul longs not to be *unclothed* but *clothed over*, *so that what is mortal may be swallowed up by life*, the word of 1 Corinthians 15:54.
- **v7 "we walk by faith, not by sight"** — *ou dia eidous*, **not by form**, the word God used of Moses: *Mouth to mouth I speak with him, plainly*, *en eidei* in the Greek, *and not in riddles* (Numbers 12:8). What Moses had, seeing God's form, believers do not yet have; they walk by faith until they see *face to face* (1 Corinthians 13:12).
- **v17 "there is a new creation: the old things have passed away; new things have come"** — *kainē ktisis* has no verb: **there is a new creation**, the world made new, or **he is a new creation**, the believer made new. Paul's other use is the world's: *neither circumcision counts for anything nor uncircumcision, but a new creation* (Galatians 6:15). The words are Isaiah's in the Greek, *Do not remember… the things of old* (*ta archaia*)… *I am about to do a new thing* (*kaina*) (Isaiah 43:18–19).
- **v21 "He made Him who knew no sin to be sin for us"** — *hamartian*, **sin**. In Hebrew one word, *chatat*, is both **sin** and **sin offering**: the anointed priest brings *for the sin he has committed… a young bull… as a sin offering*, *chatato… le-chatat*, the same word twice (Leviticus 4:3). So *to be sin* can mean to bear sin itself, or **to be a sin offering**, as Romans 8:3 has *as an offering for sin*. Christ takes the place of sin, and those in Him receive the place of *the righteousness of God*.
