# 2 Corinthians 1

## Greeting

**1** Paul, an apostle of Christ Jesus by the will of God, and our brother Timothy, to the church of God that is in Corinth, with all the saints throughout Achaia:

**2** grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

## The God of All Comfort

**3** Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of compassion and God of all comfort,

**4** who comforts us in all our trouble, so that we can comfort those who are in any trouble with the comfort with which we ourselves are comforted by God.

**5** For as the sufferings of Christ overflow to us, so through Christ our comfort overflows too.

**6** If we are troubled, it is for your comfort and salvation; if we are comforted, it is for your comfort, which works in you when you patiently endure the same sufferings that we suffer.

**7** Our hope for you is firm, for we know that as you share in our sufferings, so you also share in our comfort.

**8** We do not want you to be unaware, brothers, of the trouble we went through in Asia. We were so utterly weighed down, beyond our strength, that we despaired even of life.

**9** Indeed, we felt within ourselves that we had received the sentence of death, so that we would not rely on ourselves but on God who raises the dead.

**10** He rescued us from so deadly a peril, and He will rescue us; on Him we have set our hope that He will rescue us again,

**11** as you also help us by your prayers, so that many will give thanks on our behalf for the gift granted us through the prayers of many.

## Yes in Christ

**12** For this is our boast, the testimony of our conscience: that we have conducted ourselves in the world, and above all toward you, with holiness and godly sincerity, not by earthly wisdom but by the grace of God.

**13** For we write you nothing other than what you read and understand, and I hope you will understand fully,

**14** as you have understood us in part, that we are your boast as you are ours on the day of our Lord Jesus.

**15** Sure of this, I wanted to come to you first, so that you might have a second grace.

**16** I meant to visit you on my way to Macedonia, and to come back to you from Macedonia and have you send me on my way to Judea.

**17** When I wanted to do this, did I make my plans lightly? Or do I make my plans by worldly standards, ready to say "Yes, yes" and "No, no" at once?

**18** As God is faithful, our word to you has not been "Yes" and "No."

**19** For the Son of God, Jesus Christ, whom we preached among you, Silvanus and Timothy and I, was not "Yes" and "No," but in Him it has always been "Yes."

**20** For all the promises of God find their "Yes" in Him. That is why it is through Him that we say our "Amen" to God, for His glory.

**21** It is God who makes us firm with you in Christ, and who has anointed us;

**22** He has also sealed us and given us the Spirit in our hearts as a pledge.

**23** I call on God as witness against my soul that it was to spare you that I did not come again to Corinth.

**24** Not that we lord it over your faith; we work with you for your joy, for you stand firm in your faith.

## Notes

- **vv3–7 "the God of all comfort, who comforts us… so that we can comfort"** — *paraklēsis* and *parakaleō*, **comfort**, ten times in five verses. It is Isaiah's word in the Greek, *Comfort, comfort My people* (Isaiah 40:1), and *I, I am He who comforts you* (51:12); and the word of the *Advocate*, *paraklētos*, whom Jesus promised (John 14:16). The comfort Paul receives is not for him to keep: it overflows to others as Christ's sufferings overflow to him.
- **vv19–20 "in Him it has always been 'Yes'… through Him that we say our 'Amen'"** — *Amen* is a Hebrew word, from the root of being firm and sure (the *trusted* of Genesis 15:6). Isaiah calls the LORD *Elohei amen*, *the God of truth*, word for word **the God of Amen** (Isaiah 65:16). Every promise God made is *Yes* in Christ, and the church answers it with *Amen*.
- **vv21–22 "makes us firm… in Christ, and who has anointed us… sealed us and given us the Spirit… as a pledge"** — *Christon… chrisas*: *Christ* means **the Anointed**, and God has *anointed* those who are in Him. *Arrabōn*, **pledge**, is a Hebrew word in Greek letters, the *pledge* Judah left with Tamar, *Your seal and your cord, and your staff* (Genesis 38:18), in the Greek *arrabōn*. The *seal* and the *pledge* there were the token that the full payment would come; here the Spirit is that token.
