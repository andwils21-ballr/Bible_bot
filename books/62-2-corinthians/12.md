# 2 Corinthians 12

## Caught Up into Paradise

**1** I must boast. It does no good, but I will go on to visions and revelations of the Lord.

**2** I know a man in Christ who fourteen years ago — whether in the body I do not know, or out of the body I do not know; God knows — was caught up to the third heaven.

**3** I know that this man — whether in the body or apart from the body I do not know; God knows —

**4** was caught up into paradise and heard words that cannot be spoken, which no human being is permitted to utter.

**5** About such a man I will boast, but about myself I will not boast, except of my weaknesses.

**6** Even if I should want to boast, I would not be a fool, for I would be speaking the truth. But I refrain, so that no one may reckon me higher than what he sees in me or hears from me,

**7** or because the revelations were so extraordinary. So, to keep me from being too elated, a thorn in the flesh was given to me, an angel of Satan to beat me, to keep me from being too elated.

**8** Three times I pleaded with the Lord about this, that it would leave me.

**9** But He said to me, "My grace is sufficient for you, for power is made perfect in weakness." So I will boast all the more gladly of my weaknesses, so that the power of Christ may pitch its tent over me.

**10** That is why I am content with weaknesses, with insults, hardships, persecutions and distresses, for Christ's sake. For when I am weak, then I am strong.

## Paul's Concern for the Corinthians

**11** I have become a fool! You forced me to it. I ought to have been commended by you. For I was not in the least inferior to these super-apostles, even though I am nothing.

**12** The signs of an apostle were done among you with all endurance, by signs and wonders and mighty works.

**13** For in what were you treated worse than the other churches, except that I myself did not burden you? Forgive me this wrong!

**14** This is the third time I am ready to come to you, and I will not be a burden, for I seek not what is yours but you. Children should not have to save up for their parents, but parents for their children.

**15** I will most gladly spend and be spent for your souls. If I love you more, am I to be loved less?

**16** Granted, I did not weigh you down myself. But cunning as I am, I caught you by deceit!

**17** Did I take advantage of you through any of those I sent to you?

**18** I urged Titus to go, and I sent the brother with him. Did Titus take advantage of you? Did we not walk by the same Spirit? Did we not take the same steps?

**19** Have you been thinking all along that we are defending ourselves to you? We speak in the sight of God, in Christ. Everything we do, beloved, is for building you up.

**20** For I am afraid that when I come I may find you not as I want you to be, and you may find me not as you want me to be; that there may be quarreling, jealousy, fits of anger, rivalries, slander, gossip, being puffed up, and disorder.

**21** I am afraid that when I come again my God may humble me before you, and I may mourn over many of those who sinned before and have not repented of the impurity, sexual immorality and sensuality they have practiced.

## Notes

- **v7 "a thorn in the flesh… an angel of Satan to beat me"** — *skolops*, a pointed stake or thorn. Paul does not say what it was. In the Greek Bible the word stands twice for enemies: the peoples left in the land, *barbs in your eyes*, *skolopes en tois ophthalmois* (Numbers 33:55), and Israel's scornful neighbors, *a pricking brier* (Ezekiel 28:24). Read as an illness, it fits Galatians 4:13, where Paul first preached *because of a weakness of the flesh*. *Kolaphizō*, **beat**, is the word for the blows at Jesus' trial, *struck Him with their fists* (Matthew 26:67).
- **v9 "so that the power of Christ may pitch its tent over me"** — *episkēnōsē*, from *skēnē*, **tent**, the word of John's *The Word became flesh and pitched His tent among us* (John 1:14). The body is the earthly tent in which we groan (5:1–4); over that weak tent the power of Christ pitches its own.
