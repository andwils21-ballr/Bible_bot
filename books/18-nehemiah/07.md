# Nehemiah 7

## The City Guarded

**1** And it came to pass, when the wall had been built and I had set up the doors, that the gatekeepers, the singers and the Levites were appointed.

**2** I put my brother Hanani, and Hananiah the commander of the citadel, in charge of Jerusalem, for he was a faithful man and feared God more than many.

**3** I said to them, "The gates of Jerusalem are not to be opened until the sun is hot, and while the guards are still standing there, let them shut the doors and bar them. Appoint guards from the inhabitants of Jerusalem, each at his post and each opposite his own house."

**4** The city was wide and large, but the people in it were few, and no houses had been built.

## The Record Found

**5** Then my God put it into my heart to gather the nobles, the officials and the people to be enrolled by genealogy. I found the book of the genealogy of those who came up at the first, and I found written in it:

**6** These are the people of the province who came up out of the captivity of the exiles, whom Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had carried away. They returned to Jerusalem and Judah, each to his own town.

**7** They came with Zerubbabel, Jeshua, Nehemiah, Azariah, Raamiah, Nahamani, Mordecai, Bilshan, Mispereth, Bigvai, Nehum and Baanah. The number of the men of the people of Israel:

**66** The whole assembly together was forty-two thousand three hundred and sixty,

**68** *(This verse is not in the Hebrew or the Greek of Nehemiah; see the note.)*

**69** Their camels were four hundred and thirty-five, and their donkeys six thousand seven hundred and twenty.

## Gifts for the Work

**70** Some of the heads of the fathers' houses gave to the work. The governor gave to the treasury a thousand darics of gold, fifty basins and five hundred and thirty priests' garments.

**71** Some of the heads of the fathers' houses gave to the treasury of the work twenty thousand darics of gold and two thousand two hundred minas of silver.

**72** What the rest of the people gave was twenty thousand darics of gold, two thousand minas of silver and sixty-seven priests' garments.

**73** So the priests, the Levites, the gatekeepers, the singers, some of the people, the temple servants and all Israel lived in their towns.

When the seventh month came, the sons of Israel were in their towns.

## Notes

- **vv1–73** — the Hebrew has 72 verses here. English v68 has no counterpart in the Hebrew, and vv69–73 are Hebrew vv68–72. The rendering follows the English numbering.
- **vv6–73 and Ezra 2:1–70** — the same list, copied from *the book of the genealogy of those who came up at the first* (v5). Many numbers differ, and some names: Ezra's *Seraiah, Reelaiah… Rehum* are here *Azariah, Raamiah… Nehum*, and Nehemiah names twelve leaders where Ezra names eleven. The listed numbers here add up to 31,089; Ezra's add up to 29,818; both give the same total, 42,360 (v66; Ezra 2:64).
- **v2 "for he was a faithful man and feared God more than many"** — *ki-ish emet*, **a man of truth**. Nehemiah's test for the man who holds the gates.
- **v3 "until the sun is hot"** — not at dawn: the gates stay shut until the day is well begun and the city is awake to guard them.
- **v4 "the people in it were few, and no houses had been built"** — the wall is done, but the city inside it is nearly empty. The list that follows is found to fill it (11:1–2).
- **v68** — English Bibles have a verse here, *Their horses were seven hundred and thirty-six, their mules two hundred and forty-five*, the same as Ezra 2:66. The Hebrew of Nehemiah lacks it, and so does the Greek, which goes straight from the singers to the camels. The verse number is kept so that the numbering matches, and the line is left out.
- **v73 "When the seventh month came"** — the same words that open Ezra 3:1. In Ezra they lead to the rebuilding of the altar; here, to the reading of the book of the instruction (8:1).
