# Nehemiah 2

## Before the King

**1** And it came to pass in the month of Nisan, in the twentieth year of Artaxerxes the king, when wine was before him, that I took up the wine and gave it to the king. I had not been sad in his presence before.

**2** The king said to me, "Why is your face sad, though you are not sick? This is nothing but sadness of heart." I was very much afraid.

**3** I said to the king, "May the king live forever! Why should my face not be sad, when the city, the place of my fathers' graves, lies in ruins, and its gates have been consumed by fire?"

**4** The king said to me, "What are you asking for?" So I prayed to the God of heaven,

**5** and I said to the king, "If it pleases the king, and if your servant has found favor in your sight, send me to Judah, to the city of my fathers' graves, that I may rebuild it."

**6** The king said to me, with the queen sitting beside him, "How long will your journey be, and when will you return?" So it pleased the king to send me, and I set him a time.

**7** I said to the king, "If it pleases the king, let letters be given me to the governors of the province Beyond the River, so that they will let me pass through until I come to Judah,

**8** and a letter to Asaph, the keeper of the king's forest, that he may give me timber to make beams for the gates of the citadel of the house, for the wall of the city and for the house that I will occupy." The king granted me this, for the good hand of my God was on me.

**9** I came to the governors of the province Beyond the River and gave them the king's letters. The king had sent with me officers of the army and horsemen.

**10** When Sanballat the Horonite and Tobiah the Ammonite servant heard this, it displeased them greatly that someone had come to seek the good of the sons of Israel.

## The Walls Inspected by Night

**11** I came to Jerusalem and was there three days.

**12** Then I arose in the night, I and a few men with me. I told no one what my God had put into my heart to do for Jerusalem. There was no animal with me except the animal I was riding.

**13** I went out by night through the Valley Gate toward the Dragon's Spring and to the Dung Gate, and I examined the walls of Jerusalem, which were broken down, and its gates, which had been consumed by fire.

**14** Then I went on to the Fountain Gate and to the King's Pool, but there was no room for the animal under me to pass.

**15** So I went up by the valley in the night and examined the wall. Then I turned back and entered by the Valley Gate, and so returned.

**16** The officials did not know where I had gone or what I was doing, for I had not yet told the Jews, the priests, the nobles, the officials or the rest who were to do the work.

## "Let Us Rise and Build"

**17** Then I said to them, "You see the trouble we are in, how Jerusalem lies in ruins and its gates are burned with fire. Come, let us build the wall of Jerusalem, so that we may no longer be a disgrace."

**18** I told them of the hand of my God, which had been good on me, and also of the words the king had spoken to me. They said, "Let us rise and build." So they strengthened their hands for the good work.

**19** But when Sanballat the Horonite, Tobiah the Ammonite servant and Geshem the Arab heard of it, they mocked us and despised us and said, "What is this thing you are doing? Are you rebelling against the king?"

**20** I answered them, "The God of heaven, He will give us success, and we His servants will rise and build. But you have no portion, no right and no memorial in Jerusalem."

## Notes

- **vv1–2 "I had not been sad in his presence… Why is your face sad, though you are not sick?"** — *maddua panekha ra'im*, the question Joseph put to Pharaoh's cupbearer in prison: *Why are your faces bad today?* (Genesis 40:7). Now the cupbearer is the one with the sad face, and the king asks.
- **v3 "the city, the place of my fathers' graves"** — he does not name Jerusalem. A letter to an Artaxerxes had called it *that rebellious and wicked city*, and the king had stopped its rebuilding (Ezra 4:12, 21); Nehemiah speaks of his fathers' graves.
- **v4 "So I prayed to the God of heaven, and I said to the king"** — the prayer comes between the king's question and the answer, in the time it takes to draw breath.
- **v6 "with the queen sitting beside him"** — *ha-shegal*, the word of the psalm, *the queen stands at your right hand in gold of Ophir* (Psalm 45:9).
- **v8 "the keeper of the king's forest"** — *pardes*, a Persian word for an enclosed park, the word behind English *paradise*.
- **v8 "for the good hand of my God was on me"** — Ezra's refrain (Ezra 7:6, 9, 28; 8:18, 22, 31), now in Nehemiah's mouth (also v18).
- **v10 "Tobiah the Ammonite servant"** — the law says *No Ammonite… shall enter the assembly of the LORD* (Deuteronomy 23:3), and the book will end with Tobiah turned out of a chamber of the house (13:1–8).
- **v18 "So they strengthened their hands"** — *va-yechazzequ yedeihem*, the reverse of the people of the land who *kept weakening the hands of the people of Judah* (Ezra 4:4).
- **v20 "no portion, no right and no memorial in Jerusalem"** — *cheleq u-tsedaqah ve-zikkaron*: no share of the land, no legal claim, and no name remembered there.
