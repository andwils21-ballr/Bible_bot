# Acts 25

## Before Festus

**1** Three days after Festus had arrived in the province, he went up from Caesarea to Jerusalem.

**2** The chief priests and the leading men of the Jews brought their case against Paul before him, and they urged him,

**3** asking as a favor against Paul that he send for him to come to Jerusalem, planning an ambush to kill him on the way.

**4** Festus answered that Paul was being kept at Caesarea and that he himself was about to go there shortly.

**5** "So," he said, "let the men of authority among you go down with me, and if there is anything wrong in the man, let them accuse him."

**6** After staying among them not more than eight or ten days, he went down to Caesarea. The next day he took his seat on the judgment seat and ordered Paul to be brought.

**7** When he had come, the Jews who had come down from Jerusalem stood around him, bringing many serious charges against him that they could not prove.

**8** Paul said in his defense, "I have committed no offense against the Law of the Jews, or against the temple, or against Caesar."

**9** But Festus, wanting to do the Jews a favor, answered Paul, "Do you wish to go up to Jerusalem and be tried there on these charges before me?"

**10** Paul said, "I am standing before Caesar's judgment seat, where I ought to be tried. I have done no wrong to the Jews, as you yourself know very well.

**11** If then I am a wrongdoer and have committed anything deserving death, I do not refuse to die. But if there is nothing to their charges against me, no one can hand me over to them as a favor. I appeal to Caesar."

**12** Then Festus, when he had conferred with his council, answered, "You have appealed to Caesar; to Caesar you will go."

## King Agrippa

**13** After some days had passed, King Agrippa and Bernice arrived at Caesarea to pay their respects to Festus.

**14** Since they were staying there many days, Festus laid Paul's case before the king, saying, "There is a man here left as a prisoner by Felix.

**15** When I was in Jerusalem, the chief priests and the elders of the Jews brought their case against him, asking for a sentence of condemnation against him.

**16** I answered them that it is not the custom of the Romans to hand over any man as a favor before the accused has met his accusers face to face and has had an opportunity to make his defense against the charge.

**17** So when they had come together here, I made no delay, but the next day took my seat on the judgment seat and ordered the man to be brought.

**18** When the accusers stood up, they brought no charge against him of the evil things I had supposed.

**19** Instead they had certain questions against him about their own religion and about a certain Jesus, who had died, whom Paul asserted to be alive.

**20** Being at a loss how to inquire into these things, I asked whether he would be willing to go to Jerusalem and be tried there on these matters.

**21** But when Paul appealed to be kept in custody for the decision of the Emperor, I ordered him to be held until I could send him to Caesar."

**22** Agrippa said to Festus, "I would like to hear the man myself." "Tomorrow," he said, "you will hear him."

**23** So the next day Agrippa and Bernice came with great pomp and entered the audience hall with the commanders and the leading men of the city. At Festus' command, Paul was brought in.

**24** Festus said, "King Agrippa and all who are present with us, you see this man about whom the whole Jewish people petitioned me, both in Jerusalem and here, shouting that he ought not to live any longer.

**25** But I found that he had done nothing deserving death, and since he himself appealed to the Emperor, I decided to send him.

**26** But I have nothing definite to write to my lord about him. So I have brought him before you all, and especially before you, King Agrippa, so that after the examination has taken place I may have something to write.

**27** For it seems to me unreasonable, in sending a prisoner, not to indicate the charges against him."

## Notes

- **vv1–5 "asking as a favor… planning an ambush to kill him on the way"** — the plot of the forty again (23:12–15), two years later, now laid before a new governor. *Charin*, **a favor**, the word that runs through the chapter: the Jews ask a *favor* (v3), Festus wants *to do the Jews a favor* (v9), and Paul says *no one can hand me over to them as a favor*, *charisasthai* (v11). The same words were used of Felix, *wanting to do the Jews a favor* (24:27).
- **v8 "against the Law of the Jews, or against the temple, or against Caesar"** — three charges in one sentence, the charges of 21:28 and 24:5–6, with Caesar added for a Roman judge.
- **vv10–12 "I appeal to Caesar… You have appealed to Caesar; to Caesar you will go"** — *Kaisara epikaloumai*, **I appeal to Caesar**, word for word **I call upon Caesar**: the verb of *Everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved* (2:21) and of Ananias' *calling on His name* (22:16). The Lord had said *you must testify also in Rome* (23:11); the appeal is how he goes.
- **v16 "before the accused has met his accusers face to face"** — Festus sets out the Roman rule; Paul had said of the Jews from Asia that they *ought to be here before you to bring charges* (24:19).
- **v19 "their own religion… a certain Jesus, who had died, whom Paul asserted to be alive"** — *deisidaimonias*, **religion**, the word Paul used of the Athenians, *very religious* (17:22). A Roman governor sums up the gospel in one clause, and it is the women's report at the tomb, angels *who said that He was alive* (Luke 24:23).
- **vv21, 25 "the Emperor"** — *Sebastou*, word for word **the revered one**, the Greek for the Latin title *Augustus*. *Tō kyriō*, **my lord** (v26), of the emperor, the word the book uses of Jesus.
- **vv23–27 "with great pomp… shouting that he ought not to live any longer… nothing deserving death… nothing definite to write"** — *phantasias*, **pomp**, word for word **show**. The crowd's shout is the one of 22:22, *He should not be allowed to live!* Festus finds what Lysias found, *nothing deserving death or chains* (23:29), and still sends him on, with nothing to write.
