# Acts 15

## The Question

**1** Some men came down from Judea and were teaching the brothers, "Unless you are circumcised according to the custom of Moses, you cannot be saved."

**2** When Paul and Barnabas had no small dissension and debate with them, Paul and Barnabas and some others of them were appointed to go up to Jerusalem to the apostles and elders about this question.

**3** So, being sent on their way by the church, they passed through both Phoenicia and Samaria, telling in full of the turning of the Gentiles, and they brought great joy to all the brothers.

**4** When they arrived in Jerusalem, they were welcomed by the church and the apostles and the elders, and they reported all that God had done with them.

**5** But some of the believers who belonged to the party of the Pharisees stood up and said, "They must be circumcised and ordered to keep the Law of Moses."

## Peter Speaks

**6** The apostles and the elders gathered to look into this matter.

**7** After much debate, Peter stood up and said to them, "Brothers, you know that in the early days God made a choice among you, that by my mouth the Gentiles should hear the word of the good news and believe.

**8** God, who knows the heart, bore witness to them by giving them the Holy Spirit just as He did to us,

**9** and He made no distinction between us and them, having cleansed their hearts by faith.

**10** So now, why are you putting God to the test by placing a yoke on the neck of the disciples that neither our fathers nor we have been able to bear?

**11** No, we believe that we are saved through the grace of the Lord Jesus, in the same way as they are."

**12** The whole assembly fell silent, and they listened to Barnabas and Paul telling of all the signs and wonders God had done through them among the Gentiles.

## James Speaks

**13** When they had finished speaking, James answered, "Brothers, listen to me.

**14** Simeon has told how God first came to take from the Gentiles a people for His name.

**15** The words of the prophets agree with this, as it is written:

**16** 'After this I will return,  
and I will rebuild the fallen tent of David;  
I will rebuild its ruins,  
and I will set it up again,

**17** so that the rest of mankind may seek the Lord,  
and all the nations over whom My name is called,  
says the Lord, who makes these things

**18** known from of old.'

**19** Therefore my judgment is that we should not trouble those from the Gentiles who are turning to God,

**20** but should write to them to keep away from the pollutions of idols, from sexual immorality, from what has been strangled, and from blood.

**21** For from ancient generations Moses has had in every city those who proclaim him, since he is read aloud in the synagogues every sabbath."

## The Letter

**22** Then it seemed good to the apostles and the elders, with the whole church, to choose men from among them and send them to Antioch with Paul and Barnabas: Judas called Barsabbas, and Silas, leading men among the brothers.

**23** By their hand they wrote: "The apostles and the elders, your brothers, to the brothers from the Gentiles in Antioch and Syria and Cilicia: Greetings.

**24** Since we have heard that some men from among us, to whom we gave no instructions, have troubled you with words, unsettling your souls,

**25** it seemed good to us, having come to one mind, to choose men and send them to you with our beloved Barnabas and Paul,

**26** men who have risked their lives for the name of our Lord Jesus Christ.

**27** So we have sent Judas and Silas, who will tell you the same things themselves, by word of mouth.

**28** For it seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us to lay on you no greater burden than these necessary things:

**29** that you keep away from what has been sacrificed to idols, from blood, from what has been strangled, and from sexual immorality. If you keep yourselves from these, you will do well. Farewell."

**30** So they were sent off and went down to Antioch, and having gathered the assembly, they delivered the letter.

**31** When they had read it, they rejoiced at its encouragement.

**32** Judas and Silas, who were prophets themselves, encouraged the brothers with many words and strengthened them.

**33** After they had spent some time there, they were sent off in peace by the brothers to those who had sent them.

**35** But Paul and Barnabas stayed in Antioch, teaching and proclaiming the good news of the word of the Lord, with many others as well.

## Paul and Barnabas Part

**36** After some days Paul said to Barnabas, "Let us go back and visit the brothers in every city where we proclaimed the word of the Lord, and see how they are."

**37** Barnabas wanted to take John, who was called Mark, with them as well.

**38** But Paul thought it right not to take with them the one who had left them in Pamphylia and had not gone on with them to the work.

**39** The disagreement became so sharp that they parted from each other. Barnabas took Mark and sailed away to Cyprus,

**40** but Paul chose Silas and set out, committed by the brothers to the grace of the Lord.

**41** He went through Syria and Cilicia, strengthening the churches.

## Notes

- **vv1–5 "Unless you are circumcised according to the custom of Moses, you cannot be saved… the party of the Pharisees"** — *tō ethei tō Mōuseōs*, **the custom of Moses**, the word Stephen's accusers used, *will change the customs that Moses handed down to us* (6:14). *Hairesis*, **party**, the word used of the Sadducees (5:17) and later of the believers themselves (24:5). *Epistrophēn*, **turning**, the noun of *turned to the Lord* (11:21).
- **vv7–11 "by my mouth the Gentiles should hear… God, who knows the heart… a yoke on the neck… saved through the grace of the Lord Jesus, in the same way as they are"** — Peter's own account of Cornelius (10:34–48; 11:15–17). *Kardiognōstēs*, **who knows the heart**, a word the New Testament has only here and in the prayer of 1:24, *Lord, who knows the hearts of all*. *Zygon*, **yoke**. The order of the last line turns the debate around: the Jews are saved *in the same way as they*, the Gentiles, not the other way.
- **v14 "Simeon"** — *Symeōn*, the Hebrew form of Simon, Peter's own name (v7). *Laon*, **a people**, the word the Greek Old Testament uses for Israel, here taken *from the Gentiles*.
- **vv16–18 "After this I will return, and I will rebuild the fallen tent of David… so that the rest of mankind may seek the Lord… known from of old"** — Amos 9:11–12 in the words of its Greek, *hopōs ekzētēsōsin hoi kataloipoi tōn anthrōpōn*, **so that the rest of mankind may seek**. The Hebrew of Amos has *so that they may possess the remnant of Edom*: *Edom* and *adam*, **mankind**, differ by one letter, as do *yirshu*, **possess**, and *yidreshu*, **seek**. The Greek of Amos opens *On that day*; here it is *After this I will return*. *Skēnē*, **tent**, stands in the Greek of Amos for *sukkah*, a booth of branches. The last words, *known from of old*, are not in Amos. They recall the Greek of Isaiah 45:21, *who made these things heard from the beginning?*
- **v17 "all the nations over whom My name is called"** — the words of Amos 9:12, the law's *the name of the LORD is called over you* (Deuteronomy 28:10), which was said of Israel. James reads it of *the nations*, *ta ethnē*, the same word as *Gentiles* in vv3, 7, 12, 14, 19.
- **vv20, 29 "the pollutions of idols, sexual immorality, what has been strangled, and blood"** — four things, and Leviticus 17–18 lays each one on *the guest living among* Israel, not on Israel alone: sacrifices brought nowhere but to the LORD (17:8–9), no blood (17:10–12), the blood of a hunted animal poured out on the ground (17:13–14), and the forbidden unions, *neither the native nor the guest* (18:26). *Pniktou*, **what has been strangled**, choked, and so not bled. *Alisgēmatōn*, **pollutions**, from the verb of Daniel 1:8, *Daniel resolved that he would not defile himself with the king's food*; the letter of v29 puts it plainly, *eidōlothytōn*, **what has been sacrificed to idols**.
- **v21 "Moses has had in every city those who proclaim him"** — the reason is given in one clause and not explained. It may say that the Gentiles already hear the law each sabbath, or that these four keep fellowship with the Jews in every city possible. The verse does not choose.
- **vv22–29 "it seemed good to the Holy Spirit and to us… Farewell"** — *edoxen*, **it seemed good**, three times (vv22, 25, 28), and the third time it is the Holy Spirit's before it is theirs. *Anaskeuazontes*, **unsettling**, word for word **dismantling**. *Paradedōkosi tas psychas*, **risked their lives**, word for word **handed over their lives**, the verb used of Jesus being handed over (Luke 24:7). *Errōsthe*, **farewell**, word for word **be strong**. The chapter has no v34; it is not in the edition, so the numbering skips from 33 to 35.
- **vv36–41 "John, who was called Mark… The disagreement became so sharp that they parted"** — Mark left them at Perga (13:13). *Paroxysmos*, **sharp disagreement**, a sharpening or provoking, the word of *stir one another up to love* (Hebrews 10:24). Barnabas is not named again in Acts.
