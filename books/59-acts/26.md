# Acts 26

## The Hope of the Promise

**1** Agrippa said to Paul, "You are permitted to speak for yourself." Then Paul stretched out his hand and made his defense:

**2** "I consider myself fortunate, King Agrippa, that it is before you I am to make my defense today against all the accusations of the Jews,

**3** especially because you are familiar with all the customs and questions of the Jews. So I beg you to listen to me patiently.

**4** My way of life from my youth, spent from the beginning among my own nation and in Jerusalem, is known to all the Jews.

**5** They have known me for a long time, if they are willing to testify, that I lived as a Pharisee, according to the strictest sect of our religion.

**6** And now I stand here on trial because of my hope in the promise made by God to our fathers,

**7** the promise our twelve tribes hope to attain as they earnestly worship night and day. It is for this hope, O king, that I am accused by Jews.

**8** Why is it judged incredible by any of you that God raises the dead?

## The Road to Damascus a Third Time

**9** I myself was convinced that I ought to do many things against the name of Jesus of Nazareth.

**10** That is what I did in Jerusalem. I locked up many of the saints in prisons, having received authority from the chief priests, and when they were put to death I cast my vote against them.

**11** In all the synagogues I punished them often and tried to force them to blaspheme, and in raging fury against them I pursued them even to foreign cities.

**12** In this way I was journeying to Damascus with the authority and commission of the chief priests,

**13** when at midday on the road, O king, I saw a light from heaven, brighter than the sun, shining around me and those who were traveling with me.

**14** When we had all fallen to the ground, I heard a voice saying to me in the Hebrew language, 'Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting Me? It is hard for you to kick against the goads.'

**15** I said, 'Who are You, Lord?' The Lord said, 'I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting.

**16** But get up and stand on your feet, for I have appeared to you for this purpose, to appoint you as a servant and a witness to the things in which you have seen Me and to those in which I will appear to you,

**17** rescuing you from the people and from the Gentiles, to whom I am sending you

**18** to open their eyes, so that they may turn from darkness to light and from the power of Satan to God, so that they may receive forgiveness of sins and a place among those who are made holy by faith in Me.'

## Obedient to the Vision

**19** So, King Agrippa, I was not disobedient to the heavenly vision,

**20** but declared first to those in Damascus, then in Jerusalem and throughout all the region of Judea, and also to the Gentiles, that they should repent and turn to God, doing deeds worthy of repentance.

**21** For this reason the Jews seized me in the temple and tried to kill me.

**22** To this day I have had the help that comes from God, and so I stand here testifying to small and great, saying nothing but what the prophets and Moses said would happen:

**23** that the Messiah would suffer, and that, as the first to rise from the dead, He would proclaim light both to our people and to the Gentiles."

## Almost Persuaded

**24** As he was saying these things in his defense, Festus said in a loud voice, "You are out of your mind, Paul! Your great learning is driving you out of your mind."

**25** But Paul said, "I am not out of my mind, most excellent Festus. I am speaking words of truth and sound sense.

**26** For the king knows about these things, and to him I speak boldly. I am persuaded that none of these things has escaped his notice, for this was not done in a corner.

**27** King Agrippa, do you believe the prophets? I know that you believe."

**28** Agrippa said to Paul, "In a short time you would persuade me to become a Christian?"

**29** Paul said, "Whether short or long, I would pray to God that not only you but also all who hear me today might become such as I am, except for these chains."

**30** Then the king rose, and the governor and Bernice and those who were sitting with them.

**31** When they had withdrawn, they said to one another, "This man is doing nothing deserving death or chains."

**32** Agrippa said to Festus, "This man could have been set free if he had not appealed to Caesar."

## Notes

- **vv6–8 "my hope in the promise made by God to our fathers… our twelve tribes… Why is it judged incredible… that God raises the dead?"** — *to dōdekaphylon*, **our twelve tribes**, one word in Greek, the whole people as one body. The hope Paul is tried for is the one the tribes *earnestly worship night and day* to reach; the question to the king is why the raising of the dead should be the thing not believed.
- **vv9–11 "I cast my vote against them… in raging fury against them"** — *katēnenka psēphon*, **I cast my vote**, word for word **I brought down a pebble**, the stone dropped in a vote, the word of *a white stone* in Revelation 2:17. *Emmainomenos*, **in raging fury**, from *mania*, madness: the word comes back in Festus' mouth, *You are out of your mind*, *mainē* (v24). Paul was the madman then; Festus thinks he is one now.
- **vv13–15 "at midday… brighter than the sun… in the Hebrew language… It is hard for you to kick against the goads"** — the third telling, after 9:3–6 and 22:6–10, with the words of those chapters kept where the Greek is the same. New here: *brighter than the sun*, the voice speaking *in the Hebrew language*, and the saying *pros kentra laktizein*, **to kick against the goads**, the pointed sticks that drive an ox. To fight the One driving him is to wound himself.
- **vv16–18 "stand on your feet… rescuing you from the people and from the Gentiles… to open their eyes, so that they may turn from darkness to light"** — the call of the prophets. *Son of man, stand on your feet* (Ezekiel 2:1); *I am with you to deliver you*, *exaireisthai se*, the verb of v17, to Jeremiah (Jeremiah 1:8, in the Greek); *to open the eyes that are blind* and *I will turn the darkness before them into light* (Isaiah 42:7, 16). *Klēron en tois hēgiasmenois*, **a place among those who are made holy**, the words of the farewell at Miletus (20:32).
- **v20 "doing deeds worthy of repentance"** — John's word at the Jordan, *Bear fruits worthy of repentance* (Luke 3:8).
- **vv22–23 "small and great… the Messiah would suffer… as the first to rise from the dead, He would proclaim light both to our people and to the Gentiles"** — *ei pathētos*, word for word **whether the Messiah is one who suffers**, the question the prophets answer. *Light… to the Gentiles*, as Simeon said of the child, *a light for revelation to the nations* (Luke 2:32), and as Paul read Isaiah at Antioch (13:47).
- **vv24–26 "Your great learning is driving you out of your mind… words of truth and sound sense… not done in a corner"** — *ta polla grammata*, **your great learning**, word for word **your many letters**. *Sōphrosynēs*, **sound sense**, the opposite of *mania*. *Ouk en gōnia*, **not in a corner**: what happened to Jesus was public.
- **v28 "In a short time you would persuade me to become a Christian?"** — *En oligō me peitheis Christianon poiēsai*, word for word **in little you persuade me to make a Christian**. *En oligō* can mean **in a short time** or **by a little**, *almost*; and the sentence can be a question, a jest, or a statement. *Christianos* is in the Greek New Testament three times: at Antioch (11:26), here, and in 1 Peter 4:16. Paul answers *whether short or long*, *kai en oligō kai en megalō*, taking up the king's own word.
- **vv31–32 "nothing deserving death or chains… could have been set free if he had not appealed to Caesar"** — the words of Lysias' letter, *nothing deserving death or chains* (23:29). The appeal that took him out of the Jews' hands now keeps him in Rome's.
