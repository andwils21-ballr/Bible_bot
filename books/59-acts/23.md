# Acts 23

## Paul Before the Council

**1** Paul looked intently at the council and said, "Brothers, I have lived my life before God in all good conscience up to this day."

**2** The high priest Ananias ordered those standing beside him to strike him on the mouth.

**3** Then Paul said to him, "God is going to strike you, you whitewashed wall! Do you sit there to judge me according to the Law, and yet, against the Law, you order me to be struck?"

**4** Those standing by said, "Do you insult God's high priest?"

**5** Paul said, "I did not know, brothers, that he was the high priest; for it is written, 'You shall not speak evil of a ruler of your people.'"

**6** When Paul perceived that one part were Sadducees and the other Pharisees, he cried out in the council, "Brothers, I am a Pharisee, a son of Pharisees. It is for the hope and the resurrection of the dead that I am on trial."

**7** When he said this, a dispute broke out between the Pharisees and the Sadducees, and the assembly was divided.

**8** For the Sadducees say that there is no resurrection, nor angel, nor spirit, but the Pharisees acknowledge them all.

**9** There was a great uproar, and some of the scribes of the Pharisees' party stood up and argued sharply, "We find nothing wrong in this man. What if a spirit or an angel has spoken to him —"

**10** When the dispute became violent, the commander was afraid that Paul would be torn to pieces by them, and he ordered the troops to go down, take him by force from among them, and bring him into the barracks.

**11** The following night the Lord stood by him and said, "Take heart, for as you have testified about Me in Jerusalem, so you must testify also in Rome."

## The Plot

**12** When day came, the Jews formed a conspiracy and bound themselves by an oath neither to eat nor drink until they had killed Paul.

**13** There were more than forty who made this plot.

**14** They went to the chief priests and the elders and said, "We have bound ourselves by an oath to taste nothing until we have killed Paul.

**15** So now you, together with the council, give notice to the commander to bring him down to you, as though you meant to look into his case more closely. We are ready to kill him before he arrives."

**16** But the son of Paul's sister heard of the ambush, and he went and entered the barracks and told Paul.

**17** Paul called one of the centurions and said, "Take this young man to the commander, for he has something to report to him."

**18** So he took him and brought him to the commander and said, "Paul the prisoner called me and asked me to bring this young man to you, for he has something to tell you."

**19** The commander took him by the hand, drew him aside, and asked him privately, "What is it that you have to report to me?"

**20** He said, "The Jews have agreed to ask you to bring Paul down to the council tomorrow, as though they meant to inquire somewhat more closely about him.

**21** But do not be persuaded by them, for more than forty of their men are lying in ambush for him, who have bound themselves by an oath neither to eat nor drink until they have killed him. They are ready now, waiting for your consent."

**22** So the commander dismissed the young man, ordering him, "Tell no one that you have reported these things to me."

## To Caesarea

**23** Then he called two of the centurions and said, "Get ready two hundred soldiers, seventy horsemen and two hundred spearmen to go as far as Caesarea at the third hour of the night.

**24** Provide mounts for Paul to ride, and bring him safely to Felix the governor."

**25** He wrote a letter to this effect:

**26** "Claudius Lysias, to His Excellency the governor Felix: Greetings.

**27** This man was seized by the Jews and was about to be killed by them when I came on them with the troops and rescued him, having learned that he was a Roman.

**28** Wanting to know the charge for which they were accusing him, I brought him down to their council.

**29** I found that he was accused over questions of their Law, but charged with nothing deserving death or chains.

**30** When I was informed that there would be a plot against the man, I sent him to you at once, ordering his accusers also to state their case against him before you."

**31** So the soldiers, as they had been ordered, took Paul and brought him by night to Antipatris.

**32** The next day they let the horsemen go on with him, and they returned to the barracks.

**33** When the horsemen had come to Caesarea and delivered the letter to the governor, they presented Paul to him as well.

**34** When he had read it, he asked what province he was from. Learning that he was from Cilicia,

**35** he said, "I will hear your case when your accusers also arrive." Then he ordered him to be kept under guard in Herod's praetorium.

## Notes

- **v1 "I have lived my life before God in all good conscience"** — *pepoliteumai*, **I have lived my life**, from *politeia*, citizenship: to live as a citizen lives, in public and under law. The word Paul uses to the Philippians, *only live your life worthily of the good news* (Philippians 1:27).
- **vv2–3 "strike him on the mouth… God is going to strike you, you whitewashed wall!"** — *Typtein… typtein*, **strike… strike**, the same verb twice: the high priest's blow returned as God's. *Tiche kekoniamene*, **whitewashed wall**, the picture of Ezekiel 13, a flimsy wall that the prophets *plaster with whitewash*, and *it will fall* (13:10–11). Jesus used the same verb of the scribes and Pharisees, *whitewashed tombs* (Matthew 23:27).
- **vv4–5 "I did not know, brothers, that he was the high priest… You shall not speak evil of a ruler of your people"** — the quotation is the Greek of Exodus 22:28, *archonta tou laou sou ouk ereis kakōs*, where the Hebrew reads *you shall not curse a leader among your people*. Paul answers from the Law he is accused of breaking.
- **vv6–9 "I am a Pharisee, a son of Pharisees… the Sadducees say that there is no resurrection, nor angel, nor spirit… What if a spirit or an angel has spoken to him"** — Luke names the Sadducees as those *who say that there is no resurrection* (Luke 20:27). *Eschisthē*, **was divided**, the split of 14:4. The scribes' last sentence is broken off in the Greek, *ei de pneuma elalēsen autō ē angelos* — **if a spirit spoke to him, or an angel** — and the uproar takes the rest.
- **v11 "Take heart… so you must testify also in Rome"** — *dei*, **you must**: Paul had said *I must also see Rome* (19:21); now the Lord says it. *Tharsei*, **take heart**, Jesus' word to the paralyzed man (Matthew 9:2) and to the disciples on the water (Matthew 14:27).
- **vv12–14 "bound themselves by an oath… We have bound ourselves by an oath"** — *anethematisan*, from *anathema*, **a curse**: they put themselves under a curse if they ate before the killing. In v14 it is doubled, *anathemati anethematisamen*, word for word **with a curse we have cursed ourselves**, the Hebrew way of saying it in Greek words.
- **v16 "the son of Paul's sister"** — the only word in the book about Paul's own family.
- **vv23–24 "two hundred soldiers, seventy horsemen and two hundred spearmen… at the third hour of the night"** — *dexiolabous*, **spearmen**, a word found nowhere else in the Greek Bible, word for word **right-hand-takers**; its meaning is not certain. *The third hour of the night*, about nine in the evening. Four hundred and seventy men for one prisoner, against forty.
- **vv26–30 "To His Excellency the governor Felix… having learned that he was a Roman… nothing deserving death or chains"** — *kratistō*, **His Excellency**, the title Luke gives Theophilus, *most excellent Theophilus* (Luke 1:3). The letter says Lysias rescued him *having learned that he was a Roman*; chapter 22 tells that he learned it after he had bound him and ordered him flogged (22:24–29). *Nothing deserving death*: Pilate said of Jesus, *I find no guilt in this man* (Luke 23:4).
