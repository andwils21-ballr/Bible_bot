# Acts 16

## Timothy

**1** He came to Derbe and to Lystra. A disciple was there named Timothy, the son of a Jewish woman who was a believer, but his father was a Greek.

**2** He was well spoken of by the brothers at Lystra and Iconium.

**3** Paul wanted him to go out with him, and he took him and circumcised him because of the Jews who were in those places, for they all knew that his father was a Greek.

**4** As they went on through the cities, they handed on to them the decisions reached by the apostles and elders in Jerusalem, for them to keep.

**5** So the churches were strengthened in the faith and grew in number every day.

## The Man of Macedonia

**6** They went through the region of Phrygia and Galatia, having been forbidden by the Holy Spirit to speak the word in Asia.

**7** When they had come up to Mysia, they tried to go into Bithynia, but the Spirit of Jesus did not allow them.

**8** So they passed by Mysia and went down to Troas.

**9** A vision appeared to Paul in the night: a man of Macedonia was standing there, pleading with him and saying, "Come over to Macedonia and help us."

**10** When he had seen the vision, we sought at once to set out for Macedonia, concluding that God had called us to proclaim the good news to them.

## Lydia

**11** So, setting sail from Troas, we ran a straight course to Samothrace, and the next day to Neapolis,

**12** and from there to Philippi, which is a leading city of the district of Macedonia, a Roman colony. We stayed in this city some days.

**13** On the sabbath day we went outside the gate to a river, where we supposed there was a place of prayer, and we sat down and spoke to the women who had gathered.

**14** A woman named Lydia was listening, a seller of purple cloth from the city of Thyatira, a worshiper of God. The Lord opened her heart to pay attention to what Paul was saying.

**15** When she and her household had been baptized, she urged us, saying, "If you have judged me to be faithful to the Lord, come and stay at my house." She prevailed on us.

## The Slave Girl

**16** As we were going to the place of prayer, we were met by a slave girl who had a spirit of divination and brought her masters much gain by fortune-telling.

**17** She followed Paul and us, crying out, "These men are servants of the Most High God, who are proclaiming to you the way of salvation."

**18** She kept doing this for many days. Paul, greatly troubled, turned and said to the spirit, "I command you in the name of Jesus Christ to come out of her." It came out that very hour.

**19** But when her masters saw that their hope of gain had gone out, they seized Paul and Silas and dragged them into the marketplace before the rulers.

**20** They brought them to the magistrates and said, "These men are throwing our city into an uproar. They are Jews,

**21** and they are proclaiming customs that we as Romans may not accept or practice."

**22** The crowd joined in the attack on them, and the magistrates tore their garments off them and ordered them to be beaten with rods.

**23** After laying many blows on them, they threw them into prison and ordered the jailer to keep them securely.

**24** Having received such an order, he threw them into the inner prison and fastened their feet in the stocks.

## The Jailer

**25** About midnight Paul and Silas were praying and singing hymns to God, and the prisoners were listening to them.

**26** Suddenly there was a great earthquake, so that the foundations of the prison were shaken. At once all the doors were opened, and everyone's chains were unfastened.

**27** When the jailer woke and saw the prison doors open, he drew his sword and was about to kill himself, supposing that the prisoners had escaped.

**28** But Paul called out with a loud voice, "Do yourself no harm, for we are all here."

**29** He called for lights and rushed in, and trembling, he fell down before Paul and Silas.

**30** He brought them out and said, "Sirs, what must I do to be saved?"

**31** They said, "Believe in the Lord Jesus, and you will be saved, you and your household."

**32** They spoke the word of the Lord to him and to all who were in his house.

**33** He took them that same hour of the night and washed their wounds, and he was baptized at once, he and all his family.

**34** He brought them up into his house and set a meal before them, and he rejoiced with his whole household that he had believed in God.

## Romans

**35** When day came, the magistrates sent the officers, saying, "Let those men go."

**36** The jailer reported these words to Paul: "The magistrates have sent word that you are to be released. So come out now and go in peace."

**37** But Paul said to them, "They beat us in public, uncondemned, men who are Romans, and threw us into prison; and now are they throwing us out in secret? No! Let them come themselves and take us out."

**38** The officers reported these words to the magistrates, and they were afraid when they heard that they were Romans.

**39** So they came and pleaded with them, and they took them out and asked them to leave the city.

**40** They went out of the prison and went to Lydia's house. When they had seen the brothers, they encouraged them and departed.

## Notes

- **vv1–3 "the son of a Jewish woman who was a believer, but his father was a Greek… circumcised him because of the Jews"** — one chapter after the letter that laid no circumcision on the Gentile believers (15:19, 28–29), Paul circumcises Timothy. The letter was written *to the brothers from the Gentiles* (15:23); Timothy's mother is a Jew, and the reason given is that *they all knew that his father was a Greek*.
- **v4 "the decisions reached by the apostles and elders"** — *ta dogmata*, **the decisions**, the word of *a decree went out from Caesar Augustus* (Luke 2:1). In the next chapter Paul's accusers say he acts *against the decrees of Caesar* (17:7), the same word again.
- **vv6–10 "forbidden by the Holy Spirit… the Spirit of Jesus did not allow them… we sought at once"** — two roads closed in two verses, by the Spirit named two ways, and a third road opened by a vision. From v10 the account says *we*: the writer is now in the company. The first person last appeared in the book's first line, *In the first account, Theophilus, I wrote* (1:1).
- **v12 "a leading city of the district of Macedonia, a Roman colony"** — *kolōnia*, the Latin *colonia*, a city of Roman citizens; it is why the charge in v21 is *we as Romans*. The edition marks *a leading city of the district* as a place where manuscripts differ.
- **vv13–15 "a place of prayer… Lydia… a worshiper of God. The Lord opened her heart"** — *proseuchē*, **a place of prayer**, word for word **a prayer**. *Porphyropōlis*, **a seller of purple cloth**. *Sebomenē ton theon*, **a worshiper of God**, the words used of Titius Justus at Corinth (18:7). *Diēnoixen*, **opened**, the verb of Luke 24:45, *Then He opened their minds to understand the Scriptures*.
- **vv16–18 "a spirit of divination… fortune-telling… servants of the Most High God… come out of her"** — *pneuma pythōna*, word for word **a spirit, a python**, the Greek name for a spirit that spoke oracles. *Manteuomenē*, **fortune-telling**, the Greek of Deuteronomy 18:10 for the *soothsayer* the law forbids. *The Most High God* is what the possessed man in the country of the Gerasenes cried out, *Jesus, Son of the Most High God* (Luke 8:28). What she says is true; Paul silences the spirit that says it, as Jesus rebuked the demons *and would not let them speak* (Luke 4:41).
- **v19 "their hope of gain had gone out"** — *exēlthen*, **had gone out**, the same verb as *it came out* in v18. When the spirit went out of the girl, the masters' money went out with it.
- **vv25–34 "praying and singing hymns… what must I do to be saved?… you will be saved, you and your household… rejoiced"** — the crowd at Pentecost asked *what shall we do?* (2:37). *You will be saved, you and all your household* is the promise the angel gave Cornelius (11:14), and as with Lydia (v15), the house is baptized with its head. *Ēgalliasato*, **rejoiced**, the word of the first believers who *shared their food with gladness* (2:46). *Panoikei*, **with his whole household**.
- **vv35–39 "the officers… They beat us in public, uncondemned, men who are Romans"** — *rhabdouchoi*, **officers**, word for word **rod-holders**, the men who had carried out *beaten with rods*, *rhabdizein* (v22). *Akatakritous*, **uncondemned**, the word Paul uses again in Jerusalem when he is stretched out to be flogged: *Is it lawful for you to flog a man who is a Roman and uncondemned?* (22:25). Only now does the book say that Paul is a Roman.
