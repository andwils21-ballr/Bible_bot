# Acts 28

## Malta

**1** Once we had been brought safely through, we learned that the island was called Malta.

**2** The islanders showed us no ordinary kindness. They lit a fire and welcomed us all, because of the rain that had set in and the cold.

**3** Paul had gathered a bundle of sticks and was putting them on the fire, when a viper came out because of the heat and fastened on his hand.

**4** When the islanders saw the creature hanging from his hand, they said to one another, "This man must be a murderer. He has been saved from the sea, but Justice has not let him live."

**5** But he shook the creature off into the fire and suffered no harm.

**6** They were waiting for him to swell up or suddenly fall down dead. But after they had waited a long time and saw nothing unusual happen to him, they changed their minds and said that he was a god.

**7** In the country around that place were estates belonging to the leading man of the island, whose name was Publius. He welcomed us and entertained us hospitably for three days.

**8** The father of Publius was lying sick with fever and dysentery. Paul went in to him, prayed, laid his hands on him and healed him.

**9** After this, the rest of the people on the island who had diseases also came and were cured.

**10** They honored us in many ways, and when we set sail they put on board what we needed.

## On to Rome

**11** After three months we set sail in an Alexandrian ship that had wintered at the island, with the Twin Brothers as its figurehead.

**12** We put in at Syracuse and stayed there three days.

**13** From there we cast off and reached Rhegium. A day later a south wind sprang up, and on the second day we came to Puteoli.

**14** There we found brothers and were invited to stay with them seven days. In this way we came to Rome.

**15** The brothers there had heard about us, and they came as far as the Forum of Appius and Three Taverns to meet us. When Paul saw them, he thanked God and took heart.

**16** When we entered Rome, Paul was allowed to live by himself, with the soldier who guarded him.

## The Jews of Rome

**17** After three days he called together the leading men of the Jews. When they had gathered, he said to them, "Brothers, though I had done nothing against our people or the customs of our fathers, I was handed over as a prisoner from Jerusalem into the hands of the Romans.

**18** They examined me and wanted to release me, because there was no cause for death in me.

**19** But when the Jews objected, I was compelled to appeal to Caesar — not that I had any charge to bring against my nation.

**20** For this reason I have asked to see you and speak with you, since it is because of the hope of Israel that I am wearing this chain."

**21** They said to him, "We have received no letters from Judea about you, and none of the brothers who have come here has reported or said anything evil about you.

**22** But we would like to hear from you what you think, for as to this sect, we know that everywhere it is spoken against."

## This Salvation of God

**23** They set a day with him and came to him at his lodging in greater numbers. From morning until evening he explained it to them, testifying to the kingdom of God and trying to persuade them about Jesus from the Law of Moses and from the Prophets.

**24** Some were persuaded by what he said, but others did not believe.

**25** They could not agree among themselves, and as they were leaving, Paul made one statement: "The Holy Spirit spoke rightly through Isaiah the prophet to your fathers,

**26** saying, 'Go to this people and say: You will keep on hearing but never understand, and you will keep on seeing but never perceive.

**27** For the heart of this people has grown fat, and with their ears they hear with difficulty, and their eyes they have shut; so that they may not see with their eyes, and hear with their ears, and understand with their heart, and turn back, and I would heal them.'

**28** So let it be known to you that this salvation of God has been sent to the Gentiles; they will listen."

## Unhindered

**30** Paul stayed two whole years in his own rented lodging, and welcomed all who came to him,

**31** proclaiming the kingdom of God and teaching about the Lord Jesus Christ with all boldness, unhindered.

## Notes

- **vv1, 4, 6 "brought safely through… saved from the sea, but Justice has not let him live… said that he was a god"** — *diasōthentes* and *diasōthenta*, the word that ended the shipwreck, *all were brought safely to land* (27:44), now in the islanders' mouth too. *Hē dikē*, **justice**, is also the name of a goddess, the one who repays; Greek has no capital letters, so the verse cannot show which the islanders mean. When nothing happens, the man Justice was after becomes *a god*.
- **v2 "The islanders showed us no ordinary kindness"** — *hoi barbaroi*, the people who did not speak Greek, not a word for cruelty: Paul uses it of a man whose language he cannot follow (1 Corinthians 14:11). *Philanthrōpia*, **kindness**, word for word **love of mankind**, is in the New Testament here and in Titus 3:4 only, where it is the kindness *of God our Savior*. The centurion treated Paul *kindly*, *philanthrōpōs* (27:3).
- **vv3–6 "a viper… suffered no harm… said that he was a god"** — *echidna*, the word of John at the Jordan, *Brood of vipers!* (Luke 3:7); only here is the snake real. Jesus had said, *I have given you authority to tread on snakes and scorpions… and nothing will harm you* (Luke 10:19). The scene runs the other way from Lystra, where the crowd cried *The gods have come down to us* and then stoned Paul (14:11, 19): here he is a murderer first and a god after.
- **v11 "with the Twin Brothers as its figurehead"** — *parasēmō Dioskourois*, **marked with the Dioskouroi**, word for word **the sons of Zeus**. The islanders have just called Paul a god; he sails on under the sign of two.
- **vv14–16 "In this way we came to Rome… took heart… When we entered Rome"** — *kai houtōs*, **in this way**, the words that ended the shipwreck, *In this way all were brought safely to land* (27:44). Paul had said *I must also see Rome* (19:21), and the Lord had said *Take heart… you must testify also in Rome* (23:11); now, meeting the brothers, Paul *took heart*, *tharsos*, the word of that night. The narrator's *we* ends here, at the city.
- **vv17–22 "handed over… into the hands of the Romans… no cause for death in me… everywhere it is spoken against"** — *paredothēn eis tas cheiras*, the words of Agabus, *hand him over into the hands of the Gentiles* (21:11), and of Jesus about Himself, *handed over into the hands of sinful men* (Luke 24:7). *Mēdemian aitian thanatou*, **no cause for death**, what Paul said at Antioch of Jesus before Pilate (13:28). *Antilegetai*, **is spoken against**, Simeon's word for the child, *a sign that is spoken against* (Luke 2:34).
- **vv25–27 "Go to this people and say… and I would heal them"** — Isaiah 6:9–10 in the Greek, word for word as Jesus quotes it in Matthew 13:14–15, and the English here is Matthew's. The Hebrew of Isaiah differs: there the prophet is told *Make the heart of this people fat* (Isaiah 6:10), and here the heart *has grown fat*. Luke's Gospel has the same words in short, where Jesus says why He speaks in parables: *seeing they may not see, and hearing they may not understand* (Luke 8:10). The Gospel used them of the crowds; Acts ends on them.
- **v28 "this salvation of God"** — *to sōtērion tou theou*. Luke uses this form of the word three times in his two books: Simeon holding the child, *my eyes have seen Your salvation* (Luke 2:30); John at the Jordan, quoting Isaiah, *all flesh shall see the salvation of God* (Luke 3:6); and here, Paul's last word in the book. What *all flesh* was to see is now *sent to the Gentiles*.
- **v29** — the edition has no v29, so the numbering skips from 28 to 30.
- **vv30–31 "proclaiming the kingdom of God… with all boldness, unhindered"** — the book began with the risen Jesus *speaking about the kingdom of God* (1:3) and ends with Paul proclaiming it. *Meta pasēs parrēsias*, **with all boldness**, the prayer of the first believers, *grant to Your servants to speak Your word with all boldness* (4:29). *Akōlytōs*, **unhindered**, is the last word of Acts and in the New Testament only here; its verb is the eunuch's question, *What prevents me from being baptized?* (8:36). The book does not say what happened to Paul after the two years.
