# Acts 18

## Corinth

**1** After this Paul left Athens and went to Corinth.

**2** There he found a Jew named Aquila, a native of Pontus, who had recently come from Italy with Priscilla his wife, because Claudius had ordered all the Jews to leave Rome. He went to see them,

**3** and because he was of the same trade, he stayed with them and they worked together, for they were tentmakers by trade.

**4** Every sabbath he reasoned in the synagogue and tried to persuade both Jews and Greeks.

**5** When Silas and Timothy came down from Macedonia, Paul gave himself wholly to the word, testifying to the Jews that the Messiah was Jesus.

**6** When they opposed him and spoke abusively, he shook out his garments and said to them, "Your blood be on your own heads! I am innocent. From now on I will go to the Gentiles."

**7** He left there and went to the house of a man named Titius Justus, a worshiper of God, whose house was next door to the synagogue.

**8** Crispus, the ruler of the synagogue, believed in the Lord with his whole household, and many of the Corinthians who heard Paul believed and were baptized.

**9** The Lord said to Paul one night in a vision, "Do not be afraid, but go on speaking and do not be silent,

**10** for I am with you, and no one will attack you to harm you, for I have many people in this city."

**11** He settled there a year and six months, teaching the word of God among them.

## Before Gallio

**12** But when Gallio was proconsul of Achaia, the Jews with one mind rose up against Paul and brought him before the judgment seat,

**13** saying, "This man is persuading people to worship God in ways contrary to the law."

**14** But when Paul was about to open his mouth, Gallio said to the Jews, "If it were a matter of some wrongdoing or vicious crime, O Jews, I would have reason to bear with you.

**15** But since it is a question of words and names and your own law, see to it yourselves. I do not wish to be a judge of these things."

**16** He drove them away from the judgment seat.

**17** Then they all seized Sosthenes, the ruler of the synagogue, and beat him in front of the judgment seat. But Gallio paid no attention to any of this.

## The Return to Antioch

**18** After staying many days longer, Paul took leave of the brothers and sailed for Syria, and with him Priscilla and Aquila. At Cenchreae he had his hair cut off, for he was under a vow.

**19** They came to Ephesus, and he left them there; but he himself went into the synagogue and reasoned with the Jews.

**20** When they asked him to stay for a longer time, he declined.

**21** But on taking leave of them he said, "I will come back to you again, if God wills," and he set sail from Ephesus.

**22** When he had landed at Caesarea, he went up and greeted the church, and then went down to Antioch.

**23** After spending some time there, he set out and went from place to place through the region of Galatia and Phrygia, strengthening all the disciples.

## Apollos

**24** A Jew named Apollos, a native of Alexandria, came to Ephesus. He was an eloquent man, powerful in the Scriptures.

**25** He had been instructed in the way of the Lord, and being fervent in spirit, he spoke and taught accurately the things concerning Jesus, though he knew only the baptism of John.

**26** He began to speak boldly in the synagogue, but when Priscilla and Aquila heard him, they took him aside and explained to him the way of God more accurately.

**27** When he wished to cross over to Achaia, the brothers encouraged him and wrote to the disciples to welcome him. When he arrived, he greatly helped those who through grace had believed,

**28** for he powerfully refuted the Jews in public, showing by the Scriptures that the Messiah was Jesus.

## Notes

- **vv2–3 "Aquila… Priscilla his wife… Claudius had ordered all the Jews to leave Rome… tentmakers by trade"** — *chōrizesthai*, **to leave**, the verb of v1, *Paul left Athens*. *Skēnopoioi*, **tentmakers**, word for word **tent-makers**, from *skēnē*. Paul tells the elders of Ephesus the same, *these hands supplied my needs and the needs of those who were with me* (20:34).
- **v5 "Paul gave himself wholly to the word"** — *syneicheto tō logō*, word for word **was held together by the word**, pressed and occupied by it. The same verb is used of Simon's mother-in-law, *suffering from a high fever* (Luke 4:38), and of Jesus, *how distressed I am until it is accomplished* (Luke 12:50).
- **v6 "he shook out his garments… Your blood be on your own heads! I am innocent"** — the watchman's law of Ezekiel 33:4: whoever hears the horn and takes no warning, *his blood will be on his own head*. Paul has warned them, and the blood is no longer his to answer for. He says the same to the elders of Ephesus, *I am innocent of the blood of all* (20:26), *katharos*, **innocent**, word for word **clean**. At Antioch of Pisidia they *shook off the dust from their feet* (13:51).
- **vv7–8 "Titius Justus, a worshiper of God… Crispus, the ruler of the synagogue, believed… with his whole household"** — Lydia was *a worshiper of God* in the same words (16:14), and the household believed with its head, as hers did and the jailer's (16:15, 34). Paul names Crispus among the few he baptized himself at Corinth: *I baptized none of you except Crispus and Gaius* (1 Corinthians 1:14).
- **vv9–10 "Do not be afraid… for I am with you… for I have many people in this city"** — the word to Jeremiah, *Do not be afraid of them, for I am with you to deliver you* (Jeremiah 1:8). *Laos… polys*, **many people**, word for word **a great people**: *laos* is the word James used of the Gentiles, God taking from them *a people for His name* (15:14). The people are in the city before they have believed.
- **vv12–17 "Gallio was proconsul of Achaia… the judgment seat… a question of words and names and your own law… Sosthenes"** — *bēma*, **the judgment seat**, the raised platform of a Roman court. Gallio refuses the case: *words and names and your own law* are not a crime for his court. A Sosthenes is named with Paul at the head of 1 Corinthians (1:1); the text does not say whether he is this one.
- **v18 "he had his hair cut off, for he was under a vow"** — *keiramenos*, **having had his hair cut**, a masculine participle that can belong to Paul, the subject of the sentence, or to Aquila, the name just before it. The Greek allows both, and the English keeps *he*. *Euchē*, **vow**, the word of the Greek of Numbers 6:2 for the Nazirite's vow, which ends with the head shaved *at the entrance of the tent of meeting* (6:18). This cutting is at Cenchreae, before he sails.
- **v21 "I will come back to you again, if God wills"** — he does, in the next chapter (19:1). The edition marks the opening of the verse as a place where manuscripts differ.
- **vv24–28 "an eloquent man, powerful in the Scriptures… instructed in the way of the Lord… fervent in spirit… knew only the baptism of John"** — *logios*, **eloquent**, a man of words, which can mean skilled in speech or learned. *The way of the Lord* is the way John was sent to prepare, *Prepare the way of the Lord* (Luke 3:4), and John's baptism is all Apollos knows. *Zeōn tō pneumati*, **fervent in spirit**, word for word **boiling in the spirit**, the words Paul uses in Romans 12:11. *Akribōs*, **accurately**, then *akribesteron*, **more accurately**: Priscilla and Aquila take what he had right and add to it.
