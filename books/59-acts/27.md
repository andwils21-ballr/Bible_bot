# Acts 27

## The Voyage Begins

**1** When it was decided that we should sail for Italy, they handed Paul and some other prisoners over to a centurion named Julius, of the Augustan Cohort.

**2** We went aboard a ship from Adramyttium that was about to sail to the ports along the coast of Asia, and put to sea. Aristarchus, a Macedonian from Thessalonica, was with us.

**3** The next day we put in at Sidon, and Julius treated Paul kindly and let him go to his friends and be cared for.

**4** Putting to sea from there, we sailed under the shelter of Cyprus, because the winds were against us.

**5** When we had sailed across the open sea along Cilicia and Pamphylia, we came down to Myra in Lycia.

**6** There the centurion found an Alexandrian ship sailing for Italy and put us on board.

**7** For a good many days we sailed slowly, and arrived off Cnidus only with difficulty. Since the wind did not let us go on, we sailed under the shelter of Crete, off Salmone.

**8** Coasting along it with difficulty, we came to a place called Fair Havens, near the town of Lasea.

## Paul's Warning

**9** Much time had passed, and sailing was now dangerous, because even the Fast was already over. So Paul warned them,

**10** "Men, I can see that this voyage will end in damage and great loss, not only of the cargo and the ship but also of our lives."

**11** But the centurion was persuaded by the pilot and the owner of the ship more than by what Paul said.

**12** Since the harbor was not fit to winter in, the majority decided to put to sea from there, in the hope of reaching Phoenix somehow and wintering there. It is a harbor of Crete facing southwest and northwest.

## The Storm

**13** When a south wind began to blow gently, they thought they had what they wanted. So they weighed anchor and sailed along Crete, close to the shore.

**14** But before long a wind of hurricane force, called the Northeaster, rushed down from the island.

**15** The ship was caught and could not face the wind, so we gave way to it and were driven along.

**16** Running under the shelter of a small island called Cauda, we were able with difficulty to get the ship's boat under control.

**17** After hoisting it aboard, they used supports to hold the ship together, binding it round underneath. Then, afraid that they would run aground on the Syrtis, they lowered the gear, and so they were driven along.

**18** We were being battered so violently by the storm that the next day they began to throw the cargo overboard,

**19** and on the third day they threw the ship's tackle overboard with their own hands.

**20** When neither sun nor stars appeared for many days, and no small storm was pressing on us, at last all hope of our being saved was being taken from us.

## No Loss of Life

**21** They had gone a long time without food. Then Paul stood up among them and said, "Men, you should have listened to me and not put to sea from Crete, and so been spared this damage and loss.

**22** But now I urge you to keep up your courage, for there will be no loss of life among you, only of the ship.

**23** For last night an angel of the God to whom I belong and whom I worship stood by me

**24** and said, 'Do not be afraid, Paul. You must stand before Caesar, and God has granted you all who are sailing with you.'

**25** So keep up your courage, men, for I trust God that it will be just as I have been told.

**26** But we must run aground on some island."

## The Fourteenth Night

**27** When the fourteenth night came, as we were being driven across the Sea of Adria, about midnight the sailors suspected that they were nearing land.

**28** They took soundings and found twenty fathoms. A little farther on they took soundings again and found fifteen fathoms.

**29** Afraid that we might run aground somewhere on the rocks, they let down four anchors from the stern and prayed for day to come.

**30** The sailors were trying to escape from the ship, and had lowered the boat into the sea on the pretext of putting out anchors from the bow,

**31** when Paul said to the centurion and the soldiers, "Unless these men stay in the ship, you cannot be saved."

**32** Then the soldiers cut the ropes of the boat and let it fall away.

## Bread Before Dawn

**33** While day was coming on, Paul kept urging them all to take some food. "Today is the fourteenth day," he said, "that you have been waiting and going without food, having eaten nothing.

**34** So I urge you to take some food; this is for your safety. Not a hair will perish from the head of any of you."

**35** When he had said this, he took bread, gave thanks to God in front of them all, broke it, and began to eat.

**36** They all took courage and took some food themselves.

**37** In all we were two hundred and seventy-six souls on the ship.

**38** When they had eaten enough, they lightened the ship by throwing the wheat into the sea.

## The Shipwreck

**39** When day came, they did not recognize the land, but they noticed a bay with a beach, and they decided to run the ship ashore there if they could.

**40** So they cast off the anchors and left them in the sea, and at the same time loosened the ropes that held the steering oars. Then they hoisted the foresail to the wind and made for the beach.

**41** But they struck a place where two seas met and ran the vessel aground. The bow stuck fast and could not be moved, and the stern was being broken up by the pounding.

**42** The soldiers' plan was to kill the prisoners, so that none of them would swim away and escape.

**43** But the centurion, wanting to bring Paul safely through, kept them from their plan. He ordered those who could swim to jump overboard first and get to land,

**44** and the rest to follow, some on planks and some on pieces of the ship. In this way all were brought safely to land.

## Notes

- **v1 "we should sail"** — the *we* of the narrator comes back here, for the first time since Jerusalem (*Paul went in with us to James*, 21:18), and stays to the end of the voyage (28:16). The one telling the story is on the ship.
- **v9 "the Fast was already over"** — *tēn nēsteian*, **the Fast**, with no other name. The one day of fasting the Law sets is the Day of Atonement, *in the seventh month, on the tenth of the month, you shall afflict yourselves* (Leviticus 16:29). Luke dates the danger of the sea by Israel's calendar: the voyage is already late in the year.
- **vv10, 21–22 "damage and great loss… of our lives… no loss of life among you, only of the ship"** — *hybreōs kai pollēs zēmias*, and in v21 *tēn hybrin tautēn kai tēn zēmian*, **this damage and loss**: Paul's first warning, quoted back to them. Then he changes one part of it. In v10 the loss is of *the cargo and the ship but also of our lives*, *psychōn*; in v22 there will be *no loss of life*, *apobolē psychēs oudemia*, *only of the ship*. What changed between the two is the night of v23. In v37 the count is of *psychai*, **souls**: two hundred and seventy-six of them, and every one is kept.
- **v14 "the Northeaster"** — *Eurakylōn*, a sailors' word made of the Greek *Euros*, the east wind, and the Latin *Aquilo*, the north wind; it is in the New Testament only here. *Typhōnikos*, **of hurricane force**, is from *typhōn*, a whirlwind. In v15 the ship *could not face the wind*, *antophthalmein*, word for word **look it in the eye**.
- **v17 "they lowered the gear"** — *chalasantes to skeuos*, **having let down the gear**. *Skeuos* is any piece of equipment, and the verse does not say which: a sea anchor dragged behind to slow the drift, or the sail. Either way the point is the same: they are trying not to be blown onto the sands of the Syrtis.
- **vv18–19, 38 "throw the cargo overboard… lightened the ship"** — *ekbolēn epoiounto*, word for word **they made a throwing-out**, the words of the Greek Jonah, where the sailors in the storm *made a throwing-out of the gear that was in the ship into the sea, to lighten it* (Jonah 1:5, *ekbolēn epoiēsanto… tou kouphisthēnai*); v38's *ekouphizon*, **they lightened**, is the same verb. In Jonah the storm was there because of the man of God on board (Jonah 1:12). Here the man of God on board is the reason everyone lives: *God has granted you all who are sailing with you* (v24).
- **vv20, 31, 34, 43–44 "saved… safety… bring safely through… brought safely"** — one word family, *sōzō*, **save**, and *diasōzō*, **bring safely through**, six times in the chapter and twice more in the next (28:1, 4). It is the Gospel's word for salvation, *to seek and to save the lost* (Luke 19:10), here used of men in the sea.
- **vv23–24 "stood by me… You must stand before Caesar… God has granted you all"** — *parestē*, **stood by**, and *parastēnai*, **stand before**, are one verb: the angel stands by Paul because Paul must stand before Caesar. *Dei*, **it is necessary**, the word of the Lord's promise in the barracks, *so you must testify also in Rome* (23:11), and of v26, *we must run aground on some island*. *Kecharistai*, **has granted**, from *charis*, grace: the lives of the two hundred and seventy-six (v37) are given to Paul as a gift.
- **v29 "prayed for day to come"** — *ēuchonto*, from *euchomai*, which means **pray** or **wish**. Paul's *I would pray to God* (26:29) is the same verb.
- **vv34–35 "not a hair will perish from the head of any of you… he took bread, gave thanks to God… broke it"** — the words of Jesus, *not a hair of your head will perish* (Luke 21:18): *thrix… apo tēs kephalēs… apoleitai*, and the word *thrix*, hair, is in Luke and Acts only these two times. Then the three verbs of the Last Supper, *He took bread, and when He had given thanks, He broke it* (Luke 22:19, *labōn arton eucharistēsas eklasen*), done here in front of soldiers, sailors and prisoners, on a ship about to break up.
