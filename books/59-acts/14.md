# Acts 14

## Iconium

**1** At Iconium they went together into the synagogue of the Jews and spoke in such a way that a great number of both Jews and Greeks believed.

**2** But the Jews who would not believe stirred up the Gentiles and poisoned their minds against the brothers.

**3** So they stayed there a long time, speaking boldly for the Lord, who bore witness to the word of His grace, granting signs and wonders to be done by their hands.

**4** But the people of the city were divided; some sided with the Jews, and some with the apostles.

**5** When an attempt was made by both Gentiles and Jews, with their rulers, to mistreat them and stone them,

**6** they learned of it and fled to Lystra and Derbe, cities of Lycaonia, and to the surrounding region;

**7** and there they went on proclaiming the good news.

## Lystra

**8** At Lystra there sat a man who could not use his feet, lame from his mother's womb, who had never walked.

**9** He listened to Paul speaking. Paul looked straight at him, and seeing that he had faith to be made well,

**10** said with a loud voice, "Stand upright on your feet." He leaped up and began to walk.

**11** When the crowds saw what Paul had done, they raised their voices, saying in Lycaonian, "The gods have come down to us in the likeness of men!"

**12** They called Barnabas Zeus, and Paul Hermes, because he was the chief speaker.

**13** The priest of Zeus, whose temple was just outside the city, brought oxen and garlands to the gates and wanted to offer sacrifice with the crowds.

**14** But when the apostles Barnabas and Paul heard of it, they tore their garments and rushed out into the crowd, crying out,

**15** "Men, why are you doing these things? We too are men, of like nature with you, and we bring you good news, that you should turn from these empty things to the living God, who made the heaven and the earth and the sea and everything in them.

**16** In past generations He allowed all the nations to walk in their own ways;

**17** yet He did not leave Himself without witness, for He did good, giving you rains from heaven and fruitful seasons, filling your hearts with food and gladness."

**18** Even with these words they could scarcely keep the crowds from offering sacrifice to them.

**19** But Jews came there from Antioch and Iconium, and having won over the crowds, they stoned Paul and dragged him out of the city, supposing that he was dead.

**20** But when the disciples gathered around him, he got up and went into the city. The next day he went on with Barnabas to Derbe.

## The Return to Antioch

**21** When they had proclaimed the good news in that city and had made many disciples, they returned to Lystra and to Iconium and to Antioch,

**22** strengthening the souls of the disciples, encouraging them to remain in the faith, and saying that through many afflictions we must enter the kingdom of God.

**23** When they had appointed elders for them in every church, with prayer and fasting they committed them to the Lord in whom they had believed.

**24** Then they passed through Pisidia and came to Pamphylia.

**25** When they had spoken the word in Perga, they went down to Attalia,

**26** and from there they sailed to Antioch, where they had been committed to the grace of God for the work that they had completed.

**27** When they arrived and gathered the church together, they reported all that God had done with them, and how He had opened a door of faith to the Gentiles.

**28** They stayed there no little time with the disciples.

## Notes

- **vv1–7 "a great number of both Jews and Greeks believed… the word of His grace… the people of the city were divided"** — *ekakōsan tas psychas*, **poisoned their minds**, word for word **made their souls evil**. *Eschisthē*, **were divided**, a split, as the crowds over Jesus (John 7:43). *Apostolois*, **apostles**, here of Paul and Barnabas, the ones *sent*.
- **vv8–10 "lame from his mother's womb, who had never walked… Stand upright on your feet… He leaped up"** — the man of 3:2 was *lame from his mother's womb* in the same words, and Peter healed him as Paul heals this one. *Hēlato*, **leaped**, as *the lame will leap like a deer* (Isaiah 35:6). *Pistin tou sōthēnai*, **faith to be made well**, word for word **to be saved**.
- **vv11–13 "in Lycaonian… The gods have come down to us in the likeness of men… Zeus… Hermes… oxen and garlands"** — *Lykaonisti*, **in Lycaonian**, the speech of the land. *Hermēs*, Hermes, *because he was the chief speaker*. *Stemmata*, **garlands**, wreaths.
- **vv14–18 "they tore their garments… We too are men, of like nature with you… the living God, who made the heaven and the earth and the sea… giving you rains from heaven and fruitful seasons"** — *Homoiopatheis*, **of like nature**, of the same feelings and frailty. *The living God*, *the LORD is the true God; He is the living God* (Jeremiah 10:10), the Maker of *the heavens and the earth, the sea and everything in them* (Exodus 20:11). *Who gives the rain in its season* (Jeremiah 5:24); *You give them their food in due season* (Psalm 145:15). *Mataiōn*, **empty things**.
- **vv19–20 "they stoned Paul and dragged him out of the city, supposing that he was dead… he got up"** — Paul watched Stephen stoned (7:58); now he is the one stoned.
- **vv21–23 "through many afflictions we must enter the kingdom of God… appointed elders for them in every church, with prayer and fasting"** — *we must*, *dei*, with *us*: Paul speaks for himself as well. *Cheirotonēsantes*, **appointed**, word for word **stretching out the hand**, as in a vote. As the Spirit set them apart *after fasting and praying* (13:3).
- **vv24–28 "Perga… Attalia… committed to the grace of God for the work that they had completed… a door of faith to the Gentiles"** — the first journey ends where it began (13:1–3). *To ergon ho eplērōsan*, **the work that they had completed**, the *work to which I have called them* (13:2).
