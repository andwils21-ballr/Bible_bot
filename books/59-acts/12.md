# Acts 12

## James Killed, Peter Imprisoned

**1** About that time Herod the king laid violent hands on some who belonged to the church.

**2** He killed James the brother of John with the sword.

**3** When he saw that it pleased the Jews, he went on to arrest Peter also. (This was during the days of Unleavened Bread.)

**4** When he had seized him, he put him in prison and handed him over to four squads of four soldiers each to guard him, intending to bring him out to the people after the Passover.

**5** So Peter was kept in prison, but earnest prayer for him was being made to God by the church.

## The Angel and the Iron Gate

**6** On the very night when Herod was about to bring him out, Peter was sleeping between two soldiers, bound with two chains, and guards before the door were keeping watch over the prison.

**7** Suddenly an angel of the Lord stood there, and a light shone in the cell. He struck Peter on the side and woke him, saying, "Get up quickly." The chains fell from his hands.

**8** The angel said to him, "Fasten your belt and put on your sandals." He did so. Then he said to him, "Put your cloak around you and follow me."

**9** He went out and followed him. He did not know that what was being done by the angel was real, but thought he was seeing a vision.

**10** When they had passed the first and the second guard, they came to the iron gate leading into the city. It opened for them by itself, and they went out and went along one street, and at once the angel left him.

**11** When Peter came to himself, he said, "Now I know for certain that the Lord has sent His angel and rescued me from the hand of Herod and from all that the Jewish people were expecting."

**12** When he realized this, he went to the house of Mary, the mother of John who was called Mark, where many were gathered together and praying.

**13** When he knocked at the door of the gateway, a servant girl named Rhoda came to answer.

**14** Recognizing Peter's voice, in her joy she did not open the gate, but ran in and reported that Peter was standing at the gate.

**15** They said to her, "You are out of your mind!" But she kept insisting that it was so. They said, "It is his angel."

**16** But Peter kept on knocking, and when they opened, they saw him and were amazed.

**17** Motioning to them with his hand to be silent, he told them how the Lord had brought him out of the prison. He said, "Report these things to James and to the brothers." Then he departed and went to another place.

**18** When day came, there was no small stir among the soldiers over what had become of Peter.

**19** When Herod had searched for him and not found him, he examined the guards and ordered that they be led away. Then he went down from Judea to Caesarea and stayed there.

## The Death of Herod

**20** Herod was furious with the people of Tyre and Sidon. They came to him with one mind, and having won over Blastus, the king's chamberlain, they asked for peace, because their country was fed by the king's country.

**21** On an appointed day Herod put on his royal robes, took his seat on the throne and gave a speech to them.

**22** The people kept shouting, "The voice of a god, and not of a man!"

**23** At once an angel of the Lord struck him down, because he did not give God the glory; and he was eaten by worms and breathed his last.

**24** But the word of God kept growing and multiplying.

**25** Barnabas and Saul returned when they had completed their service in Jerusalem, bringing with them John who was called Mark.

## Notes

- **vv1–5 "Herod the king… killed James the brother of John with the sword… the days of Unleavened Bread… after the Passover"** — *Hērōdēs ho basileus*, **Herod the king**, not the Herod of the Gospels, who was *tetrarch of Galilee* (Luke 3:1). James is the son of Zebedee, called with his brother (Mark 3:17). The arrest falls at the Passover, as Jesus' did. *Four squads of four*, sixteen soldiers.
- **vv6–11 "sleeping between two soldiers, bound with two chains… an angel of the Lord… Fasten your belt and put on your sandals… the iron gate… opened… by itself"** — the Passover night was eaten *your waists belted, your sandals on your feet* (Exodus 12:11), ready to go out. *The messenger of the LORD camps around those who fear Him, and rescues them* (Psalm 34:7); *My God sent His messenger and shut the mouths of the lions* (Daniel 6:22). *Automatē*, **by itself**, the root of *automatic*. *He brought them out of darkness… and broke their chains apart* (Psalm 107:14).
- **vv12–17 "Mary, the mother of John who was called Mark… Rhoda… in her joy she did not open the gate… It is his angel… Report these things to James"** — *Rhodē*, **Rose**. *Mainē*, **You are out of your mind**, the same charge made against Paul (26:24). *Ho angelos estin autou*, **It is his angel**: they think his angel has come, as *their angels in heaven* (Matthew 18:10). *James* here is another James than the one killed in v2; a James speaks for the church at Jerusalem in 15:13 and 21:18.
- **vv18–19 "no small stir among the soldiers… ordered that they be led away"** — *apachthēnai*, **led away**, to execution.
- **vv20–23 "the king's chamberlain… The voice of a god, and not of a man!… because he did not give God the glory… eaten by worms"** — *koitōn*, **chamberlain**, the officer over the king's bedchamber. The king of Tyre said, *I am a god*, and was told, *yet you are a man and not a god* (Ezekiel 28:2). *The messenger of the LORD went out and struck down* the camp of Assyria (2 Kings 19:35); *epataxen*, **struck**, is the verb of v7, where the angel *struck* Peter to wake him. *Skōlēkobrōtos*, **eaten by worms**, one word.
- **vv24–25 "the word of God kept growing and multiplying… John who was called Mark"** — as 6:7. *Plērōsantes tēn diakonian*, **having completed their service**, the relief of 11:29–30.
