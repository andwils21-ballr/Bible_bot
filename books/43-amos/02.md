# Amos 2

## Moab

**1** This is what the LORD says:  
For three transgressions of Moab,  
and for four, I will not turn it back,  
because he burned the bones of the king of Edom to lime.

**2** I will send fire on Moab,  
and it will devour the strongholds of Kerioth;  
Moab will die in the uproar,  
with a war cry, with the sound of the horn.

**3** I will cut off the ruler from among her  
and kill all her princes with him,  
says the LORD.

## Judah

**4** This is what the LORD says:  
For three transgressions of Judah,  
and for four, I will not turn it back,  
because they rejected the law of the LORD  
and did not keep His statutes;  
their lies led them astray,  
the lies their fathers followed.

**5** I will send fire on Judah,  
and it will devour the strongholds of Jerusalem.

## Israel

**6** This is what the LORD says:  
For three transgressions of Israel,  
and for four, I will not turn it back,  
because they sell the righteous for silver,  
and the needy for a pair of sandals.

**7** They trample the head of the poor  
into the dust of the earth,  
and turn aside the way of the humble;  
a man and his father go to the same girl,  
and so profane My holy name.

**8** They stretch out beside every altar  
on garments taken in pledge,  
and in the house of their God they drink  
the wine of those who have been fined.

**9** Yet it was I who destroyed the Amorite before them,  
whose height was like the height of the cedars,  
and who was as strong as the oaks;  
I destroyed his fruit above  
and his roots below.

**10** It was I who brought you up from the land of Egypt  
and led you in the wilderness forty years,  
to take possession of the land of the Amorite.

**11** I raised up some of your sons as prophets  
and some of your young men as Nazirites.  
Is it not so, sons of Israel?  
declares the LORD.

**12** But you made the Nazirites drink wine,  
and you commanded the prophets, saying,  
"You shall not prophesy."

**13** I am about to press you down in your place,  
as a cart full of sheaves presses down.

**14** Flight will fail the swift;  
the strong will not muster his strength,  
and the warrior will not save his life.

**15** The one who handles the bow will not stand;  
the swift of foot will not escape,  
and the horseman will not save his life.

**16** The bravest of heart among the warriors  
will flee naked on that day,  
declares the LORD.

## Notes

- **v1 "he burned the bones of the king of Edom to lime"** — the charge against Moab is a wrong done to Edom, not to Israel. *Sid*, **lime**, is the word of *plaster them with plaster* (Deuteronomy 27:2): the bones burned down to powder. Moab and Edom were at war in the days of Elisha, when the king of Moab tried *to break through to the king of Edom, but they could not* (2 Kings 3:26).
- **vv2–3 "the strongholds of Kerioth… the ruler"** — Kerioth is a town of Moab in Jeremiah, *Kerioth is taken, and the strongholds are seized* (Jeremiah 48:41); *ha-qeriyyot* can also be read **the cities**. *Sha'on*, **uproar**, is Jeremiah's word for Moab, *the crown of the sons of tumult* (Jeremiah 48:45). *Shofet*, **the ruler**, is the word for a judge, used here of the one who rules Moab.
- **vv4–5 "they rejected the law of the LORD… their lies led them astray"** — the nations are charged with what they did to other peoples; Judah is charged with the law, as Hosea charged the north, *Because you have rejected knowledge… since you have forgotten the law of your God* (Hosea 4:6). *Kizveihem*, **their lies**, are the gods that failed them, and the fathers followed them first. The fire comes to Jerusalem in the words of the oracles before it.
- **v6 "they sell the righteous for silver, and the needy for a pair of sandals"** — *tsaddiq* is the one in the right in a case, as in *declaring the righteous one righteous and the wicked one wicked* (Deuteronomy 25:1). Sold *for silver*, he loses his case to a bribe, or he is sold for his debt; *for a pair of sandals*, *ba'avur na'alayim*, puts the price of a man at what is worn on the feet. The eighth oracle is Israel's, and only Israel's charge runs past one verse, through v8.
- **v7 "They trample the head of the poor into the dust of the earth"** — *ha-sho'afim*: the verb is **trample** in the psalm, *man tramples on me* (Psalm 56:1), and the Greek has *treading*. It is also **pant**, as in *they pant for air like jackals* (Jeremiah 14:6); read that way, the line is *who pant after the dust of the earth on the head of the poor*, men greedy even for the dust a mourner puts on his head, as the elders of Israel *put dust on their heads* (Joshua 7:6). Amos uses the word again in 8:4.
- **v7 "a man and his father go to the same girl, and so profane My holy name"** — *na'arah*, **a girl**; the Greek has *the same servant girl*. The law kept a slave girl given to a son from his father: *If he designates her for his son, he shall do for her according to the ruling for daughters* (Exodus 21:9). *To profane My holy name* is the law's own sentence, *You shall not profane My holy name* (Leviticus 22:32).
- **v8 "garments taken in pledge… the wine of those who have been fined"** — the law said of a poor man's cloak, *If you take your neighbor's cloak as a pledge, you shall return it to him before the sun goes down* (Exodus 22:26). Here they sleep on it beside the altar. *Anushim*, **those who have been fined**, is the verb of the law's penalty, *he shall be fined* (Exodus 21:22): the wine comes from fines, and it is drunk in the house of their God.
- **vv9–10 "the Amorite… his fruit above and his roots below… brought you up from the land of Egypt"** — the scouts said, *all the people we saw in it are men of great size* (Numbers 13:32). *His fruit above and his roots below* is the reverse of Isaiah's promise to Judah, *take root downward and bear fruit upward* (Isaiah 37:31): the Amorite was destroyed from the top and the bottom both. The conquest is told in v9 before the exodus in v10. *Forty years* is Moses' count, *I have led you forty years in the wilderness* (Deuteronomy 29:5).
- **vv11–12 "you made the Nazirites drink wine… You shall not prophesy"** — the Nazirite *shall keep apart from wine and strong drink* (Numbers 6:3). The two gifts of v11 are undone one each in v12: the Nazirite given wine, the prophet given silence. Amos himself is told not to prophesy at Bethel (7:12–13).
- **vv13–16 "I am about to press you down… Flight will fail the swift… flee naked on that day"** — *me'iq*, **press**, is found only in this verse, from the root of *the pressure of the wicked* (Psalm 55:3); the Greek has *I roll under you, as a wagon full of straw rolls*. *Ve-avad manos mi-qal*, **flight will fail the swift**, is Jeremiah's line, *Flight will fail the shepherds* (Jeremiah 25:35). Seven fighters are named in vv14–16, from the swift to the bravest of heart, and not one escapes.
