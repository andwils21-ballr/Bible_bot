# Wisdom of Solomon 4

**1** Better is childlessness with virtue,  
for in the memory of virtue is immortality,  
because it is known both by God and by men.

**2** When it is present, people imitate it,  
and they long for it when it has gone;  
and through all time it marches in triumph, wearing a crown,  
having won the contest for prizes that are undefiled.

**3** But the many children of the ungodly will be of no use;  
from bastard slips they will not strike deep roots  
or take a firm hold.

**4** For even if they put forth branches for a while,  
standing unsteadily they will be shaken by the wind,  
and by the violence of the winds they will be uprooted.

**5** The branches will be broken off before they come to maturity,  
and their fruit will be useless,  
not ripe enough to eat, and good for nothing.

**6** For children born of unlawful unions  
are witnesses of evil against their parents when they are examined.

**7** But the righteous man, though he die early, will be at rest.

**8** For old age is not honored for length of time,  
nor measured by number of years;

**9** but understanding is gray hair for men,  
and a spotless life is ripe old age.

**10** Having pleased God, he was loved by Him,  
and while living among sinners he was taken up.

**11** He was caught away so that evil might not change his understanding,  
or deceit beguile his soul.

**12** For the spell of worthlessness obscures what is good,  
and the roving of desire undermines an innocent mind.

**13** Made perfect in a short time,  
he fulfilled long years;

**14** for his soul was pleasing to the Lord;  
therefore He took him quickly from the wickedness around him.  
But the peoples saw and did not understand,  
nor take such a thing to heart,

**15** that grace and mercy are with His chosen ones,  
and that He watches over His holy ones.

**16** The righteous man who has died will condemn the ungodly who are living,  
and youth quickly made perfect will condemn the long old age of the unrighteous.

**17** For they will see the end of the wise man,  
and will not understand what the Lord purposed for him,  
and for what end He kept him safe.

**18** They will see, and will despise him,  
but the Lord will laugh them to scorn.

**19** After this they will become a dishonored corpse,  
and an outrage among the dead forever,  
for He will dash them speechless and headlong,  
and shake them from their foundations;  
they will be left utterly dry and barren,  
and they will be in anguish,  
and the memory of them will perish.

**20** They will come with dread when their sins are counted up,  
and their lawless deeds will convict them to their face.

## Notes

- **v10 "Having pleased God… he was taken up"** — the words of Enoch in the Greek of Genesis 5:24: *Enoch pleased God… because God took him up* (*euarestein*, *metatithenai*). The book does not name him; it makes his story the pattern for every righteous person who dies young. Hebrews 11:5 uses the same two verbs of Enoch.
- **v11 "He was caught away"** — *hērpagē*, the verb of a sudden snatching away (1 Thessalonians 4:17, *we… shall be caught up*).
- **v12 "the spell of worthlessness"** — *baskania*, the evil eye, a bewitching glance that darkens what is good.
- **vv13–14 "Made perfect in a short time, he fulfilled long years"** — a short life can be a full one; the measure is not years (vv8–9).
- **v15** — the line of 3:9, "grace and mercy are with His chosen ones," now with God's watching over His holy ones.
- **v18 "the Lord will laugh them to scorn"** — *ekgelasetai*, the verb of the Greek of Psalm 2:4, *He who sits in the heavens will laugh at them*.
- **v19 "dash them speechless and headlong"** — *prēneis*, face down: the fall of those who mocked the righteous (2:17–20).
