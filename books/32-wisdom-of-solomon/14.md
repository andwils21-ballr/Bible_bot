# Wisdom of Solomon 14

**1** Again, one who is setting out on a voyage  
and is about to cross wild waves  
calls upon a piece of wood more fragile than the ship that carries him.

**2** For it was desire for gain that planned that ship,  
and wisdom, the fashioner, built it;

**3** but it is Your providence, Father, that steers it,  
because You have given it a way in the sea,  
and a safe path through the waves,

**4** showing that You can save from every danger,  
so that even one without skill may go aboard.

**5** It is Your will that the works of Your wisdom should not be idle;  
therefore men trust their lives even to the smallest piece of wood,  
and passing through the surge on a raft they come safely through.

**6** For even in the beginning, when the proud giants were perishing,  
the hope of the world took refuge on a raft,  
and, steered by Your hand,  
left to the world the seed of a new generation.

**7** For blessed is the wood through which righteousness comes.

**8** But the idol made with hands is accursed, and so is he who made it —  
he because he made it,  
and the perishable thing because it was called a god.

**9** For equally hateful to God  
are the ungodly man and his ungodliness;

**10** for what was done will be punished together with the one who did it.

**11** Therefore there will be a visitation also on the idols of the nations,  
because, though part of God's creation, they became a detestable thing,  
a stumbling block to the souls of men,  
and a snare to the feet of the foolish.

**12** For the devising of idols was the beginning of fornication,  
and their invention was the corruption of life.

**13** For they did not exist from the beginning,  
nor will they exist forever;

**14** through the vanity of men they entered the world,  
and therefore their speedy end has been planned.

**15** For a father, consumed with grief at an untimely bereavement,  
made an image of his child, so suddenly taken from him;  
and he now honored as a god what was once a dead human being,  
and handed on to his household secret rites and initiations.

**16** Then the ungodly custom, grown strong with time, was kept as a law,  
and at the command of monarchs carved images were worshipped.

**17** When men could not honor rulers in their presence,  
because they lived far off,  
they pictured their distant appearance  
and made a visible image of the king they honored,  
so that by their zeal they might flatter the absent one as though present.

**18** Then the ambition of the craftsman  
spurred on even those who did not know the king  
to greater worship.

**19** For he, perhaps wishing to please his ruler,  
skillfully forced the likeness into a finer form,

**20** and the multitude, drawn by the charm of his work,  
now counted as an object of worship  
the one whom shortly before they had honored as a man.

**21** This became a trap for mankind,  
because men, enslaved to misfortune or to royal power,  
gave to stones and wood the name that is not to be shared.

**22** Afterward it was not enough for them to err about the knowledge of God,  
but though they live in a great war of ignorance,  
they call such great evils peace.

**23** For whether they perform child-murdering rites, or secret mysteries,  
or hold frenzied revels with strange customs,

**24** they no longer keep either their lives or their marriages pure,  
but one kills another by treachery,  
or grieves him by adultery,

**25** and all is a confused mixture of blood and murder,  
theft and deceit, corruption, faithlessness,  
tumult, perjury, confusion over what is good,

**26** forgetfulness of favors, defilement of souls,  
exchange of nature, disorder in marriage,  
adultery and debauchery.

**27** For the worship of nameless idols  
is the beginning and cause and end of every evil.

**28** For their worshippers either rave in their merriment,  
or prophesy lies, or live unrighteously,  
or readily commit perjury;

**29** for since they trust in lifeless idols,  
they swear wicked oaths and expect to suffer no harm.

**30** But just penalties will overtake them on both counts:  
because they thought wrongly about God in devoting themselves to idols,  
and because in deceit they swore unrighteously,  
despising holiness.

**31** For it is not the power of the things by which men swear,  
but the penalty owed to those who sin,  
that always pursues the transgression of the unrighteous.

## Notes

- **v3 "Your providence, Father"** — *pronoia*, God's forethought, which steers the ship that wisdom built. "A way in the sea, and a safe path through the waves" — Isaiah 43:16, *who makes a way in the sea, a path in the mighty waters*.
- **vv6–7 "the proud giants… the hope of the world took refuge on a raft… blessed is the wood"** — the giants of Genesis 6:4 and Noah's ark (10:4, "a paltry piece of wood"): the wood that God steers is blessed, the wood men carve into gods is cursed (v8).
- **v12 "the beginning of fornication"** — *porneia*, in the Prophets' sense of giving oneself to other gods (Hosea 4:12; Jeremiah 3:9), and in the plain sense that vv24–26 list.
- **vv15–21** — an account of how idol worship began: a grieving father's image of his dead child, then images of distant kings, polished by craftsmen until the crowd worships them.
- **v21 "the name that is not to be shared"** — *to akoinōnēton onoma*: the name of God, which belongs to Him alone (Isaiah 42:8, *My glory I give to no other*).
- **v22 "they call such great evils peace"** — as in Jeremiah 6:14, *saying, "Peace, peace," when there is no peace*.
- **v26 "exchange of nature"** — *geneseōs enallagē*, "the exchange of what one is by birth"; Paul says those who exchanged God's glory for images *exchanged natural relations for those that are against nature* (Romans 1:23, 26).
- **v27 "nameless idols"** — *anōnymōn*: gods without names worth speaking (Exodus 23:13, *The name of other gods you shall not mention*), set against "the name" of v21.
