# Wisdom of Solomon 1

**1** Love righteousness, you who judge the earth;  
think of the Lord with goodness,  
and seek Him in simplicity of heart,

**2** for He is found by those who do not test Him,  
and shows Himself to those who do not distrust Him.

**3** For crooked thoughts separate from God,  
and His power, when it is tested, exposes the foolish.

**4** Wisdom will not enter a soul that plots evil,  
nor dwell in a body in debt to sin.

**5** For the holy spirit of discipline flees deceit,  
and rises and leaves foolish thoughts,  
and is put to shame when wrongdoing draws near.

**6** For wisdom is a spirit that loves mankind,  
and will not hold a blasphemer guiltless for his words,  
because God is witness of his inmost being,  
a true overseer of his heart,  
and a hearer of his tongue.

**7** For the Spirit of the Lord has filled the world,  
and that which holds all things together  
knows every voice.

**8** Therefore no one who speaks what is unrighteous will escape notice,  
and justice, when it convicts, will not pass him by.

**9** For inquiry will be made into the plans of the ungodly,  
and the report of his words will come to the Lord  
to convict him of his lawless deeds;

**10** for a jealous ear hears all things,  
and the murmur of grumbling is not hidden.

**11** Beware, then, of useless grumbling,  
and keep your tongue from slander;  
for no secret word goes for nothing,  
and a lying mouth kills the soul.

**12** Do not seek death by the error of your life,  
nor bring on destruction by the works of your hands;

**13** for God did not make death,  
and He takes no delight in the destruction of the living.

**14** For He created all things that they might exist,  
and the things that come into being in the world are wholesome;  
there is no poison of destruction in them,  
and the kingdom of Hades is not on earth.

**15** For righteousness is immortal.

**16** But the ungodly summoned death by their hands and their words;  
thinking him a friend, they pined for him,  
and made a covenant with him,  
because they are fit to belong to his party.

## Notes

- **v1 "Love righteousness, you who judge the earth"** — rendered from the Greek, in which the book was written; Swete's text, verse numbers as the English. The book speaks in the voice of the king who prayed for wisdom (9:7–8; 1 Kings 3:9), though it never gives his name. It opens by calling to rulers, as Psalm 2:10 does: *Now therefore, kings, be wise; be warned, judges of the earth*, where the Greek has the same *hoi krinontes tēn gēn*.
- **v5 "the holy spirit of discipline"** — *hagion pneuma paideias*: the spirit of wisdom (v6, "wisdom is a spirit"), set in lowercase since it is not named "of God" or "of the Lord." "Discipline" is *paideia*, the Greek Proverbs' word for *musar*, "discipline" (Proverbs 1:2, 7).
- **v6 "a spirit that loves mankind"** — *philanthrōpon*, the word behind "philanthropy." "His inmost being" — literally "his kidneys," the seat of feeling beside the heart (Psalm 7:9; Jeremiah 17:10).
- **v7 "the Spirit of the Lord… that which holds all things together"** — *to synechon ta panta*, neuter, the power that keeps the world from falling apart. Paul says the same of Christ with a related verb: *in Him all things hold together* (Colossians 1:17).
- **vv13–14 "God did not make death… He created all things that they might exist"** — death is no part of the world God made; the next chapter says how it came in (2:24).
- **v16 "made a covenant with him"** — *synthēkē*, the word of Isaiah 28:15 in the Greek: *we have made a covenant with Hades, and an agreement with death*. "Death" in the first line names the "him" of the Greek.
