# Wisdom of Solomon 10

**1** She guarded the first-formed father of the world,  
when he alone had been created,  
and she delivered him from his own transgression,

**2** and gave him strength to rule over all things.

**3** But when an unrighteous man turned away from her in his anger,  
he perished in his rage that murdered his brother.

**4** When the earth was flooded because of him,  
wisdom saved it again,  
steering the righteous man on a paltry piece of wood.

**5** She, when the nations in their common wickedness had been confounded,  
recognized the righteous man and kept him blameless before God,  
and kept him strong against his tenderness for his child.

**6** She rescued a righteous man when the ungodly were perishing,  
as he fled the fire that came down on the Five Cities.

**7** Evidence of their wickedness still remains:  
a smoking wasteland,  
plants that bear fruit that never ripens,  
and a pillar of salt standing  
as a monument to an unbelieving soul.

**8** For because they passed wisdom by,  
they were not only kept from knowing what is good,  
but also left the world a reminder of their folly,  
so that their failures could not go unnoticed.

**9** But wisdom rescued from troubles those who served her.

**10** She, when a righteous man fled from his brother's wrath,  
guided him on straight paths;  
she showed him the kingdom of God,  
and gave him knowledge of holy things;  
she prospered him in his hardships,  
and made his labors abundant.

**11** When those who oppressed him were greedy,  
she stood by him and made him rich.

**12** She protected him from his enemies,  
and kept him safe from those who lay in wait for him;  
in his hard contest she gave him the victory,  
so that he might know that godliness is stronger than anything.

**13** She, when a righteous man was sold, did not abandon him,  
but rescued him from sin.

**14** She went down with him into the pit,  
and did not leave him in his chains,  
until she brought him the scepter of a kingdom  
and authority over those who had lorded it over him;  
she showed those who had accused him to be liars,  
and gave him everlasting glory.

**15** She delivered a holy people and a blameless seed  
from a nation of oppressors.

**16** She entered the soul of the Lord's servant,  
and withstood dread kings with wonders and signs.

**17** She gave holy people the reward of their labors;  
she guided them along a marvelous way,  
and became a shelter for them by day  
and a flame of stars through the night.

**18** She brought them over the Red Sea,  
and led them through deep waters;

**19** but she drowned their enemies,  
and cast them up from the depth of the abyss.

**20** Therefore the righteous plundered the ungodly;  
they sang, Lord, of Your holy name,  
and praised with one accord Your hand that fought for them;

**21** for wisdom opened the mouths of the mute,  
and made the tongues of infants speak clearly.

## Notes

- **vv1–21** — the story from Adam to the Exodus, told without a single name. Each turn opens with "She" (*hautē*): wisdom is the one at work. The names are Adam (vv1–2), Cain (v3), Noah (v4), Abraham (v5), Lot (vv6–7), Jacob (vv10–12), Joseph (vv13–14), Moses and Israel (vv15–21).
- **v4 "because of him"** — the Flood is laid at Cain's door: the violence that filled the earth (Genesis 6:11) began with his. "A paltry piece of wood" — the ark.
- **v5 "when the nations… had been confounded"** — Babel (Genesis 11:9), which comes before Abraham's call. "Against his tenderness for his child" — Isaac on the altar (Genesis 22).
- **vv6–7 "the Five Cities… a pillar of salt"** — the cities of the plain (Genesis 14:2), and Lot's wife, who looked back (19:26). "Fruit that never ripens" — *atelesin hōrais*, fruit whose seasons never come to their end.
- **v10 "she showed him the kingdom of God, and gave him knowledge of holy things"** — Jacob at Bethel, the ladder and God above it (Genesis 28:12–17). *Hagiōn* may also be "holy ones," the messengers on the ladder.
- **v12 "in his hard contest she gave him the victory"** — Jacob at the Jabbok, *you have striven with God and with men, and have prevailed* (Genesis 32:28).
- **v14 "into the pit"** — *lakkos*, the dungeon where Joseph lay (Genesis 40:15). "Those who had accused him" — Potiphar's wife (39:17–18).
- **v16 "the Lord's servant"** — *therapōn*, the word the Greek uses of Moses, *My servant Moses… faithful in all My house* (Numbers 12:7), taken up in Hebrews 3:5.
- **v18 "the Red Sea"** — the Greek name for the sea the Hebrew calls *yam suf*, the Sea of Reeds (Exodus 15:4, 22).
- **v21 "opened the mouths of the mute… the tongues of infants"** — Moses, slow of speech, given a mouth (Exodus 4:10–12); and Psalm 8:2, *out of the mouth of children and nursing infants*.
