# Wisdom of Solomon 8

**1** She reaches mightily from one end to the other,  
and she orders all things well.

**2** I loved her and sought her from my youth;  
I desired to take her for my bride,  
and I became a lover of her beauty.

**3** She glorifies her noble birth by living with God,  
and the Master of all loved her.

**4** For she is an initiate in the knowledge of God,  
and the one who chooses His works.

**5** If riches are a desirable possession in life,  
what is richer than wisdom, who works all things?

**6** If understanding is at work,  
who more than she is the fashioner of what exists?

**7** If anyone loves righteousness,  
her labors are virtues;  
for she teaches self-control and understanding,  
righteousness and courage;  
nothing in life is more useful for men than these.

**8** If anyone longs for wide experience,  
she knows the things of old, and infers the things to come;  
she understands turns of speech and the answers to riddles;  
she knows beforehand signs and wonders,  
and the outcomes of seasons and times.

**9** Therefore I resolved to take her to live with me,  
knowing that she would give me good counsel,  
and encouragement in cares and grief.

**10** Because of her I shall have glory among the crowds  
and honor before the elders, though I am young.

**11** I shall be found keen in judgment,  
and in the sight of rulers I shall be admired.

**12** When I am silent they will wait for me,  
and when I speak they will pay attention;  
and if I speak at length,  
they will put their hands on their mouths.

**13** Because of her I shall have immortality,  
and leave an everlasting memory to those who come after me.

**14** I shall govern peoples,  
and nations will be subject to me;

**15** dread monarchs will be afraid when they hear of me;  
among the people I shall show myself good,  
and in war courageous.

**16** When I come into my house, I shall find rest with her,  
for companionship with her has no bitterness,  
and life with her has no pain,  
but gladness and joy.

**17** When I had considered these things within myself,  
and pondered in my heart  
that in kinship with wisdom there is immortality,

**18** and in friendship with her pure delight,  
and in the labors of her hands unfailing wealth,  
and in the practice of her company understanding,  
and renown in sharing her words,  
I went about seeking how to take her for my own.

**19** As a child I was gifted by nature,  
and a good soul fell to my lot;

**20** or rather, being good, I came into an undefiled body.

**21** But I knew that I would not possess her unless God gave her —  
and it was a mark of understanding to know whose gift she was —  
so I appealed to the Lord and entreated Him,  
and with my whole heart I said:

## Notes

- **v1 "She reaches mightily from one end to the other"** — the close of 7:22–30: wisdom passes through all things and sets them in order, *diatenei… dioikei*, "she stretches… she governs."
- **v2 "to take her for my bride"** — wisdom as the wife a young man should seek, as in Proverbs 4:6–9 and 7:4, *Say to wisdom, "You are my sister."*
- **vv3, 9, 16 "living with God… to live with me… life with her"** — *symbiōsis*, "living together," each time: she shares God's life, and the king wants her to share his.
- **v4 "an initiate in the knowledge of God"** — *mystis*, one admitted to secret rites: she knows God's knowledge from the inside. "The one who chooses His works" — *hairetis*: she chooses what He makes, or works beside Him in it (7:22, "the fashioner of all things").
- **v7 "self-control and understanding, righteousness and courage"** — *sōphrosynē, phronēsis, dikaiosynē, andreia*, the four virtues the Greeks counted chief. The book says wisdom teaches all four.
- **v12 "they will put their hands on their mouths"** — as the princes did before Job, *laid their hand on their mouth* (Job 29:9).
- **vv19–20 "a good soul fell to my lot; or rather, being good, I came into an undefiled body"** — the speaker corrects himself: not a body that was given a soul, but a good self that came into a body.
- **v21 "I would not possess her"** — *enkratēs*, "having mastery," with no object written: of wisdom, or of himself, "self-controlled" (the word of Titus 1:8). "To know whose gift she was" — wisdom is asked for, not earned (James 1:5).
