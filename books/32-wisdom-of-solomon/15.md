# Wisdom of Solomon 15

**1** But You, our God, are kind and true,  
patient, and governing all things in mercy.

**2** For even if we sin, we are Yours, knowing Your power;  
but we will not sin, knowing that we are counted as Yours.

**3** For to know You is complete righteousness,  
and to know Your power is the root of immortality.

**4** For neither has the evil invention of human art misled us,  
nor the fruitless toil of painters,  
a figure smeared with varied colors,

**5** the sight of which brings fools to disgrace,  
so that they long for the breathless form of a dead image.

**6** Lovers of evil, and worthy of such hopes,  
are those who make them, those who long for them, and those who worship them.

**7** For a potter kneads the soft earth  
and laboriously molds each vessel for our service;  
from the same clay he fashions  
both the vessels that serve clean uses and those for the opposite,  
all alike;  
but what the use of each shall be,  
the potter decides.

**8** With misspent toil he forms a futile god from the same clay —  
this man who a short time before was made of earth  
and after a little while goes back to the earth from which he was taken,  
when the soul that was lent him is demanded back.

**9** But his concern is not that he must grow weary,  
nor that his life is short,  
but he competes with goldsmiths and silversmiths,  
and imitates workers in bronze,  
and counts it his glory that he molds counterfeits.

**10** His heart is ashes,  
his hope is cheaper than dirt,  
and his life is of less worth than clay,

**11** because he did not know the One who formed him  
and inspired him with an active soul  
and breathed into him a living spirit.

**12** But he considered our life a game,  
and our existence a festival for profit,  
for he says one must make gain from anything, even from evil.

**13** For this man knows better than all others that he sins,  
when he makes from earthy matter  
fragile vessels and carved images.

**14** But most foolish of all, and more miserable than an infant's soul,  
are the enemies of Your people who oppressed them.

**15** For they thought that all the idols of the nations were gods,  
which have no use of their eyes to see,  
nor noses to draw breath,  
nor ears to hear,  
nor fingers on their hands to feel,  
and their feet are useless for walking.

**16** For a man made them,  
and one whose spirit is borrowed formed them;  
for no man can form a god like himself.

**17** He is mortal, and what he makes with lawless hands is dead;  
for he is better than the objects he worships,  
since he has life, but they never have.

**18** They worship even the most hateful animals,  
which, compared with others for stupidity, are worse;

**19** and even as animals they have no beauty to make them desirable,  
but have escaped both the praise of God and His blessing.

## Notes

- **v1 "kind and true, patient"** — the words God spoke of Himself to Moses, *slow to anger, and abundant in loyal love and truth*; the Greek of Exodus 34:6 has the same *makrothymos*, "patient."
- **v3 "to know You is complete righteousness"** — compare John 17:3, *this is eternal life, that they know You, the only true God*.
- **v7 "what the use of each shall be, the potter decides"** — Jeremiah 18:6; Paul, *has the potter no right over the clay, to make out of the same lump one vessel for honor and another for dishonor?* (Romans 9:21, with the same *kerameus*, *pēlos*, "from the same").
- **v8 "goes back to the earth from which he was taken"** — Genesis 3:19. "When the soul that was lent him is demanded back" — *apaitētheis*, the verb of Luke 12:20, *this night your soul is demanded of you*.
- **v10 "His heart is ashes"** — Isaiah 44:20, *he feeds on ashes*, said of the man who worships what his own hands made.
- **v11 "breathed into him a living spirit"** — *emphysēsanta*, the verb of the Greek of Genesis 2:7, *He breathed into his face the breath of life*. The potter forgets the Potter who made him.
- **v15 "no use of their eyes to see, nor noses… nor ears… and their feet are useless"** — Psalm 115:4–7 and 135:15–17.
- **v16 "one whose spirit is borrowed"** — *to pneuma dedanismenos*: his breath is on loan from God (v8).
- **vv18–19 "the most hateful animals… escaped both the praise of God and His blessing"** — Egypt worshipped snakes and crocodiles; at creation God saw the animals were good and blessed them (Genesis 1:21–25), but the serpent was cursed (3:14).
