# Wisdom of Solomon 18

**1** But for Your holy ones there was very great light.  
The others heard their voices but did not see their forms,  
and because these too had suffered, they called them blessed,

**2** and they gave thanks that, though wronged before, they did them no harm,  
and begged their pardon for having been their enemies.

**3** Instead of this You gave a pillar of flaming fire  
as a guide for their unknown journey,  
and a harmless sun for their honorable wandering.

**4** For those men deserved to be deprived of light and imprisoned in darkness,  
who had kept Your sons imprisoned,  
through whom the imperishable light of the law was to be given to the world.

**5** When they had resolved to kill the infants of Your holy ones,  
and one child had been exposed and rescued,  
You took away a multitude of their children in punishment,  
and destroyed them all together in mighty water.

**6** That night was made known beforehand to our fathers,  
so that, knowing for certain the oaths they trusted,  
they might take heart.

**7** Your people looked for the salvation of the righteous  
and the destruction of their enemies.

**8** For by the same means by which You punished our adversaries,  
You called us to Yourself and glorified us.

**9** For the holy children of good men offered sacrifice in secret,  
and with one mind they bound themselves to the divine law,  
that the holy ones would share alike the same good things and dangers,  
while the fathers already led the songs of praise.

**10** But the discordant cry of their enemies echoed back,  
and the piteous lament for children was carried far.

**11** The slave was punished with the same penalty as the master,  
and the common man suffered the same as the king;

**12** all together, by one kind of death,  
they had dead past counting.  
For the living were not enough even to bury them,  
since in one instant their most prized offspring had been destroyed.

**13** For though they had disbelieved everything because of their sorceries,  
when their firstborn were destroyed,  
they acknowledged Your people to be God's son.

**14** For while gentle silence enveloped all things,  
and night in its swift course was at its middle,

**15** Your all-powerful word leaped from heaven, from the royal throne,  
a stern warrior, into the middle of the doomed land,

**16** carrying the sharp sword of Your unfeigned command;  
and standing, it filled all things with death,  
and touched heaven while it stood on the earth.

**17** Then at once apparitions in dreadful dreams terrified them,  
and unexpected fears came upon them;

**18** and one here, another there, thrown down half dead,  
made known why he was dying;

**19** for the dreams that troubled them had foretold this,  
so that they might not perish without knowing why they suffered.

**20** The trial of death touched the righteous also,  
and a plague came upon the multitude in the wilderness;  
but the wrath did not last long.

**21** For a blameless man was quick to fight for them;  
he brought the weapon of his own ministry,  
prayer and the atonement of incense;  
he withstood the anger and put an end to the disaster,  
showing that he was Your servant.

**22** He overcame the trouble, not by strength of body,  
not by force of arms,  
but by a word he subdued the punisher,  
appealing to the oaths and covenants given to the fathers.

**23** For when the dead had already fallen on one another in heaps,  
he stood between and held back the wrath,  
and cut off its way to the living.

**24** For on his long robe the whole world was shown,  
and the glories of the fathers were engraved on the four rows of stones,  
and Your majesty was on the diadem upon his head.

**25** To these the destroyer yielded, and these were feared;  
for the mere taste of the wrath was enough.

## Notes

- **vv1–2** — the Greek of these lines is hard. As it stands, the Egyptians in the dark hear the Israelites, call them blessed because they too had suffered (under Egypt) and were now spared, and beg pardon of them.
- **v3 "a pillar of flaming fire"** — *by night in a pillar of fire to give them light* (Exodus 13:21).
- **v4 "the imperishable light of the law was to be given to the world"** — Israel as *a light to the nations* (Isaiah 42:6; 49:6).
- **v5 "one child had been exposed and rescued… destroyed them all together in mighty water"** — Moses, saved from the Nile (Exodus 2:3–10); and the sea that drowned Pharaoh's army (14:28). Those who drowned Israel's sons were drowned.
- **v9 "offered sacrifice in secret"** — the Passover, kept in each house behind the blood on the doorposts (Exodus 12:3–11, 21–28).
- **v13 "Your people to be God's son"** — the word God sent to Pharaoh: *Israel is My son, My firstborn* (Exodus 4:22). The death of Egypt's firstborn answered it.
- **vv14–16 "Your all-powerful word leaped from heaven… a stern warrior… touched heaven while it stood on the earth"** — the destroyer of the Passover night (Exodus 12:23, 29), drawn like *the messenger of the LORD standing between earth and heaven, with a drawn sword in his hand* (1 Chronicles 21:16). Revelation names a rider *the Word of God*, from whose mouth comes *a sharp sword* (Revelation 19:13, 15).
- **vv20–23 "a blameless man… prayer and the atonement of incense… he stood between"** — Aaron in the plague after Korah's rebellion: *He stood between the dead and the living, and the plague was stopped* (Numbers 16:46–48). "Your servant" — *therapōn*, the word used of Moses in 10:16.
- **v24 "on his long robe the whole world… the four rows of stones… the diadem"** — the high priest's garments (Exodus 28:4, 17–21, 36–38): the robe, the twelve stones engraved with the tribes' names, the gold plate on his turban.
- **v25 "the destroyer"** — *ho olothreuōn*, the destroyer of Exodus 12:23; Paul speaks of those *destroyed by the destroyer* in the wilderness (1 Corinthians 10:10).
