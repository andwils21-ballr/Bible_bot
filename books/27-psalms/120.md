# Psalms 120

*A song of ascents.*

**1** In my distress I called to the LORD,  
and He answered me.

**2** LORD, deliver my soul from lying lips,  
from a deceitful tongue.

**3** What will He give you,  
and what more will He do to you,  
you deceitful tongue?

**4** A warrior's sharp arrows,  
with burning coals of the broom tree!

**5** Woe is me, that I live as a guest in Meshech,  
that I dwell among the tents of Kedar!

**6** Too long has my soul dwelt  
with those who hate peace.

**7** I am for peace,  
but when I speak, they are for war.

---

## Notes

- **The title, "A song of ascents"** — the first of fifteen (120–134), probably sung by pilgrims going up to Jerusalem for the feasts, or on the steps of the temple.
- **v3 "What will He give you, and what more will He do to you"** — the form of an oath, *May God do so to you and more also* (1 Samuel 3:17), turned on the lying tongue.
- **v4 "coals of the broom tree"** — the broom's wood burns hot and long (1 Kings 19:4); lies wound like arrows and burn like coals, and God's judgment answers them in kind.
- **v5 "Meshech… Kedar"** — a people of the far north (Genesis 10:2; Ezekiel 38:2) and a tribe of the Arabian desert (Genesis 25:13); not one place but a picture of living far from home among hostile people.
- **v7 "I am for peace"** — literally "I — peace"; the pilgrim song begins far from Zion, longing for the city of peace (122:6–8).
