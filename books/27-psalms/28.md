# Psalms 28

*Of David.*

**1** To You, LORD, I call;  
my rock, do not be deaf to me,  
or, if You are silent to me,  
I will become like those who go down to the pit.

**2** Hear the sound of my pleas for mercy  
when I cry to You for help,  
when I lift up my hands  
toward Your holy inner sanctuary.

**3** Do not drag me away with the wicked,  
with those who do iniquity,  
who speak peace with their neighbors  
while evil is in their hearts.

**4** Give to them according to their work  
and according to the evil of their deeds;  
give to them according to the work of their hands;  
pay them back what they deserve.

**5** Because they do not consider the deeds of the LORD  
or the work of His hands,  
He will tear them down and not build them up.

**6** Blessed be the LORD,  
for He has heard the sound of my pleas for mercy.

**7** The LORD is my strength and my shield;  
in Him my heart trusts, and I am helped;  
my heart exults,  
and with my song I give thanks to Him.

**8** The LORD is their strength;  
He is the saving stronghold of His anointed.

**9** Save Your people and bless Your inheritance;  
shepherd them and carry them forever.

## Notes

- **v1 "do not be deaf to me"** — *al techerash*, the silence of God is the psalmist's fear; **the pit**, *bor*, is the grave (30:3; 88:4).
- **v2 "Your holy inner sanctuary"** — *devir*, the innermost room of the temple, where the ark stood (1 Kings 6:5, 19–23). Prayer was made with lifted hands toward it (1 Kings 8:29–30; 1 Timothy 2:8).
- **v3 "who speak peace with their neighbors while evil is in their hearts"** — the double heart of 12:2; *with his mouth each speaks peace to his neighbor, but in his heart he plans an ambush* (Jeremiah 9:8).
- **v4 "pay them back what they deserve"** — the plea that God's justice match the deed (62:12; Revelation 22:12).
- **v5 "He will tear them down and not build them up"** — the words of Jeremiah's call, *to break down… to build* (Jeremiah 1:10; 24:6).
- **v6 "Blessed be the LORD, for He has heard"** — the turn from plea to thanks comes in the middle of the psalm, as in 6:8–9 and 22:21.
- **v8 "The LORD is their strength"** — *oz lamo*, **strength to them**, His people; the Greek, Syriac and some Hebrew manuscripts read *le-ammo*, **the strength of His people**. **His anointed** is the king (2:2; 20:6).
- **v9 "shepherd them and carry them forever"** — the LORD as shepherd (23:1), who carries His lambs in His arms (Isaiah 40:11; Deuteronomy 1:31).
