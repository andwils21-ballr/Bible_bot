# Psalms 51

*For the director. A psalm of David, when Nathan the prophet came to him, after he had gone in to Bathsheba.*

**1** Be gracious to me, God, according to Your loyal love;  
according to the greatness of Your compassion, blot out my transgressions.

**2** Wash me thoroughly from my iniquity,  
and cleanse me from my sin.

**3** For I know my transgressions,  
and my sin is always before me.

**4** Against You, You only, have I sinned  
and done what is evil in Your sight,  
so that You are right when You speak  
and blameless when You judge.

**5** I was brought forth in iniquity,  
and in sin my mother conceived me.

**6** You desire truth in the inward parts,  
and in the hidden place You teach me wisdom.

**7** Purge me with hyssop, and I will be clean;  
wash me, and I will be whiter than snow.

**8** Let me hear joy and gladness;  
let the bones You have crushed rejoice.

**9** Hide Your face from my sins,  
and blot out all my iniquities.

**10** Create in me a clean heart, God,  
and renew a steadfast spirit within me.

**11** Do not cast me away from Your presence,  
and do not take Your Holy Spirit from me.

**12** Restore to me the joy of Your salvation,  
and uphold me with a willing spirit.

**13** Then I will teach transgressors Your ways,  
and sinners will return to You.

**14** Deliver me from bloodguilt, God,  
the God of my salvation,  
and my tongue will sing aloud of Your righteousness.

**15** Lord, open my lips,  
and my mouth will declare Your praise.

**16** For You do not delight in sacrifice, or I would give it;  
You are not pleased with burnt offering.

**17** The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit;  
a broken and crushed heart, God, You will not despise.

**18** Do good to Zion in Your good pleasure;  
build the walls of Jerusalem.

**19** Then You will delight in sacrifices of righteousness,  
in burnt offering and whole offering;  
then bulls will be offered on Your altar.

## Notes

- **The title** — David's repentance after Nathan's parable (2 Samuel 12:1–13); the Hebrew title takes two verses, so the Hebrew numbers run two ahead. The fourth of the penitential psalms (see 6).
- **vv1–2 "blot out… wash… cleanse"** — three words for removing sin (as a debt struck out of a book, as stains scrubbed from cloth, as uncleanness purified) for the three words for sin of 32:1–2.
- **v4 "Against You, You only, have I sinned"** — David had sinned against Uriah and Bathsheba, but all sin is first against God (2 Samuel 12:13; Genesis 39:9). Paul quotes the second half from the Greek, *that You may be justified in Your words* (Romans 3:4).
- **v5 "in sin my mother conceived me"** — not a charge against his mother, but a confession that sin reaches back to the beginning of his life (58:3; Job 14:4).
- **v7 "Purge me with hyssop"** — *techatte'eni*, **un-sin me**; hyssop was used to sprinkle the blood and water of cleansing (Leviticus 14:4–7; Numbers 19:18). *Though your sins are like scarlet, they shall be as white as snow* (Isaiah 1:18).
- **v10 "Create in me a clean heart"** — *bera*, the verb of Genesis 1:1, used only of God's own work: a new creation (Ezekiel 36:26; 2 Corinthians 5:17).
- **v11 "Your Holy Spirit"** — *ruach qodshekha*, capitalized as the word table has it; one of only three places in the Old Testament with this name (Isaiah 63:10–11). David remembered Saul, from whom the Spirit departed (1 Samuel 16:14).
- **v15 "Lord, open my lips"** — the words that open the daily prayers of the Church.
- **vv16–17 "The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit"** — as in 50:8–15 and 34:18.
- **vv18–19** — a prayer for the walls of Jerusalem, perhaps added when the city lay in ruins; then sacrifices offered rightly will please God.
