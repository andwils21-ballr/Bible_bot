# Psalms 2

**1** Why do the nations rage,  
and the peoples plot in vain?

**2** The kings of the earth take their stand,  
and the rulers take counsel together,  
against the LORD and against His anointed:

**3** "Let us burst their bonds apart  
and cast their cords away from us."

**4** He who sits in the heavens laughs;  
the Lord holds them in derision.

**5** Then He will speak to them in His anger  
and terrify them in His fury:

**6** "As for Me, I have set My king  
on Zion, My holy mountain."

**7** I will tell of the decree of the LORD.  
He said to me, "You are My son;  
today I have fathered you.

**8** Ask of Me, and I will make the nations your inheritance,  
and the ends of the earth your possession.

**9** You will break them with a rod of iron  
and dash them in pieces like a potter's vessel."

**10** Now therefore, kings, be wise;  
be warned, judges of the earth.

**11** Serve the LORD with fear,  
and rejoice with trembling.

**12** Kiss the son, or he will be angry, and you will perish in the way,  
for his anger is quickly kindled.  
Blessed are all who take refuge in him.

## Notes

- **v2 "His anointed"** — *meshicho*, **His messiah**, the king anointed with oil (1 Samuel 16:13). The apostles read the psalm of Jesus: *the kings of the earth set themselves… against the Lord and against His Anointed* (Acts 4:25–27). The psalm itself is rendered as the Old Testament has it, with lowercase for the king; the New Testament quotations will capitalize.
- **v4 "the Lord"** — *Adonai*, after *He who sits in the heavens*; God's laughter at the plots of kings (37:13; 59:8).
- **v6 "on Zion, My holy mountain"** — the hill of Jerusalem where the king was enthroned and the temple stood.
- **v7 "You are My son; today I have fathered you"** — the king becomes God's son on the day of his anointing: *I will be to him a father, and he shall be to Me a son* (2 Samuel 7:14). The voice from heaven at Jesus' baptism, *You are My beloved Son* (Mark 1:11), and Acts 13:33 and Hebrews 1:5; 5:5 take up these words.
- **v9 "break them with a rod of iron"** — the Greek reads *shepherd them with a rod of iron*, from the same letters with other vowels, and Revelation 2:27; 12:5; 19:15 follow the Greek.
- **v12 "Kiss the son"** — *nashqu var*: *bar* is the Aramaic word for **son**, where the Hebrew word is *ben* (v7). The kiss is the act of homage to a king (1 Samuel 10:1). The Greek reads **take hold of instruction**; others read **kiss purely**, or move the words: *with trembling kiss his feet*. Whether **he** in the rest of the verse is the LORD or the son is not certain.
- **v12 "Blessed are all who take refuge in him"** — the psalm ends with the word that began Psalm 1, *ashrei*, **blessed**; the two psalms open the book together.
