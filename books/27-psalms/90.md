# Psalms 90

*A prayer of Moses, the man of God.*

**1** Lord, You have been our dwelling place  
in all generations.

**2** Before the mountains were born,  
or You brought forth the earth and the world,  
from everlasting to everlasting You are God.

**3** You turn mortals back to dust  
and say, "Return, children of man."

**4** For a thousand years in Your sight  
are like yesterday when it is past,  
or like a watch in the night.

**5** You sweep them away; they are like a sleep,  
like grass that springs up in the morning:

**6** in the morning it flourishes and springs up;  
by evening it withers and dries.

**7** For we are consumed by Your anger;  
by Your wrath we are terrified.

**8** You have set our iniquities before You,  
our secret sins in the light of Your face.

**9** For all our days pass away under Your wrath;  
we bring our years to an end like a sigh.

**10** The days of our years are seventy years,  
or if by strength, eighty years;  
yet their pride is only trouble and sorrow,  
for it is soon gone, and we fly away.

**11** Who knows the power of Your anger,  
and Your wrath as the fear of You deserves?

**12** So teach us to number our days,  
so that we may gain a heart of wisdom.

**13** Return, LORD — how long?  
Have compassion on Your servants.

**14** Satisfy us in the morning with Your loyal love,  
so that we may sing for joy and be glad all our days.

**15** Make us glad for as many days as You have afflicted us,  
for as many years as we have seen trouble.

**16** Let Your work be seen by Your servants,  
and Your splendor by their children.

**17** Let the favor of the Lord our God be on us,  
and establish the work of our hands for us —  
yes, establish the work of our hands.

## Notes

- **The title, "A prayer of Moses, the man of God"** — the only psalm credited to Moses, and the first of the fourth book of the Psalms. "The man of God" is Moses' title in Deuteronomy 33:1.
- **v3 "You turn mortals back to dust"** — *dakka*, crushed dust; the curse of Genesis 3:19, *to dust you shall return*, in other words. "Return" in the second line is the same verb.
- **v4 "a thousand years in Your sight are like yesterday"** — 2 Peter 3:8 draws on this: *with the Lord one day is as a thousand years, and a thousand years as one day.*
- **v4 "a watch in the night"** — a few hours of the night, gone in sleep.
- **v10 "seventy years… eighty years"** — the span of a full life; Moses himself lived to 120 (Deuteronomy 34:7).
- **v12 "teach us to number our days"** — to count them, and so to know they are few and use them well.
- **v13 "Return, LORD"** — *shuvah*, the same verb as v3; mortals return to dust, and the psalmist asks God to return to them. "Have compassion" (*hinnachem*) is the word of Exodus 32:12, where Moses prays the same.
- **v17 "establish the work of our hands"** — the closing request repeated, as the Hebrew does.
