# Psalms 16

*A Miktam of David.*

**1** Keep me, God, for in You I take refuge.

**2** I say to the LORD, "You are my Lord;  
I have no good apart from You."

**3** As for the holy ones who are in the land,  
they are the noble, in whom is all my delight.

**4** The sorrows of those who run after another god will multiply;  
I will not pour out their drink offerings of blood  
or take their names on my lips.

**5** The LORD is my allotted portion and my cup;  
You hold my lot.

**6** The lines have fallen for me in pleasant places;  
indeed, my inheritance is beautiful to me.

**7** I bless the LORD who gives me counsel;  
even in the nights my heart instructs me.

**8** I have set the LORD always before me;  
because He is at my right hand, I will not be moved.

**9** Therefore my heart is glad, and my glory rejoices;  
my flesh also will dwell in safety.

**10** For You will not abandon my soul to Sheol,  
nor let Your faithful one see the pit.

**11** You make known to me the path of life;  
in Your presence is fullness of joy;  
at Your right hand are pleasures forever.

## Notes

- **The title "A Miktam"** — *mikhtam*, a word of uncertain meaning, used in the titles of Psalms 16 and 56–60; perhaps **an inscription**, or **a golden** poem.
- **v2 "I say to the LORD"** — the Hebrew has *amart*, **you have said**, as though the psalmist speaks to his own soul; the Greek and many Hebrew manuscripts read *amarti*, **I say**, followed here.
- **v3 "the holy ones who are in the land"** — the faithful of Israel, or, as some read it, the gods of the land whom the psalmist renounces in v4; the Hebrew of vv3–4 is hard.
- **v4 "drink offerings of blood"** — offerings poured out to foreign gods.
- **vv5–6 "my allotted portion… my lot… the lines… my inheritance"** — the words of the dividing of the land by lot and measuring line (Joshua 14–19). The Levites had no land: *the LORD is his inheritance* (Deuteronomy 10:9; Numbers 18:20).
- **v7 "my heart instructs me"** — *kilyotai*, **my kidneys**, the inner self (7:9).
- **v9 "my glory rejoices"** — *kevodi*, the soul or the self (7:5; 30:12); the Greek reads **my tongue**, which Peter quotes (Acts 2:26).
- **v10 "nor let Your faithful one see the pit"** — *shachat*, **the pit**, the grave; the Greek reads *diaphthora*, **corruption**. Peter and Paul apply the verse to the resurrection of Jesus, whose body did not see corruption (Acts 2:25–31; 13:35–37). The psalm is rendered as the Old Testament has it; the quotations in Acts will capitalize.
- **v11 "the path of life"** — Peter quotes the Greek, *You have made known to me the paths of life* (Acts 2:28). **Pleasures forever** at God's right hand is the end of the way that began in Psalm 1, *the way of the righteous*.
