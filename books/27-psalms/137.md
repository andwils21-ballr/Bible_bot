# Psalms 137

**1** By the rivers of Babylon,  
there we sat down, and we wept  
when we remembered Zion.

**2** On the poplars there  
we hung up our lyres.

**3** For there our captors asked us for songs,  
and our tormentors for joy:  
"Sing us one of the songs of Zion!"

**4** How could we sing the LORD's song  
in a foreign land?

**5** If I forget you, Jerusalem,  
let my right hand forget!

**6** Let my tongue cling to the roof of my mouth  
if I do not remember you,  
if I do not set Jerusalem  
above my highest joy.

**7** Remember, LORD, against the sons of Edom  
the day of Jerusalem,  
how they said, "Tear it down, tear it down,  
down to its foundations!"

**8** Daughter of Babylon, doomed to destruction,  
blessed is the one who repays you  
for what you have done to us.

**9** Blessed is the one who seizes your little ones  
and dashes them against the rock.

## Notes

- **v1 "By the rivers of Babylon"** — the Euphrates and its canals, where the exiles were settled (Ezekiel 1:1, by the river Chebar).
- **v3 "our tormentors"** — *tolalenu* occurs only here; the meaning is uncertain, perhaps "those who plundered us."
- **v5 "let my right hand forget!"** — the Hebrew gives no object: forget its skill on the lyre, or forget how to move at all. Some read *tikhchash*, "wither," by changing one letter.
- **v7 "the sons of Edom… the day of Jerusalem"** — Edom cheered and joined in the city's fall to Babylon (Obadiah 10–14; Lamentations 4:21–22).
- **vv8–9** — the cry of people who had seen it done to their own children (Lamentations 2:11, 20; 2 Kings 8:12); the psalm asks for the very judgment the prophets announced on Babylon (Isaiah 13:16; Jeremiah 51:24, 56). It is a prayer handed to God, not an act taken into their own hands (Romans 12:19).
- **v8 "doomed to destruction"** — *hashedudah*, "the devastated one"; some read *hashodedah*, "you destroyer."
