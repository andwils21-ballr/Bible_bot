# Psalms 13

*For the director. A psalm of David.*

**1** How long, LORD? Will You forget me forever?  
How long will You hide Your face from me?

**2** How long must I take counsel in my soul,  
with sorrow in my heart all the day?  
How long will my enemy be exalted over me?

**3** Look on me and answer me, LORD my God;  
light up my eyes, or I will sleep the sleep of death,

**4** and my enemy will say, "I have overcome him,"  
and my foes will rejoice because I am shaken.

**5** But I have trusted in Your loyal love;  
my heart will rejoice in Your salvation.

**6** I will sing to the LORD,  
because He has dealt bountifully with me.

---

## Notes

- **vv1–2 "How long?"** — *ad anah*, four times: the cry of the lament psalms (6:3; 74:10; 89:46). The psalm moves in three steps, from complaint (vv1–2) to prayer (vv3–4) to trust (vv5–6).
- **v1 "Will You forget me forever?"** — the opposite of *He does not forget the cry of the afflicted* (9:12); the sufferer feels what faith denies.
- **v3 "light up my eyes"** — give me life and strength again (1 Samuel 14:27, 29; Ezra 9:8); dim eyes are a sign of death.
- **v3 "or I will sleep the sleep of death"** — death as a sleep from which there is no waking (Jeremiah 51:39); the fear runs on into v4, where the enemy would boast over him.
- **v5 "Your loyal love"** — *chesed*, the ground of trust when God seems far.
- **v6 "He has dealt bountifully with me"** — *gamal alai*, the past tense of faith: the psalmist sings as though the answer had already come. The Greek adds a line, *and I will sing to the name of the Lord Most High*, from 7:17.
