# Psalms 151

*This psalm was written by David himself, though it is outside the number, when he fought Goliath in single combat.*

**1** I was small among my brothers  
and the youngest in my father's house;  
I tended my father's sheep.

**2** My hands made an instrument;  
my fingers tuned a harp.

**3** Who will tell my Lord?  
The Lord Himself — He Himself hears.

**4** He sent His angel  
and took me from my father's sheep  
and anointed me with the oil of His anointing.

**5** My brothers were handsome and tall,  
but the Lord was not pleased with them.

**6** I went out to meet the Philistine,  
and he cursed me by his idols.

**7** But I drew his own sword from him and beheaded him,  
and took away the disgrace from the sons of Israel.

## Notes

- **The source** — this psalm is not in the Hebrew Bible held here; it is rendered from the Greek of Swete's Septuagint, where it stands after Psalm 150. The Ethiopian Psalter counts it among the Psalms. *Kyrios* is "Lord," and *angelos* "angel," as in the other books rendered from the Greek.
- **The title, "outside the number"** — the Greek Psalter counts 150 psalms; this one is added after them. A longer Hebrew form, in two parts, was found among the Dead Sea Scrolls (11QPs^a).
- **v1 "the youngest… I tended my father's sheep"** — as in 1 Samuel 16:11.
- **v2 "My hands made an instrument"** — David the musician (1 Samuel 16:18, 23); the Greek tradition takes the psalm as his own song.
- **v4 "He sent His angel"** — the Hebrew form found at Qumran reads "His prophet," that is, Samuel (1 Samuel 16:12–13).
- **v5 "the Lord was not pleased with them"** — as in 1 Samuel 16:7, *the LORD does not look on what man looks on*.
- **v6 "he cursed me by his idols"** — 1 Samuel 17:43, *the Philistine cursed David by his gods*.
- **v7 "I drew his own sword from him and beheaded him… took away the disgrace from the sons of Israel"** — 1 Samuel 17:51 and 17:26, and "disgrace" follows the English there.
