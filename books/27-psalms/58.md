# Psalms 58

*For the director: "Do Not Destroy." Of David. A Miktam.*

**1** Do you indeed speak righteousness, you gods?  
Do you judge the children of man uprightly?

**2** No, in your heart you work injustice;  
on the earth you weigh out the violence of your hands.

**3** The wicked go astray from the womb;  
they err from birth, speaking lies.

**4** They have venom like the venom of a snake,  
like the deaf cobra that stops its ear,

**5** that does not hear the voice of the charmers  
or the skilled caster of spells.

**6** God, break the teeth in their mouth;  
tear out the fangs of the young lions, LORD.

**7** Let them melt away like water that runs off;  
when he bends his arrows, let them be cut off.

**8** Let them be like the snail that dissolves as it goes,  
like a woman's stillborn child, that never sees the sun.

**9** Before your pots feel the thorns,  
whether green or burning, He will sweep them away.

**10** The righteous will rejoice when he sees the vengeance;  
he will wash his feet in the blood of the wicked.

**11** Mankind will say, "Surely there is fruit for the righteous;  
surely there is a God who judges on the earth."

## Notes

- **v1 "you gods"** — the Hebrew has *elem*, **silence**; read with other vowels as *elim*, **gods**, the rulers and judges who were to give justice in God's name (82:1, 6; Exodus 21:6). The Greek reads *if indeed you speak righteousness*.
- **v2 "you weigh out the violence of your hands"** — judges who use the scales of justice to measure out injustice.
- **vv4–5 "the deaf cobra… the charmers"** — a snake that will not be charmed, as the wicked will not listen (Jeremiah 8:17; Ecclesiastes 10:11).
- **v6 "break the teeth in their mouth"** — the wicked as lions, as in 3:7.
- **vv7–9** — images of passing away: water soaking into the ground, a snail's trail, a stillborn child, thorns burned up under a pot before it heats. The Hebrew of vv7 and 9 is very hard, and the readings are uncertain.
- **v10 "he will wash his feet in the blood of the wicked"** — the language of total victory in battle (68:23; Isaiah 63:3); the psalm cries for God's justice, not private revenge (Romans 12:19).
- **v11 "Surely there is a God who judges on the earth"** — *Elohim shofetim*: the answer to the unjust *gods* of v1.
