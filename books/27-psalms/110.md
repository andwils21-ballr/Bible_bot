# Psalms 110

*Of David. A psalm.*

**1** The LORD says to my lord:  
"Sit at My right hand  
until I make your enemies  
a footstool for your feet."

**2** The LORD will send out from Zion  
your mighty scepter;  
rule among your enemies!

**3** Your people will offer themselves freely  
on the day of your power,  
in holy splendor;  
from the womb of the dawn,  
the dew of your youth is yours.

**4** The LORD has sworn  
and will not change His mind:  
"You are a priest forever  
after the order of Melchizedek."

**5** The Lord is at your right hand;  
He will shatter kings on the day of His wrath.

**6** He will judge among the nations,  
filling them with corpses;  
He will shatter the head over the wide earth.

**7** He will drink from the brook by the way;  
so he will lift up his head.

## Notes

- **v1 "The LORD says to my lord"** — *neum YHWH la'doni*: the LORD speaks to the king, David's "lord." The king's pronouns stay lowercase here, as in Psalms 2 and 45. Jesus asks how David's son can be David's Lord (Matthew 22:41–45), and it is among the Old Testament verses the New Testament quotes most often (Acts 2:34–35; 1 Corinthians 15:25; Hebrews 1:13; 10:12–13).
- **v1 "Sit at My right hand"** — the place of honor and shared rule.
- **v3** — one of the hardest verses in the Psalms. "Holy splendor" (*hadrei qodesh*) appears in some Hebrew manuscripts as *harerei qodesh*, "the holy mountains"; "the dew of your youth" may mean the king's vigor renewed each morning, or his young warriors. The Greek reads "from the womb before the morning star I begot you."
- **v4 "You are a priest forever after the order of Melchizedek"** — the king of Salem who was also priest of God Most High (Genesis 14:18–20); Hebrews 5–7 builds its teaching on Christ's priesthood on this verse.
- **v5 "The Lord is at your right hand"** — *Adonai*, God, now at the king's right hand, fighting for him.
- **vv6–7** — "He" in v6 is God; in v7 the subject turns back to the king, refreshed on the march like Gideon's men at the water (Judges 7:5–6), lifting his head in victory.
