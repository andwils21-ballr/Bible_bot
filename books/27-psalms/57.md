# Psalms 57

*For the director: "Do Not Destroy." Of David. A Miktam, when he fled from Saul, in the cave.*

**1** Be gracious to me, God, be gracious to me,  
for in You my soul takes refuge;  
in the shadow of Your wings I take refuge,  
till the storms of ruin pass by.

**2** I call to God Most High,  
to God who completes His work for me.

**3** He will send from heaven and save me;  
He puts to shame the one who tramples on me. *Selah*  
God will send out His loyal love and His truth.

**4** My soul is among lions;  
I lie down among those who blaze,  
the children of man, whose teeth are spears and arrows,  
and their tongue a sharp sword.

**5** Be exalted above the heavens, God;  
let Your glory be over all the earth.

**6** They prepared a net for my steps;  
my soul was bowed down.  
They dug a pit before me;  
they have fallen into it themselves. *Selah*

**7** My heart is steadfast, God,  
my heart is steadfast;  
I will sing and make music.

**8** Wake up, my glory!  
Wake up, harp and lyre!  
I will wake the dawn.

**9** I will give thanks to You, Lord, among the peoples;  
I will sing praises to You among the nations.

**10** For Your loyal love is great, reaching to the heavens,  
and Your truth to the clouds.

**11** Be exalted above the heavens, God;  
let Your glory be over all the earth.

## Notes

- **The title "Do Not Destroy… in the cave"** — *al tashchet*, perhaps a tune (58; 59; 75); the cave is Adullam or the cave at En-gedi, where David spared Saul (1 Samuel 22:1; 24:3).
- **v1 "in the shadow of Your wings"** — as in 17:8 and 36:7; **the storms of ruin**, *havvot*.
- **v3 "God will send out His loyal love and His truth"** — *chesed* and *emet* as God's messengers sent to rescue (43:3).
- **v4 "those who blaze"** — *lohatim*, men aflame with hatred, like fire-breathing beasts. Their **tongue a sharp sword** (55:21; 64:3).
- **vv5, 11** — the refrain, the same in both places.
- **v6 "they have fallen into it themselves"** — as in 7:15 and 9:15.
- **vv7–11** — repeated as the first half of Psalm 108 (108:1–5).
- **v8 "I will wake the dawn"** — the singer rises before the sun to praise God, waking the morning instead of being woken by it.
