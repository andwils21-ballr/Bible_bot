# Psalms 49

*For the director. Of the sons of Korah. A psalm.*

**1** Hear this, all you peoples;  
listen, all who live in the world,

**2** both low and high,  
rich and poor together.

**3** My mouth will speak wisdom;  
the meditation of my heart will be understanding.

**4** I will incline my ear to a proverb;  
I will open my riddle on the lyre.

**5** Why should I fear in days of trouble,  
when the iniquity of those who dog my heels surrounds me,

**6** those who trust in their wealth  
and boast of the abundance of their riches?

**7** Truly no man can ransom his brother  
or give to God the price of his life,

**8** for the ransom of their soul is costly,  
and it must be given up forever,

**9** that he should live on forever  
and never see the pit.

**10** For he sees that even the wise die;  
the fool and the stupid alike perish  
and leave their wealth to others.

**11** Their graves are their homes forever,  
their dwellings to all generations,  
though they named lands after themselves.

**12** Man in his splendor does not stay the night;  
he is like the beasts that perish.

**13** This is the way of those who have foolish confidence,  
and of those after them who approve their words. *Selah*

**14** Like sheep they are appointed for Sheol;  
death will shepherd them,  
and the upright will rule over them in the morning;  
their form will waste away in Sheol, far from their dwelling.

**15** But God will ransom my soul from the hand of Sheol,  
for He will take me. *Selah*

**16** Do not be afraid when a man becomes rich,  
when the glory of his house increases.

**17** For when he dies he will take nothing away;  
his glory will not go down after him.

**18** Though while he lives he counts his soul blessed,  
and men praise you when you do well for yourself,

**19** he will go to the generation of his fathers,  
who will never again see the light.

**20** Man in his splendor, without understanding,  
is like the beasts that perish.

## Notes

- **The psalm** — a wisdom poem, a **riddle** (v4) on death and riches, addressed to all peoples like the book of Proverbs.
- **v5 "those who dog my heels"** — *avon aqevai*, **the iniquity of my heels**, of those who lie in wait at my heels (56:6).
- **vv7–9 "no man can ransom his brother"** — *kofer*, the ransom price of a life (Exodus 21:30); for death no ransom is enough. *What shall a man give in return for his soul?* (Mark 8:37).
- **v11 "Their graves are their homes forever"** — the Greek and Syriac read *qivram*, **their graves**; the Hebrew has *qirbam*, **their inward thought**: *their inward thought is that their houses are forever*. The witnesses are followed here.
- **vv12, 20** — the refrain differs by one letter: v12 *bal yalin*, **does not stay the night**; v20 *ve-lo yavin*, **without understanding**.
- **v14 "death will shepherd them"** — death as the shepherd of the wicked, against *the LORD is my shepherd* (23:1). The rest of the verse is very hard.
- **v15 "for He will take me"** — *yiqqacheni*, the verb used of Enoch, *God took him* (Genesis 5:24), and of Elijah (2 Kings 2:3): the hope of life with God beyond Sheol (73:24).
- **v17 "when he dies he will take nothing away"** — *we brought nothing into the world, and we cannot take anything out* (1 Timothy 6:7; Job 1:21).
