# Psalms 20

*For the director. A psalm of David.*

**1** May the LORD answer you in the day of trouble;  
may the name of the God of Jacob set you on high.

**2** May He send you help from the sanctuary  
and support you from Zion.

**3** May He remember all your offerings  
and accept your burnt offering as rich. *Selah*

**4** May He give you your heart's desire  
and fulfill all your plans.

**5** May we shout for joy over your salvation,  
and in the name of our God set up our banners.  
May the LORD fulfill all your requests.

**6** Now I know that the LORD saves His anointed;  
He will answer him from His holy heaven  
with the saving might of His right hand.

**7** Some trust in chariots and some in horses,  
but we call on the name of the LORD our God.

**8** They have bowed down and fallen,  
but we have risen and stand upright.

**9** LORD, save the king!  
May He answer us on the day we call.

## Notes

- **The psalm** — a prayer for the king before battle, spoken by the people (vv1–5), answered by a single voice, perhaps a priest or prophet (v6), and closed by the people again (vv7–9). Psalm 21 is the thanksgiving after the victory.
- **v1 "the name of the God of Jacob"** — the name stands for God Himself, present and acting (Exodus 23:21; Proverbs 18:10). **Set you on high** is *yesaggevkha*, to set in a high, safe place.
- **v3 "accept your burnt offering as rich"** — *yedashneh*, **find it fat**; the fat of the sacrifice was the LORD's (Leviticus 3:16). Sacrifice was offered before battle (1 Samuel 7:9; 13:9–12).
- **v5 "set up our banners"** — *nidgol*; the Greek reads *we will be made great*, from another root.
- **v6 "His anointed"** — *meshicho*, the king, as in 2:2.
- **v7 "Some trust in chariots and some in horses"** — the Hebrew has no verb in the first line; *we call on* (*nazkir*, **make mention of**) supplies the contrast. *A king is not saved by his great army… the war horse is a false hope* (33:16–17; Deuteronomy 17:16; Isaiah 31:1).
- **v9 "LORD, save the king!"** — following the accents of the Hebrew text; the Greek divides the line differently: *LORD, save the king, and answer us on the day we call*. With another division it can be read *LORD, save; may the King answer us*, the King being God.
