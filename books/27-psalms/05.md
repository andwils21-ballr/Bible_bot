# Psalms 5

*For the director: for the flutes. A psalm of David.*

**1** Listen to my words, LORD;  
consider my sighing.

**2** Pay attention to the sound of my cry,  
my King and my God,  
for to You I pray.

**3** LORD, in the morning You hear my voice;  
in the morning I set it out before You, and watch.

**4** For You are not a God who delights in wickedness;  
evil cannot live as a guest with You.

**5** The boastful will not stand before Your eyes;  
You hate all who do iniquity.

**6** You destroy those who speak lies;  
the LORD abhors the man of bloodshed and deceit.

**7** But I, through the greatness of Your loyal love, will come into Your house;  
I will bow down toward Your holy temple in fear of You.

**8** Lead me, LORD, in Your righteousness because of those who watch me;  
make Your way straight before me.

**9** For there is nothing reliable in their mouth;  
their inner part is ruin;  
their throat is an open grave;  
they make their tongue smooth.

**10** Hold them guilty, God;  
let them fall by their own counsels;  
for their many transgressions drive them out,  
for they have rebelled against You.

**11** But let all who take refuge in You rejoice;  
let them sing for joy forever,  
and shelter them,  
so that those who love Your name may exult in You.

**12** For You bless the righteous, LORD;  
You surround him with favor as with a shield.

## Notes

- **The title "for the flutes"** — *el ha-nechilot*, a word found only here; perhaps wind instruments, or a tune.
- **v3 "in the morning I set it out before You"** — *e'erokh*, the verb for laying out the wood and the offering on the altar (Leviticus 1:7–8) and for setting out a case in court (Job 23:4). The morning prayer is laid before God like the morning sacrifice.
- **v4 "evil cannot live as a guest with You"** — *lo yegurkha ra*, from *gur*, as the word table has it: *LORD, who shall live as a guest in Your tent?* (15:1).
- **v6 "the LORD abhors"** — *yeta'ev*, from the root of *to'evah*; **the man of bloodshed** is *ish damim*, as David himself was called (2 Samuel 16:7–8).
- **v7 "Your holy temple"** — *heikhal*, the palace of the King, here the house of God, whether the tent or the temple.
- **v8 "those who watch me"** — *shorerai*, enemies who lie in wait. The written form has *hoshar*, a spelling of the same verb **make straight**, as read.
- **v9 "their throat is an open grave"** — quoted by Paul among the verses showing that *none is righteous* (Romans 3:13).
- **v12 "as with a shield"** — *tsinnah*, the large shield that covers the whole body (1 Samuel 17:7).
