# Psalms 41

*For the director. A psalm of David.*

**1** Blessed is the one who considers the poor;  
in the day of trouble the LORD delivers him;

**2** the LORD keeps him and keeps him alive;  
he is called blessed in the land;  
You do not give him up to the will of his enemies.

**3** The LORD sustains him on his sickbed;  
in his illness You turn over all his bed.

**4** As for me, I said, "LORD, be gracious to me;  
heal my soul, for I have sinned against You."

**5** My enemies speak evil of me:  
"When will he die, and his name perish?"

**6** If one comes to see me, he speaks emptiness;  
his heart gathers trouble for itself;  
he goes out and speaks of it.

**7** All who hate me whisper together against me;  
they plot harm against me:

**8** "A thing of worthlessness is poured into him;  
now that he lies down, he will not rise again."

**9** Even the man of my peace, in whom I trusted,  
who ate my bread, has lifted up his heel against me.

**10** But You, LORD, be gracious to me and raise me up,  
so that I may repay them.

**11** By this I know that You delight in me:  
my enemy does not shout in triumph over me.

**12** As for me, You uphold me in my integrity  
and set me before Your face forever.

**13** Blessed be the LORD, the God of Israel,  
from everlasting to everlasting.  
Amen and Amen.

## Notes

- **v1 "Blessed is the one who considers the poor"** — *maskil el dal*, who pays heed to the weak, as God does; the psalm begins and the first book of the Psalms ends with *blessed* (1:1).
- **v3 "You turn over all his bed"** — God as nurse, changing the sick man's bed; or, **You change all his lying down**, turning sickness to health.
- **v4 "heal my soul, for I have sinned against You"** — sickness and sin together, as in 38:3.
- **v8 "A thing of worthlessness"** — *devar beliyya'al*, a deadly disease, or a curse laid on him.
- **v9 "the man of my peace… has lifted up his heel against me"** — *ish shelomi*, a trusted friend, as Ahithophel was to David (2 Samuel 15:12). Jesus quotes the verse of Judas, *He who ate My bread has lifted his heel against Me* (John 13:18).
- **v10 "so that I may repay them"** — the king's right to deal with rebels; the psalmist asks to be raised to do it.
- **v12 "my integrity"** — *tummi*, as in 26:1.
- **v13 "Blessed be the LORD, the God of Israel… Amen and Amen"** — the doxology that closes the first of the five books of the Psalms (1–41); the others end at 72, 89, 106 and 150.
