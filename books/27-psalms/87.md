# Psalms 87

*Of the sons of Korah. A psalm. A song.*

**1** His foundation is on the holy mountains.

**2** The LORD loves the gates of Zion  
more than all the dwellings of Jacob.

**3** Glorious things are spoken of you,  
city of God. *Selah*

**4** "I will count Rahab and Babylon  
among those who know Me;  
Philistia and Tyre, with Cush —  
'This one was born there.'"

**5** Of Zion it will be said,  
"Each one was born in her,"  
and the Most High Himself will establish her.

**6** The LORD will write when He registers the peoples,  
"This one was born there." *Selah*

**7** Singers and dancers alike will say,  
"All my springs are in you."

---

## Notes

- **v1 "His foundation is on the holy mountains"** — the psalm begins abruptly, with God's founding of Zion (78:68–69; Isaiah 14:32).
- **v4 "Rahab and Babylon"** — Rahab, the sea monster, is a name for Egypt (Isaiah 30:7; 51:9). Egypt and Babylon, the great oppressors, with Philistia, Tyre and Cush, are counted as natives of Zion.
- **v4 "I will count… among those who know Me"** — *azkir*, "I will record" or "make mention"; God speaks, enrolling the nations.
- **v5 "Each one was born in her"** — literally "a man and a man," one after another; the nations become citizens of Zion, as Isaiah 2:2–4 and 19:23–25 foresee. Paul speaks of *the Jerusalem above… our mother* (Galatians 4:26).
- **v6 "when He registers the peoples"** — a census of the city's citizens (Isaiah 4:3; Ezekiel 13:9).
- **v7 "All my springs are in you"** — Zion as the source of life and joy (46:4; Ezekiel 47:1–12). The line is short and abrupt in the Hebrew.
