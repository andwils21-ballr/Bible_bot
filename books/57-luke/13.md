# Luke 13

## Repent or Perish

**1** At that very time some were present who told Him about the Galileans whose blood Pilate had mixed with their sacrifices.

**2** He answered them, "Do you think that these Galileans were worse sinners than all the other Galileans, because they suffered this?

**3** No, I tell you; but unless you repent, you will all perish as they did.

**4** Or those eighteen on whom the tower in Siloam fell and killed them: do you think that they were worse offenders than all the others who live in Jerusalem?

**5** No, I tell you; but unless you repent, you will all perish in the same way."

**6** He told this parable: "A man had a fig tree planted in his vineyard, and he came looking for fruit on it and found none.

**7** He said to the vinedresser, 'For three years now I have come looking for fruit on this fig tree and have found none. Cut it down; why should it use up the ground?'

**8** He answered him, 'Sir, let it alone this year also, until I dig around it and put on manure.

**9** If it bears fruit next year, well; but if not, you can cut it down.'"

## A Woman Set Free on the Sabbath

**10** He was teaching in one of the synagogues on the sabbath.

**11** There was a woman who for eighteen years had had a spirit that crippled her; she was bent over and could not stand up straight at all.

**12** When Jesus saw her, He called her over and said to her, "Woman, you are set free from your weakness."

**13** He laid His hands on her, and at once she stood up straight and glorified God.

**14** But the synagogue leader, indignant that Jesus had healed on the sabbath, said to the crowd, "There are six days on which work ought to be done; come on those days and be healed, and not on the sabbath day."

**15** The Lord answered him, "Hypocrites! Does not each of you on the sabbath untie his ox or his donkey from the manger and lead it away to water it?

**16** This woman, a daughter of Abraham whom Satan bound for eighteen long years, ought she not to be set free from this bond on the sabbath day?"

**17** As He said this, all His opponents were put to shame, and the whole crowd rejoiced at all the glorious things being done by Him.

## The Mustard Seed and the Yeast

**18** He said, "What is the kingdom of God like, and to what shall I compare it?

**19** It is like a mustard seed, which a man took and threw into his garden; and it grew and became a tree, and the birds of the sky nested in its branches."

**20** Again He said, "To what shall I compare the kingdom of God?

**21** It is like yeast, which a woman took and hid in three measures of flour until all of it was leavened."

## The Narrow Door

**22** He went through towns and villages, teaching, and making His way to Jerusalem.

**23** Someone said to Him, "Lord, will only a few be saved?" He said to them,

**24** "Strive to enter through the narrow door; for many, I tell you, will seek to enter and will not be able.

**25** Once the master of the house has got up and shut the door, and you begin to stand outside and knock at the door, saying, 'Lord, open to us,' he will answer you, 'I do not know where you come from.'

**26** Then you will begin to say, 'We ate and drank in your presence, and you taught in our streets.'

**27** But he will say, 'I tell you, I do not know where you come from. Depart from me, all you workers of wrongdoing!'

**28** There will be weeping and grinding of teeth, when you see Abraham and Isaac and Jacob and all the prophets in the kingdom of God, but yourselves thrown out.

**29** People will come from east and west, and from north and south, and recline at table in the kingdom of God.

**30** Some are last who will be first, and some are first who will be last."

## Jerusalem, Jerusalem

**31** At that very hour some Pharisees came and said to Him, "Get away from here, for Herod wants to kill You."

**32** He said to them, "Go and tell that fox, 'I am driving out demons and performing healings today and tomorrow, and on the third day I reach My goal.'

**33** Yet I must go on My way today and tomorrow and the next day, for it cannot be that a prophet should perish outside Jerusalem.

**34** Jerusalem, Jerusalem, who kills the prophets and stones those sent to her! How often I wanted to gather your children together, as a hen gathers her brood under her wings, and you were not willing.

**35** Your house is left to you. I tell you, you will not see Me until the time comes when you say, 'Blessed is the one who comes in the name of the Lord.'"

## Notes

- **vv1–5 "the Galileans whose blood Pilate had mixed with their sacrifices… the tower in Siloam… unless you repent"** — two disasters told only in Luke, one by a man's cruelty and one by accident. *Opheiletai*, **offenders**, word for word **debtors**, the word of the prayer (11:4).
- **vv6–9 "a fig tree planted in his vineyard… three years… let it alone this year also"** — the fig tree and the vineyard, the prophets' figures for Israel, *there are no grapes on the vine, no figs on the fig tree* (Jeremiah 8:13). Where Mark tells of a fig tree cursed (Mark 11:13–14), Luke tells of one given another year. *If it bears fruit next year, well*: the Greek leaves the sentence unfinished.
- **vv10–17 "a spirit that crippled her… bent over… six days on which work ought to be done… a daughter of Abraham whom Satan bound"** — *pneuma astheneias*, **a spirit that crippled her**, word for word **a spirit of weakness**. The leader quotes the commandment, *Six days you shall work* (Deuteronomy 5:13). *Lyei*, **untie**, and *lythēnai*, **be set free**, one verb: the ox is untied on the sabbath, so the woman is set free. *Idou deka kai oktō etē*, **eighteen long years**, the *idou* giving the weight of the time.
- **vv18–21 "a mustard seed… became a tree… yeast… three measures of flour"** — as Matthew 13:31–33. Three measures is the flour Sarah baked for the three visitors, *three measures of fine flour* (Genesis 18:6).
- **vv22–30 "making His way to Jerusalem… the narrow door… Depart from me, all you workers of wrongdoing… from east and west"** — the journey of 9:51. Matthew has *the narrow gate* (Matthew 7:13). The master's words are the psalm's, *Depart from me, all who do iniquity* (Psalm 6:8). The gathering from four directions is the psalm's, *from the east and from the west, from the north and from the sea* (Psalm 107:3), and the feast is Isaiah's *feast of rich food* for *all peoples* (Isaiah 25:6).
- **vv31–33 "Herod wants to kill You… that fox… on the third day I reach My goal… a prophet should perish outside Jerusalem"** — *teleioumai*, **I reach My goal**, word for word **I am completed**: His work ends in Jerusalem. *Dei*, **must**, as in 9:22.
- **vv34–35 "Jerusalem, Jerusalem… as a hen gathers her brood under her wings… Your house is left to you"** — as Matthew 23:37–39, where Matthew has *desolate*. The wings are God's own, *Like an eagle that stirs up its nest… He spread His wings* (Deuteronomy 32:11). *Your house… left*, as Jeremiah warned, *this house shall become a desolation* (Jeremiah 22:5). The last line is Psalm 118:26, said again at the entry into the city (19:38).
