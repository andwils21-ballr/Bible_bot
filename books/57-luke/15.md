# Luke 15

## The Lost Sheep

**1** All the tax collectors and sinners were coming to Him to listen.

**2** The Pharisees and the scribes were grumbling, saying, "This man welcomes sinners and eats with them."

**3** So He told them this parable:

**4** "Which man of you, if he has a hundred sheep and has lost one of them, does not leave the ninety-nine in the wilderness and go after the one that is lost until he finds it?

**5** When he has found it, he lays it on his shoulders, rejoicing.

**6** He comes home, calls together his friends and his neighbors, and says to them, 'Rejoice with me, for I have found my sheep that was lost.'

**7** I tell you that in the same way there will be more joy in heaven over one sinner who repents than over ninety-nine righteous people who have no need of repentance.

## The Lost Coin

**8** "Or what woman, if she has ten silver coins and loses one coin, does not light a lamp, sweep the house and search carefully until she finds it?

**9** When she has found it, she calls together her friends and neighbors, saying, 'Rejoice with me, for I have found the coin that I had lost.'

**10** In the same way, I tell you, there is joy in the presence of the angels of God over one sinner who repents."

## The Lost Son

**11** He said, "A man had two sons.

**12** The younger of them said to his father, 'Father, give me the share of the property that falls to me.' So he divided his living between them.

**13** Not many days later the younger son gathered everything together and went on a journey to a distant country, and there he squandered his property in reckless living.

**14** When he had spent everything, a severe famine came over that country, and he began to be in need.

**15** So he went and hired himself out to one of the citizens of that country, who sent him into his fields to feed pigs.

**16** He longed to fill his stomach with the pods that the pigs were eating, and no one gave him anything.

**17** When he came to himself, he said, 'How many of my father's hired men have more than enough bread, but I am dying here of hunger!

**18** I will get up and go to my father, and I will say to him, "Father, I have sinned against heaven and before you;

**19** I am no longer worthy to be called your son. Make me like one of your hired men."'

**20** So he got up and went to his father. While he was still a long way off, his father saw him and was filled with compassion; he ran, threw his arms around his neck and kissed him.

**21** The son said to him, 'Father, I have sinned against heaven and before you; I am no longer worthy to be called your son.'

**22** But the father said to his slaves, 'Quickly, bring out the best robe and put it on him, and put a ring on his hand and sandals on his feet.

**23** Bring the fattened calf and kill it, and let us eat and celebrate;

**24** for this son of mine was dead and has come back to life; he was lost and has been found.' So they began to celebrate.

**25** "Now his older son was in the field. As he came and drew near to the house, he heard music and dancing.

**26** He called one of the servants and asked what these things might mean.

**27** He said to him, 'Your brother has come, and your father has killed the fattened calf, because he has received him back safe and sound.'

**28** But he was angry and would not go in. His father came out and pleaded with him.

**29** But he answered his father, 'All these years I have been serving you, and I never disobeyed a command of yours; yet you never gave me even a young goat so that I could celebrate with my friends.

**30** But when this son of yours came, who has eaten up your living with prostitutes, you killed the fattened calf for him!'

**31** He said to him, 'Child, you are always with me, and all that is mine is yours.

**32** But we had to celebrate and rejoice, for this brother of yours was dead and has come to life; he was lost and has been found.'"

## Notes

- **vv1–2 "tax collectors and sinners… This man welcomes sinners and eats with them"** — the grumbling of 5:30, *Why do you eat and drink with tax collectors and sinners?* *Diegongyzon*, **were grumbling**, the word for Israel's murmuring in the wilderness in the Greek of the Law. The three parables that follow answer the charge.
- **vv3–7 "leave the ninety-nine in the wilderness… lays it on his shoulders… more joy in heaven"** — Matthew has the parable among the disciples, with sheep left *on the mountains* and the one *straying* (Matthew 18:12–14); here it answers the Pharisees, and the sheep is **lost**, *apolōlos*, the word of all three parables (vv4, 6, 8, 9, 24, 32). The shepherd who seeks the lost is the LORD's word in Ezekiel, *I will seek the lost* (Ezekiel 34:16).
- **vv8–10 "ten silver coins… light a lamp, sweep the house"** — *drachmē*, **silver coin**, a Greek coin, named only here in the New Testament. *In the presence of the angels of God* says what v7 says, *in heaven*: the joy is God's.
- **vv11–16 "the share of the property that falls to me… reckless living… to feed pigs… the pods"** — the law gave the firstborn *a double portion of all that he has* (Deuteronomy 21:17), so the younger son's share is the smaller one, and he asks for it while his father lives. *Asōtōs*, **in reckless living**, without saving, wastefully. Pigs are unclean, *it is unclean for you* (Leviticus 11:7): a Jew feeding them for a Gentile has fallen as far as he can. *Keratia*, **pods**, the pods of the carob tree.
- **vv17–21 "When he came to himself… I have sinned against heaven and before you"** — *heaven* is a way of saying God. The son says his speech in v21 without its last line, *Make me like one of your hired men*, and the father answers with the robe and the ring. The edition marks the end of v21 as a place where manuscripts differ.
- **vv20–24 "while he was still a long way off… ran… the best robe… a ring… sandals… was dead and has come back to life"** — *esplanchnisthē*, **was filled with compassion**, the word of the Samaritan (10:33). The robe, ring and sandals are marks of honor, as Pharaoh gave Joseph *his signet ring… and clothed him in garments of fine linen* (Genesis 41:42). *Nekros… anezēsen*, **dead… has come back to life**.
- **vv25–32 "All these years I have been serving you… this son of yours… this brother of yours"** — *douleuō*, **I have been serving**, the work of a slave, as the younger son wanted to be a hired man. The older son says *this son of yours*; the father answers *this brother of yours*. The parable ends without saying whether the older son goes in.
