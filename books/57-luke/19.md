# Luke 19

## Zacchaeus

**1** He entered Jericho and was passing through.

**2** There was a man named Zacchaeus; he was a chief tax collector, and he was rich.

**3** He was trying to see who Jesus was, but he could not because of the crowd, for he was small in stature.

**4** So he ran on ahead and climbed up into a sycamore-fig tree to see Him, for He was about to pass that way.

**5** When Jesus came to the place, He looked up and said to him, "Zacchaeus, hurry and come down, for today I must stay at your house."

**6** He hurried and came down, and welcomed Him with joy.

**7** When they saw it, they all began to grumble, saying, "He has gone in to be the guest of a man who is a sinner."

**8** But Zacchaeus stood and said to the Lord, "Lord, half of my possessions I give to the poor; and if I have cheated anyone of anything, I pay back four times as much."

**9** Jesus said to him, "Today salvation has come to this house, because he too is a son of Abraham.

**10** For the Son of Man came to seek and to save the lost."

## The Ten Minas

**11** As they were listening to these things, He went on to tell a parable, because He was near Jerusalem, and they supposed that the kingdom of God was going to appear at once.

**12** So He said, "A man of noble birth went to a distant country to receive a kingdom for himself and then return.

**13** He called ten of his slaves, gave them ten minas, and said to them, 'Do business with this until I come.'

**14** But his citizens hated him and sent a delegation after him, saying, 'We do not want this man to reign over us.'

**15** When he returned, having received the kingdom, he ordered these slaves to whom he had given the money to be called to him, so that he might know what they had gained by doing business.

**16** The first came before him, saying, 'Master, your mina has made ten minas more.'

**17** He said to him, 'Well done, good slave! Because you have been faithful in a very little, you will have authority over ten cities.'

**18** The second came, saying, 'Your mina, master, has made five minas.'

**19** He said to him also, 'You too, be over five cities.'

**20** Another came, saying, 'Master, here is your mina, which I kept put away in a cloth;

**21** for I was afraid of you, because you are a severe man. You take what you did not deposit and reap what you did not sow.'

**22** He said to him, 'By your own words I will judge you, you wicked slave! You knew that I am a severe man, taking what I did not deposit and reaping what I did not sow?

**23** Then why did you not put my money in the bank? At my coming I would have collected it with interest.'

**24** He said to those standing by, 'Take the mina from him and give it to the one who has the ten minas.'

**25** They said to him, 'Master, he has ten minas!'

**26** 'I tell you, to everyone who has, more will be given; but from the one who does not have, even what he has will be taken away.

**27** But as for these enemies of mine, who did not want me to reign over them, bring them here and slaughter them in front of me.'"

## The King Comes to Jerusalem

**28** When He had said these things, He went on ahead, going up to Jerusalem.

**29** When He drew near to Bethphage and Bethany, at the mount that is called the Mount of Olives, He sent two of the disciples,

**30** saying, "Go into the village ahead of you; as you enter it you will find a colt tied there, on which no one has ever sat. Untie it and bring it.

**31** If anyone asks you, 'Why are you untying it?' say this: 'The Lord needs it.'"

**32** Those who were sent went away and found it just as He had told them.

**33** As they were untying the colt, its owners said to them, "Why are you untying the colt?"

**34** They said, "The Lord needs it."

**35** They brought it to Jesus, threw their cloaks on the colt and set Jesus on it.

**36** As He rode along, they spread their cloaks on the road.

**37** As He was now drawing near, at the descent of the Mount of Olives, the whole crowd of the disciples began to praise God joyfully with a loud voice for all the mighty works they had seen,

**38** saying, "Blessed is the King who comes in the name of the Lord! Peace in heaven, and glory in the highest!"

**39** Some of the Pharisees in the crowd said to Him, "Teacher, rebuke Your disciples."

**40** He answered, "I tell you, if these are silent, the stones will cry out."

## Jesus Weeps over Jerusalem

**41** When He drew near and saw the city, He wept over it,

**42** saying, "If you, even you, had known on this day the things that make for peace! But now they are hidden from your eyes.

**43** For the days will come upon you when your enemies will build a siege wall against you, surround you and hem you in on every side.

**44** They will dash you to the ground, you and your children within you, and they will not leave one stone on another in you, because you did not know the time of your visitation."

## In the Temple

**45** He entered the temple and began to drive out those who were selling,

**46** saying to them, "It is written, 'My house shall be a house of prayer'; but you have made it a den of robbers."

**47** He was teaching daily in the temple. The chief priests and the scribes and the leaders of the people were looking for a way to destroy Him,

**48** but they could not find anything they could do, for all the people were hanging on His words.

## Notes

- **vv1–7 "a chief tax collector, and he was rich… a sycamore-fig tree… He has gone in to be the guest of a man who is a sinner"** — *architelōnēs*, **chief tax collector**, found only here in the New Testament. *Sykomorea*, **sycamore-fig**, a kind of fig tree. *Dei me meinai*, **I must stay**, the *must* of God's plan, as *the Son of Man must suffer many things* (9:22). *Diegongyzon*, **they began to grumble**, as the Pharisees did in 15:2.
- **v8 "half of my possessions I give to the poor… I pay back four times as much"** — *didōmi… apodidōmi*, **I give… I pay back**, are present tense: either a promise made on the spot, or what he says he already does. Fourfold is what the law asked of a thief who had killed or sold a sheep, *four sheep in place of the sheep* (Exodus 22:1), and what David said the rich man of Nathan's story should pay, *He shall repay the ewe lamb fourfold* (2 Samuel 12:6). *Esykophantēsa*, **I have cheated**, the word John used to the soldiers, *by false accusation* (3:14).
- **vv9–10 "he too is a son of Abraham… to seek and to save the lost"** — as the bent woman was *a daughter of Abraham* (13:16). *To apolōlos*, **the lost**, the word of the lost sheep, coin and son (15:4, 8, 24), and of the shepherd who seeks it, *I will seek the lost* (Ezekiel 34:16).
- **vv11–14 "A man of noble birth went to a distant country to receive a kingdom… We do not want this man to reign over us"** — *mna*, **mina**, a sum of silver; Matthew's parable has *talents*, a far larger sum, given *to each according to his own ability* (Matthew 25:15). Only Luke has the man going away for a kingdom and the citizens who send word after him. *Pragmateusasthe*, **do business**, trade with it.
- **vv15–27 "Well done, good slave… a severe man… put my money in the bank… slaughter them in front of me"** — *austēros*, **severe**, strict and hard to please. *Epi trapezan*, **in the bank**, word for word **on a table**, the money changer's table. v26 is the saying of 8:18 and Matthew 25:29. The last line answers v14: the citizens who would not have him as king.
- **vv28–36 "Bethphage and Bethany… a colt… on which no one has ever sat… set Jesus on it… spread their cloaks on the road"** — as Mark 11:1–8. The king who comes *riding on a donkey, on a colt* (Zechariah 9:9); an animal *on which a yoke has never come* was set apart for holy use (Numbers 19:2). David's son was set *on my own mule* when he was made king (1 Kings 1:33), and Jehu's men laid their garments *under him… "Jehu is king!"* (2 Kings 9:13). Luke has no branches and no *Hosanna*.
- **vv37–40 "Blessed is the King who comes in the name of the Lord! Peace in heaven, and glory in the highest… the stones will cry out"** — Psalm 118:26, *Blessed is he who comes in the name of the LORD*, with *the King* added, as Jesus had said they would say (13:35). *Peace in heaven, and glory in the highest* answers the angels at His birth, *Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace* (2:14). The stones that cry out are Habakkuk's, *the stone will cry out from the wall* (Habakkuk 2:11).
- **vv41–44 "He wept over it… a siege wall… dash you to the ground… the time of your visitation"** — *charax*, **siege wall**, a palisade of stakes, the siege work Ezekiel was told to act out, *build a siege wall against it* (Ezekiel 4:2). *Edaphiousin*, **they will dash to the ground**, is the verb of the Greek of Psalm 137:9 (136:9 in the Greek numbering), *dashes them against the rock*. *Not one stone on another*, as Mark 13:2. *Episkopē*, **visitation**, the coming of God to His people, as Zechariah sang, *He has visited His people* (1:68).
- **vv45–48 "My house shall be a house of prayer… a den of robbers… hanging on His words"** — Isaiah 56:7, *My house will be called a house of prayer for all the peoples*, without its last words, as Mark has them (Mark 11:17); and Jeremiah 7:11, *a den of robbers*. *Exekremato*, **were hanging on**, as a thing hangs from a peg.
