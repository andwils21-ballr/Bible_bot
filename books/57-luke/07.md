# Luke 7

## The Centurion's Servant

**1** When He had finished all His words in the hearing of the people, He entered Capernaum.

**2** A centurion there had a slave who was dear to him, who was sick and about to die.

**3** When the centurion heard about Jesus, he sent elders of the Jews to Him, asking Him to come and save the life of his slave.

**4** When they came to Jesus, they pleaded with Him earnestly, saying, "He is worthy to have You do this for him,

**5** for he loves our nation, and he himself built our synagogue for us."

**6** Jesus went with them. When He was not far from the house, the centurion sent friends to say to Him, "Lord, do not trouble Yourself, for I am not worthy to have You come under my roof.

**7** That is why I did not consider myself worthy to come to You. But say the word, and let my servant be healed.

**8** For I too am a man set under authority, with soldiers under me. I say to this one, 'Go,' and he goes, and to another, 'Come,' and he comes, and to my slave, 'Do this,' and he does it."

**9** When Jesus heard this, He was amazed at him, and turning to the crowd that was following Him, He said, "I tell you, not even in Israel have I found such faith."

**10** When those who had been sent returned to the house, they found the slave well.

## The Widow's Son at Nain

**11** Soon afterward He went to a town called Nain, and His disciples and a great crowd went with Him.

**12** As He came near the gate of the town, a dead man was being carried out, the only son of his mother, and she was a widow; and a large crowd from the town was with her.

**13** When the Lord saw her, He had compassion on her and said to her, "Do not weep."

**14** He came up and touched the bier, and the bearers stood still. He said, "Young man, I say to you, get up."

**15** The dead man sat up and began to speak, and Jesus gave him to his mother.

**16** Fear seized them all, and they glorified God, saying, "A great prophet has risen among us!" and, "God has visited His people!"

**17** This report about Him spread through all Judea and all the surrounding region.

## John's Question

**18** John's disciples told him about all these things. John called two of his disciples

**19** and sent them to the Lord to ask, "Are You the one who is to come, or should we look for another?"

**20** When the men came to Him, they said, "John the Baptist has sent us to You to ask, 'Are You the one who is to come, or should we look for another?'"

**21** In that hour He healed many of diseases and afflictions and evil spirits, and to many who were blind He gave sight.

**22** He answered them, "Go and tell John what you have seen and heard: the blind see again, the lame walk, lepers are cleansed, the deaf hear, the dead are raised, and the poor have good news preached to them.

**23** Blessed is the one who does not stumble because of Me."

**24** When John's messengers had gone, He began to speak to the crowds about John: "What did you go out into the wilderness to look at? A reed shaken by the wind?

**25** What then did you go out to see? A man dressed in soft clothing? Those who are splendidly dressed and live in luxury are in the palaces of kings.

**26** What then did you go out to see? A prophet? Yes, I tell you, and more than a prophet.

**27** This is the one of whom it is written: 'I am sending My messenger ahead of You, who will prepare Your way before You.'

**28** I tell you, among those born of women no one is greater than John; yet the least in the kingdom of God is greater than he."

**29** (All the people who heard, even the tax collectors, acknowledged God's justice, having been baptized with John's baptism;

**30** but the Pharisees and the lawyers rejected God's purpose for themselves, not having been baptized by him.)

**31** "To what, then, shall I compare the people of this generation? What are they like?

**32** They are like children sitting in the marketplace and calling out to one another, 'We played the flute for you, and you did not dance; we sang a lament, and you did not weep.'

**33** For John the Baptist has come eating no bread and drinking no wine, and you say, 'He has a demon.'

**34** The Son of Man has come eating and drinking, and you say, 'Look, a glutton and a drunkard, a friend of tax collectors and sinners!'

**35** Yet wisdom is proved right by all her children."

## A Woman Who Was a Sinner

**36** One of the Pharisees asked Him to eat with him, and He went into the Pharisee's house and reclined at table.

**37** There was a woman in the town who was a sinner. When she learned that He was reclining at table in the Pharisee's house, she brought an alabaster jar of perfume,

**38** and standing behind Him at His feet, weeping, she began to wet His feet with her tears and wiped them with the hair of her head, and she kissed His feet and anointed them with the perfume.

**39** When the Pharisee who had invited Him saw it, he said to himself, "If this man were a prophet, He would know who and what sort of woman this is who is touching Him, that she is a sinner."

**40** Jesus answered him, "Simon, I have something to say to you." He said, "Say it, Teacher."

**41** "A moneylender had two debtors: one owed five hundred denarii, and the other fifty.

**42** When they could not pay, he forgave them both. Which of them, then, will love him more?"

**43** Simon answered, "The one, I suppose, for whom he forgave more." He said to him, "You have judged rightly."

**44** Turning toward the woman, He said to Simon, "Do you see this woman? I entered your house; you gave Me no water for My feet, but she has wet My feet with her tears and wiped them with her hair.

**45** You gave Me no kiss, but from the time I came in she has not stopped kissing My feet.

**46** You did not anoint My head with oil, but she has anointed My feet with perfume.

**47** Therefore I tell you, her sins, which are many, are forgiven, for she loved much; but the one who is forgiven little loves little."

**48** He said to her, "Your sins are forgiven."

**49** Those who were reclining at table with Him began to say among themselves, "Who is this, who even forgives sins?"

**50** He said to the woman, "Your faith has saved you; go in peace."

## Notes

- **vv1–10 "a slave who was dear to him… elders of the Jews… I am not worthy to have You come under my roof… not even in Israel"** — Matthew's centurion comes himself (Matthew 8:5); Luke's sends elders and then friends, and never meets Jesus. *Doulos*, **slave** (vv2, 3, 10), and *pais*, **servant** (v7). *Axios*, **worthy**, twice: the elders say he is worthy (v4), and he says he is not (vv6–7).
- **vv11–17 "the only son of his mother, and she was a widow… Jesus gave him to his mother… God has visited His people"** — only Luke has this raising. *Kai edōken auton tē mētri autou*, **gave him to his mother**, are the words of the Greek of Elijah's raising of the widow's son, *and gave him to his mother* (1 Kings 17:23), and the crowd answers, *A great prophet has risen among us*. *Ho kyrios*, **the Lord**, Luke's title for Jesus in the telling, here for the first time (also vv19; 10:1, 39, 41). *Epeskepsato*, **has visited**, the word of Zechariah's song (1:68). *Soros*, **bier**, an open frame for carrying the dead.
- **vv18–23 "Are You the one who is to come… the blind see again, the lame walk… the dead are raised, and the poor have good news preached to them"** — Isaiah's signs, *the eyes of the blind will be opened, and the ears of the deaf unstopped* (Isaiah 35:5), and *to bring good news to the poor* (Isaiah 61:1), the words Jesus read at Nazareth (4:18). Only Luke tells that *in that hour* He healed many before answering (v21).
- **vv24–28 "a reed shaken by the wind… more than a prophet… I am sending My messenger"** — as Matthew 11:7–11, with the words of Exodus 23:20 and Malachi 3:1 (the note on Matthew 11:10). *Angeloi*, **messengers** (v24), is the word for John's men, the same word as *angel*: here plainly men.
- **vv29–30 "acknowledged God's justice… rejected God's purpose for themselves"** — Luke's own comment, set in parentheses. *Edikaiōsan ton theon*, word for word **declared God righteous**, the verb of v35, *wisdom is proved right*. *Hoi nomikoi*, **the lawyers**, experts in the law of Moses.
- **vv31–35 "We played the flute for you, and you did not dance… wisdom is proved right by all her children"** — as Matthew 11:16–19, where Matthew has *by her deeds*; Luke has *by all her children*, the people of v29.
- **vv36–50 "a woman… who was a sinner… her sins, which are many, are forgiven, for she loved much… Your faith has saved you"** — *Simon* the Pharisee, another than Simon Peter. A host gave water for the feet, a kiss and oil for the head; Simon gave none, the woman gave all three, to the feet. *Hoti ēgapēsen poly*, **for she loved much**: her love shows that she has been forgiven, as the parable says (vv41–43), the one forgiven more loves more. *Hē pistis sou sesōken se*, **Your faith has saved you**, as to the woman with the flow of blood (Mark 5:34).
