# Luke 18

## The Widow and the Judge

**1** He told them a parable to show that they ought always to pray and not lose heart,

**2** saying, "In a certain city there was a judge who did not fear God and had no respect for man.

**3** There was a widow in that city who kept coming to him, saying, 'Give me justice against my opponent.'

**4** For a while he refused; but afterward he said to himself, 'Though I do not fear God or respect man,

**5** yet because this widow keeps bothering me, I will give her justice, so that she will not wear me out by coming to me in the end.'"

**6** The Lord said, "Hear what the unjust judge says.

**7** Will not God give justice to His chosen ones, who cry to Him day and night? Will He delay long over them?

**8** I tell you, He will give them justice quickly. But when the Son of Man comes, will He find faith on the earth?"

## The Pharisee and the Tax Collector

**9** He also told this parable to some who trusted in themselves that they were righteous and looked down on everyone else:

**10** "Two men went up into the temple to pray, one a Pharisee and the other a tax collector.

**11** The Pharisee stood and prayed these things to himself: 'God, I thank You that I am not like other men — swindlers, unjust, adulterers — or even like this tax collector.

**12** I fast twice a week; I give a tenth of all that I get.'

**13** But the tax collector, standing far off, would not even lift up his eyes to heaven, but beat his breast, saying, 'God, be merciful to me, the sinner!'

**14** I tell you, this man went down to his house justified rather than the other; for everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and the one who humbles himself will be exalted."

## The Children

**15** People were bringing even their babies to Him so that He might touch them; but when the disciples saw it, they rebuked them.

**16** But Jesus called them to Him, saying, "Let the children come to Me, and do not hinder them; for the kingdom of God belongs to such as these.

**17** Truly I tell you, whoever does not receive the kingdom of God like a child will never enter it."

## The Rich Ruler

**18** A ruler asked Him, "Good Teacher, what must I do to inherit eternal life?"

**19** Jesus said to him, "Why do you call Me good? No one is good but God alone.

**20** You know the commandments: 'Do not commit adultery, do not murder, do not steal, do not give false testimony, honor your father and mother.'"

**21** He said, "All these I have kept from my youth."

**22** When Jesus heard this, He said to him, "You still lack one thing: sell all that you have and distribute it to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; then come, follow Me."

**23** But when he heard these things, he became very sad, for he was very rich.

**24** Jesus looked at him and said, "How hard it is for those who have wealth to enter the kingdom of God!

**25** For it is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle than for a rich man to enter the kingdom of God."

**26** Those who heard it said, "Then who can be saved?"

**27** He said, "What is impossible with men is possible with God."

**28** Peter said, "We have left our own things and followed You."

**29** He said to them, "Truly I tell you, there is no one who has left house or wife or brothers or parents or children for the sake of the kingdom of God,

**30** who will not receive many times as much in this time, and in the age to come eternal life."

## The Third Prediction of the Passion

**31** He took the twelve aside and said to them, "We are going up to Jerusalem, and everything that is written through the prophets about the Son of Man will be accomplished.

**32** For He will be handed over to the Gentiles; He will be mocked, mistreated and spit upon;

**33** they will flog Him and kill Him, and on the third day He will rise."

**34** But they understood none of these things; this saying was hidden from them, and they did not grasp what was said.

## The Blind Man near Jericho

**35** As He drew near to Jericho, a blind man was sitting by the road, begging.

**36** When he heard a crowd going by, he asked what this might be.

**37** They told him, "Jesus of Nazareth is passing by."

**38** He called out, "Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me!"

**39** Those who were in front rebuked him, telling him to be quiet; but he cried out all the more, "Son of David, have mercy on me!"

**40** Jesus stopped and ordered him to be brought to Him. When he came near, He asked him,

**41** "What do you want Me to do for you?" He said, "Lord, let me see again."

**42** Jesus said to him, "Receive your sight; your faith has made you well."

**43** At once he received his sight and followed Him, glorifying God. When all the people saw it, they gave praise to God.

## Notes

- **vv1–5 "a judge who did not fear God… a widow… Give me justice against my opponent… wear me out"** — the law guards the widow, *You shall not afflict any widow or orphan* (Exodus 22:22), and God *does justice for the orphan and the widow* (Deuteronomy 10:18). *Ekdikēson me*, **give me justice**, a ruling in her favor. *Hypōpiazē*, **wear me out**, word for word **give me a black eye**, said in jest. The judge gives in as the friend at midnight does, *because of his shamelessness* (11:8).
- **vv6–8 "the unjust judge… Will not God give justice to His chosen ones… will He find faith on the earth?"** — *ho kritēs tēs adikias*, word for word **the judge of unrighteousness**, as the manager of 16:8 is *ton oikonomon tēs adikias*. If an unjust judge gives way, how much more God. *Tēn pistin*, **faith**, with the article: the faith that keeps praying.
- **vv9–12 "trusted in themselves… God, I thank You that I am not like other men… I fast twice a week"** — *pros heauton*, **to himself**, may also go with *stood*: **stood by himself**. The law set one fast a year, the Day of Atonement, *you shall afflict yourselves* (Leviticus 16:29); twice a week is beyond it. The tithe was on *all the yield of your seed* (Deuteronomy 14:22); he tithes *all that I get*, as the Pharisees *tithe mint and rue and every herb* (11:42).
- **vv13–14 "standing far off… beat his breast… God, be merciful to me, the sinner… justified"** — *hilasthēti*, **be merciful**, is the verb of atonement, the word the Greek has in *atone for our sins for Your name's sake* (Psalm 79:9; 78:9 in the Greek numbering). *Tō hamartōlō*, **the sinner**, with the article. *Dedikaiōmenos*, **justified**, declared righteous by God, the word of v9's *righteous*. The last line is 14:11.
- **vv15–17 "even their babies… Let the children come to Me"** — as Mark 10:13–15, but *ta brephē*, **babies**, where Mark has *children*. Luke leaves out Mark's taking them in His arms.
- **vv18–23 "A ruler… Do not commit adultery, do not murder, do not steal… he was very rich"** — as Mark 10:17–22, where he is *a man*, and Matthew, a *young man* (Matthew 19:20); only Luke makes him *archōn*, **a ruler**. The commandments come in the order of the Greek of Deuteronomy 5:17–19, adultery before murder. *Sell all that you have and distribute it to the poor*, as Jesus said to the disciples, *Sell your possessions and give alms* (12:33).
- **vv24–30 "a camel to go through the eye of a needle… We have left our own things… many times as much"** — as Mark 10:23–30. *Trēma belonēs*, **the eye of a needle**, where Mark has *trymalias rhaphidos*, other words for the same thing. Only Luke has *wife* among the things left.
- **vv31–34 "everything that is written through the prophets… mocked, mistreated and spit upon… on the third day He will rise… hidden from them"** — the third time Jesus tells of His death (9:22, 44). *All that is written* in the prophets is, among others, the servant who gave *my cheeks to those who pull out the beard; I did not hide my face from insults and spitting* (Isaiah 50:6). The disciples do not understand, as in 9:45, *it was hidden from them*.
- **vv35–43 "As He drew near to Jericho… Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me… Receive your sight"** — as Mark 10:46–52, where it is Bartimaeus as Jesus leaves Jericho; here He is coming near it, and the man is not named. *Son of David* is the title of the king from David's line, *I will raise up your seed after you* (2 Samuel 7:12). The blind seeing is the sign Jesus named to John, *the blind see again* (7:22).
