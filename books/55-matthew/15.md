# Matthew 15

## The Tradition of the Elders

**1** Then Pharisees and scribes came to Jesus from Jerusalem and said,

**2** "Why do Your disciples break the tradition of the elders? For they do not wash their hands when they eat bread."

**3** He answered them, "Why do you also break the commandment of God for the sake of your tradition?

**4** For God said, 'Honor your father and mother,' and, 'Whoever speaks evil of father or mother shall surely die.'

**5** But you say, 'Whoever says to his father or mother, "Whatever support you might have had from me is a gift to God,"

**6** need not honor his father.' So you have made the word of God void for the sake of your tradition.

**7** Hypocrites! Isaiah prophesied rightly about you, saying:

**8** 'This people honors Me with their lips, but their heart is far from Me;

**9** in vain do they worship Me, teaching as teachings the commandments of men.'"

## What Makes a Person Unclean

**10** He called the crowd to Him and said to them, "Listen and understand:

**11** it is not what goes into the mouth that makes a person unclean, but what comes out of the mouth — this makes a person unclean."

**12** Then the disciples came and said to Him, "Do You know that the Pharisees took offense when they heard this saying?"

**13** He answered, "Every plant that My heavenly Father has not planted will be uprooted.

**14** Leave them; they are blind guides of the blind. If a blind man guides a blind man, both will fall into a pit."

**15** Peter said to Him, "Explain the parable to us."

**16** He said, "Are you also still without understanding?

**17** Do you not see that whatever goes into the mouth passes into the stomach and is expelled into the latrine?

**18** But what comes out of the mouth comes from the heart, and this makes a person unclean.

**19** For out of the heart come evil thoughts, murders, adulteries, sexual immorality, thefts, false testimony, slander.

**20** These are what make a person unclean; but to eat with unwashed hands does not make a person unclean."

## The Canaanite Woman

**21** Jesus went away from there and withdrew to the district of Tyre and Sidon.

**22** A Canaanite woman from that region came out and cried, "Have mercy on me, Lord, Son of David! My daughter is cruelly possessed by a demon."

**23** But He did not answer her a word. His disciples came and urged Him, "Send her away, for she keeps crying out after us."

**24** He answered, "I was sent only to the lost sheep of the house of Israel."

**25** But she came and knelt before Him, saying, "Lord, help me."

**26** He answered, "It is not right to take the children's bread and throw it to the dogs."

**27** She said, "Yes, Lord; yet even the dogs eat the crumbs that fall from their masters' table."

**28** Then Jesus answered her, "Woman, great is your faith! Let it be done for you as you wish." Her daughter was healed from that hour.

## Healing by the Sea of Galilee

**29** Jesus went on from there and came beside the Sea of Galilee. He went up the mountain and sat down there.

**30** Great crowds came to Him, bringing with them the lame, the blind, the maimed, the mute and many others, and they laid them at His feet, and He healed them,

**31** so that the crowd was amazed when they saw the mute speaking, the maimed made whole, the lame walking and the blind seeing; and they glorified the God of Israel.

## Seven Loaves

**32** Jesus called His disciples to Him and said, "I have compassion on the crowd, because they have stayed with Me three days now and have nothing to eat. I do not want to send them away hungry, or they may faint on the way."

**33** The disciples said to Him, "Where could we get enough bread in a deserted place to feed so great a crowd?"

**34** Jesus said to them, "How many loaves do you have?" They said, "Seven, and a few small fish."

**35** He ordered the crowd to sit down on the ground,

**36** took the seven loaves and the fish, and after giving thanks He broke them and gave them to the disciples, and the disciples to the crowds.

**37** They all ate and were satisfied, and they picked up what was left over of the broken pieces, seven baskets full.

**38** Those who ate were four thousand men, besides women and children.

**39** After sending the crowds away, He got into the boat and came to the region of Magadan.

## Notes

- **v2 "the tradition of the elders"** — *paradosis*, **what is handed down**: rules taught from teacher to student alongside the written Law. The washing of hands before bread is one of them; Jesus sets it against *the commandment of God* (v3).
- **v4 "Honor your father and mother… Whoever speaks evil of father or mother shall surely die"** — Exodus 20:12, *Honor your father and your mother*, and Exodus 21:17, *Whoever curses his father or his mother shall be put to death*. *Shall surely die* is *thanatō teleutatō*, **let him end by death**.
- **v5 "a gift to God"** — *dōron*, **a gift**, an offering vowed to the temple. The same saying in Mark gives the Hebrew word: *Korban, that is, Gift* (Mark 7:11). Once the support was vowed, the son could say his parents had no claim on it.
- **vv8–9 "This people honors Me with their lips"** — Isaiah 29:13 in its Greek form. The Hebrew ends differently: *their fear of Me is a commandment of men, learned by rote* (*mitsvat anashim melummadah*); the Greek has *in vain do they worship Me, teaching the commandments of men*, and Matthew follows the Greek.
- **v11 "makes a person unclean"** — *koinoi*, **makes common**: the opposite of holy, set apart. The dispute is about hands and bread, but v17 goes further: food itself passes through the body and out.
- **v22 "a Canaanite woman"** — *Chananaia*, the old name of the people of the land, where Mark calls her *a Greek, a Syrophoenician by birth*. Sidon was the firstborn of Canaan (Genesis 10:15), and *the border of the Canaanite was from Sidon* (Genesis 10:19). She calls Him *Son of David*, the title of 9:27.
- **v24 "the lost sheep of the house of Israel"** — the words of His charge to the twelve (10:6), now said of Himself.
- **v26 "the dogs"** — *kynaria*, the small dogs of a household, under the table. The woman takes the word as He gives it and answers with it: the dogs of the house eat the crumbs.
- **vv32–38 seven loaves** — a second feeding, told close to the first (14:15–21) but not the same: seven loaves, not five; four thousand, not five thousand; seven *spyrides*, not twelve *kophinoi*, a different word for basket; and *after giving thanks*, *eucharistēsas*, where 14:19 has *said the blessing*.
