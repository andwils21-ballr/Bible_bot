# Matthew 22

## The Wedding Feast

**1** Jesus spoke to them again in parables, saying:

**2** "The kingdom of heaven is like a king who gave a wedding feast for his son.

**3** He sent his slaves to call those who had been invited to the wedding feast, but they would not come.

**4** Again he sent other slaves, saying, 'Tell those who have been invited: Look, I have prepared my dinner; my oxen and fattened calves have been slaughtered, and everything is ready. Come to the wedding feast.'

**5** But they paid no attention and went off, one to his own field, another to his business,

**6** while the rest seized his slaves, mistreated them and killed them.

**7** The king was angry, and he sent his armies, destroyed those murderers and burned their city.

**8** Then he said to his slaves, 'The wedding is ready, but those who were invited were not worthy.

**9** So go to the places where the roads leave the town, and invite to the wedding feast as many as you find.'

**10** Those slaves went out into the roads and gathered all whom they found, both evil and good; and the wedding hall was filled with guests.

**11** But when the king came in to look at the guests, he saw there a man who was not wearing wedding clothes,

**12** and he said to him, 'Friend, how did you get in here without wedding clothes?' The man was speechless.

**13** Then the king said to the servants, 'Bind him hand and foot and throw him into the outer darkness. There will be weeping and grinding of teeth.'

**14** For many are called, but few are chosen."

## Caesar and God

**15** Then the Pharisees went and plotted how they might trap him in what he said.

**16** They sent their disciples to him, along with the Herodians, saying, "Teacher, we know that you are truthful and teach the way of God in truth, and you do not court anyone's favor, for you do not look at the face of men.

**17** So tell us what you think: is it lawful to pay the tax to Caesar, or not?"

**18** But Jesus, knowing their malice, said, "Why are you testing me, you hypocrites?

**19** Show me the coin for the tax." They brought him a denarius.

**20** He said to them, "Whose image is this, and whose inscription?"

**21** They said to him, "Caesar's." Then he said to them, "Give back, then, to Caesar the things that are Caesar's, and to God the things that are God's."

**22** When they heard it, they were amazed; and they left him and went away.

## The God of the Living

**23** That same day Sadducees, who say there is no resurrection, came to him and asked him,

**24** "Teacher, Moses said, 'If a man dies having no children, his brother shall marry his wife and raise up seed for his brother.'

**25** Now there were seven brothers among us. The first married and died, and having no seed, left his wife to his brother.

**26** So also the second and the third, down to the seventh.

**27** Last of all the woman died.

**28** In the resurrection, then, whose wife of the seven will she be? For they all had her."

**29** Jesus answered them, "You are going astray, because you know neither the Scriptures nor the power of God.

**30** For in the resurrection they neither marry nor are given in marriage, but are like angels of God in heaven.

**31** As for the resurrection of the dead, have you not read what was spoken to you by God, saying,

**32** 'I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob'? He is not the God of the dead, but of the living."

**33** When the crowds heard it, they were astonished at his teaching.

## The Great Commandment

**34** When the Pharisees heard that he had silenced the Sadducees, they gathered together.

**35** One of them, an expert in the Law, asked him a question to test him:

**36** "Teacher, which commandment in the Law is the great one?"

**37** He said to him, "'You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your mind.'

**38** This is the great and first commandment.

**39** The second is like it: 'You shall love your neighbor as yourself.'

**40** On these two commandments hang all the Law and the Prophets."

## David's Son and David's Lord

**41** While the Pharisees were gathered together, Jesus asked them,

**42** "What do you think about the Messiah? Whose son is he?" They said to him, "David's."

**43** He said to them, "How then does David, in the Spirit, call him Lord, saying,

**44** 'The Lord said to my Lord, Sit at My right hand until I put your enemies under your feet'?

**45** If David then calls him Lord, how is he his son?"

**46** No one was able to answer him a word, nor from that day did anyone dare to ask him any more questions.

## Notes

- **v7 "burned their city"** — the king's anger reaches beyond the feast: the invited guests lived in a city of their own, and it is destroyed.
- **v9 "the places where the roads leave the town"** — *tas diexodous tōn hodōn*, **the outlets of the roads**, where the streets run out into the open country.
- **v12 "Friend… The man was speechless"** — *Hetaire*, the address of 20:13 and 26:50; *ephimōthē*, **he was muzzled**, the verb used in v34 of the Sadducees, *he had silenced* them.
- **v20 "Whose image is this?"** — *eikōn*, **image**. The coin bears Caesar's image; the human being bears God's: *God created the human in His image* (Genesis 1:27), where the Greek of Genesis has *kat' eikona*.
- **v24 "raise up seed for his brother"** — Deuteronomy 25:5–6: *his brother-in-law shall go in to her and take her as his wife… and the firstborn she bears shall stand on the name of his dead brother, so that his name is not blotted out of Israel*. The wording Matthew gives is closer to Judah's words to Onan: *raise up seed for your brother* (Genesis 38:8).
- **v32 "I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob"** — God's words to Moses at the bush (Exodus 3:6), long after the three had died. *I am*, not *I was*: they are living to Him.
- **v37 "with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your mind"** — Deuteronomy 6:5, *You shall love the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your strength* (*u-vekhol me'odekha*), which follows *Hear, O Israel: the LORD our God, the LORD is one* (6:4). The Greek of Deuteronomy has *mind* (*dianoia*) in the first place; Matthew has *heart, soul, mind*.
- **v39 "You shall love your neighbor as yourself"** — Leviticus 19:18, the end of a verse that begins *You shall not take revenge and you shall not hold a grudge against the sons of your people*.
- **v40 "hang"** — *krematai*: the whole Law and the Prophets hang on these two as on a peg.
- **v44 "The Lord said to my Lord"** — Psalm 110:1, *neum YHWH la-doni*: **the utterance of the LORD to my lord**. The first *Lord* is God's name; the second, *adoni*, is David's word for his master. David calls the Messiah *my lord*, so the Messiah is more than David's son.
