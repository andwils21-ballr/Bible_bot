# Matthew 11

## John's Question

**1** And it came to pass, when Jesus had finished giving instructions to His twelve disciples, that He went on from there to teach and to preach in their towns.

**2** When John heard in prison about the works of the Messiah, he sent word through his disciples

**3** and said to Him, "Are You the one who is to come, or should we look for another?"

**4** Jesus answered them, "Go and tell John what you hear and see:

**5** the blind see again and the lame walk, those with blight are cleansed and the deaf hear, the dead are raised, and the poor have good news preached to them.

**6** Blessed is the one who does not stumble because of Me."

## More than a Prophet

**7** As they were going away, Jesus began to speak to the crowds about John: "What did you go out into the wilderness to look at? A reed shaken by the wind?

**8** What then did you go out to see? A man dressed in soft clothing? Here — those who wear soft clothing are in the houses of kings.

**9** What then did you go out to see? A prophet? Yes, I tell you, and more than a prophet.

**10** This is the one of whom it is written: 'Here — I am sending My messenger before Your face, who will prepare Your way before You.'

**11** Truly I tell you, among those born of women no one has arisen greater than John the Baptist; yet the least in the kingdom of heaven is greater than he.

**12** From the days of John the Baptist until now the kingdom of heaven is taken by force, and the violent seize it.

**13** For all the Prophets and the Law prophesied until John;

**14** and if you are willing to accept it, he is Elijah who is to come.

**15** Whoever has ears, let him hear.

## This Generation

**16** To what shall I compare this generation? It is like children sitting in the marketplaces, calling out to the others,

**17** 'We played the flute for you, and you did not dance; we sang a lament, and you did not mourn.'

**18** For John came neither eating nor drinking, and they say, 'He has a demon.'

**19** The Son of Man came eating and drinking, and they say, 'Here — a glutton and a drunkard, a friend of tax collectors and sinners!' Yet wisdom is proved right by her deeds."

**20** Then He began to reproach the towns in which most of His mighty works had been done, because they did not repent:

**21** "Woe to you, Chorazin! Woe to you, Bethsaida! For if the mighty works done in you had been done in Tyre and Sidon, they would have repented long ago in sackcloth and ashes.

**22** But I tell you, it will be more bearable for Tyre and Sidon on the day of judgment than for you.

**23** You, Capernaum, will you be lifted up to heaven? You will go down to Hades. For if the mighty works done in you had been done in Sodom, it would have remained until this day.

**24** But I tell you that it will be more bearable for the land of Sodom on the day of judgment than for you."

## Come to Me

**25** At that time Jesus said, "I praise You, Father, Lord of heaven and earth, because You have hidden these things from the wise and the understanding and revealed them to little children.

**26** Yes, Father, for this was Your good pleasure.

**27** All things have been handed over to Me by My Father. No one knows the Son except the Father, and no one knows the Father except the Son and anyone to whom the Son chooses to reveal Him.

**28** Come to Me, all you who labor and are burdened, and I will give you rest.

**29** Take My yoke upon you and learn from Me, for I am gentle and humble in heart, and you will find rest for your souls.

**30** For My yoke is easy, and My burden is light."

## Notes

- **v3 "the one who is to come"** — *ho erchomenos*, **the coming one**: John's word for the one *who comes after me* (3:11).
- **v5 the answer** — the words of Isaiah's promises, set side by side: *the eyes of the blind shall be opened, and the ears of the deaf unstopped; the lame shall leap like a deer* (Isaiah 35:5–6), and *He has anointed me to bring good news to the poor* (Isaiah 61:1, *levasser anavim*). Jesus answers John's question with what the prophets said the coming one would do. The dead raised and blight cleansed are added beyond Isaiah's lists.
- **v10 "Here — I am sending My messenger before Your face"** — two passages joined. The first words are the Greek of Exodus 23:20 exactly, *Kai idou egō apostellō ton angelon mou pro prosōpou sou*, God's promise to send a messenger before Israel on the way to the land: *Here — I am sending a messenger before you, to guard you on the way*. The rest is Malachi 3:1, *Here — I am sending My messenger, and he will clear the way before Me*. In Malachi the way is prepared *before Me*, before the LORD; in the Gospel it is *before You*, before Jesus.
- **v11 "the least in the kingdom of heaven is greater than he"** — John stands at the edge: the greatest of those before the kingdom, and less than the least within it.
- **v12 "the kingdom of heaven is taken by force"** — *biazetai*, which can be read as passive, **suffers violence**, or as middle, **forces its way forward**. The next clause, *the violent seize it*, *biastai harpazousin*, is as hard: either men who attack the kingdom or men who press into it with force. The Greek allows both readings, and the rendering keeps the ambiguity.
- **v14 "he is Elijah who is to come"** — the promise that closes the prophets: *Here — I am sending you Elijah the prophet before the great and terrible day of the LORD comes* (Malachi 4:5; 3:23 in the Hebrew). John was already dressed as Elijah (3:4).
- **v19 "wisdom is proved right by her deeds"** — *edikaiōthē*, **was shown to be righteous**. John's fasting and Jesus' eating are both called wrong by the same generation; wisdom's works, v2's *the works of the Messiah*, show who was right.
- **v23 "will you be lifted up to heaven? You will go down to Hades"** — Isaiah's taunt against the king of Babylon: *You said in your heart, "I will go up to heaven"… but you are brought down to Sheol* (Isaiah 14:13, 15). *Hades* is the Greek name for Sheol, the place of the dead.
- **v28 "I will give you rest"** — *anapausō*. In v29 *you will find rest for your souls* is Jeremiah 6:16, *u-mits'u margoa le-nafshekhem*, where the LORD says, *Stand at the crossroads… ask for the ancient paths, where the good way is, and walk in it, and you will find rest for your souls*, and the people answer, *We will not walk in it*.
- **v29 "My yoke"** — the yoke laid on a working animal's neck, the image of a load borne in service. The one who offers it calls Himself *gentle*, *praus*, the word of the Beatitude, *Blessed are the meek* (5:5).
