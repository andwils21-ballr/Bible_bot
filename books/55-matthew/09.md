# Matthew 9

## The Paralyzed Man

**1** He got into a boat, crossed over, and came to his own town.

**2** And here — they brought to him a paralyzed man lying on a bed. When Jesus saw their faith, he said to the paralyzed man, "Take heart, child; your sins are forgiven."

**3** And here — some of the scribes said to themselves, "This man is blaspheming."

**4** Jesus, knowing their thoughts, said, "Why do you think evil in your hearts?

**5** Which is easier: to say, 'Your sins are forgiven,' or to say, 'Get up and walk'?

**6** But so that you may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins" — then he said to the paralyzed man, "Get up, pick up your bed, and go home."

**7** He got up and went home.

**8** When the crowds saw it, they were afraid, and they glorified God, who had given such authority to human beings.

## The Call of Matthew

**9** As Jesus went on from there, he saw a man called Matthew sitting at the tax booth, and he said to him, "Follow me." He got up and followed him.

**10** And it came to pass, as he was reclining at table in the house, that here — many tax collectors and sinners came and reclined with Jesus and his disciples.

**11** When the Pharisees saw this, they said to his disciples, "Why does your teacher eat with tax collectors and sinners?"

**12** When he heard it, he said, "It is not the healthy who need a doctor, but those who are ill.

**13** Go and learn what this means: 'I desire mercy, not sacrifice.' For I did not come to call the righteous, but sinners."

## The Bridegroom

**14** Then the disciples of John came to him and said, "Why do we and the Pharisees fast often, but your disciples do not fast?"

**15** Jesus said to them, "Can the wedding guests mourn while the bridegroom is with them? But the days will come when the bridegroom is taken away from them, and then they will fast.

**16** No one sews a patch of unshrunk cloth on an old garment, for the patch pulls away from the garment, and the tear becomes worse.

**17** Nor do people pour new wine into old wineskins. Otherwise the skins burst, the wine spills out, and the skins are ruined. But they pour new wine into fresh wineskins, and both are kept safe."

## A Daughter and a Woman

**18** While he was saying these things to them, here — a leader came and knelt before him, saying, "My daughter has just died; but come and lay your hand on her, and she will live."

**19** Jesus got up and followed him, and so did his disciples.

**20** And here — a woman who had suffered from bleeding for twelve years came up behind him and touched the tassel of his cloak;

**21** for she said to herself, "If I only touch his cloak, I will be made well."

**22** Jesus turned, saw her, and said, "Take heart, daughter; your faith has made you well." The woman was made well from that hour.

**23** When Jesus came into the leader's house and saw the flute players and the crowd in an uproar,

**24** he said, "Go away, for the girl is not dead but asleep." They laughed at him.

**25** But when the crowd had been put out, he went in and took her by the hand, and the girl got up.

**26** The news of this went out through all that region.

## Two Blind Men and a Mute Man

**27** As Jesus went on from there, two blind men followed him, crying out, "Have mercy on us, Son of David!"

**28** When he had gone into the house, the blind men came to him, and Jesus said to them, "Do you believe that I am able to do this?" They said to him, "Yes, Lord."

**29** Then he touched their eyes, saying, "Let it be done for you according to your faith."

**30** Their eyes were opened. Jesus sternly warned them, "See that no one knows of this."

**31** But they went out and spread the news about him through all that region.

**32** As they were going out, here — a mute man possessed by a demon was brought to him.

**33** When the demon had been driven out, the mute man spoke; and the crowds were amazed and said, "Nothing like this has ever been seen in Israel."

**34** But the Pharisees said, "He drives out the demons by the ruler of the demons."

## The Harvest

**35** Jesus went through all the towns and villages, teaching in their synagogues, preaching the good news of the kingdom, and healing every disease and every sickness.

**36** When he saw the crowds, he was moved with compassion for them, because they were harassed and helpless, like sheep that have no shepherd.

**37** Then he said to his disciples, "The harvest is plentiful, but the workers are few;

**38** so ask the Lord of the harvest to send out workers into his harvest."

## Notes

- **v1 "his own town"** — Capernaum, where he settled in 4:13, not Nazareth.
- **v2 "your sins are forgiven"** — the passive leaves unsaid who forgives. The scribes hear the unspoken answer, *God*, and call it blasphemy (v3). Jesus' reply claims the authority *on earth* for *the Son of Man* (v6), and the crowd in v8 praises God *who had given such authority to human beings*, plural: the forgiveness goes out through men.
- **v9 "a man called Matthew"** — *Maththaios*, at the tax booth. In the list of the twelve he is *Matthew the tax collector* (10:3).
- **v13 "I desire mercy, not sacrifice"** — Hosea 6:6, *ki chesed chafatsti ve-lo zavach*. *Chesed* is steadfast love, loyalty to a bond; the Greek has *eleos*, **mercy**. Hosea's own second line shows the sense: *the knowledge of God more than burnt offerings* (*me-olot*), first things before second, not offerings abolished; Jesus quotes it here against those who will not eat with sinners, and again in 12:7.
- **v15 "the wedding guests"** — *hoi huioi tou nymphōnos*, **the sons of the bridal chamber**, the groom's friends at the feast. The groom is here, so it is a wedding, not a fast; *taken away* is the first hint of his death.
- **v17 "new wine into fresh wineskins"** — new wine still working swells the skin; an old skin gives way under it, and *both are kept safe* only when the new goes into the new.
- **v18 "a leader"** — *archōn*, a ruler or official; Matthew does not say of what. *Knelt before him* is *prosekynei*, the verb of the magi's worship in 2:11.
- **v20 "a woman who had suffered from bleeding for twelve years"** — under the Law such a woman was unclean *all the days of her unclean flow* (Leviticus 15:25). She touches *the tassel of his cloak*, *tou kraspedou*, the word the Greek of Numbers uses for the tassels every Israelite was to wear on the corners of his garments (Numbers 15:38, *kraspeda*). Again the touch that should make unclean makes whole, as with the man with blight in 8:3.
- **v22 "your faith has made you well"** — *sesōken se*, **has saved you**: the same verb, *sōzō*, means to save and to heal, and is the root of *He will save* in 1:21.
- **v27 "Son of David"** — the first time anyone calls Jesus by the title of 1:1, and it is two blind men who do.
- **v36 "like sheep that have no shepherd"** — Moses' prayer for a leader after him: *so that the congregation of the LORD may not be like sheep that have no shepherd* (Numbers 27:17), answered by Joshua, the name Jesus bears (1:21).
