# Matthew 3

## John in the Wilderness

**1** In those days John the Baptist came, preaching in the wilderness of Judea,

**2** saying, "Repent, for the kingdom of heaven has come near."

**3** This is the one spoken of through Isaiah the prophet, saying, "A voice of one crying out in the wilderness: 'Prepare the way of the Lord; make His paths straight.'"

**4** John himself wore clothing of camel's hair and a leather belt around his waist, and his food was locusts and wild honey.

**5** Then Jerusalem, all Judea and all the region around the Jordan were going out to him,

**6** and they were being baptized by him in the Jordan River, confessing their sins.

**7** When he saw many of the Pharisees and Sadducees coming to his baptism, he said to them, "Brood of vipers! Who warned you to flee from the wrath to come?

**8** Bear fruit worthy of repentance, then,

**9** and do not presume to say to yourselves, 'We have Abraham as our father.' For I tell you that God is able from these stones to raise up children for Abraham.

**10** The axe is already laid at the root of the trees. Every tree, then, that does not bear good fruit is cut down and thrown into the fire.

**11** I baptize you with water for repentance, but the one who comes after me is stronger than I; I am not worthy to carry His sandals. He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and fire.

**12** His winnowing fork is in His hand, and He will clear His threshing floor and gather His wheat into the barn; but the chaff He will burn with fire that cannot be put out."

## The Baptism of Jesus

**13** Then Jesus came from Galilee to the Jordan, to John, to be baptized by him.

**14** But John tried to prevent Him, saying, "I need to be baptized by You, and You are coming to me?"

**15** Jesus answered him, "Let it be so now, for it is fitting for us in this way to fulfill all righteousness." Then he let Him.

**16** When Jesus had been baptized, He came up at once from the water. And here — the heavens were opened, and He saw the Spirit of God coming down like a dove and coming upon Him.

**17** And here — a voice from the heavens, saying, "This is My Son, the beloved, in whom I am well pleased."

## Notes

- **v2 "Repent"** — *metanoeite*, from *meta*, a change, and *noeō*, to think or perceive: **change your mind**, turn the whole way of seeing. It is the first word of John's preaching and, in the same words, of Jesus' own (4:17).
- **v2 "the kingdom of heaven"** — *hē basileia tōn ouranōn*, literally **of the heavens**, plural, as the Hebrew *shamayim* is always plural. Where Matthew has *the kingdom of heaven*, Mark has *the kingdom of God* in the same saying (Mark 1:15); the two mean the same reign. *Has come near*, *ēngiken*, is a perfect: it has arrived close and is standing there.
- **v3 "A voice of one crying out in the wilderness"** — Isaiah 40:3 in its Greek form. The Hebrew runs *kol kore ba-midbar pannu derekh YHWH*, which can be read **A voice cries: In the wilderness prepare the way of the LORD**: in Isaiah the wilderness is where the road is built; in the Gospel it is where the voice is heard. Both are true of John. Where Isaiah has *the way of YHWH*, the Greek has *the way of the Lord*, and the Gospel's Lord is Jesus.
- **v4 "camel's hair and a leather belt"** — the dress by which King Ahaziah's men recognized Elijah: *a hairy man, with a leather belt around his waist*, and the king said, *It is Elijah the Tishbite* (2 Kings 1:8). John is dressed as Elijah before anyone calls him so (11:14).
- **v7 "Brood of vipers"** — *gennēmata echidnōn*, **offspring of snakes**. It answers the claim of v9: they say they are Abraham's children; John names another parentage.
- **v9 "from these stones… children for Abraham"** — Isaiah had already joined Abraham and stone: *Look to the rock from which you were cut… look to Abraham your father* (Isaiah 51:1–2, *tsur chutsavtem… Avraham avichem*). The people were once quarried from a rock; God can do it again from the stones at the Jordan, so descent alone guarantees nothing.
- **v11 "with the Holy Spirit and fire"** — one preposition, *en*, governs both: a single baptism of Spirit and fire. v12 then shows what the fire does to chaff.
- **v15 "to fulfill all righteousness"** — Jesus' first words in the Gospel. *Righteousness*, *dikaiosynē*, is the doing of what God requires; He is baptized not because He needs cleansing, as John's protest supposes, but because it is required.
- **v17 "This is My Son, the beloved, in whom I am well pleased"** — three passages in one sentence. *My Son* is the king of Psalm 2:7, *You are My son*. *In whom I am well pleased* is the servant of Isaiah 42:1, *My chosen one, in whom My soul delights*. And *the beloved*, *ho agapētos*, is the Greek of Genesis for Isaac: *Take your son, your beloved one* (Genesis 22:2, *ton agapēton*), the son offered on the mountain.
