# Matthew 4

## The Testing in the Wilderness

**1** Then Jesus was led up into the wilderness by the Spirit, to be tested by the devil.

**2** He fasted forty days and forty nights, and afterward he was hungry.

**3** The tester came and said to him, "If you are the Son of God, tell these stones to become loaves of bread."

**4** He answered, "It is written: 'A person shall not live on bread alone, but on every word that comes out of the mouth of God.'"

**5** Then the devil took him into the holy city and set him on the pinnacle of the temple,

**6** and said to him, "If you are the Son of God, throw yourself down; for it is written, 'He will command His angels concerning you,' and 'On their hands they will lift you up, so that you will not strike your foot against a stone.'"

**7** Jesus said to him, "Again it is written: 'You shall not put the Lord your God to the test.'"

**8** Again the devil took him to a very high mountain and showed him all the kingdoms of the world and their glory,

**9** and said to him, "All these I will give you, if you will fall down and worship me."

**10** Then Jesus said to him, "Go away, Satan! For it is written: 'You shall worship the Lord your God, and Him only shall you serve.'"

**11** Then the devil left him, and here — angels came and served him.

## Light in Galilee

**12** When he heard that John had been handed over, he withdrew to Galilee.

**13** Leaving Nazareth, he came and settled in Capernaum by the sea, in the region of Zebulun and Naphtali,

**14** to fulfill what was spoken through Isaiah the prophet, saying,

**15** "Land of Zebulun and land of Naphtali, the way of the sea, beyond the Jordan, Galilee of the nations —

**16** the people who sat in darkness have seen a great light, and on those who sat in the region and shadow of death a light has dawned."

**17** From then on Jesus began to preach and to say, "Repent, for the kingdom of heaven has come near."

## The First Disciples

**18** As he walked beside the Sea of Galilee, he saw two brothers, Simon who is called Peter and Andrew his brother, casting a net into the sea, for they were fishermen.

**19** He said to them, "Come, follow me, and I will make you fishers of people."

**20** At once they left their nets and followed him.

**21** Going on from there, he saw two other brothers, James the son of Zebedee and John his brother, in the boat with Zebedee their father, mending their nets, and he called them.

**22** At once they left the boat and their father and followed him.

## Teaching and Healing

**23** He went throughout all Galilee, teaching in their synagogues, preaching the good news of the kingdom, and healing every disease and every sickness among the people.

**24** The report of him went out through all Syria. They brought him all who were ill with various diseases and gripped by pains: those possessed by demons, those with seizures, and the paralyzed; and he healed them.

**25** Great crowds followed him from Galilee and the Decapolis, from Jerusalem and Judea, and from beyond the Jordan.

## Notes

- **vv1–11 forty in the wilderness** — every answer Jesus gives comes from Deuteronomy 6–8, Moses' words to Israel after forty years in the wilderness: *the LORD your God led you these forty years in the wilderness, to humble you, to test you* (*lenassotekha*), *to know what was in your heart* (Deuteronomy 8:2). Israel was called *My son, My firstborn* (Exodus 4:22); the voice at the Jordan has just called Jesus *My Son* (3:17). The son is tested where the people were tested, and answers with the people's own lesson.
- **v2 "forty days and forty nights"** — the words used of Moses on the mountain: *forty days and forty nights; he ate no bread and drank no water* (Exodus 34:28).
- **v3 "The tester"** — *ho peirazōn*, the one who tests; the same verb as *to be tested* in v1. The Greek *peirazō* covers both testing and tempting.
- **v4 "not live on bread alone"** — Deuteronomy 8:3, said of the manna: *He let you hunger and fed you with manna… to make you know that a person does not live on bread alone, but on everything that comes out of the mouth of the LORD*. Hunger was part of the lesson then as now.
- **v6 "He will command His angels concerning you"** — Psalm 91:11–12. The devil quotes Scripture too, and leaves out the psalm's next words: *to guard you in all your ways*. The psalm promises protection on the road, not a leap from the temple.
- **v7 "You shall not put the Lord your God to the test"** — Deuteronomy 6:16, which goes on: *as you tested Him at Massah*. At Massah the people tested the LORD by saying, *Is the LORD among us or not?* (Exodus 17:7). To throw oneself down, to see whether God will catch, is the same question.
- **v10 "You shall worship the Lord your God, and Him only shall you serve"** — Deuteronomy 6:13, where the Hebrew reads *et YHWH elohekha tira*, **you shall fear the LORD your God**. The Gospel has *proskynēseis*, **you shall worship**, which answers the devil's *worship me* in v9 word for word, and adds *only*, *monō*.
- **vv15–16 "Galilee of the nations… a light has dawned"** — Isaiah 9:1–2 (8:23–9:1 in the Hebrew), promised to the land of Zebulun and Naphtali. Isaiah has *the people who walked in darkness*; the Gospel has *the people who sat in darkness*.
- **v19 "fishers of people"** — *halieis anthrōpōn*, **fishers of human beings**: the men are called in the words of their own trade.
- **v24 "those with seizures"** — *selēniazomenous*, literally **moonstruck**, from *selēnē*, the moon: the Greek name for what was thought to come with the moon's phases, the illness we call epilepsy.
