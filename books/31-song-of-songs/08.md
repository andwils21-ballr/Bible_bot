# Song of Songs 8

**1** If only you were like a brother to me,  
who nursed at my mother's breasts!  
If I found you outside, I would kiss you,  
and no one would despise me.

**2** I would lead you and bring you  
into my mother's house;  
she would teach me.  
I would give you spiced wine to drink,  
the juice of my pomegranate.

**3** His left hand is under my head,  
and his right hand embraces me.

**4** I charge you, daughters of Jerusalem:  
why should you stir up or awaken love  
until it pleases?

**5** Who is this coming up from the wilderness,  
leaning on her beloved?  
Under the apple tree I awakened you;  
there your mother was in labor with you;  
there she who bore you was in labor.

**6** Set me as a seal upon your heart,  
as a seal upon your arm,  
for love is strong as death,  
jealousy is fierce as Sheol.  
Its flashes are flashes of fire,  
the flame of Yah.

**7** Many waters cannot quench love,  
nor can rivers sweep it away.  
If a man gave all the wealth of his house for love,  
he would be utterly scorned.

**8** We have a little sister,  
and she has no breasts.  
What shall we do for our sister  
on the day when she is spoken for?

**9** If she is a wall,  
we will build on her a battlement of silver;  
but if she is a door,  
we will enclose her with boards of cedar.

**10** I am a wall,  
and my breasts are like towers;  
then I was in his eyes  
as one who finds peace.

**11** Solomon had a vineyard at Baal-hamon;  
he gave the vineyard to keepers;  
each was to bring for its fruit  
a thousand pieces of silver.

**12** My own vineyard is before me.  
The thousand are yours, Solomon,  
and two hundred for those who keep its fruit.

**13** You who dwell in the gardens,  
companions are listening for your voice;  
let me hear it.

**14** Flee, my beloved,  
and be like a gazelle  
or a young stag  
on the mountains of spices.

## Notes

- **v2 "she would teach me"** — *telammedeni*, "she would teach me," that is, her mother, or "you would teach me"; the Hebrew form allows both. The Greek has instead "into the room of her who conceived me," as in 3:4.
- **v4** — the charge of 2:7 and 3:5, but without the gazelles and does, and with *mah*, "why," for *im*: not "that you not," but "why should you?" The Greek gives the full form of 2:7.
- **v5 "Who is this coming up from the wilderness"** — the question of 3:6, now answered by the two of them coming together. "Under the apple tree" — the tree of 2:3, where she sat in his shade.
- **v6 "Set me as a seal"** — a seal was worn on a cord over the heart or as a ring on the hand (Genesis 38:18; Jeremiah 22:24), the mark of its owner. "Jealousy" — *qin'ah*, the zeal that will not share what is its own; from the same root God calls Himself "a jealous God" (Exodus 20:5). "The flame of Yah" — *shalhevetyah*: "the flame of Yah," the short form of God's name (as in Hallelujah), or "a mighty flame," with *-yah* making the word a superlative. If the first, it is the only place God is named in the Song. The Greek has "its flames."
- **v7 "he would be utterly scorned"** — *boz yavuzu lo*, the verb doubled for force: love cannot be bought.
- **vv8–10** — the brothers of 1:6 speak of their young sister: a wall to be defended, a door to be guarded. She answers that she is a wall, and in his eyes she has found *shalom*, peace, the word inside the names Shulammite and Solomon (6:13).
- **vv11–12 "My own vineyard is before me"** — *karmi shelli*, the words of 1:6, "my own vineyard I have not kept." Solomon may have his vineyard worked by keepers for a thousand pieces of silver; hers is her own to give.
- **v14 "Flee, my beloved, and be like a gazelle"** — *berach*, "flee" or "run" (the Greek has "flee"); the last line answers 2:17, "turn, my beloved, be like a gazelle," but where those mountains were cleft, these are "mountains of spices," like "the mountain of myrrh" of 4:6.
