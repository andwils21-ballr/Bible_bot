# Song of Songs 6

**1** Where has your beloved gone,  
most beautiful among women?  
Where has your beloved turned,  
that we may seek him with you?

**2** My beloved has gone down to his garden,  
to the beds of spice,  
to pasture in the gardens  
and to gather lilies.

**3** I am my beloved's, and my beloved is mine;  
he pastures among the lilies.

**4** You are beautiful, my darling, as Tirzah,  
lovely as Jerusalem,  
awesome as the bannered hosts.

**5** Turn your eyes away from me,  
for they overwhelm me.  
Your hair is like a flock of goats  
streaming down from Gilead.

**6** Your teeth are like a flock of ewes  
that have come up from the washing,  
all of them bearing twins,  
and not one among them has lost its young.

**7** Your cheeks are like halves of a pomegranate  
behind your veil.

**8** There are sixty queens  
and eighty secondary wives,  
and young women without number.

**9** My dove, my perfect one, is the only one,  
the only one of her mother,  
the pure one of her who bore her.  
The young women saw her and called her blessed;  
the queens and secondary wives also, and they praised her:

**10** "Who is this who looks down like the dawn,  
beautiful as the moon,  
pure as the sun,  
awesome as the bannered hosts?"

**11** I went down to the nut orchard  
to look at the green shoots of the valley,  
to see whether the vine had budded,  
whether the pomegranates were in bloom.

**12** Before I knew it,  
my soul had set me among the chariots  
of my noble people.

**13** Return, return, Shulammite;  
return, return, that we may look on you.  
Why should you look on the Shulammite,  
as on the dance of the two camps?

## Notes

- **v3 "I am my beloved's, and my beloved is mine"** — the words of 2:16 with the order turned around: there she begins with him, here with herself.
- **v4 "as Tirzah… as Jerusalem"** — Tirzah, the first royal city of the northern kingdom (1 Kings 14:17; 16:23), beside Jerusalem in the south; the name means "pleasing," and the Greek translates it so, "as good pleasure." "The bannered hosts" — *nidgalot*, "those under banners," troops ranked by their standards (Numbers 2:2), and the Greek has "ranks set in order"; beside the dawn, moon and sun of v10 they may also be the hosts of the sky.
- **vv5–7** — the praise of 4:1–3 comes back nearly word for word: here "from Gilead" for "from Mount Gilead," "ewes" for "shorn ewes," and the lips and mouth of 4:3 are left out.
- **v8 "secondary wives"** — *pilagshim*, the word rendered so throughout these books.
- **vv9–10 "the pure one… pure as the sun"** — *barah* both times: she is pure to her mother, and pure as the sun. "The moon… the sun" — *levanah*, "the white one," and *chammah*, "the hot one," words of poetry, not the common names.
- **v12** — the Hebrew is one of the hardest lines in the book: *lo yada'ti nafshi samatni markevot ammi nadiv*, "I did not know; my soul set me (on) the chariots of my people, a noble." *Ammi nadiv* may also be a name, Amminadib, as the Greek takes it (Aminadab).
- **v13 "Shulammite"** — *hashulammit*, with the article: a title more than a name. Its letters are those of *Shelomoh*, Solomon, and of *shalom*; the Greek has "the Shunammite," as of Abishag the Shunammite (1 Kings 1:3). This verse is the first of chapter 7 in the Hebrew; the English numbering is followed. "The two camps" — *hammachanayim*, the name Jacob gave the place where God's messengers met him (Genesis 32:2).
