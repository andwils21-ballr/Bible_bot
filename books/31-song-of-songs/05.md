# Song of Songs 5

**1** I have come to my garden, my sister, my bride;  
I have gathered my myrrh with my spice;  
I have eaten my honeycomb with my honey;  
I have drunk my wine with my milk.  
Eat, friends; drink,  
and be drunk, beloved ones!

**2** I was asleep, but my heart was awake.  
The voice of my beloved, knocking:  
"Open to me, my sister, my darling,  
my dove, my perfect one,  
for my head is wet with dew,  
my locks with the drops of the night."

**3** I had taken off my robe;  
how could I put it on?  
I had washed my feet;  
how could I soil them?

**4** My beloved put his hand in through the opening,  
and my heart was stirred for him.

**5** I arose to open to my beloved,  
and my hands dripped with myrrh,  
my fingers with liquid myrrh,  
on the handles of the bolt.

**6** I opened to my beloved,  
but my beloved had turned and gone.  
My soul went out when he spoke.  
I sought him, but did not find him;  
I called him, but he did not answer me.

**7** The watchmen found me  
as they went about the city;  
they struck me, they wounded me;  
the watchmen of the walls  
took my veil from me.

**8** I charge you, daughters of Jerusalem,  
if you find my beloved,  
what will you tell him?  
That I am sick with love.

**9** What is your beloved more than another beloved,  
most beautiful among women?  
What is your beloved more than another beloved,  
that you so charge us?

**10** My beloved is radiant and ruddy,  
raised like a banner among ten thousand.

**11** His head is finest gold;  
his locks are wavy,  
black as a raven.

**12** His eyes are like doves  
beside streams of water,  
bathed in milk,  
sitting by the full pools.

**13** His cheeks are like beds of spice,  
towers of perfume.  
His lips are lilies,  
dripping liquid myrrh.

**14** His hands are rods of gold  
set with stones of Tarshish;  
his body is a plate of ivory  
covered with sapphires.

**15** His legs are pillars of alabaster  
set on bases of fine gold.  
His appearance is like Lebanon,  
choice as the cedars.

**16** His mouth is sweetness itself,  
and he is altogether desirable.  
This is my beloved, and this is my friend,  
daughters of Jerusalem.

## Notes

- **v1 "be drunk, beloved ones"** — *dodim*, the word for "love" in 1:2 and 4:10, here most likely calling to the lovers themselves; it can also be read "be drunk with love." The Greek has "brothers." The garden of 4:12–16 is now entered: "my garden," "my myrrh," "my honey," eight times "my."
- **v2 "I was asleep, but my heart was awake"** — the night scene of 3:1–4 again, but this time she hesitates (v3) and he is gone (v6). "My perfect one" — *tammati*, from *tam*, whole, without flaw; Job is *tam* (Job 1:1).
- **v4 "my heart was stirred for him"** — literally "my inward parts murmured for him," *me'ai hamu*, the depths of a person moved, as in Isaiah 16:11 and Jeremiah 31:20.
- **v6 "My soul went out when he spoke"** — *bedabbero*, "at his speaking": her life went out of her at his voice. The search of 3:1–2 begins again: "I sought him, but did not find him."
- **v7 "The watchmen found me as they went about the city"** — the words of 3:3. Then she asked them; now they strike her.
- **v10 "raised like a banner"** — *dagul*, from *degel*, "banner": the word of 2:4, "his banner over me was love." The Greek has "chosen out from ten thousands."
- **v12 "sitting by the full pools"** — *yoshevot al millet*, "sitting on fullness": the doves sit by the brimming water, or the eyes sit full in their sockets like jewels in a setting (*millu'at*, the setting of the high priest's stones, Exodus 28:17).
- **v14 "stones of Tarshish… sapphires"** — *tarshish* is one of the stones of the high priest's breastpiece (Exodus 28:20). *Sappirim* is the stone of the pavement under God's feet in Exodus 24:10, likely lapis lazuli, deep blue with flecks of gold.
- **v16 "this is my friend"** — *re'i*, the man's form of *ra'yati*, "my darling," his name for her throughout. She gives it back to him.
