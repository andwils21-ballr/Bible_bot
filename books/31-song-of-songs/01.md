# Song of Songs 1

**1** The Song of Songs, which is Solomon's.

**2** Let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth!  
For your love is better than wine;

**3** your oils are good for fragrance;  
your name is oil poured out;  
therefore the young women love you.

**4** Draw me after you; let us run.  
The king has brought me into his chambers.  
We will exult and rejoice in you;  
we will praise your love more than wine.  
Rightly do they love you.

**5** I am black and lovely,  
daughters of Jerusalem,  
like the tents of Kedar,  
like the curtains of Solomon.

**6** Do not stare at me because I am dark,  
because the sun has looked on me.  
My mother's sons were angry with me;  
they made me keeper of the vineyards;  
my own vineyard I have not kept.

**7** Tell me, you whom my soul loves,  
where you pasture your flock,  
where you make it lie down at noon;  
for why should I be like one who is veiled  
beside the flocks of your companions?

**8** If you do not know,  
most beautiful among women,  
follow the tracks of the flock,  
and pasture your young goats  
beside the shepherds' tents.

**9** I compare you, my darling,  
to a mare among Pharaoh's chariots.

**10** Your cheeks are lovely with ornaments,  
your neck with strings of beads.

**11** We will make you ornaments of gold,  
studded with silver.

**12** While the king was at his table,  
my nard gave its fragrance.

**13** My beloved is to me a bundle of myrrh  
that lies all night between my breasts.

**14** My beloved is to me a cluster of henna blossoms  
in the vineyards of En-gedi.

**15** How beautiful you are, my darling,  
how beautiful!  
Your eyes are doves.

**16** How handsome you are, my beloved,  
how pleasant!  
Our couch is green;

**17** the beams of our house are cedar;  
our rafters are cypress.

## Notes

- **v1 "The Song of Songs"** — the Hebrew way of saying the greatest of songs, as "the holy of holies" is the most holy place and "vanity of vanities" (Ecclesiastes 1:2) is utter vanity. "Which is Solomon's" — *lishlomoh*, "of Solomon": by him, for him, or about him; the Hebrew allows all three.
- **v2 "Let him kiss me… your love is better than wine"** — she turns from "him" to "you" within one line, from speaking of him to speaking to him. "Your love" — *dodekha*, love given and shown, caresses (the same word in 4:10). The Greek reads the same letters as *dadayikh*, "your breasts."
- **v3 "your name is oil poured out"** — *shem* and *shemen*, the same play of sound as Ecclesiastes 7:1.
- **v5 "I am black and lovely"** — *shechorah*, "black," darkened by the sun (v6). The Hebrew and the Greek join the two words with "and," not "but." "The tents of Kedar" — the black goat-hair tents of Kedar, a son of Ishmael (Genesis 25:13).
- **v6 "my own vineyard I have not kept"** — her brothers set her to work in their vineyards, and she has had no care for her own. The vineyard stands for herself again in 8:12.
- **v7 "you whom my soul loves"** — her name for him; it returns four times in 3:1–4. "Like one who is veiled" — *ote'yah*, a woman wrapped in a veil, as the Greek has it. A veiled woman wandering among shepherds' camps could be taken for a woman of another sort (compare Tamar, Genesis 38:14–15).
- **v9 "my darling"** — *ra'yati*, from *re'a*, "companion, friend"; his name for her, nine times in the Song.
- **v14 "En-gedi"** — the spring in the desert above the Dead Sea where David hid from Saul (1 Samuel 24:1).
- **vv15–16 "How beautiful you are… How handsome you are"** — he speaks and she answers in his own words (*hinnakh yafah… hinnekha yafeh*).
