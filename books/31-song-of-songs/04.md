# Song of Songs 4

**1** How beautiful you are, my darling,  
how beautiful!  
Your eyes are doves  
behind your veil.  
Your hair is like a flock of goats  
streaming down from Mount Gilead.

**2** Your teeth are like a flock of shorn ewes  
that have come up from the washing,  
all of them bearing twins,  
and not one among them has lost its young.

**3** Your lips are like a scarlet thread,  
and your mouth is lovely.  
Your cheeks are like halves of a pomegranate  
behind your veil.

**4** Your neck is like the tower of David,  
built in rows of stone;  
a thousand bucklers hang on it,  
all of them shields of mighty men.

**5** Your two breasts are like two fawns,  
twins of a gazelle,  
that graze among the lilies.

**6** Until the day breathes  
and the shadows flee,  
I will go to the mountain of myrrh  
and to the hill of frankincense.

**7** You are altogether beautiful, my darling;  
there is no blemish in you.

**8** Come with me from Lebanon, my bride;  
come with me from Lebanon.  
Look down from the peak of Amana,  
from the peak of Senir and Hermon,  
from the dens of lions,  
from the mountains of leopards.

**9** You have taken my heart, my sister, my bride;  
you have taken my heart with one of your eyes,  
with one jewel of your necklace.

**10** How beautiful is your love, my sister, my bride!  
How much better is your love than wine,  
and the fragrance of your oils than any spice!

**11** Your lips drip honey from the comb, my bride;  
honey and milk are under your tongue;  
the fragrance of your garments  
is like the fragrance of Lebanon.

**12** A garden locked is my sister, my bride,  
a spring locked, a fountain sealed.

**13** Your shoots are an orchard of pomegranates  
with the choicest fruits,  
henna with nard,

**14** nard and saffron,  
calamus and cinnamon,  
with every tree of frankincense,  
myrrh and aloes,  
with all the finest spices —

**15** a fountain of gardens,  
a well of living water,  
and streams flowing from Lebanon.

**16** Awake, north wind,  
and come, south wind!  
Blow on my garden;  
let its spices flow.  
Let my beloved come to his garden  
and eat its choicest fruits.

## Notes

- **v1 "How beautiful you are, my darling, how beautiful! Your eyes are doves"** — the words of 1:15, now opening a description of her from her eyes downward; vv1–3 come back almost word for word in 6:5–7.
- **v2 "all of them bearing twins"** — every tooth matched with its pair, and none missing.
- **v4 "built in rows of stone"** — *letalpiyyot*, found only here, and its meaning is uncertain: built in courses of stone, or built "for weapons" or "for trophies." The Greek takes it as a place name, Thalpioth.
- **v7 "no blemish in you"** — *mum*, the blemish that bars a priest from serving and an animal from the altar (Leviticus 21:17–23; 22:20–21).
- **v8 "my bride"** — *kallah*, six times in 4:8–5:1 and nowhere else in the Song. "My sister" (v9) is a word of closeness for a wife, not a word of kinship.
- **v9 "You have taken my heart"** — *libbavtini*, a verb made from *lev*, "heart"; the Greek has the same, "you have hearted me." "With one of your eyes" — a single glance was enough.
- **vv10–11 "How much better is your love than wine"** — he gives back her own words of 1:2–3: *dodim*, "love," better than wine, and the fragrance of oils. "Honey and milk" — the land "flowing with milk and honey" (Exodus 3:8), with the order turned around.
- **v12 "A garden locked… a spring locked"** — the second word is *gal*, "spring" (or "heap"), one letter from *gan*, "garden"; the Greek has "garden" both times.
- **v13 "an orchard"** — *pardes*, a Persian word for a walled park (Nehemiah 2:8; Ecclesiastes 2:5). The Greek renders it *paradeisos*, its word for Eden's garden in Genesis 2:8, from which English has "paradise."
- **vv15–16 "a well of living water… Let my beloved come to his garden"** — the words of Genesis 26:19, flowing water. In v16 she answers, and in one line "my garden" becomes "his garden."
