# Song of Songs 7

**1** How beautiful are your feet in sandals,  
noble daughter!  
The curves of your thighs are like jewels,  
the work of a craftsman's hands.

**2** Your navel is a rounded bowl;  
may it never lack mixed wine.  
Your belly is a heap of wheat,  
fenced in with lilies.

**3** Your two breasts are like two fawns,  
twins of a gazelle.

**4** Your neck is like an ivory tower.  
Your eyes are pools in Heshbon,  
by the gate of Bath-rabbim.  
Your nose is like the tower of Lebanon,  
looking toward Damascus.

**5** Your head crowns you like Carmel,  
and the flowing hair of your head is like purple;  
a king is held captive in its tresses.

**6** How beautiful you are, and how pleasant,  
love, among delights!

**7** Your stature is like a palm tree,  
and your breasts are like its clusters.

**8** I said, "I will climb the palm tree  
and take hold of its fruit."  
Let your breasts be like clusters of the vine,  
and the fragrance of your breath like apples,

**9** and your mouth like the best wine,  
going down smoothly for my beloved,  
gliding over the lips of sleepers.

**10** I am my beloved's,  
and his longing is for me.

**11** Come, my beloved, let us go out into the field;  
let us lodge in the villages;

**12** let us go early to the vineyards  
and see whether the vine has budded,  
whether its blossoms have opened,  
whether the pomegranates are in bloom.  
There I will give you my love.

**13** The mandrakes give their fragrance,  
and at our doors are all choice fruits,  
new as well as old,  
which I have stored up for you, my beloved.

## Notes

- **v1 "noble daughter"** — *bat nadiv*, "daughter of a noble," a title of honor; the Greek takes it as a name, "daughter of Nadab." The Hebrew numbers this verse 7:2, since its 7:1 is English 6:13; the English numbering is followed.
- **vv1–5** — the praise now runs from her feet upward, the reverse of 4:1–5. v3 repeats 4:5 without its last line.
- **v4 "the gate of Bath-rabbim"** — a gate of Heshbon, the name meaning "daughter of many." Heshbon was the city of Sihon east of the Jordan (Numbers 21:25–26).
- **v5 "a king is held captive in its tresses"** — *rehatim*, "tresses," is the word for the troughs where Jacob's flocks came to drink (Genesis 30:38, 41): her hair flows like water in channels, and the king is caught in it.
- **v9 "going down smoothly for my beloved"** — in the middle of the line his praise becomes her answer: "for my beloved." "Smoothly" — *lemeisharim*, the word of 1:4, "rightly." "The lips of sleepers" — the Greek reads "my lips and teeth."
- **v10 "his longing is for me"** — *teshuqah*, found only here and in Genesis 3:16 ("Toward your man shall be your longing, and he shall rule over you") and 4:7. Here the longing runs the other way, from the man to the woman, and nothing is said of rule. The words of 2:16 and 6:3 come a third time, changed.
- **v11 "in the villages"** — *bakkefarim*, "villages," or "among the henna bushes," the plant of 1:14 and 4:13 (*kofer*, plural *kefarim*).
- **v13 "The mandrakes"** — *duda'im*, the plant Leah and Rachel bargained over, believed to help a woman conceive (Genesis 30:14–16). The word sounds like *dodai*, "my love," in v12, and *dodi*, "my beloved," at the end of this verse.
