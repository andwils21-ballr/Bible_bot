# Hosea 12

## Jacob Struggled with God

**1** Ephraim feeds on the wind  
and chases the east wind all day long;  
he multiplies lies and violence.  
They make a covenant with Assyria,  
and oil is carried to Egypt.

**2** The LORD has a case against Judah;  
He will punish Jacob according to his ways;  
according to his deeds He will repay him.

**3** In the womb he took his brother by the heel,  
and in his vigor he struggled with God.

**4** He struggled with the messenger and prevailed;  
he wept and pleaded with him.  
At Bethel He found him,  
and there He spoke with us.

**5** The LORD, the God of hosts —  
the LORD is His remembrance.

**6** As for you, return to your God;  
keep loyal love and justice,  
and wait for your God always.

## False Scales

**7** A trader with false scales in his hand,  
he loves to exploit.

**8** Ephraim says, "Surely I have become rich;  
I have found wealth for myself;  
in all my labors they will find no iniquity in me,  
nothing that is sin."

**9** But I have been the LORD your God  
ever since the land of Egypt;  
I will make you live in tents again,  
as in the days of the appointed feast.

**10** I spoke to the prophets;  
it was I who multiplied visions,  
and through the prophets I gave parables.

**11** If Gilead is wickedness,  
they have surely come to nothing;  
in Gilgal they sacrifice bulls;  
their altars too are like heaps of stones  
on the furrows of the field.

## By a Prophet

**12** Jacob fled to the country of Aram;  
Israel served for a wife,  
and for a wife he kept watch.

**13** By a prophet the LORD brought Israel up from Egypt,  
and by a prophet he was kept.

**14** Ephraim has provoked bitter anger;  
so his Lord will leave his bloodguilt on him  
and repay him for his scorn.

## Notes

- **v1 "Ephraim feeds on the wind and chases the east wind"** — *ro'eh ruach*, the words Isaiah uses of the man who worships what he carved, *He feeds on ashes* (Isaiah 44:20). In 8:7 *they sow the wind*; here they live on it. *Qadim*, **the east wind**, comes back in 13:15 as *the wind of the LORD*, rising from the wilderness. A covenant with Assyria and oil sent to Egypt is the dove of 7:11: *they call to Egypt, they go to Assyria*.
- **v2 "The LORD has a case against Judah"** — *riv*, the word of 4:1, *the LORD has a case against the inhabitants of the land*. The case names Judah first, straight after 11:12, then moves to the people's father, Jacob, whose life the next verses tell. *Yashiv lo*, **He will repay him**, comes back as the chapter's last words: *his Lord will… repay him for his scorn* (v14).
- **vv3–4 "he took his brother by the heel… struggled with God… struggled with the messenger and prevailed"** — *aqav*, the verb of Esau's complaint, *he has heeled me these two times* (Genesis 27:36), from the birth at which Jacob's hand was *holding the heel of Esau* (Genesis 25:26). *Sarah et Elohim*, **struggled with God**, and *va-yukhal*, **prevailed**, are the words of the name Israel: *you have struggled with God and with men, and you have prevailed* (Genesis 32:28). Genesis calls the wrestler *a man* (32:24); Hosea calls him God, then a *mal'akh*, a messenger. Genesis tells of no tears at the river: one of the two asks to be let go, and the other will not let go without a blessing (32:26). The verse does not say which of them wept and pleaded.
- **vv4–5 "At Bethel He found him, and there He spoke with us… the LORD is His remembrance"** — *yimtsa'ennu*, **he found him**, names neither. God came to Jacob at Bethel, *God appeared to Jacob again… and blessed him* (Genesis 35:9), and Jacob found God there, *Surely the LORD is in this place* (Genesis 28:16); the Greek has *they found Me*. In 9:10 the LORD is the finder: *Like grapes in the wilderness I found Israel*. *Immanu*, **with us**: what was said to Jacob there was said to his sons; the Greek has *to them*. *Zikhro*, **His remembrance**, is the word of the burning bush, *This is My name forever, and this is My remembrance for all generations* (Exodus 3:15). Bethel is the town Hosea calls Beth-aven (4:15; 10:5).
- **v7 "A trader with false scales in his hand"** — *Kena'an*, **Canaan**, the name of the land, is also the word for a trader, as in Ezekiel's *a land of trade* (Ezekiel 17:4); the Greek keeps the name, *Chanaan*. Read as the name, Ephraim is called after the people who held the land before Israel. *Moznei mirmah*, **false scales**, are what Proverbs condemns, *A false balance is detestable to the LORD* (Proverbs 11:1), and *mirmah*, **deceit**, is the word of 11:12, *the house of Israel with deceit*. *La'ashoq*, **to exploit**, is the verb of the law, *You shall not exploit your neighbor* (Leviticus 19:13).
- **vv3, 8, 11 "vigor… wealth… wickedness"** — one set of Hebrew letters, *on*, *on* and *aven*, three times: Jacob *in his vigor* struggled with God (v3), the word of *my strength and the first of my vigor* (Genesis 49:3); Ephraim boasts *I have found wealth for myself* (v8); and *Gilead is wickedness* (v11). The Greek reads the end of v8 as the answer to the boast, not part of it: *all his labors will not be found for him, because of the iniquities which he sinned*.
- **v9 "I have been the LORD your God ever since the land of Egypt"** — said again in 13:4. The law of weights ends the same way: *You shall have honest scales… I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt* (Leviticus 19:36); here the words answer the trader's false scales. The feast kept the tents in memory, *so that your generations may know that I made the sons of Israel live in booths when I brought them out of the land of Egypt* (Leviticus 23:43), though Hosea's word is *ohalim*, **tents**, not the feast's *sukkot*, **booths**. The verse leaves open whether living in tents is the loss of their houses or a return to the wilderness where the LORD would *speak to her heart* (2:14).
- **v10 "through the prophets I gave parables"** — *adammeh*, from *damah*. One *damah* means **to liken**, and the Greek reads it so, *I was likened*; the prophets speak in likenesses all through this book: the morning cloud, the dove, the lion. The other *damah* means **to destroy**, the verb of 4:6, *My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge*, and of 10:15. Read that way, the line is *through the prophets I will bring destruction*, as in 6:5, *I have hewn them by the prophets*.
- **v11 "Gilead… Gilgal… like heaps of stones"** — *Gil'ad*, *Gilgal* and *gallim*, **heaps**, sound alike. In the hill country of Gilead, Jacob and Laban piled stones for their covenant and named the heap Galeed: *This heap is a witness between me and you today* (Genesis 31:48). In 6:8 *Gilead is a city of evildoers*, and Gilgal is where *I came to hate them* (9:15). *The furrows of the field* are where judgment sprang up like poison in 10:4.
- **vv12–13 "for a wife he kept watch… by a prophet he was kept"** — *shamar* and *nishmar*, the same verb twice. Jacob *served seven years for Rachel* (Genesis 29:20) and kept Laban's flock, *I will graze your flock, I will keep it* (Genesis 30:31); Israel was brought out of Egypt and kept by a prophet. The prophet is Moses: *There has not risen a prophet since in Israel like Moses* (Deuteronomy 34:10). *His Lord* in v14 is *adonav*, **his master**, the word a servant uses of the one he serves; the Greek has *Kyrios*.
