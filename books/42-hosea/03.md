# Hosea 3

**1** The LORD said to me, "Go again, love a woman who is loved by a companion and is an adulteress, as the LORD loves the sons of Israel, though they turn to other gods and love cakes of raisins."

**2** So I bought her for myself for fifteen shekels of silver and a homer and a lethech of barley.

**3** I said to her, "You are to stay with me for many days. You will not prostitute yourself or belong to any man, and I too will be so toward you."

**4** For the sons of Israel will stay many days without king or prince, without sacrifice or standing stone, without ephod or household gods.

**5** Afterward the sons of Israel will return and seek the LORD their God and David their king, and they will come trembling to the LORD and to His goodness in the end of days.

## Notes

- **v1 "a woman who is loved by a companion and is an adulteress"** — *ahuvat re'a*. *Re'a* is a neighbor or a lover, and also a husband: Jeremiah says *as a woman betrays her companion, so you have betrayed Me* (Jeremiah 3:20). Read that way, the one who loves her is Hosea, as *the LORD loves the sons of Israel* though they turn to other gods; read the other way, she is loved by another man. The woman is not named, and the chapter does not say whether she is Gomer. The Greek reads the same letters as *ra'*, **evil**: *a woman who loves evil things*. *Cakes of raisins* were a festival food, given out by David when the ark came to Jerusalem (2 Samuel 6:19); here they go with the other gods.
- **v2 "fifteen shekels of silver and a homer and a lethech of barley"** — *va-ekkereha*, **I bought her**, from a rare verb of buying. *Shekels* is not in the Hebrew, which has *fifteen of silver*. The *lethech* is a measure found only here, and its size is not given. The Greek has *a homer of barley and a jar of wine*.
- **v3 "and I too will be so toward you"** — *ve-gam ani elayikh*, **and also I toward you**: four words with no verb. They can mean that he will keep himself for her as she keeps herself for him, or that he too will stay away from her for those days, as Israel will be without its king and its sacrifices (v4).
- **v4 "without ephod or household gods"** — *teraphim*, the gods Rachel stole from Laban (Genesis 31:19). The list sets what the law allowed beside what it forbade: king and prince, sacrifice and ephod, with the *standing stone* the law rules out, *You shall not set up for yourself a standing stone* (Deuteronomy 16:22), and the *teraphim*. All of them are taken away together.
- **v5 "David their king… in the end of days"** — a king of David's line, as Ezekiel says, *My servant David will be king over them* (Ezekiel 37:24). *In the end of days*, *be-acharit ha-yamim*, is Jacob's phrase (Genesis 49:1).
