# Hosea 4

## No Knowledge of God

**1** Hear the word of the LORD, sons of Israel,  
for the LORD has a case against the inhabitants of the land:  
there is no faithfulness, no loyal love,  
and no knowledge of God in the land.

**2** Swearing and lying, murder and stealing and adultery break out,  
and bloodshed follows bloodshed.

**3** Therefore the land mourns,  
and all who live in it waste away,  
with the beasts of the field and the birds of the heavens;  
even the fish of the sea are taken away.

## Like People, Like Priest

**4** Yet let no one bring a case, and let no one rebuke,  
for your people are like those who contend with the priest.

**5** You will stumble by day,  
and the prophet will stumble with you by night,  
and I will destroy your mother.

**6** My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge.  
Because you have rejected knowledge,  
I reject you from being a priest to Me;  
since you have forgotten the law of your God,  
I also will forget your children.

**7** The more they increased, the more they sinned against Me;  
I will exchange their glory for shame.

**8** They feed on the sin of My people,  
and they set their hearts on their iniquity.

**9** It will be the same for the people as for the priest:  
I will punish them for their ways  
and repay them for their deeds.

**10** They will eat but not be satisfied;  
they will give themselves to prostitution but not increase,  
for they have stopped heeding the LORD.

## A Spirit of Prostitution

**11** Prostitution, wine and new wine take away the understanding.

**12** My people consult their piece of wood,  
and their staff gives them answers;  
for a spirit of prostitution has led them astray,  
and they have prostituted themselves, leaving their God.

**13** On the tops of the mountains they sacrifice,  
and on the hills they burn incense,  
under oak and poplar and terebinth,  
because their shade is good.  
Therefore your daughters prostitute themselves,  
and your brides commit adultery.

**14** I will not punish your daughters when they prostitute themselves,  
nor your brides when they commit adultery;  
for the men themselves go aside with prostitutes  
and sacrifice with consecrated women,  
and a people without understanding comes to ruin.

**15** Though you prostitute yourself, Israel,  
let Judah not become guilty.  
Do not go to Gilgal,  
do not go up to Beth-aven,  
and do not swear, "As the LORD lives."

**16** For Israel is as stubborn as a stubborn heifer;  
now will the LORD pasture them  
like a lamb in a broad place?

**17** Ephraim is joined to idols;  
let him alone.

**18** When their drink is gone, they give themselves to prostitution;  
her rulers love shame above all.

**19** A wind has wrapped her up in its wings,  
and they will be ashamed of their sacrifices.

## Notes

- **vv4–6 "like those who contend with the priest… I will destroy your mother… My people are destroyed"** — v4 is obscure: the words say the people are *like those who contend with a priest*, the man the law condemns for *not listening to the priest* (Deuteronomy 17:12), and then the speech turns on the priest himself (*you*, vv4–9). *Damiti*, **I will destroy**, and *nidmu*, **are destroyed**, are the same verb, one verse apart: the priest's *mother* and *My people* go down together.
- **vv8–9 "They feed on the sin of My people… the same for the people as for the priest"** — *chattat* is both **sin** and **sin offering**, which the priest was to eat: *The priest who offers it for sin shall eat it* (Leviticus 6:26). The more the people sin, the more the priests eat. *Kha-am ka-kohen* is Isaiah's line for a judgment that spares no rank: *It will be the same for the people as for the priest* (Isaiah 24:2).
- **v15 "Gilgal… Beth-aven"** — *Beit Aven*, **House of Wickedness**, a town *near Beth-aven, east of Bethel* (Joshua 7:2). In 10:5 Hosea speaks of *the calves of Beth-aven*, and the calf stood at Bethel (1 Kings 12:29), *House of God*: the name puts *aven*, wickedness, in the place of God.
- **vv16–19 the end of the chapter** — *Ephraim*, the tribe of Joseph's younger son, stands for the whole northern kingdom from here on. The last three verses are among the hardest in the book. *Now will the LORD pasture them like a lamb in a broad place?* (v16) can also be read as a statement, the LORD letting a stubborn heifer graze alone in the open. In v18 the Hebrew runs *their drink has turned… they love, give! shame, her shields*: each word is clear, the sentence is not.
