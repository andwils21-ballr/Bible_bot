# Hosea 8

## The Calf of Samaria

**1** Set the horn to your lips!  
One comes like an eagle over the house of the LORD,  
because they have broken My covenant  
and rebelled against My law.

**2** To Me they cry,  
"My God, we know You — we, Israel!"

**3** Israel has rejected the good;  
the enemy will pursue him.

**4** They made kings, but not through Me;  
they made princes, but I did not know it.  
With their silver and their gold  
they made idols for themselves,  
to be cut off.

**5** Your calf is rejected, Samaria;  
My anger burns against them.  
How long will they be incapable of innocence?

**6** For it is from Israel:  
a craftsman made it,  
and it is not God;  
the calf of Samaria will be broken to splinters.

## Sowing the Wind

**7** For they sow the wind,  
and they will reap the whirlwind.  
The standing grain has no head;  
the sprout will yield no flour;  
and if it does yield,  
strangers will swallow it up.

**8** Israel is swallowed up;  
now they are among the nations  
like a jar no one wants.

**9** For they have gone up to Assyria,  
a wild donkey wandering alone;  
Ephraim has hired lovers.

**10** Even though they hire among the nations,  
I will now gather them up,  
and they will soon writhe  
under the burden of the king of princes.

## Altars for Sinning

**11** Because Ephraim has multiplied altars for sinning,  
they have become to him altars for sinning.

**12** Were I to write for him the many things of My law,  
they would be counted as something strange.

**13** As for the sacrifices of My gifts,  
they sacrifice flesh and eat it,  
but the LORD does not accept them.  
Now He will remember their iniquity  
and punish their sins;  
they will return to Egypt.

**14** Israel has forgotten his Maker  
and built palaces,  
and Judah has multiplied fortified cities;  
but I will send fire on his cities,  
and it will devour her strongholds.

## Notes

- **v1 "like an eagle over the house of the LORD"** — *ka-nesher*, the curse of the law: *a nation from far away… swooping as the eagle swoops* (Deuteronomy 28:49).
- **vv2–3 "My God, we know You… Israel has rejected the good"** — the cry claims the *knowledge of God* that 4:1 found missing. *Zanach*, **rejected**, comes again in v5: Israel has rejected the good, and the calf is rejected.
- **v4 "They made kings, but not through Me"** — *himlikhu… hesiru*, **made kings… made princes**; the second verb can also be heard as **removed**, the way kings were set up and struck down (7:7). *To be cut off* closes the verse without a subject: the idols, or Israel with them.
- **vv5–6 "Your calf… a craftsman made it, and it is not God"** — the calf of the north, which Jeroboam set up with the words of Aaron's calf: *These are your gods, Israel, who brought you up from the land of Egypt* (1 Kings 12:28). *Shevavim*, **splinters**, is found only here.
- **v7 "they sow the wind, and they will reap the whirlwind… the sprout will yield no flour"** — *tsemach beli ya'aseh qemach*, a rhyme in Hebrew, **sprout… flour**. *Zera*, **sow**, is the root of *Jezreel* (1:4; 2:23): what God sows bears, and what Israel sows is wind.
- **v8 "like a jar no one wants"** — *ki-khli ein chefets bo*, the words Jeremiah uses of King Coniah, *a jar no one wants* (Jeremiah 22:28).
- **v9 "a wild donkey wandering alone… Ephraim"** — *pere*, **wild donkey**, sounds like *Efrayim*. It is what Ishmael was to be, *a wild donkey of a man* (Genesis 16:12). *Hitnu ahavim*, **they have hired lovers**, is the reverse of the prostitute's wages of 2:12: here Ephraim pays.
- **v10 "they will soon writhe under the burden of the king of princes"** — *va-yachellu me'at mi-massa*: obscure. *Yachellu* can be **begin**, or **writhe** as in labor; *massa* is a **burden** or a **tribute**. The Greek reads *they will cease a little from anointing king and princes*.
- **v13 "they will return to Egypt"** — the exodus run backward: the people brought out of Egypt go back to it (9:3; 11:5).
- **v14 "I will send fire on his cities, and it will devour her strongholds"** — the refrain Amos uses against every nation in turn (Amos 1:4–2:5).
