# 2 Kings 24

## Jehoiakim and the Raiders

**1** In his days Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came up, and Jehoiakim became his servant for three years. Then he turned and rebelled against him.

**2** The LORD sent against him bands of Chaldeans, bands of Arameans, bands of Moabites and bands of Ammonites, and He sent them against Judah to destroy it, according to the word of the LORD that He spoke by His servants the prophets.

**3** Surely at the command of the LORD this came upon Judah, to remove them from His presence for the sins of Manasseh, according to all that he had done,

**4** and also for the innocent blood that he shed, for he filled Jerusalem with innocent blood, and the LORD was not willing to pardon.

**5** The rest of the acts of Jehoiakim and all that he did, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?

**6** Jehoiakim lay down with his fathers, and Jehoiachin his son reigned in his place.

**7** The king of Egypt did not come out of his land again, for the king of Babylon had taken all that belonged to the king of Egypt, from the Brook of Egypt to the river Euphrates.

## Jehoiachin and the First Exile

**8** Jehoiachin was eighteen years old when he began to reign, and he reigned three months in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Nehushta daughter of Elnathan of Jerusalem.

**9** He did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD, according to all that his father had done.

**10** At that time the servants of Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came up to Jerusalem, and the city came under siege.

**11** Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came to the city while his servants were besieging it,

**12** and Jehoiachin king of Judah went out to the king of Babylon, he and his mother, his servants, his officials and his eunuchs. The king of Babylon took him in the eighth year of his reign.

**13** He carried out from there all the treasures of the house of the LORD and the treasures of the king's house, and cut in pieces all the vessels of gold that Solomon king of Israel had made in the temple of the LORD, as the LORD had spoken.

**14** He carried into exile all Jerusalem, all the officials and all the men of worth, ten thousand exiles, and all the craftsmen and the smiths. None remained except the poorest people of the land.

**15** He carried Jehoiachin into exile to Babylon, and the king's mother, the king's wives, his eunuchs and the leading men of the land he led into exile from Jerusalem to Babylon.

**16** All the men of valor, seven thousand, and the craftsmen and the smiths, a thousand, all of them strong and fit for war, the king of Babylon brought into exile to Babylon.

## Zedekiah

**17** The king of Babylon made Mattaniah, Jehoiachin's uncle, king in his place, and changed his name to Zedekiah.

**18** Zedekiah was twenty-one years old when he began to reign, and he reigned eleven years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Hamutal daughter of Jeremiah of Libnah.

**19** He did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD, according to all that Jehoiakim had done.

**20** For because of the anger of the LORD it came to this in Jerusalem and Judah, until He cast them out from His presence. Zedekiah rebelled against the king of Babylon.

## Notes

- **v2 "according to the word of the LORD that He spoke by His servants the prophets"** — the word of 21:10–14, spoken in Manasseh's days: *I will… give them into the hand of their enemies*.
- **vv3–4 "at the command of the LORD… the LORD was not willing to pardon"** — *al pi YHWH*, **at the mouth of the LORD**; the Greek reads *because of the anger of the LORD*, as v20 has it in the Hebrew. Josiah's turning did not cancel the sentence on Manasseh's sins (23:26).
- **v7 "from the Brook of Egypt to the river Euphrates"** — the borders promised to Abram, *from the river of Egypt to the great river, the river Euphrates* (Genesis 15:18), now held by Babylon.
- **v8 "Jehoiachin"** — called *Coniah* by Jeremiah: *though Coniah… were the signet ring on My right hand, yet I would tear you off* (Jeremiah 22:24). Matthew lists him as *Jeconiah* in the line of Jesus, *at the time of the deportation to Babylon* (Matthew 1:11–12).
- **v12 "Jehoiachin king of Judah went out to the king of Babylon"** — *yatsa al*, **went out to**, the words of surrender, as the men of Jabesh said, *Tomorrow we will come out to you* (1 Samuel 11:10). Jehoiachin gives himself up, and the city is not destroyed this time.
- **v13 "cut in pieces all the vessels of gold… as the LORD had spoken"** — the word Isaiah gave Hezekiah: *all that is in your house… shall be carried to Babylon* (20:17).
- **vv14–16 "ten thousand exiles… seven thousand… a thousand"** — the numbers given in v14 and in v16 are two counts of the same exile. Jeremiah gives a third: *in the seventh year, three thousand and twenty-three* (Jeremiah 52:28). Written *asarah*, read *aseret*; written *ulei*, read *eilei*, **the leading men** (v15).
- **v14 "the men of worth… the poorest people of the land"** — *gibborei ha-chayil*, the men Menahem taxed fifty shekels a head (15:20). Those left behind are *dallat am ha-arets*, **the poor of the people of the land**.
- **v17 "Mattaniah, Jehoiachin's uncle"** — *dodo*, his father's brother: Josiah's son, *the third, Zedekiah* in Chronicles' list (1 Chronicles 3:15). Babylon renames him as Egypt renamed Eliakim (23:34). Written *Chamital*, read *Chamutal* (v18).
- **v20 "because of the anger of the LORD it came to this"** — *ki al af YHWH*, the reading the Greek gives in v3 as well.
