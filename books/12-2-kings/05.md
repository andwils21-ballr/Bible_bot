# 2 Kings 5

## The Girl from Israel

**1** Naaman, commander of the army of the king of Aram, was a great man before his master and highly regarded, because by him the LORD had given victory to Aram. The man was a mighty man of valor, but he had blight.

**2** Now the Arameans had gone out on raids and had taken captive from the land of Israel a little girl, and she served Naaman's wife.

**3** She said to her mistress, "If only my lord were with the prophet who is in Samaria! Then he would take away his blight."

**4** So Naaman went in and told his lord, "Thus and thus spoke the girl from the land of Israel."

**5** The king of Aram said, "Go now, and I will send a letter to the king of Israel." So he went, taking with him ten talents of silver, six thousand shekels of gold and ten changes of clothes.

**6** He brought the letter to the king of Israel, which said, "Now when this letter comes to you, know that I have sent Naaman my servant to you, so that you may take away his blight."

**7** When the king of Israel read the letter, he tore his clothes and said, "Am I God, to put to death and to make alive, that this man sends word to me to take away a man's blight? Only consider, and see how he is seeking a quarrel with me."

## Wash in the Jordan

**8** When Elisha the man of God heard that the king of Israel had torn his clothes, he sent to the king, saying, "Why have you torn your clothes? Let him come to me, and he shall know that there is a prophet in Israel."

**9** So Naaman came with his horses and his chariots and stood at the door of Elisha's house.

**10** Elisha sent a messenger to him, saying, "Go and wash in the Jordan seven times, and your flesh shall be restored to you, and you shall be clean."

**11** But Naaman was angry and went away and said, "I thought that he would surely come out to me and stand and call on the name of the LORD his God, and wave his hand over the place and take away the blight.

**12** Are not Abana and Pharpar, the rivers of Damascus, better than all the waters of Israel? Could I not wash in them and be clean?" So he turned and went away in a rage.

**13** But his servants came near and spoke to him and said, "My father, if the prophet had told you to do some great thing, would you not have done it? How much more, then, when he says to you, 'Wash, and be clean'?"

**14** So he went down and dipped himself seven times in the Jordan, according to the word of the man of God, and his flesh was restored like the flesh of a little child, and he was clean.

## Naaman's Confession

**15** Then he returned to the man of God, he and all his company, and he came and stood before him and said, "Now I know that there is no God in all the earth except in Israel. So now please accept a gift from your servant."

**16** But he said, "As the LORD lives, before whom I stand, I will accept nothing." He urged him to accept it, but he refused.

**17** Then Naaman said, "If not, please let your servant be given two mules' load of earth, for your servant will no longer offer burnt offering or sacrifice to other gods, but only to the LORD.

**18** In this matter may the LORD pardon your servant: when my master goes into the house of Rimmon to bow down there, leaning on my arm, and I bow down in the house of Rimmon — when I bow down in the house of Rimmon, may the LORD pardon your servant in this matter."

**19** He said to him, "Go in peace." So he went from him some distance.

## Gehazi

**20** Gehazi, the servant of Elisha the man of God, said, "My master has spared this Naaman the Aramean, in not accepting from his hand what he brought. As the LORD lives, I will run after him and take something from him."

**21** So Gehazi pursued Naaman. When Naaman saw someone running after him, he got down from the chariot to meet him and said, "Is all well?"

**22** He said, "All is well. My master has sent me, saying, 'Just now two young men of the sons of the prophets have come to me from the hill country of Ephraim. Please give them a talent of silver and two changes of clothes.'"

**23** Naaman said, "Be pleased to accept two talents." He urged him, and tied up two talents of silver in two bags, with two changes of clothes, and gave them to two of his servants, and they carried them before him.

**24** When he came to the hill, he took them from their hand and stored them in the house, and he sent the men away, and they left.

**25** He went in and stood before his master, and Elisha said to him, "Where have you been, Gehazi?" He said, "Your servant has not gone anywhere."

**26** But he said to him, "Did not my heart go with you when a man turned from his chariot to meet you? Is it a time to accept silver and to accept clothes, olive groves and vineyards, sheep and oxen, male servants and female servants?

**27** Therefore the blight of Naaman shall cling to you and to your seed forever." So he went out from his presence with blight, white as snow.

## Notes

- **v1 "a mighty man of valor, but he had blight"** — *gibbor chayil metsora*: the sentence builds his greatness and ends on one word. *Metsora*, from *tsara'at*, **blight**, the disease of Leviticus 13. *By him the LORD had given victory to Aram*: the God of Israel gave the Aramean his victories.
- **vv2, 14 "a little girl… like the flesh of a little child"** — *na'arah qetannah* and *na'ar qatan*. The captive girl sets it in motion, and Naaman's flesh comes back like a small boy's.
- **v7 "Am I God, to put to death and to make alive?"** — the words of the Song of Moses, *I put to death and I make alive* (Deuteronomy 32:39).
- **vv10, 14 "wash in the Jordan seven times… and you shall be clean"** — the law's cleansing from blight was done *seven times* (Leviticus 14:7). The prophet does not come out; he sends a *messenger*.
- **v12 "Abana and Pharpar"** — the written text has *Avanah*, the read form *Amanah*; the text keeps the familiar name *Abana*, which the Greek also has.
- **v15 "there is no God in all the earth except in Israel"** — a foreign commander says it after seven dips in Israel's river. Jesus names him: *there were many lepers in Israel in the time of Elisha the prophet, and none of them was cleansed except Naaman the Syrian* (Luke 4:27).
- **v17 "two mules' load of earth"** — soil of the land of Israel, to worship the LORD on in Aram.
- **v18 "the house of Rimmon… may the LORD pardon your servant"** — *Rimmon*, the god of Damascus. Naaman asks in advance for pardon for bowing when his duty requires it; Elisha answers only *Go in peace*.
- **vv20–26 "As the LORD lives, I will run after him… Did not my heart go with you"** — Gehazi takes the oath his master used to refuse the gift (v16) to go and get it. *Olive groves and vineyards, sheep and oxen*: the things the silver would buy.
- **v27 "with blight, white as snow"** — as Miriam was *blighted, white as snow* (Numbers 12:10), and Moses' hand (Exodus 4:6).
