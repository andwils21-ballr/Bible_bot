# 2 Kings 7

## Elisha's Word

**1** Elisha said, "Hear the word of the LORD. Thus says the LORD: 'About this time tomorrow a seah of fine flour shall be sold for a shekel, and two seahs of barley for a shekel, in the gate of Samaria.'"

**2** Then the officer on whose arm the king leaned answered the man of God, "Even if the LORD should make lattices in the heavens, could this thing be?" He said, "You shall see it with your own eyes, but you shall not eat of it."

## Four Men with Blight

**3** Now there were four men with blight at the entrance of the gate. They said to one another, "Why are we sitting here until we die?

**4** If we say, 'Let us go into the city,' the famine is in the city, and we shall die there; if we sit here, we die. So now come, let us go over to the camp of the Arameans. If they spare our lives, we shall live, and if they kill us, we shall only die."

**5** So they got up at twilight to go to the camp of the Arameans. When they came to the edge of the camp of the Arameans, there was no one there.

**6** For the Lord had made the army of the Arameans hear the sound of chariots and the sound of horses, the sound of a great army, so that they said to one another, "The king of Israel has hired against us the kings of the Hittites and the kings of Egypt to come against us."

**7** So they got up and fled in the twilight, and left their tents, their horses and their donkeys, the camp just as it was, and fled for their lives.

**8** When these men with blight came to the edge of the camp, they went into one tent, ate and drank, and carried off silver, gold and clothing from it, and went and hid them. Then they came back and went into another tent and carried off things from it, and went and hid them.

**9** Then they said to one another, "We are not doing right. This day is a day of good news, and we are keeping silent. If we wait until the morning light, punishment will overtake us. So now come, let us go and tell the king's household."

**10** So they came and called to the gatekeepers of the city and told them, "We came to the camp of the Arameans, and there was no one there, not a human voice — only the horses tied and the donkeys tied, and the tents as they were."

**11** Then the gatekeepers called out, and it was told within the king's household.

## The King's Suspicion

**12** The king got up in the night and said to his servants, "Let me tell you what the Arameans have done to us. They know that we are hungry, so they have gone out of the camp to hide themselves in the open country, thinking, 'When they come out of the city, we will take them alive and get into the city.'"

**13** One of his servants answered, "Let some men take five of the horses that are left, which remain in the city — they are like all the multitude of Israel that have already perished — and let us send and see."

**14** So they took two chariots with horses, and the king sent them after the army of the Arameans, saying, "Go and see."

**15** They went after them as far as the Jordan, and the whole road was full of clothing and equipment that the Arameans had thrown away in their haste. The messengers returned and told the king.

**16** Then the people went out and plundered the camp of the Arameans. So a seah of fine flour was sold for a shekel, and two seahs of barley for a shekel, according to the word of the LORD.

**17** Now the king had appointed the officer on whose arm he leaned to have charge of the gate, and the people trampled him in the gate, and he died, as the man of God had said when the king came down to him.

**18** For it came to pass, as the man of God had said to the king, "Two seahs of barley shall be sold for a shekel, and a seah of fine flour for a shekel, about this time tomorrow in the gate of Samaria,"

**19** the officer had answered the man of God, "Even if the LORD should make lattices in the heavens, could such a thing be?" He had said, "You shall see it with your own eyes, but you shall not eat of it."

**20** So it happened to him: the people trampled him in the gate, and he died.

## Notes

- **v1 "a seah of fine flour… for a shekel"** — from a donkey's head for eighty shekels (6:25) to fine flour for one, in a day.
- **v2 "the officer on whose arm the king leaned… lattices in the heavens"** — *ha-shalish*, a high officer; Naaman used the same picture of his master *leaning on my arm* (5:18). *Arubbot ba-shamayim*, the *lattices of the heavens* that were opened at the flood (Genesis 7:11).
- **v3 "four men with blight at the entrance of the gate"** — *metsora'im*, fixed as **blight**; outside the city by the law, *his home shall be outside the camp* (Leviticus 13:46).
- **v6 "the Lord had made the army of the Arameans hear the sound of chariots"** — *Adonai*. The Arameans flee from an army that is heard and not seen; in 6:17 Elisha's servant saw an army that the enemy did not.
- **v9 "This day is a day of good news"** — *besorah*, the word of the runners who carried the news of Absalom's death (2 Samuel 18:20–27).
- **v13 "which remain in the city — they are like all the multitude of Israel that have already perished"** — the Hebrew repeats *which remain in it, they are like all the multitude of Israel*, a clause written twice; the Greek has it once, and so does the text.
- **v14 "two chariots with horses"** — the servant spoke of five horses; the king sends two chariot teams.
- **vv17–20 "the people trampled him in the gate"** — the narrator repeats the prophecy and the officer's answer word for word, so that the fulfillment matches the word exactly: he *saw* the grain sold, in the gate, and did not eat.
