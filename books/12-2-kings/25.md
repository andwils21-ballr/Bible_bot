# 2 Kings 25

## The Siege and the Breach

**1** And it came to pass in the ninth year of his reign, in the tenth month, on the tenth day of the month, that Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came, he and all his army, against Jerusalem. He encamped against it, and they built a siege wall all around it.

**2** So the city was under siege until the eleventh year of King Zedekiah.

**3** In the fourth month, on the ninth day of the month, the famine was severe in the city, and there was no bread for the people of the land.

**4** Then the city was breached, and all the men of war fled by night by the way of the gate between the two walls, by the king's garden, while the Chaldeans were all around the city. The king went by the way of the Arabah.

**5** But the army of the Chaldeans pursued the king and overtook him in the plains of Jericho, and all his army was scattered from him.

**6** They seized the king and brought him up to the king of Babylon at Riblah, and they passed sentence on him.

**7** They slaughtered the sons of Zedekiah before his eyes, then put out the eyes of Zedekiah, bound him in bronze fetters and brought him to Babylon.

## The Burning of the House of the LORD

**8** In the fifth month, on the seventh day of the month, which was the nineteenth year of King Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, Nebuzaradan, the chief of the slaughterers, a servant of the king of Babylon, came to Jerusalem.

**9** He burned the house of the LORD, the king's house and all the houses of Jerusalem; every great house he burned with fire.

**10** All the army of the Chaldeans who were with the chief of the slaughterers broke down the walls around Jerusalem.

**11** The rest of the people who were left in the city, the deserters who had gone over to the king of Babylon, and the rest of the multitude, Nebuzaradan the chief of the slaughterers carried into exile.

**12** But the chief of the slaughterers left some of the poorest of the land to be vinedressers and plowmen.

**13** The pillars of bronze that were in the house of the LORD, the stands and the bronze Sea that were in the house of the LORD, the Chaldeans broke in pieces and carried the bronze to Babylon.

**14** They took the pots, the shovels, the snuffers, the dishes and all the vessels of bronze used in the service.

**15** The firepans and the basins, what was of gold, in gold, and what was of silver, in silver, the chief of the slaughterers took.

**16** The two pillars, the one Sea and the stands that Solomon had made for the house of the LORD — the bronze of all these vessels was beyond weighing.

**17** The height of the one pillar was eighteen cubits, and on it was a capital of bronze. The height of the capital was three cubits, with a network and pomegranates all around the capital, all of bronze; and the second pillar had the same, with the network.

**18** The chief of the slaughterers took Seraiah the chief priest and Zephaniah the second priest and the three keepers of the threshold.

**19** From the city he took one official who was set over the men of war, and five men of those who saw the king's face who were found in the city, the secretary of the commander of the army, who mustered the people of the land, and sixty men of the people of the land who were found in the city.

**20** Nebuzaradan the chief of the slaughterers took them and brought them to the king of Babylon at Riblah.

**21** The king of Babylon struck them down and put them to death at Riblah in the land of Hamath. So Judah was carried into exile from its land.

## Gedaliah

**22** Over the people who remained in the land of Judah, whom Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had left, he appointed Gedaliah son of Ahikam, son of Shaphan.

**23** When all the commanders of the forces and their men heard that the king of Babylon had appointed Gedaliah, they came to Gedaliah at Mizpah: Ishmael son of Nethaniah, Johanan son of Kareah, Seraiah son of Tanhumeth the Netophathite and Jaazaniah son of the Maacathite, they and their men.

**24** Gedaliah swore to them and to their men, and said to them, "Do not be afraid of the servants of the Chaldeans. Live in the land and serve the king of Babylon, and it will go well with you."

**25** And it came to pass in the seventh month that Ishmael son of Nethaniah, son of Elishama, of the royal seed, came with ten men, and they struck down Gedaliah, and he died, along with the Judeans and the Chaldeans who were with him at Mizpah.

**26** Then all the people, both small and great, and the commanders of the forces arose and went to Egypt, for they were afraid of the Chaldeans.

## Jehoiachin Released

**27** And it came to pass in the thirty-seventh year of the exile of Jehoiachin king of Judah, in the twelfth month, on the twenty-seventh day of the month, that Evil-merodach king of Babylon, in the year that he began to reign, lifted the head of Jehoiachin king of Judah out of prison.

**28** He spoke kindly to him and set his throne above the thrones of the kings who were with him in Babylon.

**29** So Jehoiachin put off his prison garments, and he ate bread before the king continually all the days of his life.

**30** For his allowance, a continual allowance was given him by the king, a portion each day, all the days of his life.

## Notes

- **vv1–21 and Jeremiah 52** — the same account closes the book of Jeremiah (Jeremiah 52:4–27, 31–34), and Jeremiah 39 tells it again in part.
- **v3 "In the fourth month, on the ninth day"** — the Hebrew has only *on the ninth of the month*, with no month named. Jeremiah gives it twice, *in the fourth month, on the ninth of the month* (Jeremiah 52:6; 39:2). The text follows Jeremiah.
- **v4 "all the men of war fled by night"** — the Hebrew has no verb: *all the men of war by night*. Jeremiah has *fled and went out of the city by night* (Jeremiah 52:7), and the Greek *went out by night*. The text follows them.
- **v7 "put out the eyes of Zedekiah… brought him to Babylon"** — Ezekiel had said of the prince: *I will bring him to Babylon, the land of the Chaldeans, yet he shall not see it, and there he shall die* (Ezekiel 12:13).
- **v8 "Nebuzaradan, the chief of the slaughterers"** — *rav tabbachim*, the title of Potiphar, *chief of the slaughterers* (Genesis 37:36). *On the seventh day*: Jeremiah has *the tenth day* (Jeremiah 52:12). The text keeps the Hebrew.
- **vv13–17 "The pillars of bronze… The height of the capital was three cubits"** — Solomon's pillars, *Jachin* and *Boaz* (1 Kings 7:21). Their capitals were *five cubits* high (1 Kings 7:16), and Jeremiah also has five (Jeremiah 52:22). The Hebrew here and the Greek both have three; the text keeps them.
- **v19 "five men of those who saw the king's face"** — Jeremiah has *seven* (Jeremiah 52:25). *Saw the king's face*: those with access to the king.
- **v21 "So Judah was carried into exile from its land"** — the same words as the end of Israel: *Israel was carried into exile from its own land* (17:23).
- **v26 "went to Egypt"** — the people return to the land of the exodus, as the curses had said: *The LORD will bring you back to Egypt* (Deuteronomy 28:68).
- **vv27–30 "lifted the head of Jehoiachin… ate bread before the king continually"** — *nasa et rosh*, the words of Joseph's prison: *Pharaoh will lift your head and restore you to your place* (Genesis 40:13). The book ends with the king of David's line eating at a foreign king's table, as Mephibosheth *always ate at the king's table* (2 Samuel 9:13). Jeremiah dates the release *on the twenty-fifth day* (Jeremiah 52:31).
