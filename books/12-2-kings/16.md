# 2 Kings 16

## Ahaz King of Judah

**1** In the seventeenth year of Pekah son of Remaliah, Ahaz son of Jotham king of Judah began to reign.

**2** Ahaz was twenty years old when he began to reign, and he reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem. He did not do what was right in the eyes of the LORD his God, as David his father had done.

**3** He walked in the way of the kings of Israel. He even made his son pass through the fire, according to the detestable practices of the nations whom the LORD had driven out before the sons of Israel.

**4** He sacrificed and burned incense on the high places, on the hills and under every leafy tree.

## Ahaz Calls on Assyria

**5** Then Rezin king of Aram and Pekah son of Remaliah king of Israel came up to Jerusalem to wage war. They besieged Ahaz but could not overcome him.

**6** At that time Rezin king of Aram recovered Elath for Aram and drove the men of Judah out of Elath. The Edomites came to Elath and have lived there to this day.

**7** Ahaz sent messengers to Tiglath-pileser king of Assyria, saying, "I am your servant and your son. Come up and save me from the hand of the king of Aram and from the hand of the king of Israel, who are rising up against me."

**8** Ahaz took the silver and gold that was found in the house of the LORD and in the treasuries of the king's house, and sent it to the king of Assyria as a bribe.

**9** The king of Assyria listened to him. The king of Assyria went up against Damascus and took it, carried its people into exile to Kir, and put Rezin to death.

## The Altar from Damascus

**10** King Ahaz went to Damascus to meet Tiglath-pileser king of Assyria, and he saw the altar that was at Damascus. King Ahaz sent Uriah the priest the likeness of the altar and its pattern, in all its workmanship.

**11** Uriah the priest built the altar; according to all that King Ahaz had sent from Damascus, so Uriah the priest made it, before King Ahaz came back from Damascus.

**12** When the king came from Damascus, the king saw the altar. The king drew near to the altar and went up on it,

**13** and he burned his burnt offering and his grain offering, poured out his drink offering, and dashed the blood of his peace offerings on the altar.

**14** The bronze altar that was before the LORD he moved from the front of the house, from between his altar and the house of the LORD, and put it on the north side of his altar.

**15** King Ahaz commanded Uriah the priest, saying, "On the great altar burn the morning burnt offering and the evening grain offering, the king's burnt offering and his grain offering, with the burnt offering of all the people of the land, their grain offering and their drink offerings. Dash on it all the blood of the burnt offerings and all the blood of the sacrifices. The bronze altar shall be for me to inquire by."

**16** Uriah the priest did according to all that King Ahaz commanded.

**17** King Ahaz cut off the panels of the stands and removed the basins from them. He took down the Sea from the bronze oxen that were under it and put it on a pavement of stone.

**18** The covered way for the Sabbath that they had built in the house, and the king's outer entrance, he turned away from the house of the LORD because of the king of Assyria.

**19** The rest of the acts of Ahaz that he did, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?

**20** Ahaz lay down with his fathers and was buried with his fathers in the City of David, and Hezekiah his son reigned in his place.

## Notes

- **v3 "made his son pass through the fire, according to the detestable practices of the nations"** — *to'avot*, **detestable things**. The law names it first among the practices of the nations: *There shall not be found among you anyone who makes his son or his daughter pass through the fire* (Deuteronomy 18:10). Ahaz is the first king of Judah of whom it is said.
- **vv5–7 "Rezin… and Pekah… I am your servant and your son"** — the war of Isaiah 7, when the prophet met Ahaz and told him, *Be careful and be quiet; do not fear* (Isaiah 7:4). Here Ahaz turns to Assyria instead, calling himself the Assyrian king's *servant* and *son*.
- **v6 "recovered Elath for Aram… The Edomites came to Elath"** — the Hebrew writes *Arameans* in the second clause and the scribes read *Edomites*; the text follows the read form, and so does the Greek. In Hebrew *Aram* and *Edom* differ by one letter. Elath had been built and restored to Judah by Azariah (14:22).
- **v8 "as a bribe"** — *shochad*, which the law forbids: *You shall not take a bribe* (Exodus 23:8). The silver comes out of the house of the LORD.
- **v9 "carried its people into exile to Kir"** — as Amos said of Aram: *the people of Aram shall go into exile to Kir* (Amos 1:5). Amos also names Kir as the place the LORD brought Aram from (Amos 9:7).
- **v10 "the likeness of the altar and its pattern"** — *tavnit*, the word of the dwelling in the wilderness: *According to all that I show you — the pattern of the dwelling* (Exodus 25:9). The pattern for the LORD's altar now comes from Damascus.
- **vv12–13 "went up on it, and he burned his burnt offering"** — the king offers on the new altar himself.
- **v15 "The bronze altar shall be for me to inquire by"** — *yihyeh li le-vaqqer*. *Baqqer* is **to inquire**, **to examine**, as of reading omens from an offering; the Greek reads *la-boqer*, **for the morning**. The text keeps the Hebrew; what Ahaz meant to do at the old altar is not said.
- **v18 "The covered way for the Sabbath"** — written *meisakh*, read *musakh*, found only here; **covered** is the sense of the root *sakhakh*, **to cover**. The Greek has *the foundation of the seat*. What was done to it, and why *because of the king of Assyria*, the text does not explain.
