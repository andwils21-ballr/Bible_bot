# 2 Kings 18

## Hezekiah King of Judah

**1** And it came to pass in the third year of Hoshea son of Elah, king of Israel, that Hezekiah son of Ahaz king of Judah began to reign.

**2** He was twenty-five years old when he began to reign, and he reigned twenty-nine years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Abi daughter of Zechariah.

**3** He did what was right in the eyes of the LORD, according to all that David his father had done.

**4** He removed the high places, broke the pillars and cut down the Asherah. He broke in pieces the bronze serpent that Moses had made, for until those days the sons of Israel had been burning incense to it; it was called Nehushtan.

**5** He trusted in the LORD, the God of Israel, so that after him there was none like him among all the kings of Judah, nor among those who were before him.

**6** He held fast to the LORD; he did not turn from following Him, but kept His commandments that the LORD commanded Moses.

**7** The LORD was with him; wherever he went out, he prospered. He rebelled against the king of Assyria and did not serve him.

**8** He struck down the Philistines as far as Gaza and its territory, from watchtower to fortified city.

## Samaria Taken

**9** And it came to pass in the fourth year of King Hezekiah, which was the seventh year of Hoshea son of Elah, king of Israel, that Shalmaneser king of Assyria came up against Samaria and besieged it.

**10** At the end of three years they took it. In the sixth year of Hezekiah, which was the ninth year of Hoshea king of Israel, Samaria was taken.

**11** The king of Assyria carried Israel into exile to Assyria and put them in Halah and on the Habor, the river of Gozan, and in the cities of the Medes,

**12** because they did not listen to the voice of the LORD their God but transgressed His covenant, all that Moses the servant of the LORD commanded; they would neither listen nor do it.

## Sennacherib Comes Up

**13** In the fourteenth year of King Hezekiah, Sennacherib king of Assyria came up against all the fortified cities of Judah and took them.

**14** Hezekiah king of Judah sent to the king of Assyria at Lachish, saying, "I have done wrong; withdraw from me. Whatever you put on me I will bear." The king of Assyria laid on Hezekiah king of Judah three hundred talents of silver and thirty talents of gold.

**15** Hezekiah gave him all the silver that was found in the house of the LORD and in the treasuries of the king's house.

**16** At that time Hezekiah stripped the doors of the temple of the LORD and the doorposts that Hezekiah king of Judah had overlaid, and gave the gold to the king of Assyria.

**17** The king of Assyria sent the Tartan, the Rab-saris and the Rabshakeh from Lachish to King Hezekiah with a great army to Jerusalem. They went up and came to Jerusalem, and when they arrived, they stood by the conduit of the upper pool, which is on the highway to the Washer's Field.

**18** When they called for the king, Eliakim son of Hilkiah, who was over the household, Shebnah the secretary and Joah son of Asaph the recorder came out to them.

## The Rabshakeh's Speech

**19** The Rabshakeh said to them, "Say to Hezekiah, 'Thus says the great king, the king of Assyria: What is this confidence in which you trust?

**20** You say — but it is only a word of the lips — that you have counsel and strength for war. Now, in whom do you trust, that you have rebelled against me?

**21** Now you are trusting in the staff of this crushed reed, in Egypt, which will pierce the hand of any man who leans on it and go into it. Such is Pharaoh king of Egypt to all who trust in him.

**22** But if you say to me, "We trust in the LORD our God," is it not He whose high places and altars Hezekiah has removed, saying to Judah and to Jerusalem, "You shall bow down before this altar in Jerusalem"?'

**23** So now, make a wager with my master the king of Assyria: I will give you two thousand horses, if you are able on your part to put riders on them.

**24** How then can you turn back a single governor among the least of my master's servants, when you trust in Egypt for chariots and horsemen?

**25** Now, have I come up against this place to destroy it without the LORD? The LORD said to me, 'Go up against this land and destroy it.'"

**26** Then Eliakim son of Hilkiah, Shebnah and Joah said to the Rabshakeh, "Please speak to your servants in Aramaic, for we understand it. Do not speak to us in the language of Judah in the hearing of the people who are on the wall."

**27** But the Rabshakeh said to them, "Has my master sent me to speak these words to your master and to you, and not to the men sitting on the wall, who are doomed with you to eat their own excrement and drink their own urine?"

**28** Then the Rabshakeh stood and called out in a loud voice in the language of Judah, and he spoke and said, "Hear the word of the great king, the king of Assyria!

**29** Thus says the king: 'Do not let Hezekiah deceive you, for he will not be able to deliver you out of his hand.

**30** Do not let Hezekiah make you trust in the LORD, saying, "The LORD will surely deliver us, and this city will not be given into the hand of the king of Assyria."

**31** Do not listen to Hezekiah, for thus says the king of Assyria: Make peace with me and come out to me, and each of you will eat from his own vine and his own fig tree, and each will drink the water of his own cistern,

**32** until I come and take you away to a land like your own land, a land of grain and new wine, a land of bread and vineyards, a land of olive oil and honey, so that you may live and not die. Do not listen to Hezekiah when he misleads you, saying, "The LORD will deliver us."

**33** Has any of the gods of the nations ever delivered his land out of the hand of the king of Assyria?

**34** Where are the gods of Hamath and Arpad? Where are the gods of Sepharvaim, Hena and Ivvah? Have they delivered Samaria out of my hand?

**35** Who among all the gods of the lands have delivered their lands out of my hand, that the LORD should deliver Jerusalem out of my hand?'"

**36** But the people were silent and did not answer him a word, for the king's command was, "Do not answer him."

**37** Then Eliakim son of Hilkiah, who was over the household, Shebna the secretary and Joah son of Asaph the recorder came to Hezekiah with their clothes torn, and told him the words of the Rabshakeh.

## Notes

- **vv13–37 and Isaiah 36** — the same account stands in Isaiah 36–39, nearly word for word. Isaiah lacks vv14–16, the tribute, and in v17 sends only the Rabshakeh.
- **v4 "the bronze serpent that Moses had made… it was called Nehushtan"** — *nechash ha-nechoshet*, **the serpent of bronze**, from the wilderness: *Moses made a bronze serpent and set it on the pole* (Numbers 21:9). *Nechushtan* joins the two words, *nachash*, **serpent**, and *nechoshet*, **bronze**. What the LORD gave for healing had become something burned incense to.
- **vv5, 19–24, 30 "He trusted in the LORD… What is this confidence in which you trust?"** — *batach*, **to trust**, said of Hezekiah in v5 and then used by the Rabshakeh eight times (vv19 twice, 20, 21 twice, 22, 24, 30): the whole speech is an attack on that one word.
- **v5 "after him there was none like him"** — the same is said of Josiah: *before him there was no king like him… and after him none arose like him* (23:25). Hezekiah is unmatched in trust, Josiah in turning to the LORD with all his heart.
- **v17 "the Tartan, the Rab-saris and the Rabshakeh"** — titles, not names. *Rav-shaqeh* is **chief cupbearer** in its Hebrew sound, from *shaqah*, **to give drink**; *rav-saris*, **chief official**. The text keeps the received forms.
- **v21 "the staff of this crushed reed, in Egypt"** — the same picture in Ezekiel: Egypt *has been a staff of reed to the house of Israel; when they took hold of you by the hand, you splintered* (Ezekiel 29:6–7).
- **v23 "make a wager with my master"** — *hit'arev*, from *arav*, **to pledge**, the root of *the sons of pledges*, the hostages Jehoash took from Jerusalem (14:14).
- **v27 "to eat their own excrement and drink their own urine"** — the text follows the read forms, *tso'atam* and *mei ragleihem*, **the water of their feet**; the written forms are coarser words for the same things. The meaning is the same either way.
- **vv31–32 "each of you will eat from his own vine and his own fig tree… a land of olive oil and honey"** — the Rabshakeh offers the peace of Solomon's days, *each man under his vine and under his fig tree* (1 Kings 4:25), and a land described as the LORD described Canaan, *a land of olive oil and honey* (Deuteronomy 8:8), only it lies in exile. *Make peace with me* is *asu itti verakhah*, **make a blessing with me**.
- **v34 "Hena and Ivvah"** — Isaiah 36:19 and the Greek here lack these two names.
