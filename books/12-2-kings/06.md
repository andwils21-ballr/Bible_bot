# 2 Kings 6

## The Axe Head

**1** The sons of the prophets said to Elisha, "The place where we live under your charge is too cramped for us.

**2** Let us go to the Jordan, and each of us take a beam from there, and let us make a place there for us to live." He said, "Go."

**3** Then one of them said, "Please be willing to go with your servants." He answered, "I will go."

**4** So he went with them. When they came to the Jordan, they cut down trees.

**5** And it came to pass, as one of them was felling a beam, that the iron head fell into the water. He cried out and said, "Alas, my lord! It was borrowed."

**6** The man of God said, "Where did it fall?" When he showed him the place, he cut off a stick and threw it in there, and made the iron float.

**7** He said, "Pick it up." So he reached out his hand and took it.

## Horses and Chariots of Fire

**8** Now the king of Aram was at war with Israel. He took counsel with his servants, saying, "At such and such a place shall be my camp."

**9** But the man of God sent word to the king of Israel, "Beware of passing that place, for the Arameans are going down there."

**10** So the king of Israel sent to the place that the man of God told him. He warned him about it, and he was on his guard there, not once or twice.

**11** The heart of the king of Aram was greatly troubled by this. He called his servants and said to them, "Will you not tell me which of us is for the king of Israel?"

**12** One of his servants said, "No one, my lord the king. But Elisha, the prophet who is in Israel, tells the king of Israel the words that you speak in your bedroom."

**13** He said, "Go and see where he is, so that I may send and seize him." It was told him, "He is in Dothan."

**14** So he sent there horses and chariots and a great army. They came by night and surrounded the city.

**15** When the servant of the man of God rose early and went out, an army with horses and chariots was surrounding the city. His servant said to him, "Alas, my lord! What shall we do?"

**16** He said, "Do not be afraid, for those who are with us are more than those who are with them."

**17** Then Elisha prayed and said, "O LORD, please open his eyes, so that he may see." So the LORD opened the eyes of the young man, and he saw: the mountain was full of horses and chariots of fire all around Elisha.

**18** When the Arameans came down against him, Elisha prayed to the LORD and said, "Strike this people with blindness." So He struck them with blindness, according to the word of Elisha.

**19** Elisha said to them, "This is not the way, and this is not the city. Follow me, and I will bring you to the man you are seeking." So he led them to Samaria.

**20** And it came to pass, as soon as they came into Samaria, that Elisha said, "O LORD, open the eyes of these men, so that they may see." So the LORD opened their eyes, and they saw that they were in the middle of Samaria.

**21** When the king of Israel saw them, he said to Elisha, "My father, shall I strike them? Shall I strike them?"

**22** He answered, "You shall not strike them. Would you strike those whom you have taken captive with your sword and with your bow? Set bread and water before them, so that they may eat and drink and go to their master."

**23** So he prepared a great feast for them, and they ate and drank; then he sent them away, and they went to their master. The raiding bands of Aram did not come again into the land of Israel.

## The Famine in Samaria

**24** And it came to pass after this that Ben-hadad king of Aram mustered all his army and went up and besieged Samaria.

**25** There was a great famine in Samaria as they besieged it, until a donkey's head was sold for eighty shekels of silver, and a quarter of a kab of dove's dung for five shekels of silver.

**26** And it came to pass, as the king of Israel was passing by on the wall, that a woman cried out to him, "Help, my lord the king!"

**27** He said, "If the LORD does not help you, how shall I help you? From the threshing floor, or from the winepress?"

**28** Then the king said to her, "What is troubling you?" She answered, "This woman said to me, 'Give your son, so that we may eat him today, and we will eat my son tomorrow.'

**29** So we boiled my son and ate him. On the next day I said to her, 'Give your son, so that we may eat him,' but she has hidden her son."

**30** And it came to pass, when the king heard the words of the woman, that he tore his clothes. He was passing by on the wall, and the people looked, and there was sackcloth underneath, on his flesh.

**31** He said, "So may God do to me, and more also, if the head of Elisha son of Shaphat remains on him today."

**32** Elisha was sitting in his house, and the elders were sitting with him. The king had sent a man ahead of him, but before the messenger came to him, Elisha said to the elders, "Do you see how this son of a murderer has sent to take off my head? Look, when the messenger comes, shut the door and hold the door fast against him. Is not the sound of his master's feet behind him?"

**33** While he was still speaking with them, the messenger came down to him and said, "This evil is from the LORD! Why should I wait for the LORD any longer?"

## Notes

- **v5 "It was borrowed"** — *ve-hu sha'ul*, **and it was borrowed**: the loss would fall on the borrower.
- **v8 "At such and such a place"** — *peloni almoni*, the phrase Boaz uses for the unnamed kinsman in Ruth 4:1; here the king's plans are kept vague in the telling.
- **vv12, 17 "the words that you speak in your bedroom… he saw"** — the prophet who hears the king's private words asks that his servant may *see*. The chapter turns on eyes: opened (v17), struck blind (v18), opened again in Samaria (v20).
- **v13 "He is in Dothan"** — the place where Joseph found his brothers (Genesis 37:17).
- **v17 "horses and chariots of fire all around Elisha"** — the chariot of fire that took Elijah (2:11), and the words Elisha cried then, *the chariots of Israel and its horsemen* (2:12).
- **v18 "Strike this people with blindness"** — *ba-sanverim*, the word used of the men of Sodom at Lot's door, rendered in Genesis *blindings* (Genesis 19:11), and found only in these two places.
- **vv21–22 "My father, shall I strike them?… Set bread and water before them"** — the king of Israel calls the prophet *my father*, as Elisha called Elijah (2:12). The prisoners are fed and sent home, and the raids stop (v23).
- **v25 "a donkey's head… a quarter of a kab of dove's dung"** — food no one would eat, sold at a high price. The written text has *chorei yonim*, **dove's dung**; the read form is *divyonim*, another word for the same thing. The Greek has *fifty* shekels for the head.
- **vv28–29 "So we boiled my son and ate him"** — the curse of the covenant: *You will eat the fruit of your womb, the flesh of your sons and your daughters… in the siege* (Deuteronomy 28:53).
- **vv32–33 "the messenger came down to him and said, 'This evil is from the LORD!'"** — *ha-mal'akh*, **the messenger**. *Ha-melekh*, **the king**, differs by one letter, and in the next verse it is the king's officer who answers Elisha (7:2). The Hebrew and the Greek both have *messenger*, and the text keeps it.
