# 2 Kings 8

## The Shunammite's Land Restored

**1** Now Elisha had said to the woman whose son he had restored to life, "Get up and go, you and your household, and live as a guest wherever you can, for the LORD has called for a famine, and it will come on the land for seven years."

**2** So the woman got up and did according to the word of the man of God. She went with her household and lived as a guest in the land of the Philistines seven years.

**3** And it came to pass at the end of seven years that the woman returned from the land of the Philistines, and she went out to appeal to the king for her house and her field.

**4** Now the king was talking with Gehazi the servant of the man of God, saying, "Tell me, please, all the great things that Elisha has done."

**5** And it came to pass, as he was telling the king how he had restored the dead to life, that the woman whose son he had restored to life appealed to the king for her house and her field. Gehazi said, "My lord the king, this is the woman, and this is her son whom Elisha restored to life."

**6** When the king asked the woman, she told him. So the king appointed an official for her, saying, "Restore all that was hers, together with all the produce of the field from the day she left the land until now."

## Hazael

**7** Elisha came to Damascus. Ben-hadad the king of Aram was sick, and he was told, "The man of God has come here."

**8** The king said to Hazael, "Take a gift in your hand and go to meet the man of God, and inquire of the LORD through him, saying, 'Shall I recover from this sickness?'"

**9** So Hazael went to meet him, and took a gift with him, all kinds of good things of Damascus, forty camels' load. He came and stood before him and said, "Your son Ben-hadad king of Aram has sent me to you, saying, 'Shall I recover from this sickness?'"

**10** Elisha said to him, "Go, say to him, 'You shall surely recover.' But the LORD has shown me that he shall surely die."

**11** He fixed his gaze and stared until he was ashamed, and the man of God wept.

**12** Hazael said, "Why does my lord weep?" He answered, "Because I know the evil that you will do to the sons of Israel. You will set their fortresses on fire, you will kill their young men with the sword, dash their little ones in pieces and rip open their pregnant women."

**13** Hazael said, "What is your servant, the dog, that he should do this great thing?" Elisha answered, "The LORD has shown me that you shall be king over Aram."

**14** Then he left Elisha and came to his master, who said to him, "What did Elisha say to you?" He answered, "He told me that you would surely recover."

**15** But on the next day he took the blanket and dipped it in water and spread it over his face, so that he died. Hazael reigned in his place.

## Jehoram King of Judah

**16** In the fifth year of Joram son of Ahab king of Israel, Jehoshaphat being king of Judah, Jehoram son of Jehoshaphat king of Judah began to reign.

**17** He was thirty-two years old when he began to reign, and he reigned eight years in Jerusalem.

**18** He walked in the way of the kings of Israel, as the house of Ahab had done, for the daughter of Ahab was his wife. He did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD.

**19** Yet the LORD was not willing to destroy Judah, for the sake of David His servant, since He had promised to give him a lamp for his sons forever.

**20** In his days Edom revolted from under the hand of Judah and set up a king of their own.

**21** Then Joram crossed over to Zair with all his chariots. He rose by night and struck the Edomites who had surrounded him and the commanders of the chariots, but the people fled to their tents.

**22** So Edom revolted from under the hand of Judah to this day. Libnah revolted at the same time.

**23** The rest of the acts of Joram and all that he did, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?

**24** Joram lay down with his fathers and was buried with his fathers in the City of David, and Ahaziah his son reigned in his place.

## Ahaziah King of Judah

**25** In the twelfth year of Joram son of Ahab king of Israel, Ahaziah son of Jehoram king of Judah began to reign.

**26** Ahaziah was twenty-two years old when he began to reign, and he reigned one year in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Athaliah, the daughter of Omri king of Israel.

**27** He walked in the way of the house of Ahab and did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD, as the house of Ahab had done, for he was son-in-law to the house of Ahab.

**28** He went with Joram son of Ahab to war against Hazael king of Aram at Ramoth-gilead, and the Arameans wounded Joram.

**29** King Joram returned to be healed in Jezreel of the wounds that the Arameans had given him at Ramah, when he fought against Hazael king of Aram. Ahaziah son of Jehoram king of Judah went down to see Joram son of Ahab in Jezreel, because he was sick.

## Notes

- **vv1–6 "the woman whose son he had restored to life… live as a guest"** — the Shunammite of chapter 4. *Guri ba-asher taguri*, from *gur*, fixed as **live as a guest**. Her appeal reaches the king at the very moment Gehazi is telling her story (v5).
- **v10 "Go, say to him, 'You shall surely recover.' But the LORD has shown me that he shall surely die"** — the written text has *lo*, **not**: *say, 'You shall not recover.'* The read form is *lo*, **to him**: *say to him, 'You shall surely recover.'* The two words sound the same. The text follows the read form, and the Greek has it too: the sickness will not kill him, but he will die.
- **v11 "He fixed his gaze and stared until he was ashamed"** — who stared and who was ashamed is not said; the Hebrew has only *he*. The Greek has the same.
- **v12 "dash their little ones in pieces and rip open their pregnant women"** — the same horrors Hosea foresees for Samaria, *their little ones shall be dashed in pieces, and their pregnant women ripped open* (Hosea 13:16).
- **v13 "What is your servant, the dog"** — the words of self-abasement, as Mephibosheth called himself *a dead dog* (2 Samuel 9:8). Elijah had been told to anoint Hazael (1 Kings 19:15); Elisha tells him he will be king.
- **v15 "he took the blanket and dipped it in water and spread it over his face"** — *ha-makhber*, with this vowel pattern found only here; the same letters spell the bronze *grating* of the altar in Exodus (27:4). The Hebrew does not name who took it; the next words, *Hazael reigned in his place*, point to him.
- **v16 "Jehoshaphat being king of Judah"** — the phrase stands in both the Hebrew and the Greek, though Jehoshaphat's son is the one beginning to reign. The text keeps it.
- **vv18, 26 "the daughter of Ahab was his wife… Athaliah, the daughter of Omri"** — *bat*, **daughter**, also used of a granddaughter: Athaliah is Ahab's daughter and Omri's granddaughter. The marriage brings the house of Ahab into David's line.
- **v19 "to give him a lamp for his sons forever"** — *nir*, the promise of 1 Kings 11:36.
- **vv20–22 "Edom revolted from under the hand of Judah"** — as Isaac said to Esau, *you will tear his yoke off your neck* (Genesis 27:40).
