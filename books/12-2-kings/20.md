# 2 Kings 20

## Fifteen Years Added

**1** In those days Hezekiah became sick and was near death. Isaiah the prophet son of Amoz came to him and said to him, "Thus says the LORD: 'Set your house in order, for you shall die; you shall not live.'"

**2** Then he turned his face to the wall and prayed to the LORD, saying,

**3** "Please, O LORD, remember how I have walked before You in faithfulness and with a whole heart, and have done what is good in Your eyes." Hezekiah wept bitterly.

**4** Before Isaiah had gone out of the middle court, the word of the LORD came to him, saying,

**5** "Turn back and say to Hezekiah the leader of My people, 'Thus says the LORD, the God of David your father: I have heard your prayer; I have seen your tears. I will heal you. On the third day you shall go up to the house of the LORD.

**6** I will add fifteen years to your days. I will deliver you and this city out of the hand of the king of Assyria, and I will defend this city for My own sake and for the sake of David My servant.'"

**7** Isaiah said, "Take a cake of figs." They took it and laid it on the boil, and he recovered.

## The Shadow on the Steps

**8** Hezekiah said to Isaiah, "What is the sign that the LORD will heal me, and that I shall go up to the house of the LORD on the third day?"

**9** Isaiah said, "This is the sign to you from the LORD, that the LORD will do what He has spoken: shall the shadow go forward ten steps, or go back ten steps?"

**10** Hezekiah answered, "It is easy for the shadow to lengthen ten steps. Rather, let the shadow go back ten steps."

**11** Isaiah the prophet called to the LORD, and He brought the shadow back ten steps, by which it had gone down on the steps of Ahaz.

## The Envoys from Babylon

**12** At that time Berodach-baladan son of Baladan, king of Babylon, sent letters and a gift to Hezekiah, for he had heard that Hezekiah had been sick.

**13** Hezekiah was glad of them, and he showed them all his treasure house, the silver, the gold, the spices and the fine oil, his armory and all that was found in his treasuries. There was nothing in his house or in all his realm that Hezekiah did not show them.

**14** Then Isaiah the prophet came to King Hezekiah and said to him, "What did these men say? From where did they come to you?" Hezekiah said, "They have come from a far country, from Babylon."

**15** He said, "What have they seen in your house?" Hezekiah answered, "They have seen all that is in my house. There is nothing in my treasuries that I did not show them."

**16** Then Isaiah said to Hezekiah, "Hear the word of the LORD:

**17** The days are coming when all that is in your house, and what your fathers have stored up until this day, shall be carried to Babylon. Nothing shall be left, says the LORD.

**18** Some of your sons, who will come from you, whom you will father, shall be taken away, and they shall be eunuchs in the palace of the king of Babylon."

**19** Hezekiah said to Isaiah, "The word of the LORD that you have spoken is good." For he said, "Is it not so, if there will be peace and security in my days?"

**20** The rest of the acts of Hezekiah and all his might, and how he made the pool and the conduit and brought water into the city, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Judah?

**21** Hezekiah lay down with his fathers, and Manasseh his son reigned in his place.

## Notes

- **vv1–11 and Isaiah 38** — Isaiah tells the same story and adds the song Hezekiah wrote after he recovered (Isaiah 38:9–20); there the fig cake comes at the end (Isaiah 38:21).
- **v3 "in faithfulness and with a whole heart"** — *be-emet u-ve-levav shalem*, the *whole heart* the narrator found lacking in Solomon: *his heart was not whole with the LORD his God* (1 Kings 11:4).
- **v4 "the middle court"** — the read form *chatser*, **court**; the written form is *ha-ir*, **the city**. The Greek has *the middle court*.
- **v5 "the leader of My people… the God of David your father"** — *nagid*, the title the LORD gave Saul and David (1 Samuel 9:16; 13:14). Isaiah's copy lacks *the leader of My people*, *I will heal you* and *On the third day you shall go up to the house of the LORD*.
- **v7 "a cake of figs… on the boil"** — *shechin*, the word of the boils of Egypt (Exodus 9:9) and of the boil the priest inspects (Leviticus 13:18).
- **vv9–11 "ten steps… the steps of Ahaz"** — *ma'alot*, **steps**, the word of the stairway. The *steps of Ahaz* are named nowhere else. *Ma'alot* is also the word of the *Songs of Ascents* (Psalms 120–134).
- **v12 "Berodach-baladan son of Baladan"** — Isaiah's copy and the Greek spell the name *Merodach*; *b* and *m* are both made with the lips.
- **v13 "Hezekiah was glad of them"** — the Hebrew has *va-yishma*, **he listened to them**; Isaiah's copy has *va-yismach*, **he was glad**, and so does the Greek. The two words differ by one letter, and the scene, the king showing all his treasure, bears out *glad*. The text follows Isaiah and the Greek.
- **v18 "they shall be eunuchs in the palace of the king of Babylon"** — Daniel opens with it: Nebuchadnezzar orders *the chief of his eunuchs* to bring *some of the sons of Israel, of the royal seed* (Daniel 1:3). Written *yiqqach*, read *yiqqachu*, **they shall be taken**.
- **v19 "Is it not so, if there will be peace and security in my days?"** — *shalom ve-emet*. The same *emet* Hezekiah claimed in his prayer (v3), now as *security* for his own lifetime.
