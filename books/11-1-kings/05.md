# 1 Kings 5

## Solomon's Request

**1** Hiram king of Tyre sent his servants to Solomon when he heard that they had anointed him king in place of his father, for Hiram had always been a friend of David.

**2** Solomon sent word to Hiram:

**3** "You know that David my father could not build a house for the name of the LORD his God because of the wars that surrounded him, until the LORD put his enemies under the soles of his feet.

**4** But now the LORD my God has given me rest on every side; there is no adversary and no misfortune.

**5** So I intend to build a house for the name of the LORD my God, as the LORD said to David my father, 'Your son, whom I will set on your throne in your place, shall build the house for My name.'

**6** So now command that cedars be cut for me from Lebanon. My servants will work with your servants, and I will pay you wages for your servants according to all that you say, for you know that there is no one among us who knows how to cut timber like the Sidonians."

## The Treaty

**7** And it came to pass, when Hiram heard the words of Solomon, that he rejoiced greatly and said, "Blessed be the LORD today, who has given David a wise son over this great people."

**8** Hiram sent word to Solomon: "I have heard the message you sent me. I will do all you desire concerning cedar timber and cypress timber.

**9** My servants will bring it down from Lebanon to the sea, and I will make it into rafts by sea to the place you direct me, and I will break them up there, and you shall take it away. You in turn shall meet my desire by giving food for my household."

**10** So Hiram gave Solomon all the cedar timber and cypress timber he desired,

**11** and Solomon gave Hiram twenty thousand cors of wheat as food for his household, and twenty thousand baths of beaten oil. Solomon gave this to Hiram year by year.

**12** The LORD gave Solomon wisdom, as He had promised him. There was peace between Hiram and Solomon, and the two of them made a covenant.

## The Forced Labor

**13** King Solomon raised forced labor from all Israel, and the forced labor numbered thirty thousand men.

**14** He sent them to Lebanon, ten thousand a month in relays; they would be a month in Lebanon and two months at home. Adoniram was over the forced labor.

**15** Solomon also had seventy thousand burden-bearers and eighty thousand stonecutters in the hill country,

**16** besides Solomon's chief officers who were over the work, three thousand three hundred, who had charge of the people doing the work.

**17** At the king's command they quarried great stones, costly stones, to lay the foundation of the house with dressed stones.

**18** So Solomon's builders and Hiram's builders and the men of Gebal shaped them, and prepared the timber and the stones to build the house.

## Notes

- **vv1–18 in the Hebrew** — the Hebrew numbers this chapter 5:15–32; the English numbering is used here.
- **v1 "for Hiram had always been a friend of David"** — *ohev*, **one who loves**, here the loyalty of an ally. Hiram had sent David cedar and craftsmen to build his house (2 Samuel 5:11).
- **v3 "because of the wars that surrounded him"** — Chronicles gives David's own account of it: *You have shed much blood and have waged great wars; you shall not build a house for My name* (1 Chronicles 22:8).
- **v4 "there is no adversary and no misfortune"** — *ein satan*, **no adversary**. Later in the reign *the LORD raised up an adversary against Solomon, Hadad the Edomite* (11:14), with the same word.
- **v5 "Your son… shall build the house for My name"** — the word of Nathan, *He shall build a house for My name* (2 Samuel 7:13).
- **v9 "I will make it into rafts"** — *dovrot*, found only here: logs lashed together and floated down the coast.
- **v11 "twenty thousand baths of beaten oil"** — the Hebrew has *twenty cors of beaten oil*. The Greek has *twenty thousand baths*, and so does Chronicles for the oil sent to Hiram's woodcutters (2 Chronicles 2:10). Beside twenty thousand cors of wheat, twenty cors of oil is a small fraction. The text follows the Greek and Chronicles.
- **vv13–14 "raised forced labor from all Israel"** — *mas*. Later the text says *of the sons of Israel Solomon made no slaves* (9:22); the *mas* here is labor by turns, a month away and two at home. After Solomon, Israel will ask Rehoboam to lighten *the heavy yoke your father put on us* (12:4).
- **v16 "three thousand three hundred"** — Chronicles has *three thousand six hundred* (2 Chronicles 2:18), and the Greek agrees with Chronicles. But each book also gives a second group of officers: *five hundred and fifty* (9:23) and *two hundred and fifty* (2 Chronicles 8:10). The totals are the same: 3,300 + 550 = 3,850 = 3,600 + 250. The text keeps the Hebrew.
- **v18 "the men of Gebal"** — *ha-givlim*, from Gebal, a town on the coast north of Sidon. The Greek lacks the phrase and adds that they prepared the stones and timber *three years*.
