# 1 Kings 10

## The Queen of Sheba

**1** When the queen of Sheba heard of the fame of Solomon concerning the name of the LORD, she came to test him with hard questions.

**2** She came to Jerusalem with a very great retinue, with camels carrying spices and very much gold and precious stones. When she came to Solomon, she spoke with him of all that was on her heart.

**3** Solomon answered all her questions; there was nothing hidden from the king that he did not explain to her.

**4** When the queen of Sheba had seen all the wisdom of Solomon, the house that he had built,

**5** the food of his table, the seating of his servants, the attendance of his ministers and their clothing, his cupbearers, and his burnt offerings that he offered at the house of the LORD, there was no longer any spirit in her.

**6** She said to the king, "The report was true that I heard in my own land of your words and of your wisdom,

**7** but I did not believe the reports until I came and my own eyes had seen it. Not even the half was told me; your wisdom and prosperity surpass the report that I heard.

**8** Happy are your men! Happy are these servants of yours, who stand continually before you and hear your wisdom!

**9** Blessed be the LORD your God, who has delighted in you and set you on the throne of Israel! Because the LORD loved Israel forever, He has made you king, to do justice and righteousness."

**10** Then she gave the king a hundred and twenty talents of gold, and a very great quantity of spices and precious stones. Never again did such an abundance of spices come as those the queen of Sheba gave to King Solomon.

**11** Moreover, the fleet of Hiram, which brought gold from Ophir, brought from Ophir a very great quantity of almug wood and precious stones.

**12** From the almug wood the king made supports for the house of the LORD and for the king's house, and lyres and harps for the singers. No such almug wood has come or been seen to this day.

**13** King Solomon gave to the queen of Sheba all that she desired, whatever she asked, besides what he gave her out of King Solomon's bounty. Then she turned and went back to her own land, she and her servants.

## Solomon's Gold

**14** The weight of gold that came to Solomon in one year was six hundred and sixty-six talents of gold,

**15** besides what came from the traders and the commerce of the merchants, and from all the kings of the Arabs and the governors of the land.

**16** King Solomon made two hundred large shields of beaten gold; six hundred shekels of gold went into each shield.

**17** He made three hundred shields of beaten gold; three minas of gold went into each shield. The king put them in the House of the Forest of Lebanon.

**18** The king also made a great throne of ivory and overlaid it with the finest gold.

**19** The throne had six steps, and the top of the throne was rounded at the back. On each side of the seat were armrests, and two lions standing beside the armrests.

**20** Twelve lions stood there on the six steps, one on each side. Nothing like it was made in any kingdom.

**21** All King Solomon's drinking vessels were of gold, and all the vessels of the House of the Forest of Lebanon were of pure gold. None was of silver; silver was not considered as anything in the days of Solomon.

**22** For the king had a fleet of ships of Tarshish at sea with the fleet of Hiram. Once every three years the fleet of ships of Tarshish came, bringing gold, silver, ivory, apes and peacocks.

**23** So King Solomon exceeded all the kings of the earth in riches and in wisdom.

**24** All the earth sought the presence of Solomon to hear his wisdom, which God had put in his heart.

**25** Each of them brought his gift, articles of silver and articles of gold, garments, weapons, spices, horses and mules, so much year by year.

## Solomon's Horses and Chariots

**26** Solomon gathered chariots and horsemen. He had fourteen hundred chariots and twelve thousand horsemen, whom he stationed in the chariot cities and with the king in Jerusalem.

**27** The king made silver as common in Jerusalem as stones, and he made cedars as plentiful as the sycamore trees of the lowland.

**28** Solomon's horses were brought from Egypt and from Kue; the king's traders bought them from Kue at a price.

**29** A chariot could be brought up from Egypt for six hundred shekels of silver, and a horse for a hundred and fifty. So too, through them, they were exported to all the kings of the Hittites and the kings of Aram.

## Notes

- **v1 "concerning the name of the LORD, she came to test him with hard questions"** — *le-shem YHWH*: the fame of Solomon was bound up with the name of his God. The Greek has *the name of Solomon and the name of the LORD*. *Chidot*, **riddles**, the word of Samson's riddle at his wedding (Judges 14:12).
- **v5 "there was no longer any spirit in her"** — *ve-lo hayah vah od ruach*, the words used of the kings of Canaan when they heard what the LORD had done, *there was no longer any spirit in them* (Joshua 5:1).
- **v9 "to do justice and righteousness"** — *mishpat u-tsedaqah*, what was said of David, *David did justice and righteousness for all his people* (2 Samuel 8:15). The foreign queen names the task of the king.
- **vv11–12 "almug wood"** — *atsei almuggim*, known only from this passage. Chronicles spells it *algummim* (2 Chronicles 9:10), and the Greek has *hewn wood*. What tree it was is not known.
- **v14 "six hundred and sixty-six talents of gold"** — Chronicles gives the same figure (2 Chronicles 9:13).
- **vv16–17 "shields of beaten gold… in the House of the Forest of Lebanon"** — these are the shields Shishak king of Egypt will carry away in the days of Rehoboam, *all the shields of gold that Solomon had made* (14:25–26).
- **v19 "the top of the throne was rounded at the back"** — *rosh agol*, **a round top**. The Greek has *calves' heads*, reading *egel*, **calf**, for *agol*, **round**. The text keeps the Hebrew.
- **v22 "ships of Tarshish… apes and peacocks"** — Tarshish is the far port Jonah sailed for from Joppa (Jonah 1:3); *ships of Tarshish* are the ships that make such voyages. *Tukkiyyim*, here **peacocks**, is found only in this verse and its parallel (2 Chronicles 9:21); what bird or animal it names is uncertain.
- **vv26–29 "Solomon gathered chariots and horsemen… horses were brought from Egypt"** — the law of the king: *he shall not multiply horses for himself, nor make the people return to Egypt in order to multiply horses* (Deuteronomy 17:16). The same law warns against much silver and gold (17:17) and many wives, and the next chapter opens with his wives.
- **v28 "from Egypt and from Kue"** — *u-miqveh*, read as the name of a land, *Kue*, and not the common word *miqveh*, **a gathering**. The Greek has *from Thekoue*. Chronicles has *miqve* both times (2 Chronicles 1:16).
