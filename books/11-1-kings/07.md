# 1 Kings 7

## Solomon's Palace

**1** Solomon was building his own house thirteen years, and he finished all his house.

**2** He built the House of the Forest of Lebanon, a hundred cubits long, fifty cubits wide and thirty cubits high, on four rows of cedar pillars, with cedar beams on the pillars.

**3** It was roofed with cedar above the side chambers that rested on the pillars, forty-five of them, fifteen in a row.

**4** There were window frames in three rows, and window faced window three times.

**5** All the doorways and doorposts had square frames, and window faced window three times.

**6** He made the Hall of Pillars fifty cubits long and thirty cubits wide, with a porch in front of them, and pillars and a canopy in front of them.

**7** He made the Hall of the Throne where he was to judge, the Hall of Judgment. It was covered with cedar from floor to floor.

**8** His own house where he was to live, in the other court behind the hall, was of the same work. Solomon also made a house like this hall for Pharaoh's daughter, whom he had taken as his wife.

**9** All these were of costly stones, cut to measure, sawed with saws inside and outside, from the foundation to the coping, and from the outside to the great court.

**10** The foundation was of costly stones, great stones, stones of ten cubits and stones of eight cubits.

**11** Above were costly stones cut to measure, and cedar.

**12** The great court had three courses of dressed stone all around and a course of cedar beams, like the inner court of the house of the LORD and the porch of the house.

## Hiram the Craftsman

**13** King Solomon sent and brought Hiram from Tyre.

**14** He was the son of a widow of the tribe of Naphtali, and his father was a man of Tyre, a worker in bronze. He was filled with wisdom, understanding and knowledge to do any work in bronze. He came to King Solomon and did all his work.

## The Two Pillars

**15** He cast the two pillars of bronze. Eighteen cubits was the height of one pillar, and a line of twelve cubits measured the circumference of the second.

**16** He made two capitals of cast bronze to set on the tops of the pillars. The height of the one capital was five cubits, and the height of the other capital was five cubits.

**17** There were networks of lattice work and wreaths of chain work for the capitals on the tops of the pillars, seven for the one capital and seven for the other capital.

**18** He made the pomegranates, two rows around on the one network, to cover the capital that was on the top of the pillar, and he did the same for the other capital.

**19** The capitals that were on the tops of the pillars in the porch were of lily work, four cubits.

**20** The capitals on the two pillars also had pomegranates above, close to the rounded part beside the network. There were two hundred pomegranates in rows all around on the second capital.

**21** He set up the pillars at the porch of the nave. He set up the pillar on the right and called its name Jachin, and he set up the pillar on the left and called its name Boaz.

**22** On the tops of the pillars was lily work. So the work of the pillars was finished.

## The Sea

**23** He made the sea of cast metal. It was round, ten cubits from brim to brim and five cubits high, and a line of thirty cubits measured its circumference.

**24** Under its brim, all around, were gourds encircling it, ten to a cubit, going around the sea. The gourds were in two rows, cast with it when it was cast.

**25** It stood on twelve oxen, three facing north, three facing west, three facing south and three facing east. The sea was set on them, and all their hindquarters were toward the inside.

**26** Its thickness was a handbreadth, and its brim was made like the brim of a cup, like a lily blossom. It held two thousand baths.

## The Stands and the Basins

**27** He made the ten stands of bronze. Each stand was four cubits long, four cubits wide and three cubits high.

**28** This was the construction of the stands: they had panels, and the panels were between the frames.

**29** On the panels that were between the frames were lions, oxen and cherubim. On the frames, above and below the lions and oxen, were wreaths of hanging work.

**30** Each stand had four bronze wheels and axles of bronze, and its four feet had supports. The supports were cast beneath the basin, with wreaths at the side of each.

**31** Its mouth within the crown and above was a cubit. Its mouth was round, like the work of a pedestal, a cubit and a half. On its mouth also were carvings, and their panels were square, not round.

**32** The four wheels were under the panels, and the axles of the wheels were in the stand. The height of each wheel was a cubit and a half.

**33** The wheels were made like chariot wheels; their axles, their rims, their spokes and their hubs were all cast.

**34** There were four supports at the four corners of each stand; the supports were of one piece with the stand.

**35** On the top of the stand was a round band half a cubit high, and on the top of the stand its stays and its panels were of one piece with it.

**36** On the surfaces of its stays and on its panels he engraved cherubim, lions and palm trees, according to the space of each, with wreaths all around.

**37** In this way he made the ten stands. All of them were cast alike, of one measure and one form.

**38** He made ten basins of bronze; each basin held forty baths, and each basin was four cubits. There was one basin on each of the ten stands.

**39** He set the stands, five on the south side of the house and five on the north side of the house, and he set the sea on the south side of the house, at the southeast corner.

## Hiram's Work Completed

**40** Hiram made the basins, the shovels and the bowls. So Hiram finished all the work that he did for King Solomon on the house of the LORD:

**41** the two pillars, and the two bowls of the capitals that were on the tops of the pillars, and the two networks to cover the two bowls of the capitals that were on the tops of the pillars;

**42** the four hundred pomegranates for the two networks, two rows of pomegranates for each network, to cover the two bowls of the capitals that were on the pillars;

**43** the ten stands, and the ten basins on the stands;

**44** the one sea, and the twelve oxen under the sea;

**45** and the pots, the shovels and the bowls. All these vessels that Hiram made for King Solomon for the house of the LORD were of burnished bronze.

**46** In the plain of the Jordan the king cast them, in the clay ground between Succoth and Zarethan.

**47** Solomon left all the vessels unweighed, because there were so very many; the weight of the bronze was not found out.

## The Furnishings of Gold

**48** Solomon made all the vessels that were in the house of the LORD: the altar of gold, and the table of gold on which was the bread of the Presence;

**49** the lampstands of pure gold, five on the right and five on the left in front of the inner sanctuary; the flowers, the lamps and the tongs, of gold;

**50** the cups, the snuffers, the bowls, the dishes for incense and the fire pans, of pure gold; and the sockets of gold for the doors of the inner house, the Most Holy Place, and for the doors of the house, the nave.

**51** So all the work that King Solomon did on the house of the LORD was finished. Solomon brought in the things that David his father had dedicated, the silver, the gold and the vessels, and put them in the treasuries of the house of the LORD.

## Notes

- **vv1–12 in the Greek** — the Greek sets Solomon's own buildings after the furnishings of the house of the LORD, so that its chapter 7 runs from Hiram (v13) to the end and then vv1–12. The text keeps the Hebrew order.
- **v1 "thirteen years"** — the house of the LORD took seven (6:38).
- **vv4–7, 17–20, 28–36 (the building terms)** — many of the words here are found only in these descriptions (*shequfim*, *mechezeh*, *shelabbim*, *loyot*, *chishurim*), and the exact form of the windows, the capitals and the stands is uncertain. The text gives the plainest sense each phrase allows; *from floor to floor* (v7) is kept as the Hebrew has it.
- **v14 "the son of a widow of the tribe of Naphtali… filled with wisdom, understanding and knowledge"** — the words used of Bezalel, who made the tabernacle: *I have filled him with the Spirit of God — with wisdom and with understanding and with knowledge and with every kind of craft* (Exodus 31:3). Chronicles calls Hiram's mother *a woman of the daughters of Dan* (2 Chronicles 2:14); Bezalel's helper Oholiab was of the tribe of Dan (Exodus 31:6).
- **vv15–16 "Eighteen cubits… a line of twelve cubits… five cubits"** — the account of the pillars' destruction also has *eighteen cubits* for the pillar but *three cubits* for the capital (2 Kings 25:17). Chronicles has *thirty-five cubits* for the pillars (2 Chronicles 3:15). The Greek has *fourteen cubits* around. The text keeps the Hebrew.
- **v18 "He made the pomegranates, two rows around on the one network"** — the Hebrew has *He made the pillars, and two rows…* and ends *on the top of the pomegranates*: the two words have changed places. The Greek has the pomegranates in rows on the network, as v42 lists them, *two rows of pomegranates for each network*. The text follows the Greek.
- **v21 "Jachin… Boaz"** — *Yakhin*, **He establishes**, and *Bo'az*, **in him is strength**. *Boaz* is the name of David's great-grandfather (Ruth 4:21–22).
- **v23 "ten cubits from brim to brim… a line of thirty cubits measured its circumference"** — the ratio of the two numbers is 3; the true ratio of a circle's circumference to its width is about 3.14. Chronicles has the same ten and thirty (2 Chronicles 4:2); the Greek has *thirty-three*. The written text has *qaveh*, the read form *qav*, **line**.
- **v26 "It held two thousand baths"** — Chronicles has *three thousand baths* (2 Chronicles 4:5). The text keeps the Hebrew.
- **vv48–49 "the altar of gold… the lampstands of pure gold, five on the right and five on the left"** — the tabernacle had one lampstand (Exodus 25:31); the house has ten.
