# Romans 2

## You Who Judge

**1** So you are without excuse, whoever you are, you who judge. For in judging another you condemn yourself, because you who judge do the very same things.

**2** We know that the judgment of God rightly falls on those who do such things.

**3** Do you suppose, you who judge those who do such things and yet do them yourself, that you will escape the judgment of God?

**4** Or do you despise the riches of His kindness and forbearance and patience, not knowing that God's kindness is leading you to repentance?

**5** But by your hardness and your unrepentant heart you are storing up wrath for yourself on the day of wrath, when the righteous judgment of God will be revealed.

**6** He will repay each one according to his deeds:

**7** to those who by patience in doing good seek glory and honor and immortality, eternal life;

**8** but to those who are self-seeking and disobey the truth and obey unrighteousness, wrath and fury.

**9** There will be trouble and distress for every human being who does evil, the Jew first and also the Greek,

**10** but glory and honor and peace for everyone who does good, the Jew first and also the Greek.

**11** For God shows no partiality.

## The Law and the Heart

**12** All who have sinned without the Law will also perish without the Law, and all who have sinned under the Law will be judged by the Law.

**13** For it is not the hearers of the Law who are righteous before God; it is the doers of the Law who will be justified.

**14** When Gentiles, who do not have the Law by birth, do what the Law requires, they are a law to themselves, even though they do not have the Law.

**15** They show that the work of the Law is written on their hearts, while their conscience bears witness with them, and their thoughts accuse or even defend them among themselves,

**16** on the day when, according to my gospel, God judges the secrets of people through Christ Jesus.

## You Who Call Yourself a Jew

**17** But if you call yourself a Jew, and rely on the Law and boast in God,

**18** and know His will and approve what is excellent, because you are instructed from the Law,

**19** and are sure that you yourself are a guide to the blind, a light to those in darkness,

**20** an instructor of the foolish, a teacher of children, because you have in the Law the embodiment of knowledge and truth —

**21** you, then, who teach someone else, do you not teach yourself? You who preach that one must not steal, do you steal?

**22** You who say that one must not commit adultery, do you commit adultery? You who detest idols, do you rob temples?

**23** You who boast in the Law, do you dishonor God by breaking the Law?

**24** For, as it is written, "The name of God is blasphemed among the Gentiles because of you."

## Circumcision of the Heart

**25** Circumcision is of value if you keep the Law; but if you break the Law, your circumcision has become uncircumcision.

**26** So if a man who is uncircumcised keeps the righteous requirements of the Law, will not his uncircumcision be reckoned as circumcision?

**27** The one who is uncircumcised by birth and fulfills the Law will judge you, who have the letter and circumcision and yet break the Law.

**28** For a Jew is not one outwardly, and circumcision is not what is outward, in the flesh.

**29** A Jew is one inwardly, and circumcision is of the heart, by the Spirit, not by the letter. His praise is not from people but from God.

## Notes

- **v1 "So you are without excuse… you who judge"** — *anapologētos*, the word that closed the case against the idolaters, *So they are without excuse* (1:20). The reader who has nodded through chapter 1 is now in the same place.
- **vv6, 13 "He will repay each one according to his deeds… the doers of the Law who will be justified"** — v6 is the Greek of Psalm 62:12, *You repay a man according to his work*, and of Proverbs 24:12. *Dikaiōthēsontai*, **will be justified**, is the verb of *dikaios*, **righteous**, in the same verse: in Greek *justify* and *righteous* are one word family, and so is the *righteousness of God* (1:17). Paul says here that *the doers of the Law will be justified*, and in 3:20 that *by works of the Law no flesh will be justified*. The letter holds both sentences; chapters 3–4 are where it works out how.
- **vv14–15 "Gentiles, who do not have the Law by birth… the work of the Law is written on their hearts"** — *physei*, **by nature** or **by birth**, stands between the two halves of the sentence and can go with either: Gentiles who *by nature do* what the Law requires, or Gentiles who do not have the Law *by birth*. Paul uses it of birth in Galatians 2:15, *we who are Jews by birth*, and this chapter's *uncircumcised by birth* (v27) is *ek physeōs*. *Written on their hearts* is the new covenant of Jeremiah 31:33, *on their heart I will write it*.
- **v24 "The name of God is blasphemed among the Gentiles because of you"** — Isaiah 52:5 in the Greek, *because of you… My name is blasphemed among the nations*. The Hebrew has neither *because of you* nor *among the nations*: *continually, all day long, My name is despised*, while Israel is in exile. Paul reads the Greek's *because of you* against the one who teaches the Law and breaks it.
- **vv28–29 "circumcision is of the heart, by the Spirit… His praise is not from people but from God"** — Moses had said *Circumcise, then, the foreskin of your heart* (Deuteronomy 10:16), and *The LORD your God will circumcise your heart* (30:6). *Jew* is from *Judah*, and Jacob's blessing plays on the name: *Judah — you, your brothers shall praise* (Genesis 49:8). Paul ends on the same thought: the true Jew's *praise* comes from God.
