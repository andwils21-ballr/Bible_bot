# Romans 9

## Paul's Grief for Israel

**1** I am telling the truth in Christ, I am not lying; my conscience bears me witness in the Holy Spirit

**2** that I have great sorrow and unceasing anguish in my heart.

**3** For I could wish that I myself were accursed and cut off from Christ for the sake of my brothers, my own people according to the flesh.

**4** They are Israelites; theirs are the adoption as sons, the glory, the covenants, the giving of the Law, the worship, and the promises.

**5** Theirs are the fathers, and from them, according to the flesh, comes the Messiah, who is God over all, blessed forever. Amen.

## Children of the Promise

**6** But it is not as though the word of God has failed. For not all who are descended from Israel are Israel,

**7** and not all are children of Abraham because they are his seed; but "Through Isaac shall seed be called for you."

**8** That is, it is not the children of the flesh who are children of God, but the children of the promise are reckoned as seed.

**9** For this is what the promise said: "At this time I will come, and Sarah will have a son."

**10** Not only that, but Rebekah also had children by one man, our father Isaac.

**11** Though they were not yet born and had done nothing good or bad — so that God's purpose of choosing might stand,

**12** not because of works but because of Him who calls — she was told, "The greater shall serve the lesser."

**13** As it is written: "I loved Jacob, but Esau I hated."

## Mercy and Hardening

**14** What shall we say, then? Is there unrighteousness with God? By no means!

**15** For He says to Moses, "I will have mercy on whom I have mercy, and I will have compassion on whom I have compassion."

**16** So then it does not depend on the one who wills or the one who runs, but on God who has mercy.

**17** For the Scripture says to Pharaoh, "For this very purpose I raised you up, to show My power in you, and that My name might be proclaimed in all the earth."

**18** So then He has mercy on whom He wills, and He hardens whom He wills.

## The Potter and the Clay

**19** You will say to me then, "Why does He still find fault? For who can resist His will?"

**20** But who are you, a mere human, to answer back to God? Will what is formed say to the one who formed it, "Why have you made me like this?"

**21** Has the potter no right over the clay, to make from the same lump one vessel for honor and another for dishonor?

**22** What if God, wanting to show His wrath and make known His power, has endured with much patience vessels of wrath prepared for destruction,

**23** in order to make known the riches of His glory for vessels of mercy, which He prepared beforehand for glory —

**24** even us, whom He has called, not only from the Jews but also from the Gentiles?

## Not My People

**25** As He says in Hosea:  
"Those who were not My people I will call 'My people,'  
and her who was not beloved I will call 'beloved.'

**26** In the very place where it was said to them, 'You are not My people,'  
there they will be called sons of the living God."

**27** Isaiah cries out concerning Israel: "Though the number of the sons of Israel be as the sand of the sea, only the remnant will be saved;

**28** for the Lord will carry out His word on the earth, fully and swiftly."

**29** As Isaiah said beforehand: "If the Lord of hosts had not left us a seed, we would have become like Sodom and been made like Gomorrah."

## The Stone of Stumbling

**30** What shall we say, then? That Gentiles, who did not pursue righteousness, have attained righteousness, the righteousness that comes by faith;

**31** but Israel, who pursued a law that would bring righteousness, did not reach that law.

**32** Why? Because they did not pursue it by faith, but as if it came by works. They stumbled over the stone of stumbling,

**33** as it is written: "I am laying in Zion a stone of stumbling and a rock of offense; and whoever believes in Him will not be put to shame."

## Notes

- **v3 "I could wish that I myself were accursed and cut off from Christ for the sake of my brothers"** — Moses' prayer after the golden calf: *if You would forgive their sin — and if not, blot me out, please, from Your book* (Exodus 32:32). *Anathema*, **accursed**, is the Greek Law's word for what is *devoted*, given up wholly: *Everything devoted is most holy to the LORD* (Leviticus 27:28). Paul would give up his own place in Christ for Israel, as Moses offered his for the same people.
- **v5 "the Messiah, who is God over all, blessed forever"** — *ho ōn epi pantōn theos eulogētos eis tous aiōnas*. The Greek has no punctuation, and the line can be read three ways: **the Messiah, who is God over all, blessed forever**; **the Messiah, who is over all. God be blessed forever**; or **the Messiah. God who is over all be blessed forever**. *Ho ōn*, **who is**, most naturally refers to the noun just before it, and Paul's other blessing in this letter has the same shape, *the Creator, who is blessed forever. Amen* (1:25). Where he blesses God on its own, *blessed* comes first: *Blessed be the God and Father* (2 Corinthians 1:3).
- **vv10–13 "The greater shall serve the lesser… I loved Jacob, but Esau I hated"** — the oracle of Genesis 25:23, given *before they were born or had done anything good or bad* (see the note there), and Malachi 1:2–3. In both places the two brothers are nations: *Two nations are in your belly* (Genesis 25:23), and in Malachi the word goes on to Edom's mountains and land (1:3–4). Paul reads the two as God's free choice, *not because of works*.
- **vv15–18 "I will have mercy on whom I have mercy… I raised you up… He hardens whom He wills"** — Exodus 33:19 in the Greek, the word God spoke to Moses at the cleft of the rock, where the Hebrew has *I will be gracious to whom I will be gracious*. From here to the end of chapter 11, *eleos*, **mercy**, is the theme word (9:23; 11:30–32). Pharaoh's verse is Exodus 9:16, *for this very reason I have kept you standing*, where Paul has *I raised you up*. *Sklērynei*, **hardens**, is the verb of the Greek of Exodus 4:21, *I will harden his heart*.
- **vv20–23 "the potter… vessels of wrath prepared for destruction… vessels of mercy, which He prepared beforehand for glory"** — *Will what is formed say to the one who formed it…?* is Isaiah 29:16 in the Greek, and the clay in the potter's hand is Jeremiah 18:6. The two kinds of vessel are not said the same way. Of the vessels of mercy, God is the subject: *He prepared them beforehand*. The vessels of wrath are *katērtismena*, **prepared**, with no one named as preparing them, and God has *endured* them *with much patience*.
- **vv25–26 "Those who were not My people… her who was not beloved… sons of the living God"** — the names of Hosea's children, *Lo-ammi*, **not My people**, and *Lo-ruhamah*, **no compassion** (Hosea 1:6, 9), and the promise that reverses them (1:10; 2:23). In Hosea it is said to the northern tribes of Israel; Paul reads it of those called *also from the Gentiles* (v24).
