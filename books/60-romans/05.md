# Romans 5

## Peace with God

**1** Since we have been justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ.

**2** Through Him we have also obtained access by faith into this grace in which we stand, and we boast in the hope of the glory of God.

**3** Not only that, but we also boast in our sufferings, knowing that suffering produces endurance,

**4** endurance produces tested character, and tested character produces hope.

**5** Hope does not put us to shame, because the love of God has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us.

**6** For while we were still weak, at the right time Christ died for the ungodly.

**7** One will scarcely die for a righteous person — though for a good person perhaps someone might even dare to die.

**8** But God shows His own love for us in this: while we were still sinners, Christ died for us.

**9** Much more, then, now that we have been justified by His blood, we will be saved through Him from the wrath.

**10** For if, while we were enemies, we were reconciled to God through the death of His Son, much more, now that we are reconciled, will we be saved by His life.

**11** Not only that, but we also boast in God through our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom we have now received the reconciliation.

## Adam and Christ

**12** Therefore, just as sin entered the world through one man, and death through sin, and so death spread to all people, because all sinned —

**13** for sin was in the world before the Law, but sin is not charged to anyone's account where there is no law.

**14** Yet death reigned from Adam to Moses, even over those whose sins were not like the transgression of Adam, who is a pattern of the one who was to come.

**15** But the free gift is not like the trespass. For if the many died through the trespass of the one, much more have the grace of God and the gift given by the grace of the one man Jesus Christ overflowed to the many.

**16** Nor is the gift like what came through the one who sinned. For the judgment that followed one trespass brought condemnation, but the free gift that followed many trespasses brought justification.

**17** For if, because of the trespass of the one, death reigned through that one, much more will those who receive the abundance of grace and of the gift of righteousness reign in life through the one, Jesus Christ.

**18** So then, as one trespass led to condemnation for all people, so one righteous act leads to justification and life for all people.

**19** For as through the disobedience of the one man the many were made sinners, so through the obedience of the one the many will be made righteous.

**20** The Law came in alongside, so that the trespass would increase; but where sin increased, grace overflowed all the more,

**21** so that, as sin reigned in death, grace also might reign through righteousness to eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.

## Notes

- **v12 "because all sinned"** — *eph' hō pantes hēmarton*. In Paul's other letters *eph' hō* means **because** or **for which** (2 Corinthians 5:4; Philippians 3:12; 4:10). The grammar also allows **in whom**, with *hō* pointing back to *the one man*: all sinned in Adam. On the first reading each sins and dies; on the second all sinned in the one. The sentence itself breaks off at the dash and does not finish its *just as*; v18 takes it up again: *So then, as one trespass…*
- **vv12–14 "through one man… from Adam to Moses… a pattern of the one who was to come"** — Genesis 2–3 read across the whole race: one man, one command, and death after it (*on the day you eat from it, dying you shall die*, Genesis 2:17). *Typos*, **pattern**, is the word for the *mark of the nails* (John 20:25), an impression struck into something: Adam is the first impression of Christ, and the chapter shows it by a pairing of *one* and *all*, *trespass* and *gift*, *death* and *life*.
