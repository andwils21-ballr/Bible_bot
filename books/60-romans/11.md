# Romans 11

## The Remnant

**1** I ask, then, has God rejected His people? By no means! I myself am an Israelite, of the seed of Abraham, of the tribe of Benjamin.

**2** God has not rejected His people, whom He foreknew. Do you not know what the Scripture says of Elijah, how he pleads with God against Israel?

**3** "Lord, they have killed Your prophets, they have torn down Your altars, and I alone am left, and they are seeking my life."

**4** But what was God's answer to him? "I have kept for Myself seven thousand men who have not bowed the knee to Baal."

**5** So too at the present time there is a remnant, chosen by grace.

**6** But if it is by grace, it is no longer by works; otherwise grace would no longer be grace.

**7** What then? What Israel was seeking, it did not obtain. Those who were chosen obtained it, but the rest were hardened,

**8** as it is written: "God gave them a spirit of stupor, eyes that would not see and ears that would not hear, down to this very day."

**9** David says:  
"Let their table become a snare and a trap,  
a stumbling block and a retribution for them;

**10** let their eyes be darkened so that they cannot see,  
and bend their backs forever."

## Jealousy and Fullness

**11** So I ask, did they stumble so as to fall? By no means! Rather, through their trespass salvation has come to the Gentiles, to make Israel jealous.

**12** Now if their trespass means riches for the world, and their failure means riches for the Gentiles, how much more will their fullness mean!

**13** I am speaking to you Gentiles. Since I am an apostle to the Gentiles, I make much of my ministry,

**14** in the hope that I may make my own people jealous and save some of them.

**15** For if their rejection means the reconciliation of the world, what will their acceptance mean but life from the dead?

**16** If the firstfruits are holy, so is the whole lump of dough; and if the root is holy, so are the branches.

## Grafted In

**17** But if some of the branches were broken off, and you, a wild olive shoot, were grafted in among them and came to share in the root and the richness of the olive tree,

**18** do not boast over the branches. If you do boast, remember that it is not you who support the root, but the root that supports you.

**19** You will say, "Branches were broken off so that I might be grafted in."

**20** That is true. They were broken off because of their unbelief, but you stand by faith. So do not be proud, but fear.

**21** For if God did not spare the natural branches, He will not spare you either.

**22** Look, then, at the kindness and the severity of God: severity toward those who fell, but God's kindness toward you, provided you continue in His kindness. Otherwise you too will be cut off.

**23** They too, if they do not continue in their unbelief, will be grafted in, for God is able to graft them in again.

**24** For if you were cut from what is by nature a wild olive tree, and grafted, contrary to nature, into a cultivated olive tree, how much more will these, the natural branches, be grafted back into their own olive tree.

## All Israel Will Be Saved

**25** I do not want you to be unaware of this mystery, brothers, so that you will not be wise in your own eyes: a hardening has come upon part of Israel until the fullness of the Gentiles has come in.

**26** In this way all Israel will be saved, as it is written:  
"The Deliverer will come from Zion;  
He will turn away ungodliness from Jacob.

**27** This will be My covenant with them,  
when I take away their sins."

**28** As regards the gospel, they are enemies for your sake; but as regards God's choice, they are loved for the sake of the fathers.

**29** For the gifts and the calling of God cannot be taken back.

**30** Just as you were once disobedient to God but now have received mercy because of their disobedience,

**31** so they too have now been disobedient, so that by the mercy shown to you they also may now receive mercy.

**32** For God has shut up all in disobedience, so that He may have mercy on all.

## The Depth of the Riches

**33** Oh, the depth of the riches and wisdom and knowledge of God! How unsearchable are His judgments, and His ways beyond tracing out!

**34** "For who has known the mind of the Lord,  
or who has been His counselor?"

**35** "Or who has first given to Him,  
that he should be repaid?"

**36** For from Him and through Him and to Him are all things. To Him be the glory forever. Amen.

## Notes

- **vv11, 14 "to make Israel jealous… that I may make my own people jealous"** — *parazēlōsai*, the verb of Moses' song just quoted, *I will make you jealous with what is no nation* (10:19; Deuteronomy 32:21). In the song it is God's answer to Israel's idols: *They made Me jealous with what is no god*. Paul takes the threat as the way back: the Gentiles' coming in is meant to draw Israel after them.
- **vv16–24 "the root… the branches… broken off… a wild olive shoot… grafted in… contrary to nature"** — Jeremiah's name for Israel, *A green olive tree, fair with goodly fruit: so the LORD named you*, and in the same verse *its branches are broken* (Jeremiah 11:16). *Para physin*, **contrary to nature**, is the phrase of 1:26: there it named the nations' sin; here it names God's grafting of the nations into Israel's tree. *Did not spare* (v21) is the verb of 8:32, *He who did not spare His own Son*.
- **vv25–26 "a hardening has come upon part of Israel until the fullness of the Gentiles has come in. In this way all Israel will be saved"** — *kai houtōs*, **in this way**, the manner of it, though some read it as time, **and then**. *All Israel* has been read as the people of Israel as a whole at the end; as the whole people of God, Jew and Gentile together (as 9:6, *not all who are descended from Israel are Israel*); or as every chosen Israelite through the ages. The quotation is Isaiah 59:20 with 27:9, and Paul's *from Zion* is his own: the Hebrew has *A Redeemer will come to Zion*, the Greek *for the sake of Zion*.
- **vv32–36 "that He may have mercy on all… who has first given to Him… from Him and through Him and to Him are all things"** — the argument of chapters 9–11 ends in mercy, the word of 9:15, *I will have mercy on whom I have mercy*, now *on all*. V34 is Isaiah 40:13 in the Greek (the Hebrew has *Who has marked out the Spirit of the LORD?*); v35 follows the Hebrew of Job 41:11, *Who has first given to Me, that I should repay him?* No one is God's creditor.
