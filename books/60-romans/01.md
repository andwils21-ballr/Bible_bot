# Romans 1

## Greeting

**1** Paul, a servant of Christ Jesus, called to be an apostle, set apart for the gospel of God,

**2** which He promised beforehand through His prophets in the holy Scriptures,

**3** concerning His Son, who was descended from the seed of David according to the flesh,

**4** who was appointed Son of God in power according to the Spirit of holiness by the resurrection of the dead: Jesus Christ our Lord.

**5** Through Him we have received grace and apostleship to bring about the obedience of faith among all the Gentiles, for the sake of His name,

**6** and you also are among them, called to belong to Jesus Christ.

**7** To all who are in Rome, loved by God, called to be saints: grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

## Paul's Wish to Come to Rome

**8** First, I thank my God through Jesus Christ for all of you, because your faith is being proclaimed in all the world.

**9** For God, whom I serve in my spirit in the gospel of His Son, is my witness how unceasingly I mention you,

**10** always in my prayers, asking that somehow, now at last, by the will of God, I may succeed in coming to you.

**11** For I long to see you, so that I may share with you some spiritual gift to strengthen you —

**12** that is, so that we may be encouraged together among you, each by the other's faith, both yours and mine.

**13** I do not want you to be unaware, brothers, that I have often planned to come to you, and have been prevented until now, so that I might have some fruit among you too, as I have among the rest of the Gentiles.

**14** I am a debtor both to Greeks and to non-Greeks, both to the wise and to the foolish.

**15** So, for my part, I am eager to preach the gospel to you also who are in Rome.

## The Power of God for Salvation

**16** For I am not ashamed of the gospel, because it is the power of God for salvation to everyone who believes, to the Jew first and also to the Greek.

**17** For in it the righteousness of God is revealed from faith to faith, as it is written: "The righteous will live by faith."

## The Wrath of God Revealed

**18** For the wrath of God is revealed from heaven against all the ungodliness and unrighteousness of people who suppress the truth by their unrighteousness.

**19** For what can be known about God is plain to them, because God has shown it to them.

**20** Ever since the creation of the world His invisible qualities, His eternal power and divine nature, have been clearly seen, understood through what He has made. So they are without excuse;

**21** for although they knew God, they did not glorify Him as God or give Him thanks. Instead, their thinking became empty, and their senseless heart was darkened.

**22** Claiming to be wise, they became fools,

**23** and exchanged the glory of the immortal God for an image made in the likeness of mortal man and of birds and four-footed animals and creeping things.

**24** So God handed them over, in the desires of their hearts, to impurity, to the dishonoring of their bodies among themselves.

**25** They exchanged the truth of God for the lie, and worshiped and served the creature rather than the Creator, who is blessed forever. Amen.

**26** For this reason God handed them over to dishonorable passions. Their women exchanged natural relations for what is contrary to nature,

**27** and the men likewise gave up natural relations with women and were inflamed with desire for one another, men with men committing what is shameful, and receiving in themselves the due penalty for their error.

**28** Since they did not think God worth acknowledging, God handed them over to a worthless mind, to do what ought not to be done.

**29** They are filled with every kind of unrighteousness, wickedness, greed and malice; full of envy, murder, strife, deceit and spite. They are gossips,

**30** slanderers, haters of God, insolent, arrogant, boastful, inventors of evil, disobedient to parents,

**31** senseless, faithless, heartless, ruthless.

**32** Though they know God's righteous decree, that those who do such things deserve death, they not only do them but also approve of those who practice them.

## Notes

- **vv3–4 "descended from the seed of David according to the flesh… appointed Son of God in power… by the resurrection of the dead"** — two lines set side by side: *according to the flesh*, *according to the Spirit of holiness*. *Horisthentos*, **appointed**, is the verb of Peter and Paul in Acts, *the one appointed by God to be judge* (Acts 10:42), *a man whom He has appointed… by raising Him from the dead* (17:31). Many English Bibles have **declared**. Either way He is already *His Son* in v3; what the resurrection marks is *Son of God in power*. *Ex anastaseōs nekrōn*, word for word **from a resurrection of dead ones**: Paul names Christ's rising as the resurrection of the dead, not only His own.
- **v17 "the righteousness of God is revealed from faith to faith"** — *dikaiosynē theou* can be God's own righteousness, His faithfulness to what He *promised beforehand* (v2), or the righteousness that comes from God to the one who believes. The letter goes on to use it both ways (3:5, 3:21–22). *Pistis* is both **faith** and **faithfulness**: *the faithfulness of God* in 3:3 is the same word. *Ek pisteōs eis pistin*, **from faith to faith**, has no verb, and the range is wide: from God's faithfulness to our faith, from one believer to the next, or faith from first to last.
- **v17 "The righteous will live by faith"** — Habakkuk 2:4. The Hebrew has *the righteous will live by his faithfulness*, the righteous man's own; the Greek has *ek pisteōs mou*, **by My faithfulness**, God's. Paul quotes neither pronoun, and his three words can be read **the righteous will live by faith** or **the one who is righteous by faith will live**. In Galatians 3:11–12 he sets it beside *the person who does them shall live by them* (Leviticus 18:5), where *by* goes with *live*.
- **vv21–23 "their thinking became empty… exchanged the glory of the immortal God for an image made in the likeness of"** — the words of Israel's own idolatry. *Emataiōthēsan*, **became empty**, is the Greek of Jeremiah 2:5, *went after emptiness and became empty*. *Ēllaxan tēn doxan… en homoiōmati* is Psalm 106:20 in the Greek, the golden calf: *They exchanged their Glory for the image of an ox that eats grass* (also Jeremiah 2:11). *Image* and *likeness* are the words of humankind's making (Genesis 1:26), and the list runs back through its creatures: man, birds, animals, creeping things. Paul tells the nations' fall in Israel's words, and in chapter 2 turns to the one who judges them.
- **vv24, 26, 28 "God handed them over"** — *paredōken autous*, three times. The *wrath of God… revealed* (v18) is shown here as God giving people over to what they chose. It is the verb of the gospel: Christ *was handed over for our trespasses* (4:25), and God *did not spare His own Son but handed Him over for us all* (8:32), *paredōken auton*, the same form.
