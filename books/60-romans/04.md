# Romans 4

## Reckoned as Righteousness

**1** What then shall we say that Abraham, our forefather according to the flesh, has found?

**2** For if Abraham was justified by works, he has something to boast about — but not before God.

**3** For what does the Scripture say? "Abraham believed God, and it was reckoned to him as righteousness."

**4** To one who works, his wages are not reckoned as a gift but as what is owed.

**5** But to one who does not work, but believes in Him who justifies the ungodly, his faith is reckoned as righteousness,

**6** just as David also speaks of the blessedness of the man to whom God reckons righteousness apart from works:

**7** "Blessed are those whose lawless deeds are forgiven,  
and whose sins are covered;

**8** blessed is the man whose sin the Lord will never reckon."

## Before He Was Circumcised

**9** Is this blessedness, then, for the circumcised only, or also for the uncircumcised? We say that faith was reckoned to Abraham as righteousness.

**10** How, then, was it reckoned? When he was circumcised, or when he was uncircumcised? Not when he was circumcised, but when he was uncircumcised.

**11** He received the sign of circumcision as a seal of the righteousness of the faith he had while he was uncircumcised, so that he would be the father of all who believe without being circumcised, so that righteousness would be reckoned to them also,

**12** and the father of the circumcised who are not only circumcised but who also walk in the footsteps of the faith that our father Abraham had while he was uncircumcised.

## The Promise Through Faith

**13** For the promise to Abraham and to his seed, that he would be heir of the world, did not come through the Law but through the righteousness of faith.

**14** For if those who are of the Law are heirs, faith is made empty and the promise is nullified.

**15** For the Law brings about wrath; but where there is no law, there is no transgression either.

**16** That is why it is by faith, so that it may be by grace, and the promise may be sure for all his seed — not only for those who are of the Law, but also for those who are of the faith of Abraham, who is the father of us all,

**17** as it is written: "I have made you the father of many nations." He is our father in the sight of the God in whom he believed, who gives life to the dead and calls into being what does not exist.

**18** Hoping against hope, he believed that he would become the father of many nations, according to what had been said: "So shall your seed be."

**19** He did not weaken in faith when he considered his own body, as good as dead, since he was about a hundred years old, and the deadness of Sarah's womb.

**20** No unbelief made him waver concerning the promise of God, but he grew strong in faith, giving glory to God,

**21** fully convinced that God was able to do what He had promised.

**22** That is why "it was reckoned to him as righteousness."

## Written for Us Also

**23** The words "it was reckoned to him" were not written for his sake alone,

**24** but for ours also. It will be reckoned to us who believe in Him who raised Jesus our Lord from the dead,

**25** who was handed over for our trespasses and raised for our justification.

## Notes

- **vv3, 22–24 "Abraham believed God, and it was reckoned to him as righteousness"** — Genesis 15:6 in the Greek; the Hebrew reads *He trusted in the LORD, and He reckoned it to him as righteousness*. *Logizomai*, **reckon**, is an accounting word, a sum entered to someone's account, and it is the word of this chapter: eleven times, wages *reckoned* as owed (v4), faith *reckoned* as righteousness (v5), sin the Lord *will never reckon* (v8, Psalm 32:2). Paul's last turn is that the sentence about Abraham *was not written for his sake alone*.
- **v5 "Him who justifies the ungodly"** — *ton dikaiounta ton asebē*, the very words the Greek Law forbids to a judge: *you shall not justify the ungodly for gifts* (Exodus 23:7, *I will not acquit the guilty* in the Hebrew), and *woe to those who justify the ungodly for a bribe* (Isaiah 5:23 in the Greek). *Whoever justifies the wicked… is detestable to the LORD* (Proverbs 17:15). What would be a crooked judge's sin, Paul says God does, and 3:26 has already said how He stays *righteous* in doing it.
