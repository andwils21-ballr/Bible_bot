# Reproof (Tegsat) 7

**1** The words of King Lemuel,  
the oracle that his mother taught him:

**2** What, my son? What, son of my womb?  
What, son of my vows?

**3** Do not give your strength to women,  
your ways to those who destroy kings.

**4** It is not for kings, Lemuel,  
it is not for kings to drink wine,  
nor for rulers to crave strong drink,

**5** or they will drink and forget what has been decreed  
and pervert the rights of all the afflicted.

**6** Give strong drink to the one who is perishing,  
and wine to those in bitter distress;

**7** let them drink and forget their poverty  
and remember their misery no more.

**8** Open your mouth for the mute,  
for the rights of all who are passing away.

**9** Open your mouth, judge righteously,  
and defend the rights of the poor and needy.

**10** A capable wife, who can find?  
Her worth is far above jewels.

**11** The heart of her husband trusts in her,  
and he will lack no gain.

**12** She does him good and not harm  
all the days of her life.

**13** She seeks wool and flax  
and works with willing hands.

**14** She is like the merchant ships;  
she brings her food from afar.

**15** She rises while it is still night  
and provides food for her household  
and portions for her young women.

**16** She considers a field and buys it;  
with the fruit of her hands she plants a vineyard.

**17** She girds her loins with strength  
and makes her arms strong.

**18** She perceives that her merchandise is profitable;  
her lamp does not go out at night.

**19** She puts her hands to the distaff,  
and her palms hold the spindle.

**20** She opens her hand to the poor  
and reaches out her hands to the needy.

**21** She is not afraid of snow for her household,  
for all her household are clothed in scarlet.

**22** She makes coverings for herself;  
her clothing is fine linen and purple.

**23** Her husband is known in the gates,  
when he sits among the elders of the land.

**24** She makes linen garments and sells them;  
she delivers sashes to the merchant.

**25** Strength and dignity are her clothing,  
and she laughs at the time to come.

**26** She opens her mouth with wisdom,  
and the teaching of loyal love is on her tongue.

**27** She watches over the ways of her household  
and does not eat the bread of idleness.

**28** Her children rise up and call her blessed;  
her husband also, and he praises her:

**29** "Many daughters have done capably,  
but you surpass them all."

**30** Charm is deceitful, and beauty is a breath,  
but a woman who fears the LORD is to be praised.

**31** Give her of the fruit of her hands,  
and let her works praise her in the gates.

## Notes

- **v1 "King Lemuel"** — otherwise unknown; or "Lemuel king of Massa," as in 6:1. The only section of the book spoken by a mother.
- **v2 "What, my son?"** — *bar*, the Aramaic word for "son," three times.
- **v4 "nor for rulers to crave strong drink"** — the read form *ei*, "where is"; the written form *o*, "or."
- **vv8–9 "Open your mouth for the mute"** — a king's duty to the voiceless (Psalm 72:12–14; Jeremiah 22:15–16).
- **vv10–31 "A capable wife"** — *eshet chayil*, as in Proverbs 12:4; an acrostic of twenty-two lines, one for each letter of the Hebrew alphabet. Wisdom, pictured as a woman from Proverbs 1 on, is seen at last in a household.
- **v16 "she plants a vineyard"** — the read form; the written form is a spelling variant.
- **v18 "her lamp does not go out at night"** — the read form *ballaylah*; the written form is a spelling variant.
- **v24 "to the merchant"** — *kena'ani*, "Canaanite," the traders of the coast (Zephaniah 1:11).
- **v26 "the teaching of loyal love"** — *torat chesed*, the parent's teaching of Proverbs 1:8, now in her mouth.
- **v30 "Charm is deceitful, and beauty is a breath"** — *hevel*; the book ends where it began, with the fear of the LORD (Proverbs 1:7).
