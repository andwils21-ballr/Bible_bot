# Reproof (Tegsat) 1

**1** These also are proverbs of Solomon,  
which the men of Hezekiah king of Judah copied.

**2** It is the glory of God to conceal a matter,  
but the glory of kings to search out a matter.

**3** As the heavens for height and the earth for depth,  
so the heart of kings is unsearchable.

**4** Take away the dross from the silver,  
and a vessel comes out for the smith;

**5** take away the wicked from before the king,  
and his throne will be established in righteousness.

**6** Do not put yourself forward in the king's presence  
or stand in the place of the great,

**7** for it is better to be told, "Come up here,"  
than to be put lower in the presence of a noble.  
What your eyes have seen

**8** do not bring hastily into court,  
or what will you do in the end,  
when your neighbor puts you to shame?

**9** Argue your case with your neighbor himself,  
and do not reveal another's secret,

**10** or he who hears it will bring shame upon you,  
and the ill report about you will not go away.

**11** Like apples of gold in settings of silver  
is a word spoken at the right time.

**12** Like a gold ring or an ornament of fine gold  
is a wise reprover to a listening ear.

**13** Like the cold of snow in the time of harvest  
is a faithful envoy to those who send him;  
he refreshes the soul of his masters.

**14** Like clouds and wind without rain  
is a man who boasts of a gift he does not give.

**15** With patience a ruler may be persuaded,  
and a soft tongue will break a bone.

**16** If you have found honey, eat only enough for you,  
or you will be filled with it and vomit it.

**17** Let your foot be seldom in your neighbor's house,  
or he will be filled with you and hate you.

**18** Like a club, a sword or a sharp arrow  
is a man who bears false witness against his neighbor.

**19** Like a broken tooth or a foot that slips  
is trust in a treacherous man in the day of trouble.

**20** Like one who takes off a garment on a cold day, like vinegar on soda,  
is one who sings songs to a heavy heart.

**21** If your enemy is hungry, give him bread to eat,  
and if he is thirsty, give him water to drink;

**22** for you will heap burning coals on his head,  
and the LORD will reward you.

**23** The north wind brings forth rain,  
and a secret tongue, angry looks.

**24** It is better to live in a corner of the housetop  
than in a house shared with a quarrelsome wife.

**25** Like cold water to a weary soul  
is good news from a distant land.

**26** Like a muddied spring or a polluted fountain  
is a righteous man who gives way before the wicked.

**27** It is not good to eat much honey,  
nor is it glory to search out one's own glory.

**28** Like a city broken into and left without walls  
is a man without self-control.

## Notes

- **The book** — in the Ethiopian Bible, Reproof (*Tegsat*) is a separate book made of what the Hebrew counts as Proverbs 25–31. Its chapters 1–7 are Proverbs 25–31, and its verse numbers are the same. It is rendered from the Hebrew, as Proverbs is, with the same words for wisdom (see Proverbs 1:2–6).
- **v1 "which the men of Hezekiah king of Judah copied"** — scribes of Hezekiah's court, about 700 BC, gathered a further collection of Solomon's sayings (2 Kings 18:1–7).
- **vv6–7 "it is better to be told, 'Come up here'"** — Jesus' parable of the wedding guest who takes the lowest place (Luke 14:8–11).
- **v10 "will bring shame upon you"** — *yechassedkha*, from *chesed* in its rare sense "reproach," as in Proverbs 14:34.
- **v13 "a faithful envoy"** — *tsir*, as in Proverbs 13:17.
- **v20 "like vinegar on soda"** — vinegar on *neter*, natron, fizzes and spoils it; cheerful songs do the same to a grieving heart.
- **vv21–22** — Paul quotes them from the Greek: *heap burning coals on his head… overcome evil with good* (Romans 12:20–21).
- **v24** — the same words as Proverbs 21:9, and the English there is followed; "quarrelsome" follows the read form *midyanim*.
- **v27 "nor is it glory to search out one's own glory"** — the Hebrew is terse and uncertain, "and searching out their glory, glory"; the line may mean that too much of a good thing, even honor, cloys.
