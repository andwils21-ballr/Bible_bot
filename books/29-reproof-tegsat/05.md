# Reproof (Tegsat) 5

**1** He who is often reproved, yet stiffens his neck,  
will suddenly be broken beyond healing.

**2** When the righteous increase, the people rejoice,  
but when the wicked rule, the people groan.

**3** A man who loves wisdom makes his father glad,  
but a companion of prostitutes squanders his wealth.

**4** By justice a king gives stability to the land,  
but a man who exacts gifts tears it down.

**5** A man who flatters his neighbor  
spreads a net for his feet.

**6** In the transgression of an evil man is a snare,  
but the righteous sings and rejoices.

**7** The righteous knows the cause of the poor;  
the wicked does not understand such knowledge.

**8** Scoffers set a city aflame,  
but the wise turn away wrath.

**9** If a wise man goes to law with a fool,  
the fool only rages and laughs, and there is no quiet.

**10** Men of blood hate the blameless,  
and the upright seek his life.

**11** A fool gives full vent to his spirit,  
but a wise man quietly holds it back.

**12** If a ruler listens to falsehood,  
all his officials will be wicked.

**13** The poor man and the oppressor meet together;  
the LORD gives light to the eyes of both.

**14** If a king judges the poor in truth,  
his throne will be established forever.

**15** The rod and reproof give wisdom,  
but a child left to himself brings shame to his mother.

**16** When the wicked increase, transgression increases,  
but the righteous will look on their downfall.

**17** Discipline your son, and he will give you rest;  
he will give delight to your soul.

**18** Where there is no vision, the people cast off restraint,  
but blessed is he who keeps the law.

**19** By mere words a servant is not disciplined,  
for though he understands, he will not respond.

**20** Do you see a man who is hasty in his words?  
There is more hope for a fool than for him.

**21** Whoever pampers his servant from childhood  
will in the end find him a son.

**22** An angry man stirs up strife,  
and a hot-tempered man abounds in transgression.

**23** A man's pride will bring him low,  
but he who is lowly in spirit will obtain honor.

**24** Whoever shares with a thief hates his own soul;  
he hears the curse but discloses nothing.

**25** The fear of man lays a snare,  
but whoever trusts in the LORD will be safe.

**26** Many seek the face of a ruler,  
but a man's justice comes from the LORD.

**27** An unjust man is detestable to the righteous,  
and one whose way is upright is detestable to the wicked.

## Notes

- **v1 "will suddenly be broken beyond healing"** — the words of Proverbs 6:15b.
- **v4 "a man who exacts gifts"** — *ish terumot*, a ruler who taxes heavily or takes bribes.
- **v10 "and the upright seek his life"** — the Hebrew is followed; "seek his life" here likely means the upright look after the blameless man. Many change the line to make "men of blood" the subject of both halves.
- **v13** — like Proverbs 22:2, with "the oppressor" for "the rich."
- **v18 "Where there is no vision, the people cast off restraint"** — *chazon*, prophetic revelation (1 Samuel 3:1); without God's word a people runs wild (Exodus 32:25, the same verb).
- **v20** — the second line is the same as 2:12b.
- **v21 "will in the end find him a son"** — *manon* occurs only here; "son," "heir," "grief" and "insolence" have all been proposed.
- **v24 "he hears the curse but discloses nothing"** — the curse laid on any witness who stays silent (Leviticus 5:1).
- **v25 "The fear of man lays a snare"** — as in Isaiah 51:12–13.
- **v27** — the last saying of the Hezekiah collection: the righteous and the wicked each find the other *to'evah*, "detestable."
