# Reproof (Tegsat) 3

**1** Do not boast about tomorrow,  
for you do not know what a day may bring forth.

**2** Let another praise you, and not your own mouth;  
a stranger, and not your own lips.

**3** A stone is heavy, and sand is weighty,  
but a fool's vexation is heavier than both.

**4** Wrath is cruel, anger is a flood,  
but who can stand before jealousy?

**5** Better is open rebuke  
than hidden love.

**6** Faithful are the wounds of a friend,  
but the kisses of an enemy are profuse.

**7** One who is full tramples on honey,  
but to one who is hungry every bitter thing is sweet.

**8** Like a bird that strays from its nest  
is a man who strays from his home.

**9** Oil and incense make the heart glad,  
and the sweetness of a friend comes from his earnest counsel.

**10** Do not forsake your friend and your father's friend,  
and do not go to your brother's house in the day of your calamity;  
better is a neighbor who is near than a brother who is far away.

**11** Be wise, my son, and make my heart glad,  
so that I may answer him who reproaches me.

**12** The shrewd sees evil and hides himself,  
but the simple go on and suffer for it.

**13** Take a man's garment when he has put up security for a stranger,  
and hold it in pledge when he puts it up for a foreign woman.

**14** Whoever blesses his neighbor with a loud voice, rising early in the morning,  
will be counted as cursing.

**15** A continual dripping on a day of steady rain  
and a quarrelsome wife are alike;

**16** whoever restrains her restrains the wind  
and grasps oil in his right hand.

**17** Iron sharpens iron,  
and one man sharpens another.

**18** Whoever tends a fig tree will eat its fruit,  
and whoever guards his master will be honored.

**19** As in water face reflects face,  
so the heart of man reflects the man.

**20** Sheol and Abaddon are never satisfied,  
and the eyes of man are never satisfied.

**21** The crucible is for silver, and the furnace for gold,  
and a man is tested by his praise.

**22** Though you crush a fool in a mortar with a pestle along with grain,  
his folly will not depart from him.

**23** Know well the condition of your flocks,  
and give attention to your herds,

**24** for riches do not last forever,  
nor does a crown endure to all generations.

**25** When the grass is gone and the new growth appears  
and the vegetation of the mountains is gathered,

**26** the lambs will provide your clothing,  
and the goats the price of a field;

**27** there will be enough goats' milk for your food,  
for the food of your household  
and life for your young women.

## Notes

- **v1 "Do not boast about tomorrow"** — as in James 4:13–16.
- **v6 "Faithful are the wounds of a friend"** — the honest friend's rebuke heals; the enemy's many kisses betray (Matthew 26:48–49).
- **v7 "One who is full tramples on honey"** — *tavus*, "treads underfoot"; "loathe" is kept in this rendering for *sheqets*.
- **v10 "your father's friend"** — the read form *re'a*; the written form is a spelling variant.
- **vv12, 13** — the same as Proverbs 22:3 and 20:16, and the English there is followed.
- **v15 "A continual dripping"** — as in Proverbs 19:13.
- **v19 "As in water face reflects face"** — a man sees himself in his own heart, or in the heart of another.
- **v20 "Sheol and Abaddon"** — as in Proverbs 15:11; "Abaddon" is the read form.
- **v21** — the first line is the same as Proverbs 17:3a.
- **vv23–27** — a short poem on care of the flock: wealth and crowns pass, but the herd tended year by year feeds the house.
