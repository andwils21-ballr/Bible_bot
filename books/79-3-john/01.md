# 3 John 1

**1** The elder, to Gaius the beloved, whom I love in truth.

**2** Beloved, I pray that in all things you prosper and are in good health, just as your soul prospers.

**3** For I rejoiced greatly when brothers came and testified to your truth, as indeed you walk in truth.

**4** I have no greater joy than this, to hear that my children are walking in the truth.

## Gaius and the Travelers

**5** Beloved, you do a faithful work in whatever you do for the brothers, strangers though they are.

**6** They have testified to your love before the church. You will do well to send them on their way in a manner worthy of God;

**7** for they went out for the sake of the Name, taking nothing from the Gentiles.

**8** So we ought to support such people, so that we may become fellow workers with the truth.

## Diotrephes and Demetrius

**9** I wrote something to the church; but Diotrephes, who loves to be first among them, does not receive us.

**10** So if I come, I will bring up the works he is doing, talking nonsense against us with wicked words. Not content with that, he himself does not receive the brothers, and he stops those who want to, and throws them out of the church.

**11** Beloved, do not imitate what is evil, but what is good. Whoever does good is from God; whoever does evil has not seen God.

**12** Demetrius has a good testimony from everyone, and from the truth itself. We also testify for him, and you know that our testimony is true.

**13** I had many things to write to you, but I do not want to write them to you with ink and pen.

**14** I hope to see you soon, and we will speak face to face. Peace to you. The friends greet you. Greet the friends by name.

---

## Notes

- **v8 "fellow workers with the truth"** — *synergoi tē alētheia*. The dative can mean working *for* the truth, in its service, or working *with* it, the truth itself at work alongside those who take the travelers in. In v12 the truth is a witness that speaks for Demetrius, *from the truth itself*.
- **v14** — the English numbering ends here; the Greek editions number *Peace to you…* as v15.
