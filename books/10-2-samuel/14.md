# 2 Samuel 14

## Joab's Plan

**1** Joab son of Zeruiah knew that the king's heart was on Absalom.

**2** So Joab sent to Tekoa and brought from there a wise woman. He said to her, "Pretend to be a mourner. Put on mourning clothes and do not anoint yourself with oil, but be like a woman who has been mourning many days for the dead.

**3** Then go to the king and speak to him in this way." So Joab put the words in her mouth.

## The Woman of Tekoa

**4** When the woman of Tekoa spoke to the king, she fell on her face to the ground, paid homage and said, "Save me, O king!"

**5** The king said to her, "What is troubling you?" She said, "I am truly a widow; my husband is dead.

**6** Your servant had two sons, and the two of them quarreled in the field. There was no one to part them, and one struck the other and killed him.

**7** Now the whole clan has risen against your servant, and they say, 'Hand over the one who struck his brother, so that we may put him to death for the life of his brother whom he killed, and destroy the heir as well.' So they would quench my coal that is left, and leave my husband neither name nor remnant on the face of the earth."

**8** The king said to the woman, "Go to your house, and I will give orders concerning you."

**9** The woman of Tekoa said to the king, "My lord the king, let the guilt be on me and on my father's house, and let the king and his throne be guiltless."

**10** The king said, "If anyone speaks to you, bring him to me, and he shall never touch you again."

**11** She said, "Please let the king remember the LORD your God, so that the avenger of blood may not destroy any more, and my son not be destroyed." He said, "As the LORD lives, not one hair of your son shall fall to the ground."

**12** Then the woman said, "Please let your servant speak a word to my lord the king." He said, "Speak."

**13** The woman said, "Why then have you planned such a thing against the people of God? In speaking this word the king is like one who is guilty, since the king does not bring back his own banished one.

**14** We must all die; we are like water spilled on the ground, which cannot be gathered up again. But God does not take away a life; He devises ways so that the banished one is not cast out from Him.

**15** Now I have come to speak this word to my lord the king because the people have made me afraid. Your servant thought, 'I will speak to the king; perhaps the king will do what his servant asks.

**16** For the king will hear, and deliver his servant from the hand of the man who would destroy me and my son together from the inheritance of God.'

**17** Your servant thought, 'Let the word of my lord the king bring rest,' for my lord the king is like the messenger of God, to discern good and evil. The LORD your God be with you!"

**18** Then the king answered the woman, "Do not hide from me anything I ask you." The woman said, "Let my lord the king speak."

**19** The king said, "Is the hand of Joab with you in all this?" The woman answered, "As your soul lives, my lord the king, no one can turn to the right or to the left from anything my lord the king has said. It was your servant Joab who commanded me; it was he who put all these words in your servant's mouth.

**20** Your servant Joab did this to change the face of the matter. But my lord is wise, with the wisdom of the messenger of God, to know everything that is on the earth."

**21** Then the king said to Joab, "I have done this thing. Go, bring back the young man Absalom."

**22** Joab fell on his face to the ground, paid homage and blessed the king. Joab said, "Today your servant knows that I have found favor in your eyes, my lord the king, because the king has done what your servant asked."

**23** So Joab got up, went to Geshur and brought Absalom to Jerusalem.

**24** But the king said, "Let him turn aside to his own house; he shall not see my face." So Absalom turned aside to his own house and did not see the king's face.

## Absalom

**25** In all Israel there was no man so much to be praised for his beauty as Absalom. From the sole of his foot to the crown of his head there was no blemish in him.

**26** When he cut the hair of his head — he cut it at the end of every year, because it was heavy on him — he weighed the hair of his head at two hundred shekels by the king's weight.

**27** Three sons were born to Absalom, and one daughter whose name was Tamar. She was a beautiful woman.

**28** Absalom lived two full years in Jerusalem without seeing the king's face.

**29** Then Absalom sent for Joab, to send him to the king, but he would not come to him. He sent again a second time, but he would not come.

**30** So he said to his servants, "See, Joab's field is next to mine, and he has barley there. Go and set it on fire." So Absalom's servants set the field on fire.

**31** Then Joab got up and came to Absalom at his house and said to him, "Why have your servants set my field on fire?"

**32** Absalom said to Joab, "I sent word to you: 'Come here, so that I may send you to the king to say, "Why have I come from Geshur? It would be better for me if I were still there."' Now let me see the king's face, and if there is guilt in me, let him put me to death."

**33** So Joab went to the king and told him. He called Absalom, and he came to the king and paid homage to him with his face to the ground before the king. The king kissed Absalom.

## Notes

- **v2 "a wise woman"** — *ishah chakhamah*. Another wise woman will speak for her town at Abel (20:16). *Va-yasem Yo'av et ha-devarim be-fiha*, **Joab put the words in her mouth** (v3), the words the LORD used to Moses of Aaron, *put the words in his mouth* (Exodus 4:15).
- **v7 "they would quench my coal that is left"** — *gachalti*, the one live coal kept in the ashes to light the next fire. With her last son dead, her husband's name goes out.
- **v11 "the avenger of blood"** — *go'el ha-dam*, the kinsman whose duty was to put a killer to death (Numbers 35:19). *Not one hair of your son shall fall to the ground*: the king has bound himself by oath before he knows whose son it is.
- **v13 "since the king does not bring back his own banished one"** — *nidcho*. The woman's story was a parable, as Nathan's was (12:1–7): the king has passed judgment, and the judgment applies to himself.
- **v14 "We must all die… like water spilled on the ground… God does not take away a life"** — *ve-lo yissa Elohim nefesh*. The Greek has no negative: *God will take a life*. The text keeps the Hebrew.
- **v17 "Let the word of my lord the king bring rest"** — *li-menuchah*, **for rest**. The Greek has *for sacrifices*, reading *minchah*, **offering**, for *menuchah*. The text keeps the Hebrew.
- **vv17, 20 "like the messenger of God, to discern good and evil… to know everything that is on the earth"** — *ke-mal'akh ha-Elohim*. Achish said David was *as good in my eyes as a messenger of God* (1 Samuel 29:9).
- **v25 "there was no blemish in him"** — *mum*, the word for a defect that bars an animal from the altar (Leviticus 22:20). Saul too was praised for his looks (1 Samuel 9:2).
- **v27 "Three sons… and one daughter whose name was Tamar"** — named after his sister. Later Absalom will say *I have no son to keep my name in remembrance* (18:18). The Greek adds that this Tamar became the wife of Rehoboam son of Solomon and bore him *Abiathar*; the text stays with the Hebrew.
- **v30 "set it on fire"** — the written text has *ve-hotsitiha*, **and I will set it on fire**; the text follows the read form, *ve-hatsituha*, **set it on fire**. The Greek adds that Joab's servants came to him with their clothes torn and told him. The text stays with the Hebrew.
