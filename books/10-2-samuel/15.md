# 2 Samuel 15

## Absalom Steals the Hearts of Israel

**1** And it came to pass after this that Absalom got himself a chariot and horses, and fifty men to run before him.

**2** Absalom would rise early and stand beside the road to the gate. Whenever any man had a dispute to bring before the king for judgment, Absalom would call to him and say, "What town are you from?" When he said, "Your servant is from one of the tribes of Israel,"

**3** Absalom would say to him, "See, your claims are good and right, but there is no one from the king to hear you."

**4** Then Absalom would say, "If only I were made judge in the land! Then every man who had a dispute or a case could come to me, and I would give him justice."

**5** And it would come to pass, when a man came near to pay homage to him, that he would put out his hand, take hold of him and kiss him.

**6** Absalom did this to all Israel who came to the king for judgment. So Absalom stole the hearts of the men of Israel.

## The Conspiracy at Hebron

**7** And it came to pass at the end of forty years that Absalom said to the king, "Please let me go to Hebron and pay the vow I made to the LORD.

**8** For your servant made a vow while I lived at Geshur in Aram: 'If the LORD will indeed bring me back to Jerusalem, I will serve the LORD.'"

**9** The king said to him, "Go in peace." So he got up and went to Hebron.

**10** Then Absalom sent secret messengers throughout all the tribes of Israel, saying, "As soon as you hear the sound of the shofar, say, 'Absalom has become king in Hebron!'"

**11** Two hundred men from Jerusalem went with Absalom. They had been invited and went in their innocence, knowing nothing of the matter.

**12** While he was offering the sacrifices, Absalom sent for Ahithophel the Gilonite, David's counselor, from his town, Giloh. The conspiracy grew strong, and the people with Absalom kept increasing.

## David Leaves Jerusalem

**13** A messenger came to David and said, "The hearts of the men of Israel have gone after Absalom."

**14** David said to all his servants who were with him in Jerusalem, "Get up, let us flee, or none of us will escape from Absalom. Go quickly, or he will quickly overtake us, bring disaster down on us and strike the city with the sword."

**15** The king's servants said to the king, "Whatever my lord the king chooses, your servants are ready."

**16** So the king went out, and all his household after him. The king left ten women, secondary wives, to keep the house.

**17** The king went out, and all the people after him, and they stopped at the last house.

**18** All his servants passed on beside him, and all the Cherethites and all the Pelethites, and all the Gittites, six hundred men who had come after him from Gath, passed on before the king.

**19** Then the king said to Ittai the Gittite, "Why are you also going with us? Go back and stay with the king, for you are a foreigner, and an exile from your own place as well.

**20** You came only yesterday; should I today make you wander about with us, when I go wherever I may go? Go back, and take your brothers with you. Loyal love and faithfulness be with you."

**21** But Ittai answered the king, "As the LORD lives, and as my lord the king lives, wherever my lord the king may be, whether for death or for life, there your servant will be."

**22** David said to Ittai, "Go then, pass on." So Ittai the Gittite passed on, with all his men and all the little ones who were with him.

**23** The whole land was weeping aloud as all the people passed by. The king crossed the Kidron Valley, and all the people passed on toward the wilderness.

## The Ark Sent Back

**24** Zadok came too, and all the Levites with him, carrying the ark of the covenant of God. They set down the ark of God, and Abiathar went up, until all the people had finished passing out of the city.

**25** Then the king said to Zadok, "Take the ark of God back into the city. If I find favor in the eyes of the LORD, He will bring me back and let me see both it and His dwelling.

**26** But if He says, 'I have no delight in you,' here I am; let Him do to me what is good in His eyes."

**27** The king also said to Zadok the priest, "Are you not a seer? Go back to the city in peace, and your two sons with you, Ahimaaz your son and Jonathan son of Abiathar.

**28** See, I will wait in the plains of the wilderness until word comes from you to inform me."

**29** So Zadok and Abiathar took the ark of God back to Jerusalem, and they stayed there.

## The Mount of Olives

**30** David went up the ascent of the Mount of Olives, weeping as he went, with his head covered and walking barefoot. All the people who were with him covered their heads, and they went up, weeping as they went.

**31** David was told, "Ahithophel is among the conspirators with Absalom." David said, "O LORD, please turn the counsel of Ahithophel into foolishness."

**32** And it came to pass, as David came to the summit, where God was worshiped, that Hushai the Archite came to meet him with his coat torn and earth on his head.

**33** David said to him, "If you pass on with me, you will be a burden to me.

**34** But if you return to the city and say to Absalom, 'I will be your servant, O king; as I was your father's servant in the past, so now I will be your servant,' then you can defeat the counsel of Ahithophel for me.

**35** Will not Zadok and Abiathar the priests be there with you? Whatever you hear from the king's house, tell it to Zadok and Abiathar the priests.

**36** Their two sons are there with them, Ahimaaz son of Zadok and Jonathan son of Abiathar. By them you shall send me everything you hear."

**37** So Hushai, David's friend, came into the city, just as Absalom was coming into Jerusalem.

## Notes

- **v6 "So Absalom stole the hearts of the men of Israel"** — *va-yegannev… et lev*, **stole the heart**, the words Jacob used when he slipped away from Laban, *Jacob stole the heart of Laban* (Genesis 31:20). Here it is winning loyalty by deceit.
- **v7 "at the end of forty years"** — the Hebrew and the Greek both have *forty*. David reigned forty years in all (5:4), and this is not the end of his reign; the text does not say from what point the forty are counted.
- **v10 "secret messengers"** — *meraggelim*, the word for **spies**. *Absalom has become king in Hebron*: Hebron is where David was first anointed king (2:4) and reigned seven years.
- **v12 "Ahithophel the Gilonite, David's counselor"** — his son was *Eliam son of Ahithophel the Gilonite*, one of David's mighty men (23:34), and Bathsheba was *daughter of Eliam* (11:3). The text does not say that the two Eliams are the same man.
- **vv19–21 "you are a foreigner… whether for death or for life, there your servant will be"** — *nokhri*, **a foreigner**, the word Ruth used of herself (Ruth 2:10). Ittai of Gath answers as Ruth answered Naomi, *Where you die, I will die* (Ruth 1:17).
- **vv25–26 "He will bring me back… let Him do to me what is good in His eyes"** — *hineni*, **here I am**. The words of Eli when the judgment against his house was told, *Let Him do what is good in His eyes* (1 Samuel 3:18).
- **v27 "Are you not a seer?"** — *ha-ro'eh attah*, the old word for a prophet (1 Samuel 9:9). The Greek has *See*, reading the word as a command. The sense of the Hebrew question is uncertain.
- **v28 "in the plains of the wilderness"** — the written text has *be-avrot*, **at the fords**; the read form is *be-arvot*, **in the plains**, and the text follows it. The same place is named in 17:16, where the Hebrew has *be-arvot* written.
- **v31 "turn the counsel of Ahithophel into foolishness"** — *sakkel*, from the word of Samuel to Saul, *You have acted foolishly* (1 Samuel 13:13). The prayer is answered in 17:14: *the LORD had ordained to defeat the good counsel of Ahithophel*.
- **v37 "Hushai, David's friend"** — *re'eh David*, the word of the court title *re'eh ha-melekh*, **the king's friend**, in Solomon's list of officials (1 Kings 4:5).
