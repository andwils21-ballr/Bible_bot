# 2 Samuel 1

## The Amalekite's Report

**1** And it came to pass after the death of Saul, when David had returned from striking the Amalekites, that David stayed two days in Ziklag.

**2** And it came to pass on the third day that a man came from Saul's camp with his clothes torn and dirt on his head. When he came to David, he fell to the ground and paid homage.

**3** David said to him, "Where have you come from?" He said to him, "I have escaped from the camp of Israel."

**4** David said to him, "What happened? Tell me." He said, "The people fled from the battle, and many of the people also fell and died, and Saul and Jonathan his son are also dead."

**5** David said to the young man who was telling him, "How do you know that Saul and Jonathan his son are dead?"

**6** The young man who was telling him said, "I happened to be on Mount Gilboa, and there was Saul, leaning on his spear, and the chariots and the horsemen were closing in on him.

**7** He turned around and saw me, and called to me, and I said, 'Here I am.'

**8** He said to me, 'Who are you?' I said to him, 'I am an Amalekite.'

**9** He said to me, 'Stand over me and kill me, for agony has seized me, and yet my life is still whole in me.'

**10** So I stood over him and killed him, for I knew that he could not live after he had fallen. I took the crown that was on his head and the band that was on his arm, and I have brought them here to my lord."

**11** Then David took hold of his clothes and tore them, and so did all the men who were with him.

**12** They mourned and wept and fasted until evening for Saul and for Jonathan his son, and for the people of the LORD and for the house of Israel, because they had fallen by the sword.

**13** David said to the young man who had told him, "Where are you from?" He said, "I am the son of a guest, an Amalekite."

**14** David said to him, "How were you not afraid to put out your hand to destroy the LORD's anointed?"

**15** Then David called one of the young men and said, "Go, strike him down." He struck him, and he died.

**16** David said to him, "Your blood is on your own head, for your own mouth has testified against you, saying, 'I have killed the LORD's anointed.'"

## The Song of the Bow

**17** David sang this lament over Saul and over Jonathan his son,

**18** and he said it should be taught to the sons of Judah: *The Bow*. It is written in the Book of Jashar:

**19** "Your glory, O Israel, lies slain on your high places.  
How the mighty have fallen!

**20** Tell it not in Gath,  
proclaim it not in the streets of Ashkelon,  
or the daughters of the Philistines will rejoice,  
or the daughters of the uncircumcised will exult.

**21** Mountains of Gilboa,  
let there be no dew or rain on you,  
nor fields that yield offerings!  
For there the shield of the mighty was defiled,  
the shield of Saul, no longer anointed with oil.

**22** From the blood of the slain,  
from the fat of the mighty,  
the bow of Jonathan did not turn back,  
and the sword of Saul did not return empty.

**23** Saul and Jonathan, beloved and lovely in their life,  
and in their death they were not divided.  
They were swifter than eagles,  
they were stronger than lions.

**24** Daughters of Israel, weep over Saul,  
who clothed you in scarlet and fine things,  
who put ornaments of gold on your clothing.

**25** How the mighty have fallen  
in the middle of the battle!  
Jonathan lies slain on your high places.

**26** I am distressed for you, my brother Jonathan;  
you were very dear to me.  
Your love to me was wonderful,  
more than the love of women.

**27** How the mighty have fallen,  
and the weapons of war have perished!"

## Notes

- **v1 "And it came to pass after the death of Saul"** — the book opens as Joshua opens, *after the death of Moses* (Joshua 1:1), and Judges, *after the death of Joshua* (Judges 1:1). It continues 1 Samuel without a break.
- **vv6–10 "Saul, leaning on his spear… So I stood over him and killed him"** — the account at Gilboa is different: Saul *took the sword and fell on it*, and his armor-bearer saw that he was dead (1 Samuel 31:4–5). There the archers found him; here it is chariots and horsemen. The Amalekite brings the crown and claims the death, and the claim kills him.
- **v9 "for agony has seized me"** — *ha-shavats*, a word found only here in the Bible; its meaning is not certain. The Greek has *a terrible darkness*.
- **v13 "I am the son of a guest, an Amalekite"** — *ger*, a foreigner living under Israel's protection and so under its law. He comes from the people David has just struck (v1; 1 Samuel 30:17), and the people Saul was sent to destroy (1 Samuel 15:3).
- **vv14, 16 "How were you not afraid to put out your hand to destroy the LORD's anointed?"** — what David twice refused to do: *who can put out his hand against the LORD's anointed and be guiltless?* (1 Samuel 26:9; also 24:6). *Your own mouth has testified against you*: the man is judged by his own story.
- **v18 "The Bow… the Book of Jashar"** — *qeshet*, **bow**, standing alone; the song is known by the weapon of Jonathan (v22). The Greek lacks the word. *The Book of Jashar*, *sefer ha-yashar*, **the Book of the Upright**, a lost collection of songs, is quoted also at Joshua 10:13.
- **vv19, 25 "Your glory, O Israel… on your high places"** — *ha-tsevi*, which is also **the gazelle**, the swift animal of the hills. The line can be heard both ways. The Greek has *Set up a pillar, O Israel*.
- **v21 "the shield of Saul, no longer anointed with oil"** — *beli mashiach ba-shamen*, **without anointing with oil**. A shield was oiled for battle, *Rise up, you commanders, anoint the shield!* (Isaiah 21:5); *mashiach* is also the word of *the LORD's anointed* (v14). The line holds both: the shield lies unoiled, and the anointed king is gone.
- **v23 "in their death they were not divided"** — Jonathan chose David's cause (1 Samuel 20, 23:17) and died at his father's side (1 Samuel 31:2).
- **v26 "Your love to me was wonderful"** — *ahavatkha*, the word of 1 Samuel 18:1–3, *Jonathan loved him as his own soul*. *Nifle'atah*, **wonderful**, from *pele*, what goes beyond the ordinary.
