# 2 Samuel 2

## David King in Hebron

**1** And it came to pass after this that David inquired of the LORD, "Shall I go up into one of the towns of Judah?" The LORD said to him, "Go up." David said, "Where shall I go up?" He said, "To Hebron."

**2** So David went up there, with his two wives, Ahinoam of Jezreel and Abigail the widow of Nabal of Carmel.

**3** David brought up the men who were with him, each with his household, and they settled in the towns of Hebron.

**4** Then the men of Judah came, and there they anointed David king over the house of Judah. They told David, "It was the men of Jabesh-gilead who buried Saul."

**5** David sent messengers to the men of Jabesh-gilead and said to them, "Blessed are you by the LORD, because you showed this loyal love to your lord Saul and buried him.

**6** Now may the LORD show loyal love and faithfulness to you. I too will do you good because you have done this.

**7** Now then, let your hands be strong, and be men of valor, for your lord Saul is dead, and the house of Judah has anointed me king over them."

## Ish-bosheth King over Israel

**8** But Abner son of Ner, the commander of Saul's army, took Ish-bosheth son of Saul and brought him over to Mahanaim.

**9** He made him king over Gilead, the Ashurites and Jezreel, over Ephraim and Benjamin, and over all Israel.

**10** Ish-bosheth son of Saul was forty years old when he began to reign over Israel, and he reigned two years. But the house of Judah followed David.

**11** The time that David was king in Hebron over the house of Judah was seven years and six months.

## The Pool of Gibeon

**12** Abner son of Ner and the servants of Ish-bosheth son of Saul went out from Mahanaim to Gibeon.

**13** Joab son of Zeruiah and the servants of David also went out, and they met them at the pool of Gibeon. They sat down, the one side on one side of the pool and the other on the other side of the pool.

**14** Abner said to Joab, "Let the young men arise and fight a contest before us." Joab said, "Let them arise."

**15** So they arose and went over by number: twelve for Benjamin and for Ish-bosheth son of Saul, and twelve of the servants of David.

**16** Each caught his opponent by the head and thrust his sword into his opponent's side, and they fell down together. So that place was called Helkath-hazzurim, which is in Gibeon.

**17** The battle was very hard that day, and Abner and the men of Israel were beaten before the servants of David.

## Asahel

**18** The three sons of Zeruiah were there, Joab, Abishai and Asahel. Asahel was as swift of foot as a gazelle in the field.

**19** Asahel pursued Abner, and he did not turn to the right or to the left from following Abner.

**20** Abner looked behind him and said, "Is that you, Asahel?" He said, "It is."

**21** Abner said to him, "Turn aside to your right or to your left, seize one of the young men and take his gear for yourself." But Asahel would not turn aside from following him.

**22** Abner said again to Asahel, "Turn aside from following me. Why should I strike you to the ground? How then could I lift up my face to Joab your brother?"

**23** But he refused to turn aside. So Abner struck him in the belly with the butt of his spear, and the spear came out at his back. He fell there and died on the spot. All who came to the place where Asahel had fallen and died stood still.

**24** But Joab and Abishai pursued Abner. As the sun was going down, they came to the hill of Ammah, which lies before Giah on the way to the wilderness of Gibeon.

**25** The sons of Benjamin gathered behind Abner and became one band, and they took their stand on the top of a hill.

**26** Then Abner called to Joab, "Shall the sword devour forever? Do you not know that the end will be bitter? How long will it be before you tell the people to turn back from pursuing their brothers?"

**27** Joab said, "As God lives, if you had not spoken, the people would have gone on pursuing their brothers until morning."

**28** So Joab blew the horn, and all the people stopped. They no longer pursued Israel, and they fought no more.

**29** Abner and his men went all that night through the Arabah. They crossed the Jordan, went through the whole Bithron, and came to Mahanaim.

**30** Joab came back from pursuing Abner and gathered all the people. Of David's servants nineteen men were missing, besides Asahel.

**31** But the servants of David had struck down of Benjamin and of Abner's men three hundred and sixty men, who died.

**32** They took up Asahel and buried him in his father's tomb at Bethlehem. Joab and his men marched all night, and day broke on them at Hebron.

## Notes

- **v1 "David inquired of the LORD… To Hebron"** — as he did before every move in the wilderness (1 Samuel 23:2, 30:8). Hebron was the town of Abraham's burial place (Genesis 23:19) and the portion of Caleb (Joshua 14:13), in the heart of Judah.
- **vv4–7 "the men of Jabesh-gilead who buried Saul… this loyal love"** — *chesed*. David's first act as king is to thank the town that honored Saul (1 Samuel 31:11–13), and to let it know that Judah now has a king.
- **vv8–10 "Ish-bosheth son of Saul"** — *Ish-boshet*, **man of shame**. 1 Chronicles names Saul's son *Eshbaal*, **man of Baal** (1 Chronicles 8:33, 9:39). The Hebrew of Samuel has *boshet*, **shame**, in the place of the name *Baal*, as it has *Jerubbesheth* for Jerubbaal (11:21). The Greek has *Iebosthe*.
- **v10 "he reigned two years… seven years and six months"** — the two reigns do not match in length. The text gives both figures and does not reconcile them.
- **vv14–16 "Let the young men arise and fight a contest"** — *vi-sachaqu*, **let them play**, the word for sport and entertainment. The contest ends with all twenty-four dead. *Helkath-hazzurim* is **the field of flints** or **of blades**; the Greek has *the Portion of the Plotters*.
- **v18 "as swift of foot as a gazelle in the field"** — *tsevi*, the word of David's lament, *Your glory*, or gazelle, *O Israel* (1:19).
- **vv21–23 "Why should I strike you to the ground?… the butt of his spear"** — Abner tries twice to turn Asahel away and does not want the blood feud with Joab that comes of it (3:27). He kills him with the back end of the spear, not the point. *Ha-chomesh*, **the belly**, is where Joab strikes Abner in return (3:27).
- **v26 "Shall the sword devour forever?"** — *la-netsach*, **forever**. Abner calls the men of Judah *their brothers*: the war is between tribes of one people.
- **v29 "the whole Bithron"** — *ha-bitron*, from *batar*, **to cut in two**: a ravine or cleft, or a region so named. What exactly it was is not known.
