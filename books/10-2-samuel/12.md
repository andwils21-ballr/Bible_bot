# 2 Samuel 12

## The Poor Man's Ewe Lamb

**1** The LORD sent Nathan to David. He came to him and said to him, "There were two men in one city, one rich and the other poor.

**2** The rich man had very many flocks and herds,

**3** but the poor man had nothing except one little ewe lamb, which he had bought. He raised it, and it grew up with him and with his children. It would eat from his morsel and drink from his cup and lie in his bosom, and it was like a daughter to him.

**4** A traveler came to the rich man, and he could not bear to take from his own flock or his own herd to prepare for the wayfarer who had come to him. So he took the poor man's ewe lamb and prepared it for the man who had come to him."

**5** David's anger burned hot against the man, and he said to Nathan, "As the LORD lives, the man who has done this deserves to die!

**6** He shall repay the ewe lamb fourfold, because he did this thing and because he had no pity."

## "You Are the Man"

**7** Nathan said to David, "You are the man! Thus says the LORD, the God of Israel: 'I anointed you king over Israel, and I delivered you from the hand of Saul.

**8** I gave you your master's house and your master's wives into your bosom, and I gave you the house of Israel and of Judah. If that had been too little, I would have added to you as much again and as much again.

**9** Why have you despised the word of the LORD, to do what is evil in My eyes? Uriah the Hittite you have struck down with the sword, and his wife you have taken to be your wife; him you have killed with the sword of the sons of Ammon.

**10** Now therefore the sword shall never depart from your house, because you have despised Me and have taken the wife of Uriah the Hittite to be your wife.'

**11** Thus says the LORD: 'I am about to raise up evil against you out of your own house. I will take your wives before your eyes and give them to your neighbor, and he shall lie with your wives before the eyes of this sun.

**12** For you did it in secret, but I will do this thing before all Israel and before the sun.'"

**13** David said to Nathan, "I have sinned against the LORD." Nathan said to David, "The LORD also has put away your sin; you shall not die.

**14** However, because by this deed you have utterly scorned the enemies of the LORD, the son who is born to you shall surely die."

## The Child Dies

**15** Then Nathan went to his house. The LORD struck the child that Uriah's wife had borne to David, and he became very sick.

**16** David sought God for the boy. David fasted, and he went in and lay all night on the ground.

**17** The elders of his house stood beside him to raise him from the ground, but he would not, and he did not eat bread with them.

**18** And it came to pass on the seventh day that the child died. David's servants were afraid to tell him that the child was dead, for they said, "While the child was still alive, we spoke to him, and he would not listen to our voice. How can we say to him, 'The child is dead'? He may do something terrible."

**19** When David saw that his servants were whispering together, David understood that the child was dead. David said to his servants, "Is the child dead?" They said, "He is dead."

**20** Then David got up from the ground, washed, anointed himself and changed his clothes. He went into the house of the LORD and worshiped. Then he went to his own house, and when he asked, they set bread before him, and he ate.

**21** His servants said to him, "What is this thing you have done? While the child was alive, you fasted and wept, but when the child died, you got up and ate bread."

**22** He said, "While the child was still alive, I fasted and wept, for I said, 'Who knows? The LORD may be gracious to me, and the child may live.'

**23** But now he is dead. Why should I fast? Can I bring him back again? I will go to him, but he will not return to me."

## Solomon

**24** David comforted Bathsheba his wife, and he went in to her and lay with her. She bore a son, and she called his name Solomon. The LORD loved him

**25** and sent word by the hand of Nathan the prophet, and he called his name Jedidiah, because of the LORD.

## The Capture of Rabbah

**26** Joab fought against Rabbah of the sons of Ammon and captured the royal city.

**27** Joab sent messengers to David and said, "I have fought against Rabbah; I have also captured the city of waters.

**28** So now gather the rest of the people, camp against the city and capture it, or I will capture the city myself, and it will be called by my name."

**29** So David gathered all the people and went to Rabbah, fought against it and captured it.

**30** He took the crown of their king from his head. Its weight was a talent of gold, with a precious stone, and it was placed on David's head. He brought out the spoil of the city, a very great amount.

**31** He brought out the people who were in it and set them to the saws and the iron picks and the iron axes, and made them pass through the brick kiln. So he did to all the cities of the sons of Ammon. Then David and all the people returned to Jerusalem.

## Notes

- **v3 "lie in his bosom, and it was like a daughter to him"** — *ke-vat*; *bat*, **daughter**, is the first part of Bathsheba's name. *In his bosom* returns in v8: the LORD gave David his master's wives *into your bosom*.
- **vv5–7 "deserves to die… fourfold… You are the man!"** — *ben mavet*, what Saul said of David, *he deserves to die* (1 Samuel 20:31). Fourfold is the law's payment for a stolen sheep, *four sheep in place of the sheep* (Exodus 22:1); the Greek has *sevenfold*. The text keeps the Hebrew. *Attah ha-ish*: David has passed sentence on himself.
- **v9 "to do what is evil in My eyes"** — the written text has *in his eyes*; the text follows the read form, *in My eyes*. It answers 11:27, *the thing that David had done was evil in the eyes of the LORD*. *With the sword of the sons of Ammon*: David killed Uriah by another's hand.
- **v10 "the sword shall never depart from your house"** — three of David's sons will die by violence: Amnon (13:28–29), Absalom (18:14–15) and Adonijah (1 Kings 2:25).
- **vv11–12 "before the eyes of this sun… you did it in secret"** — the threat is fulfilled on the roof where it began, when Absalom goes in to his father's concubines *in the sight of all Israel* (16:22).
- **v13 "I have sinned against the LORD… you shall not die"** — *chatati la-YHWH*. Saul said *I have sinned* twice and asked to be honored before the elders (1 Samuel 15:24, 30); David says it once and asks for nothing. Psalm 51 is headed *when Nathan the prophet came to him, after he had gone in to Bathsheba*. *You shall not die* lifts the sentence David pronounced in v5.
- **v14 "you have utterly scorned the enemies of the LORD"** — the sense is that David has scorned the LORD (v10, *you have despised Me*). *The enemies of* turns the words away from God, as in *May God do so to the enemies of David* (1 Samuel 25:22). The Greek has the same wording.
- **vv24–25 "she called his name Solomon… Jedidiah, because of the LORD"** — the written text has *he called*; the text follows the read form, *she called*. *Shelomoh* is from the root of *shalom*. *Yedidyah*, **beloved of the LORD**, follows *The LORD loved him*.
- **v30 "the crown of their king"** — *malkam*, which is also the name of the god of the Ammonites, *Milcom the loathsome thing of the Ammonites* (1 Kings 11:5). The Greek has both, *Melchol their king*.
- **v31 "set them to the saws and the iron picks and the iron axes… the brick kiln"** — *va-yasem ba-megerah*, **put them at the saw**, the verb used for setting people to work. Chronicles has *va-yasar* (1 Chronicles 20:3), one letter different, a verb whose meaning there is unclear. The written text has *malken*; the text follows the read form, *malben*, **brick kiln** or **brick mold**.
