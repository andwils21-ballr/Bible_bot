# 2 Samuel 18

## "Deal Gently with the Young Man Absalom"

**1** David mustered the people who were with him and set over them commanders of thousands and commanders of hundreds.

**2** David sent out the people, a third under the command of Joab, a third under the command of Abishai son of Zeruiah, Joab's brother, and a third under the command of Ittai the Gittite. The king said to the people, "I myself will surely go out with you too."

**3** But the people said, "You shall not go out. If we flee, they will not care about us; if half of us die, they will not care about us. But you are worth ten thousand of us. So now it is better that you be ready to help us from the city."

**4** The king said to them, "Whatever seems best to you I will do." So the king stood beside the gate, while all the people went out by hundreds and by thousands.

**5** The king commanded Joab, Abishai and Ittai, "Deal gently, for my sake, with the young man Absalom." All the people heard when the king gave all the commanders orders concerning Absalom.

## The Forest of Ephraim

**6** So the people went out into the field against Israel, and the battle was fought in the forest of Ephraim.

**7** The people of Israel were struck down there before David's servants, and the slaughter there was great on that day, twenty thousand men.

**8** The battle spread over the face of all the country, and the forest devoured more people that day than the sword devoured.

**9** Absalom happened to meet David's servants. Absalom was riding on his mule, and the mule went under the thick branches of a great oak. His head caught fast in the oak, and he was left hanging between heaven and earth, while the mule that was under him went on.

**10** A certain man saw it and told Joab, "I saw Absalom hanging in an oak."

**11** Joab said to the man who told him, "You saw him! Why did you not strike him to the ground there? I would have had to give you ten pieces of silver and a belt."

**12** But the man said to Joab, "Even if I felt the weight of a thousand pieces of silver in my hand, I would not put out my hand against the king's son. For in our hearing the king commanded you and Abishai and Ittai, 'Protect the young man Absalom, whoever you may be.'

**13** Otherwise, if I had acted treacherously against my own life — and nothing is hidden from the king — you yourself would have stood aloof."

**14** Joab said, "I will not wait like this for you." He took three rods in his hand and thrust them into Absalom's heart while he was still alive in the heart of the oak.

**15** Ten young men, Joab's armor-bearers, surrounded Absalom, struck him and killed him.

**16** Then Joab blew the shofar, and the people turned back from pursuing Israel, for Joab held the people back.

**17** They took Absalom, threw him into the great pit in the forest and raised over him a very great heap of stones. All Israel fled, each to his tent.

**18** Now Absalom in his lifetime had taken and set up for himself the pillar that is in the King's Valley, for he said, "I have no son to keep my name in remembrance." He called the pillar by his own name, and it is called Absalom's Monument to this day.

## The News Reaches David

**19** Then Ahimaaz son of Zadok said, "Let me run and bring the king the news that the LORD has delivered him from the hand of his enemies."

**20** Joab said to him, "You are not the man to bring news today. You may bring news another day, but today you shall not bring news, because the king's son is dead."

**21** Then Joab said to the Cushite, "Go, tell the king what you have seen." The Cushite paid homage to Joab and ran.

**22** Ahimaaz son of Zadok said to Joab once more, "Come what may, let me also run after the Cushite." Joab said, "Why should you run, my son, when you will have no reward for the news?"

**23** "Come what may, I will run." So he said to him, "Run." Then Ahimaaz ran by the way of the Plain and outran the Cushite.

**24** David was sitting between the two gates. The watchman went up to the roof of the gate by the wall, lifted up his eyes and saw a man running alone.

**25** The watchman called out and told the king. The king said, "If he is alone, there is news in his mouth." He kept coming closer.

**26** Then the watchman saw another man running, and the watchman called to the gatekeeper and said, "Another man is running alone!" The king said, "He too is bringing news."

**27** The watchman said, "I think the running of the first is like the running of Ahimaaz son of Zadok." The king said, "He is a good man, and he comes with good news."

**28** Ahimaaz called out and said to the king, "Peace!" He paid homage to the king with his face to the ground and said, "Blessed be the LORD your God, who has delivered up the men who raised their hand against my lord the king."

**29** The king said, "Is it peace with the young man Absalom?" Ahimaaz said, "When Joab sent the king's servant and your servant, I saw a great tumult, but I did not know what it was."

**30** The king said, "Turn aside and stand here." So he turned aside and stood still.

**31** Then the Cushite came, and the Cushite said, "Let my lord the king receive the news: the LORD has delivered you today from the hand of all who rose up against you."

**32** The king said to the Cushite, "Is it peace with the young man Absalom?" The Cushite said, "May the enemies of my lord the king, and all who rise up against you for evil, be as that young man is."

**33** The king was shaken. He went up to the room over the gate and wept, and as he went he said, "My son Absalom, my son, my son Absalom! Would that I had died instead of you, Absalom, my son, my son!"

## Notes

- **v3 "But you are worth ten thousand of us"** — the Hebrew has *ki attah kamonu asarah alafim*, **for now there are ten thousand like us**, which cuts against the argument of the verse. The Greek has *for you are like ten thousand of us*. *Attah*, **now** (עתה), and *attah*, **you** (אתה), are spelled with one letter different. The text follows the Greek.
- **v8 "the forest devoured more people that day than the sword devoured"** — *va-yirbeh ha-ya'ar le-ekhol ba-am*. The sword *devours* in Hebrew (11:25); here the forest does too, and the next verse shows how.
- **v9 "His head caught fast in the oak… between heaven and earth"** — the text says his head, not his hair; the hair of 14:26 is not mentioned. The king's sons rode mules (13:29); this one goes on without its rider.
- **vv12–13 "whoever you may be… against my own life"** — *shimru mi ba-na'ar*, **guard, whoever, the young man**; the Greek has *guard for me*. In v13 the written text has *be-nafsho*, **against his life**, and the read form *be-nafshi*, **against my own life**; the text follows the read form. The man knows that Joab would leave him to take the blame.
- **v14 "three rods… into Absalom's heart while he was still alive in the heart of the oak"** — *shevatim*, **rods** or **staffs**; the Greek has *darts*. *Lev* twice: the heart of the man and the heart of the tree.
- **v17 "raised over him a very great heap of stones"** — the burial of Achan (Joshua 7:26) and of the king of Ai (Joshua 8:29), each under *a great heap of stones*.
- **v18 "I have no son to keep my name in remembrance… Absalom's Monument"** — *yad Avshalom*, **Absalom's hand**, the word used for Saul's *monument* at Carmel (1 Samuel 15:12). Three sons were born to Absalom (14:27); the text does not say what became of them.
- **vv19, 31 "the LORD has delivered him from the hand of his enemies"** — *shefato YHWH mi-yad oyevav*, **judged him out of the hand of his enemies**: the judge gives the verdict and sets the man free. David said the same to Saul, *may He see and plead my cause and deliver me from your hand* (1 Samuel 24:15).
- **vv28–29, 32 "Peace!… Is it peace with the young man Absalom?"** — *shalom*, the second half of *Avshalom*, **the father is peace**. David asks one question of both runners.
- **v33 "Would that I had died instead of you"** — *mi yitten muti ani tachtekha*. In the Hebrew this is 19:1; the English numbering ends chapter 18 here.
