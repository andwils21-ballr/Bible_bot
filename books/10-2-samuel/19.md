# 2 Samuel 19

## Joab Rebukes the King

**1** Joab was told, "The king is weeping and mourning for Absalom."

**2** So the victory that day was turned into mourning for all the people, for the people heard that day, "The king is grieving for his son."

**3** The people stole into the city that day as people steal in who are ashamed when they flee in battle.

**4** The king covered his face, and the king cried out with a loud voice, "My son Absalom, Absalom, my son, my son!"

**5** Then Joab came into the house to the king and said, "Today you have shamed the faces of all your servants, who today have saved your life and the lives of your sons and your daughters, the lives of your wives and the lives of your secondary wives,

**6** by loving those who hate you and hating those who love you. For you have made it clear today that commanders and servants are nothing to you. Today I know that if Absalom were alive and all of us were dead today, then it would be right in your eyes.

**7** So now get up, go out and speak to the heart of your servants. For I swear by the LORD, if you do not go out, not a man will stay with you tonight, and this will be worse for you than all the evil that has come on you from your youth until now."

**8** So the king got up and sat in the gate. When all the people were told, "The king is sitting in the gate," all the people came before the king. Now Israel had fled, each to his tent.

## Bringing Back the King

**9** All the people were arguing throughout all the tribes of Israel, saying, "The king delivered us from the hand of our enemies, and he rescued us from the hand of the Philistines, and now he has fled out of the land from Absalom.

**10** But Absalom, whom we anointed over us, is dead in battle. So now why do you say nothing about bringing the king back?"

**11** King David sent word to Zadok and Abiathar the priests: "Speak to the elders of Judah and say, 'Why should you be the last to bring the king back to his house, when the word of all Israel has come to the king, to his house?

**12** You are my brothers; you are my bone and my flesh. Why should you be the last to bring the king back?'

**13** Say also to Amasa, 'Are you not my bone and my flesh? May God do so to me and more also, if you are not commander of the army before me from now on in place of Joab.'"

**14** So he turned the hearts of all the men of Judah as one man, and they sent word to the king: "Come back, you and all your servants."

## At the Jordan

**15** So the king came back and came to the Jordan, and Judah came to Gilgal to meet the king and to bring the king over the Jordan.

**16** Shimei son of Gera, the Benjaminite from Bahurim, hurried down with the men of Judah to meet King David.

**17** With him were a thousand men from Benjamin, and Ziba the servant of the house of Saul, with his fifteen sons and his twenty servants. They rushed down to the Jordan ahead of the king.

**18** They crossed the ford to bring over the king's household and to do what was good in his eyes. Shimei son of Gera fell down before the king as he was crossing the Jordan,

**19** and he said to the king, "Let not my lord count me guilty, and do not remember how your servant did wrong on the day my lord the king went out of Jerusalem. Let not the king take it to heart.

**20** For your servant knows that I have sinned. So I have come today, the first of all the house of Joseph, to come down to meet my lord the king."

**21** Abishai son of Zeruiah answered, "Shall not Shimei be put to death for this, because he cursed the LORD's anointed?"

**22** But David said, "What have I to do with you, you sons of Zeruiah, that you should be an adversary to me today? Shall any man be put to death in Israel today? For do I not know that today I am king over Israel?"

**23** The king said to Shimei, "You shall not die." Then the king swore it to him.

## Mephibosheth

**24** Mephibosheth, Saul's grandson, came down to meet the king. He had not cared for his feet or trimmed his mustache or washed his clothes from the day the king went away until the day he came back in peace.

**25** When he came to Jerusalem to meet the king, the king said to him, "Why did you not go with me, Mephibosheth?"

**26** He said, "My lord the king, my servant deceived me. For your servant said, 'I will have the donkey saddled for me, so that I may ride on it and go with the king,' because your servant is lame.

**27** He has slandered your servant to my lord the king. But my lord the king is like the messenger of God; so do what is good in your eyes.

**28** For all my father's house were nothing but men deserving death before my lord the king, yet you set your servant among those who eat at your table. What further right have I, then, to cry out any more to the king?"

**29** The king said to him, "Why speak any more of your affairs? I have decided: you and Ziba shall divide the land."

**30** Mephibosheth said to the king, "Let him even take it all, since my lord the king has come home safely."

## Barzillai

**31** Barzillai the Gileadite had come down from Rogelim, and he went on to the Jordan with the king to send him on his way over the Jordan.

**32** Barzillai was very old, eighty years old. He had provided for the king while he stayed at Mahanaim, for he was a very great man.

**33** The king said to Barzillai, "Come over with me, and I will provide for you with me in Jerusalem."

**34** But Barzillai said to the king, "How many are the days of the years of my life, that I should go up with the king to Jerusalem?

**35** I am eighty years old today. Can I tell good from bad? Can your servant taste what I eat or what I drink? Can I still hear the voice of singing men and singing women? Why then should your servant be a further burden to my lord the king?

**36** Your servant will go only a little way over the Jordan with the king. Why should the king repay me with such a reward?

**37** Please let your servant turn back, so that I may die in my own town, by the grave of my father and my mother. But here is your servant Chimham; let him go over with my lord the king, and do for him what is good in your eyes."

**38** The king said, "Chimham shall go over with me, and I will do for him what is good in your eyes. Whatever you ask of me I will do for you."

**39** Then all the people crossed the Jordan, and the king crossed. The king kissed Barzillai and blessed him, and he returned to his home.

## Judah and Israel Quarrel

**40** The king went on to Gilgal, and Chimham went on with him. All the people of Judah had brought the king over, and half the people of Israel as well.

**41** Then all the men of Israel came to the king and said to the king, "Why have our brothers the men of Judah stolen you away, and brought the king and his household over the Jordan, and all David's men with him?"

**42** All the men of Judah answered the men of Israel, "Because the king is near of kin to us. Why then are you angry over this? Have we eaten anything at all at the king's expense? Or has he given us any gift?"

**43** The men of Israel answered the men of Judah, "We have ten shares in the king, and in David also we have more than you. Why then did you treat us with contempt? Was it not we who first spoke of bringing back our king?" But the words of the men of Judah were harsher than the words of the men of Israel.

## Notes

- **v1 "Joab was told"** — in the Hebrew, chapter 19 begins with the king's cry (English 18:33), and every verse after is one number higher than in the English.
- **vv2–3 "the victory… was turned into mourning… The people stole into the city"** — *teshu'ah*, **salvation**, the word for a victory the LORD gives. *Va-yitganev*, **stole in**, is the word of Absalom, who *stole the hearts of the men of Israel* (15:6). The men of Israel will use it once more: *Why have our brothers… stolen you away?* (v41).
- **v7 "speak to the heart of your servants"** — *dabber al lev*, the words for Shechem speaking to Dinah (Genesis 34:3) and Joseph to his brothers (Genesis 50:21): to speak comfort, and to win back.
- **vv12–13 "you are my bone and my flesh… in place of Joab"** — the words all Israel said to David at Hebron, *We are your bone and your flesh* (5:1). Amasa commanded Absalom's army (17:25); David now offers him Joab's place.
- **v18 "They crossed the ford"** — *ve-averah ha-avarah*, **the crossing crossed**, noun and verb from *avar*, **to cross**. Whether the noun names a ford or a ferry here is uncertain; the Greek has *the crossing crossed* as well.
- **v20 "the first of all the house of Joseph"** — Shimei is a Benjaminite; *the house of Joseph* here names the northern tribes as a whole, against Judah.
- **v22 "that you should be an adversary to me today"** — *le-satan*, **an adversary**, the word used of the messenger of the LORD who stood in Balaam's road *as an adversary against him* (Numbers 22:22), and of David himself in the Philistines' fear that *he will become an adversary to us* (1 Samuel 29:4).
- **vv24–26 "He had not cared for his feet… my servant deceived me"** — *rimmani*, the word of Jacob to Laban, *Why have you deceived me?* (Genesis 29:25). The signs of mourning support Mephibosheth's account, and David does not decide between them: *you and Ziba shall divide the land* (v29), after he had given Ziba everything (16:4).
- **v28 "men deserving death"** — *anshei mavet*, near to *ben mavet*, the phrase for one who *deserves to die* (1 Samuel 20:31; 12:5).
- **v43 "We have ten shares in the king… the words of the men of Judah were harsher"** — ten tribes against one. The Greek adds *and I am the firstborn rather than you*. The quarrel leads straight into Sheba's revolt in the next chapter.
