# 2 Samuel 5

## The Anointing at Hebron

**1** All the tribes of Israel came to David at Hebron and said, "We are your bone and your flesh.

**2** In times past, when Saul was king over us, it was you who led Israel out and brought them in. The LORD said to you, 'You shall shepherd My people Israel, and you shall be leader over Israel.'"

**3** So all the elders of Israel came to the king at Hebron, and King David made a covenant with them at Hebron before the LORD, and they anointed David king over Israel.

**4** David was thirty years old when he began to reign, and he reigned forty years.

**5** At Hebron he reigned over Judah seven years and six months, and in Jerusalem he reigned thirty-three years over all Israel and Judah.

## Jerusalem

**6** The king and his men went to Jerusalem against the Jebusites, the inhabitants of the land. They said to David, "You will not come in here; even the blind and the lame will turn you away," meaning, "David cannot come in here."

**7** But David took the stronghold of Zion, that is, the City of David.

**8** David said on that day, "Whoever would strike the Jebusites, let him get up the water shaft to reach the lame and the blind, who are hated by David's soul." That is why they say, "The blind and the lame shall not come into the house."

**9** David lived in the stronghold and called it the City of David. David built the city all around from the Millo inward.

**10** David grew greater and greater, and the LORD, the God of hosts, was with him.

**11** Hiram king of Tyre sent messengers to David, with cedar wood, carpenters and stonemasons, and they built David a house.

**12** David knew that the LORD had established him as king over Israel, and that He had exalted his kingdom for the sake of His people Israel.

**13** David took more concubines and wives from Jerusalem after he came from Hebron, and more sons and daughters were born to David.

**14** These are the names of those born to him in Jerusalem: Shammua, Shobab, Nathan and Solomon,

**15** Ibhar, Elishua, Nepheg and Japhia,

**16** Elishama, Eliada and Eliphelet.

## Victories over the Philistines

**17** When the Philistines heard that David had been anointed king over Israel, all the Philistines went up to seek David. David heard of it and went down to the stronghold.

**18** The Philistines had come and spread out in the Valley of Rephaim.

**19** David inquired of the LORD, "Shall I go up against the Philistines? Will You give them into my hand?" The LORD said to David, "Go up, for I will certainly give the Philistines into your hand."

**20** David came to Baal-perazim, and David struck them there. He said, "The LORD has broken through my enemies before me like a breakthrough of waters." So he called the name of that place Baal-perazim.

**21** The Philistines left their idols there, and David and his men carried them away.

**22** The Philistines came up yet again and spread out in the Valley of Rephaim.

**23** When David inquired of the LORD, He said, "You shall not go up. Go around behind them and come against them opposite the balsam trees.

**24** And it shall come to pass, when you hear the sound of marching in the tops of the balsam trees, that you shall act quickly, for then the LORD will have gone out before you to strike the army of the Philistines."

**25** David did as the LORD commanded him, and he struck down the Philistines from Geba all the way to Gezer.

## Notes

- **v1 "We are your bone and your flesh"** — the words of kinship, as Laban said to Jacob, *Surely you are my bone and my flesh* (Genesis 29:14). The tribes that fought for Saul's house now claim David as their own.
- **v2 "You shall shepherd My people Israel"** — *tir'eh*, the shepherd's word; David was taken from tending the sheep (1 Samuel 16:11). *Leader*, *nagid*, the word the LORD used of Saul (1 Samuel 9:16) and then of the man after His own heart (1 Samuel 13:14).
- **v3 "a covenant with them… before the LORD"** — the king binds himself to the elders, and they anoint him. This is David's third anointing: by Samuel among his brothers (1 Samuel 16:13), by Judah at Hebron (2:4), and now by all Israel.
- **vv6–8 "even the blind and the lame will turn you away… the water shaft"** — the Jebusites boast that their walls need only the blind and the lame to defend them. David turns their taunt back: his men are to *get up the water shaft*, *ba-tsinnor*, and strike *the lame and the blind*. *Tsinnor* occurs elsewhere only in Psalm 42:7, *at the sound of your waterfalls*; its sense here is uncertain. The saying at the end became a proverb, *The blind and the lame shall not come into the house*. The Greek has *let him touch with the dagger*.
- **v10 "David grew greater and greater"** — *holekh ve-gadol*, the pattern of 3:1, *David grew stronger and stronger*, and of the boy Samuel (1 Samuel 2:26).
- **v13 "David took more concubines and wives"** — the law of the king: *He shall not multiply wives for himself, so that his heart does not turn aside* (Deuteronomy 17:17). The list of sons includes *Solomon*.
- **v20 "Baal-perazim… like a breakthrough of waters"** — *Ba'al Peratsim*, **Lord of breakthroughs**, from *parats*, **to break through**, as a flood breaks a dam. The same verb names the next place of the ark's story, *Perez-uzzah*, when *the LORD had broken out against Uzzah* (6:8).
- **v21 "The Philistines left their idols there, and David and his men carried them away"** — the reverse of the day the Philistines carried off the ark of God (1 Samuel 4:11). Chronicles adds that David ordered them burned (1 Chronicles 14:12), as the law commands (Deuteronomy 7:25).
- **vv23–24 "the sound of marching in the tops of the balsam trees"** — *ha-bekha'im*, trees named only here and in the parallel in Chronicles (1 Chronicles 14:14–15); *balsam* is one guess among several. The Greek has *the place of weeping*. The sound is the LORD's army going out ahead of David.
