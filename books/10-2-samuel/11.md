# 2 Samuel 11

## The Roof of the King's House

**1** And it came to pass at the turn of the year, at the time when kings go out, that David sent Joab, and his servants with him, and all Israel. They destroyed the sons of Ammon and besieged Rabbah. But David stayed in Jerusalem.

**2** And it came to pass one evening that David got up from his bed and walked about on the roof of the king's house, and from the roof he saw a woman bathing. The woman was very beautiful to look at.

**3** David sent and inquired about the woman, and someone said, "Is this not Bathsheba daughter of Eliam, the wife of Uriah the Hittite?"

**4** So David sent messengers and took her. She came to him, and he lay with her; she had been purifying herself from her uncleanness. Then she returned to her house.

**5** The woman conceived, and she sent and told David, "I am pregnant."

## Uriah

**6** Then David sent word to Joab: "Send me Uriah the Hittite." So Joab sent Uriah to David.

**7** When Uriah came to him, David asked about the welfare of Joab, the welfare of the people and the welfare of the war.

**8** David said to Uriah, "Go down to your house and wash your feet." Uriah went out of the king's house, and a gift from the king followed him.

**9** But Uriah slept at the entrance of the king's house with all the servants of his lord, and he did not go down to his house.

**10** They told David, "Uriah did not go down to his house." David said to Uriah, "Have you not just come from a journey? Why did you not go down to your house?"

**11** Uriah said to David, "The ark and Israel and Judah are staying in booths, and my lord Joab and the servants of my lord are camped in the open field. Shall I then go to my house to eat and to drink and to lie with my wife? As you live, and as your soul lives, I will not do this thing."

**12** David said to Uriah, "Stay here today also, and tomorrow I will send you off." So Uriah stayed in Jerusalem that day and the next.

**13** David invited him, and he ate and drank in his presence, and David made him drunk. In the evening he went out to lie on his bed with the servants of his lord, but he did not go down to his house.

## The Letter to Joab

**14** And it came to pass in the morning that David wrote a letter to Joab and sent it by the hand of Uriah.

**15** In the letter he wrote, "Put Uriah in front, where the fighting is fiercest, then draw back from him, so that he is struck down and dies."

**16** As Joab kept watch on the city, he put Uriah at the place where he knew there were men of valor.

**17** The men of the city came out and fought with Joab, and some of the people, of David's servants, fell. Uriah the Hittite also died.

**18** Then Joab sent and told David all the news of the battle.

**19** He instructed the messenger, "When you have finished telling the king all the news of the battle,

**20** if the king's anger rises and he says to you, 'Why did you go so near the city to fight? Did you not know that they would shoot from the wall?

**21** Who struck down Abimelech son of Jerubbesheth? Was it not a woman who threw an upper millstone on him from the wall, so that he died at Thebez? Why did you go so near the wall?' then you shall say, 'Your servant Uriah the Hittite is dead too.'"

**22** So the messenger went, and he came and told David all that Joab had sent him to say.

**23** The messenger said to David, "The men were stronger than we were and came out against us into the field, but we drove them back to the entrance of the gate.

**24** Then the archers shot at your servants from the wall, and some of the king's servants died. Your servant Uriah the Hittite is dead too."

**25** David said to the messenger, "Say this to Joab: 'Do not let this thing be evil in your eyes, for the sword devours now one and now another. Press your attack on the city and overthrow it.' So encourage him."

## In David's House

**26** When Uriah's wife heard that Uriah her husband was dead, she mourned for her husband.

**27** When the mourning was over, David sent and gathered her into his house, and she became his wife and bore him a son. But what David had done was evil in the eyes of the LORD.

## Notes

- **v1 "at the time when kings go out… But David stayed in Jerusalem"** — the Hebrew letters spell *ha-mal'akhim*, **the messengers**, but the aleph carries a mark (*rafe*) telling the reader not to sound it; without it the word is *kings*. The Greek has *kings*, and so does Chronicles, *at the time when kings go out* (1 Chronicles 20:1). The text follows them. The king whose time it is to go out sends others and stays home.
- **v2 "on the roof of the king's house"** — on this same roof Absalom will pitch a tent and go in to his father's secondary wives *in the sight of all Israel* (16:22), as Nathan will say, *before this sun* (12:11–12).
- **v3 "Bathsheba daughter of Eliam, the wife of Uriah the Hittite"** — *Eliam son of Ahithophel the Gilonite* (23:34) and *Uriah the Hittite* (23:39) are both named among David's mighty men. The Greek has *Eliab*; Chronicles calls her *Bath-shua daughter of Ammiel* (1 Chronicles 3:5).
- **v4 "she had been purifying herself from her uncleanness"** — *mitqaddeshet mi-tum'atah*: the purification after her monthly separation (Leviticus 15:19, 28). It places the conception after that separation, so the child of v5 is David's.
- **v7 "the welfare of Joab, the welfare of the people and the welfare of the war"** — *shalom* three times. David asks after the peace of a war he has left to others.
- **v8 "wash your feet… a gift from the king"** — go home for the night; Uriah's answer in v11 shows what he understood. *Mas'at*, the word for the portions Joseph sent from his table (Genesis 43:34).
- **v11 "The ark and Israel and Judah are staying in booths… As you live, and as your soul lives"** — *sukkot*. David told Ahimelech that *women have indeed been kept from us, as always when I set out* (1 Samuel 21:5); Uriah keeps that rule in Jerusalem. He swears by David's own life.
- **v14 "sent it by the hand of Uriah"** — Uriah carries the letter that orders his death.
- **v21 "Abimelech son of Jerubbesheth… an upper millstone… at Thebez"** — Gideon was called *Jerubbaal* (Judges 6:32); *besheth*, **shame**, stands for *baal* here, as in Ish-bosheth and Mephibosheth. The death is told in Judges 9:50–53. The Greek adds to v22 that *David was angry with Joab*, and gives David the whole speech Joab had predicted. The text stays with the Hebrew.
- **vv25, 27 "Do not let this thing be evil in your eyes… evil in the eyes of the LORD"** — *al yera be-einekha et ha-davar ha-zeh* and *va-yera ha-davar… be-einei YHWH*. David tells Joab not to let the thing be evil in his eyes; the chapter ends by saying it was evil in the LORD's.
