# 2 Samuel 13

## Amnon and Tamar

**1** And it came to pass after this that Absalom son of David had a beautiful sister whose name was Tamar, and Amnon son of David loved her.

**2** Amnon was so distressed over Tamar his sister that he made himself sick, for she was a virgin, and it seemed impossible to Amnon to do anything to her.

**3** Amnon had a friend whose name was Jonadab son of Shimeah, David's brother, and Jonadab was a very wise man.

**4** He said to him, "Why are you so haggard, son of the king, morning after morning? Will you not tell me?" Amnon said to him, "I love Tamar, my brother Absalom's sister."

**5** Jonadab said to him, "Lie down on your bed and pretend to be sick. When your father comes to see you, say to him, 'Please let my sister Tamar come and give me bread to eat. Let her prepare the food in my sight, so that I may see it and eat from her hand.'"

**6** So Amnon lay down and pretended to be sick. When the king came to see him, Amnon said to the king, "Please let my sister Tamar come and make a couple of heart-cakes in my sight, so that I may eat from her hand."

**7** Then David sent word to Tamar at the house: "Go now to your brother Amnon's house and prepare food for him."

**8** So Tamar went to her brother Amnon's house, where he was lying down. She took dough, kneaded it, made the heart-cakes in his sight and baked them.

**9** Then she took the pan and set them out before him, but he refused to eat. Amnon said, "Send everyone out from me." So everyone went out from him.

**10** Then Amnon said to Tamar, "Bring the food into the inner room, so that I may eat from your hand." Tamar took the heart-cakes she had made and brought them to Amnon her brother in the inner room.

**11** When she brought them near to him to eat, he took hold of her and said to her, "Come, lie with me, my sister."

**12** She said to him, "No, my brother! Do not violate me, for such a thing is not done in Israel. Do not do this outrage.

**13** As for me, where could I carry my disgrace? You would be like one of the fools in Israel. Now please speak to the king, for he will not keep me from you."

**14** But he would not listen to her voice. He overpowered her, violated her and lay with her.

**15** Then Amnon hated her with a very great hatred; the hatred with which he hated her was greater than the love with which he had loved her. Amnon said to her, "Get up, go!"

**16** She said to him, "No, for this wrong in sending me away is greater than the other you have done to me." But he would not listen to her.

**17** He called the young man who served him and said, "Put this woman out of my presence and bolt the door after her."

**18** She was wearing a long coat, for that is what the king's daughters who were virgins wore as robes. So his servant put her out and bolted the door after her.

**19** Tamar put ashes on her head and tore the long coat she was wearing. She put her hand on her head and went away, crying out as she went.

**20** Absalom her brother said to her, "Has Amnon your brother been with you? Now, my sister, keep quiet; he is your brother. Do not take this to heart." So Tamar lived, desolate, in her brother Absalom's house.

**21** When King David heard all these things, he was very angry. But he did not grieve the spirit of Amnon his son, because he loved him, for he was his firstborn.

**22** Absalom spoke to Amnon neither bad nor good, for Absalom hated Amnon because he had violated Tamar his sister.

## Absalom's Sheep-Shearing

**23** And it came to pass after two full years that Absalom had sheepshearers at Baal-hazor, which is near Ephraim, and Absalom invited all the king's sons.

**24** Absalom came to the king and said, "Your servant has sheepshearers. Please let the king and his servants go with your servant."

**25** The king said to Absalom, "No, my son, let us not all go, or we will be a burden to you." He pressed him, but he would not go, and he blessed him.

**26** Absalom said, "If not, please let my brother Amnon go with us." The king said to him, "Why should he go with you?"

**27** But Absalom pressed him, and he sent Amnon and all the king's sons with him. Absalom made a feast like the feast of a king.

**28** Absalom commanded his young men, "Watch now. When Amnon's heart is merry with wine and I say to you, 'Strike Amnon,' then put him to death. Do not be afraid. Is it not I who have commanded you? Be strong and be men of valor."

**29** So Absalom's young men did to Amnon as Absalom had commanded. Then all the king's sons got up, and each mounted his mule and fled.

## The News Reaches David

**30** And it came to pass, while they were on the way, that the report came to David: "Absalom has struck down all the king's sons, and not one of them is left."

**31** The king got up, tore his clothes and lay on the ground, and all his servants stood by with their clothes torn.

**32** But Jonadab son of Shimeah, David's brother, spoke up and said, "Let not my lord suppose that they have killed all the young men, the king's sons, for Amnon alone is dead. This has been Absalom's intent from the day Amnon violated his sister Tamar.

**33** So now let not my lord the king take to heart the report that all the king's sons are dead, for Amnon alone is dead."

**34** Absalom fled. The young man keeping watch lifted up his eyes and saw many people coming on the road behind him, by the side of the hill.

**35** Jonadab said to the king, "The king's sons have come. As your servant said, so it has happened."

**36** And it came to pass, as he finished speaking, that the king's sons came, and they lifted up their voices and wept. The king too and all his servants wept very bitterly.

**37** Absalom fled and went to Talmai son of Ammihud, king of Geshur. David mourned for his son every day.

**38** So Absalom fled and went to Geshur, and he was there three years.

**39** King David's heart longed to go out to Absalom, for he was comforted about Amnon, since he was dead.

## Notes

- **v6 "make a couple of heart-cakes in my sight"** — *u-telabbev… shetei levivot*. The cakes (*levivot*) appear only in this chapter (vv6, 8, 10), and the verb *libbev*, to make them, only here (vv6, 8). Both come from *lev*, **heart**. The one other use of the verb is *You have taken my heart* (Song of Songs 4:9).
- **vv12–13 "such a thing is not done in Israel. Do not do this outrage… like one of the fools"** — *lo ye'aseh khen be-Yisra'el… ha-nevalah ha-zot… ke-achad ha-nevalim*. It is the language of Dinah: *he had done an outrage in Israel… and so it is not done* (Genesis 34:7). *Nevalah* is the deed and *naval* the man who does it.
- **v14 "violated her"** — *va-ye'anneha*, the word used of Shechem with Dinah, *violated her* (Genesis 34:2).
- **v16 "this wrong in sending me away is greater than the other"** — the Hebrew begins with *al odot*, whose construction is uncertain; the Greek has *this great wrong is greater than the other*. The sense in both is that to send her away now is worse than what he has done.
- **vv18–19 "a long coat"** — *ketonet passim*, the coat Israel made for Joseph (Genesis 37:3), and nowhere else in the Hebrew Bible. Joseph's brothers stripped it from him; Tamar tears hers.
- **v21 "But he did not grieve the spirit of Amnon his son, because he loved him, for he was his firstborn"** — this sentence is in the Greek and not in the Hebrew, which ends with *he was very angry*. Amnon was David's firstborn (3:2). The same is said of Adonijah, *his father had never grieved him* (1 Kings 1:6). The text follows the Greek.
- **v27 "Absalom made a feast like the feast of a king"** — also from the Greek; the Hebrew lacks it, but v28 speaks of Amnon *merry with wine*. The words are Nabal's feast, *like the feast of a king* (1 Samuel 25:36). The text follows the Greek.
- **v32 "This has been Absalom's intent"** — *al pi Avshalom hayetah sumah*, **on Absalom's mouth it was set**.
- **v34 "by the side of the hill"** — the Greek adds that the watchman came and told the king, *I have seen men on the Horonaim road, by the side of the hill*. The text stays with the Hebrew.
- **v39 "King David's heart longed to go out to Absalom"** — the verb *va-tekhal* is feminine and has no subject: *heart* (or *soul*) is understood, as in *my soul longs, even faints* (Psalm 84:3). The Greek reads *King David ceased to go out against Absalom*. The Hebrew as it stands says longing, and chapter 14 opens with Joab knowing that *the king's heart was on Absalom* (14:1).
