# 2 Samuel 21

## The Famine and the Gibeonites

**1** There was a famine in the days of David for three years, year after year, and David sought the face of the LORD. The LORD said, "It is because of Saul and his house of bloodshed, because he put the Gibeonites to death."

**2** So the king called the Gibeonites and spoke to them. (The Gibeonites were not of the sons of Israel but of the remnant of the Amorites. The sons of Israel had sworn an oath to them, but Saul had sought to strike them down in his zeal for the sons of Israel and Judah.)

**3** David said to the Gibeonites, "What shall I do for you? How shall I make atonement, so that you will bless the inheritance of the LORD?"

**4** The Gibeonites said to him, "It is not a matter of silver or gold between us and Saul and his house, and it is not for us to put any man to death in Israel." He said, "Whatever you say, I will do for you."

**5** They said to the king, "The man who consumed us and who planned to destroy us, so that we should have no place in all the territory of Israel —

**6** let seven men of his sons be given to us, and we will hang them up before the LORD at Gibeah of Saul, the chosen of the LORD." The king said, "I will give them."

**7** But the king spared Mephibosheth son of Jonathan, son of Saul, because of the oath of the LORD that was between them, between David and Jonathan son of Saul.

**8** The king took the two sons of Rizpah daughter of Aiah, whom she had borne to Saul, Armoni and Mephibosheth, and the five sons of Michal daughter of Saul, whom she had borne to Adriel son of Barzillai the Meholathite.

**9** He handed them over to the Gibeonites, and they hung them up on the mountain before the LORD. The seven of them fell together. They were put to death in the first days of harvest, at the beginning of the barley harvest.

## Rizpah

**10** Then Rizpah daughter of Aiah took sackcloth and spread it for herself on the rock, from the beginning of harvest until water poured down on them from the heavens. She did not let the birds of the heavens rest on them by day, or the beasts of the field by night.

**11** When David was told what Rizpah daughter of Aiah, Saul's secondary wife, had done,

**12** David went and took the bones of Saul and the bones of Jonathan his son from the lords of Jabesh-gilead, who had stolen them from the square of Beth-shan, where the Philistines had hung them on the day the Philistines struck down Saul at Gilboa.

**13** He brought up from there the bones of Saul and the bones of Jonathan his son, and they gathered the bones of those who had been hung up.

**14** They buried the bones of Saul and of Jonathan his son in the land of Benjamin in Zela, in the tomb of Kish his father. They did all that the king commanded, and after that God heeded prayers for the land.

## Wars with the Philistines

**15** The Philistines were at war with Israel again, and David went down with his servants, and they fought against the Philistines. David grew weary.

**16** Ishbi-benob, who was one of the descendants of the Rapha, whose spear weighed three hundred shekels of bronze, and who was girded with a new sword, thought to kill David.

**17** But Abishai son of Zeruiah came to his help, and he struck the Philistine and killed him. Then David's men swore to him, "You shall not go out with us to battle any more, so that you do not quench the lamp of Israel."

**18** And it came to pass after this that there was again war with the Philistines at Gob. Then Sibbecai the Hushathite struck down Saph, who was one of the descendants of the Rapha.

**19** There was again war with the Philistines at Gob, and Elhanan son of Jaare-oregim, the Bethlehemite, struck down Goliath the Gittite, the shaft of whose spear was like a weaver's beam.

**20** There was again war at Gath, and there was a man of great size who had six fingers on each hand and six toes on each foot, twenty-four in number. He too was descended from the Rapha.

**21** He taunted Israel, and Jonathan son of Shimeah, David's brother, struck him down.

**22** These four were descended from the Rapha in Gath, and they fell by the hand of David and by the hand of his servants.

## Notes

- **vv1–2 "because he put the Gibeonites to death… the sons of Israel had sworn an oath to them"** — Joshua and the leaders swore to let the Gibeonites live (Joshua 9:15, 19–20). Saul's killing of them is told nowhere else; this verse is the only record of it.
- **v3 "How shall I make atonement"** — *ba-meh akhapper*, the word of the priestly law, here with the lives of the wronged for the price, since the Gibeonites refuse silver and gold (v4).
- **vv6, 9 "we will hang them up before the LORD"** — *ve-hoqa'anum*, the verb of Numbers 25:4, *hang them up before the LORD in the sun, so that the fierce anger of the LORD may turn away from Israel*. The verb in this sense is found only in these two passages. *Gibeah of Saul, the chosen of the LORD*: the Greek has *in Gibeon, Saul the chosen of the LORD*. The text keeps the Hebrew.
- **v8 "the five sons of Michal daughter of Saul, whom she had borne to Adriel"** — the Hebrew and the Greek both have *Michal*. But it was Merab, Saul's other daughter, who *was given to Adriel the Meholathite as a wife* (1 Samuel 18:19), and *Michal the daughter of Saul had no child to the day of her death* (6:23). No witness here has *Merab*. The text keeps the Hebrew.
- **v10 "until water poured down on them from the heavens"** — from the barley harvest in spring until the rain, which in the land falls in autumn and winter. The famine of v1 was three years without enough of it.
- **vv12–14 "who had stolen them from the square of Beth-shan"** — the men of Jabesh took the bodies from the wall of Beth-shan and buried the bones under the tamarisk at Jabesh (1 Samuel 31:10–13). Saul now lies in his father's tomb in Benjamin.
- **v14 "God heeded prayers for the land"** — *va-ye'ater… la-arets*. The book ends with the same words after David's altar: *the LORD heeded prayers for the land, and the plague was held back* (24:25).
- **v16 "Ishbi-benob… girded with a new sword"** — the written text reads *va-yeshvu be-Nov*, **and they stayed in Nob**; the read form is the name *Ishbi-benob*, and the text follows it. *Chadashah*, **new**, has no noun; *sword* is understood.
- **v17 "so that you do not quench the lamp of Israel"** — *ner Yisra'el*. The same image is used of David's line: *so that David My servant may always have a lamp before Me in Jerusalem* (1 Kings 11:36).
- **v19 "Elhanan son of Jaare-oregim, the Bethlehemite, struck down Goliath the Gittite"** — the Hebrew and the Greek both say it. In 1 Samuel 17 David killed the Philistine of Gath whose spear *was like a weaver's beam* (17:7). Chronicles has *Elhanan son of Jair struck down Lahmi the brother of Goliath the Gittite* (1 Chronicles 20:5). *Beit ha-Lachmi et*, **the Bethlehemite**, and *et Lachmi*, **Lahmi**, are close in their letters. *Oregim*, **weavers**, also ends the verse, and Chronicles has no *oregim* in the name. The text keeps the Hebrew.
