# Ezra Sutuel 3

## The First Vision: Ezra's Complaint

**1** In the thirtieth year after the ruin of our city I was in Babylon, I, Sutuel, who am called Ezra. I was troubled as I lay on my bed, my face was downcast, and my thoughts rose up in my heart,

**2** because I saw the desolation of Zion and the joy of those who lived in Babylon.

**3** My spirit was greatly troubled, and I began to speak words of fear to the Most High.

**4** I said, "O Lord God, did You not speak in the beginning, when You created the earth, and that by Yourself alone, and commanded the dust,

**5** and it brought forth Adam in a mortal body? He too was the work of Your hands, and You breathed into him the breath of life, and he became alive before You.

**6** You led him into paradise, which Your right hand had planted before the earth was established.

**7** You gave him a righteous commandment, and he transgressed it; and then You appointed death for him and for his generations. From him were born nations and peoples, tribes and clans without number.

**8** Every nation walked by its own will and sinned before You, and You did not stop them.

**9** Again, in its time, You brought a flood on the earth and on those who lived in the world, and destroyed them.

**10** Their judgment was alike: as You brought death on Adam, so You brought the flood on these.

**11** Yet You left one of them, Noah, with his household, and from him came all the righteous.

**12** Then, when those who lived on the earth began to multiply and increase, and their children were multiplied, and peoples and nations were born of them, they began again to sin more than those before them.

**13** When they had sinned before You, You chose for Yourself one of them, whose name was Abraham.

**14** You loved him, and to him alone You showed the end of the time, when you two were alone by night.

**15** You made an everlasting covenant with him, that You would never forsake his seed. You gave him Isaac, and to Isaac You gave Jacob and Esau.

**16** You set Jacob apart for Yourself, but Esau You put aside; and Jacob became a great multitude.

**17** When his seed went out of Egypt, You brought them to Mount Sinai.

**18** You bowed the heavens and shook the earth, You troubled the world, You made the deep tremble and stirred up the sea.

**19** Your glory passed through four gates, of fire and of earthquake, of wind and of hail, to give a law to the seed of Jacob and a commandment to the family of Israel.

**20** Yet You did not take the evil heart away from them, so that Your law might bear fruit in them.

**21** For the first man, Adam, clothed himself with an evil heart, and he transgressed and was overcome; and not he alone, but all who were born of him.

**22** So the sickness remained, with Your law, in the heart of the people, together with the evil root; and the good passed away, and the evil remained.

**23** The days passed and the years were completed, and You raised up for Yourself a servant whose name was David.

**24** You told him to build a city for Your name and to offer in it from what is Yours.

**25** This went on for many days and years. Then those who lived in the land sinned,

**26** doing nothing different from what Adam and all his generations had done, for they too had clothed themselves with the evil heart.

**27** So You handed Your city over to Your enemies.

**28** Then I said in my heart that day, 'Do those who live in Babylon do any better, that they should take the city of Zion?'

**29** But when I came here, I saw sins without number, and my soul saw many who had denied You. These thirty years my heart has been astonished

**30** at how You bear with sinners and spare the ungodly, and have cast off Your people and preserved Your enemies;

**31** and You have not told anyone at all where this way will end. Does Babylon do better than Zion?

**32** Or has any other nation known You besides Israel? Or what people has believed in Your law as Jacob has,

**33** whose reward has not appeared, and whose labor has borne no fruit? I have gone among the nations and found them rejoicing, though they do not remember Your law or Your commandments.

**34** Now weigh in the balance our sins and the sins of those who live in the world, and see on whose side even a little turns the beam of the scale.

**35** When have those who live in the world not sinned before You? Or what nation has kept Your commandments like this?

**36** You may find individual men by name who have kept Your commandments, but a nation that is perfect You will not find."

## Notes

- **The book** — Ezra Sutuel is the book other Bibles call 2 Esdras 3–14 (4 Ezra), and its chapters and verses are numbered as theirs are; the Ethiopic book begins here, at chapter 3. It is rendered from the Ethiopic as Richard Laurence translated it into English (1820) from one manuscript, compared with Dillmann's edition of the Ethiopic (1894, from ten manuscripts) and with the Latin and the Syriac. Where Laurence's manuscript lacks words that Dillmann's edition, the Latin and the Syriac have, they are kept: *Noah, with his household* (v11); Isaac, Jacob and Esau (vv15–16), a gap in the Ethiopic; *individual men by name* (v36).
- **v1 "I, Sutuel, who am called Ezra"** — the Ethiopic gives him the name *Suta'el*; the Latin has *Salathiel*, the Syriac *Shealtiel*, the father of Zerubbabel (Ezra 3:2; 1 Chronicles 3:17). The book gives Ezra this second name.
- **v14 "to him alone You showed the end of the time"** — the Latin and the Syriac read *the end of the times*, the close of the ages. Laurence's English of his manuscript has *the termination of his life*, his Latin *finem temporis ejus*, **the end of his time**. *The end of the time* leaves both readings open.
- **vv20–22 "You did not take the evil heart away from them… the evil root"** — the question the whole book asks begins here. Genesis says *the shaping of the human heart is evil from his youth* (Genesis 8:21). The law was given, but the evil heart was not taken away, and so the law could not bear its fruit.
