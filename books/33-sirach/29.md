# Sirach 29

**1** Whoever shows mercy will lend to his neighbor,  
and whoever strengthens him with his hand keeps the commandments.

**2** Lend to your neighbor in the time of his need,  
and in turn repay your neighbor on time.

**3** Keep your word and keep faith with him,  
and at all times you will find what you need.

**4** Many have treated a loan as a windfall,  
and have brought trouble on those who helped them.

**5** Until he gets it, he will kiss the lender's hand,  
and speak humbly about his neighbor's money;  
but when it is time to repay, he will drag out the time,  
repay with words of indifference,  
and blame the times.

**6** If the lender prevails, he will hardly get back half,  
and will count that a windfall;  
if not, he has been robbed of his money,  
and has made an enemy at his own cost,  
who will repay him with curses and abuse,  
and instead of honor will repay him with dishonor.

**7** Because of wickedness many have refused to lend;  
they were afraid of being robbed for nothing.

**8** Yet be patient with a man in humble circumstances,  
and for charity's sake give him time.

**9** For the commandment's sake help the poor man,  
and in his need do not send him away empty.

**10** Lose your silver for a brother and a friend,  
and do not let it rust under a stone and be lost.

**11** Lay up your treasure by the commandments of the Most High,  
and it will profit you more than gold.

**12** Store up charity in your storerooms,  
and it will rescue you from every harm;

**13** better than a mighty shield and a strong spear,  
it will fight for you against the enemy.

**14** A good man will stand surety for his neighbor,  
but one who has lost all shame will abandon him.

**15** Do not forget the kindness of your surety,  
for he has given his life for you.

**16** A sinner will ruin the goods of his surety,

**17** and one ungrateful at heart will abandon his rescuer.

**18** Surety has ruined many who were prospering,  
and tossed them like a wave of the sea;  
it has driven powerful men from their homes,  
and they have wandered among foreign nations.

**19** A sinner who falls into surety  
and chases profit from contracts will fall into lawsuits.

**20** Help your neighbor as far as you are able,  
and take care that you do not fall yourself.

**21** The first needs of life are water and bread and clothing,  
and a house to cover one's nakedness.

**22** Better is a poor man's life under a shelter of logs  
than splendid food in other men's houses.

**23** Be content with little or much.

**24** It is a miserable life to go from house to house;  
where a man lives as a guest, he will not open his mouth.

**25** You will entertain and pour drinks and get no thanks,  
and on top of this you will hear bitter words:

**26** "Come here, guest, set the table;  
if you have anything in your hand, give it to me to eat."

**27** "Out, guest, make way for someone of honor;  
my brother has come to stay with me; I need the room."

**28** These things are hard for a man of understanding:  
reproach over his lodging, and the insults of a moneylender.

## Notes

- **vv1–2 "Whoever shows mercy will lend to his neighbor"** — lending as an act of mercy, as the law commands it: *Rather, you shall open, open your hand to him, and lend, lend him enough for his need* (Deuteronomy 15:8). Jesus: *do not turn away from the one who wants to borrow from you* (Matthew 5:42).
- **vv5–6 "repay with words of indifference"** — *apodōsei*, the verb of v2, *repay your neighbor on time*. What this borrower repays is words, then curses, then dishonor (v6, the same verb twice).
- **v8 "for charity's sake give him time"** — *ep' eleēmosynēn parelkyseis auton*, "you will draw him out toward charity." Swete prints no "not." The verb means to drag out or put off; set beside *be patient* in the line before, it is the lender giving a poor man more time and counting the delay as charity.
- **v11 "Lay up your treasure"** — Tobit 4:9, *storing up a good treasure for yourself against the day of need*; and Jesus, *store up for yourselves treasures in heaven* (Matthew 6:20).
- **v15 "he has given his life for you"** — *edōken tēn psychēn autou hyper sou*: the surety pledges himself in the debtor's place. Jesus uses the same words of Himself, *to give His life as a ransom for many* (*dounai tēn psychēn autou*, Mark 10:45).
- **vv14–20** — surety again: Proverbs warns against it altogether (6:1–5); Sirach allows it within one's means (8:13), and here, *as far as you are able* (v20).
- **vv24–27 "where a man lives as a guest"** — *paroikēsei*, and *paroike*, "guest," twice in the host's two speeches: the man with no house of his own must set the table, and then give up his room.
- **v28 "the insults of a moneylender"** — the chapter ends where it began, with lending.
