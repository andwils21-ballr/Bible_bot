# Sirach 37

**1** Every friend will say, "I too am his friend,"  
but there is a friend who is a friend in name only.

**2** Is it not a grief close to death  
when a companion and friend turns into an enemy?

**3** O wicked thought, where did you roll in from,  
to cover the dry land with deceit?

**4** A companion delights in a friend's gladness,  
but in a time of trouble he will be against him.

**5** A companion toils with a friend for the sake of his belly,  
and in the face of battle he will take up the shield.

**6** Do not forget a friend in your soul,  
and do not be unmindful of him in your wealth.

**7** Every counselor praises his own counsel,  
but there is one who counsels for his own sake.

**8** Guard yourself against a counselor,  
and first find out what he needs,  
for he will take counsel for himself;  
he may cast the lot over you,

**9** and say to you, "Your way is good,"  
and then stand off to see what will happen to you.

**10** Do not consult one who looks at you with suspicion,  
and hide your counsel from those who envy you.

**11** With a woman about her rival, with a coward about war,  
with a merchant about trade, with a buyer about selling,  
with a grudging man about gratitude,  
with a merciless man about kindness,  
with a lazy man about any work,  
with a hired man far from home about finishing the job,  
with an idle servant about much work —  
do not rely on these for any counsel.

**12** But stay close to a godly man,  
whom you know to keep the commandments,  
whose soul is like your own soul,  
and who will grieve with you if you stumble.

**13** Hold fast to the counsel of your own heart,  
for no one is more trustworthy to you than it.

**14** A man's soul sometimes tells him more  
than seven watchmen sitting high on a watchtower.

**15** Above all this, pray to the Most High  
that He may direct your way in truth.

**16** Reason is the beginning of every work,  
and counsel comes before every action.

**17** A change of heart leaves its track:

**18** four things spring up, good and evil, life and death,  
and what rules over them continually is the tongue.

**19** There is a shrewd man who teaches many,  
and is useless to himself.

**20** There is one who plays the wise man in words and is hated;  
he will end up lacking all food,

**21** for grace has not been given him from the Lord,  
because he has been stripped of all wisdom.

**22** There is one who is wise for his own soul,  
and the fruits of his understanding are trustworthy on his lips.

**23** A wise man will teach his own people,  
and the fruits of his understanding are trustworthy.

**24** A wise man will be filled with blessing,  
and all who see him will call him happy.

**25** A man's life is counted in days,  
but the days of Israel are without number.

**26** The wise man will inherit trust among his people,  
and his name will live forever.

**27** My child, test your soul while you live;  
see what is bad for it, and do not give it that.

**28** For not everything is good for everyone,  
and not every soul takes pleasure in everything.

**29** Do not be greedy for every delicacy,  
and do not throw yourself upon the food;

**30** for with much eating comes sickness,  
and greed will bring on vomiting.

**31** Through greed many have died,  
but whoever takes care will add to his life.

## Notes

- **v2 "a grief close to death"** — *lypē… heōs thanatou*. In Gethsemane, on the night a friend betrayed Him, Jesus says, *My soul is deeply grieved, to the point of death* (Matthew 26:38), in the same Greek words, *perilypos… heōs thanatou*.
- **v8 "he may cast the lot over you"** — *balē epi soi klēron*: he treats you as spoil to be divided, as in Psalm 22:18, *for my clothing they cast lots* (Greek *ebalon klēron*).
- **v11 "With a woman about her rival"** — the rival wife of 26:6; each person in the list is the one with the most to gain from bad advice on that very matter.
- **v11 "a hired man far from home"** — *misthiou aphestiou*, as Swete prints it, "a hired man without a hearth": a man with no stake in the place, who will not see the job through.
- **vv13–14 "Hold fast to the counsel of your own heart… more than seven watchmen"** — after every outside counselor has been weighed, the heart is the most trustworthy, *pistoteros*; and above it, prayer (v15).
- **vv17–18 "good and evil, life and death… the tongue"** — the choice of Deuteronomy 30:15, *I have set before you today life and good, and death and evil*, and the power Proverbs gives the tongue, *Death and life are in the power of the tongue* (18:21).
- **v28 "For not everything is good for everyone"** — *ou panta pasin sympherei*. Paul uses the same verb: *All things are lawful for me, but not all things are beneficial* (*ou panta sympherei*, 1 Corinthians 6:12).
- **vv29–31** — the warning of the great table in 31:12–21 again: greed at food ends in *vomiting*, the same word, *cholera*.
