# Sirach 38

**1** Honor the physician with the honor due him for your needs,  
for the Lord created him too.

**2** For healing comes from the Most High,  
and he will receive a gift from the king.

**3** The physician's skill will lift up his head,  
and he will be admired in the presence of great men.

**4** The Lord created medicines out of the earth,  
and a sensible man will not despise them.

**5** Was not water made sweet by wood,  
so that its power might be known?

**6** He gave skill to men,  
so that He might be glorified in His wonders.

**7** With them He heals and takes away pain;

**8** the apothecary makes a mixture from them.  
His works will never come to an end,  
and from Him well-being is over the face of the earth.

**9** My child, when you are ill, do not neglect it,  
but pray to the Lord, and He will heal you.

**10** Put away your wrongdoing and make your hands straight,  
and cleanse your heart from all sin.

**11** Offer a fragrant offering and a memorial of fine flour,  
and make your offering rich, as one who will soon be no more.

**12** Then give the physician his place, for the Lord created him too;  
do not let him leave you, for you need him.

**13** There is a time when success lies in their hands too,

**14** for they too will pray to the Lord  
to grant them success in bringing relief  
and healing, to preserve life.

**15** Whoever sins before his Maker,  
may he fall into the hands of the physician.

**16** My child, let your tears fall over the dead,  
and begin the lament as one who suffers terribly;  
wrap his body as is his due,  
and do not neglect his burial.

**17** Make the weeping bitter and the mourning warm,  
and keep the mourning as he deserves,  
a day or two, to avoid reproach;  
then be comforted for your grief's sake.

**18** For grief leads to death,  
and grief of heart saps strength.

**19** When he is carried away, grief too passes;  
a poor man's life is against the heart.

**20** Do not give your heart over to grief;  
drive it away, remembering your own end.

**21** Do not forget: there is no coming back;  
you will do him no good, and you will harm yourself.

**22** Remember that his sentence will also be yours:  
"Yesterday for me, and today for you."

**23** When the dead is at rest, let his memory rest,  
and be comforted for him when his spirit has gone out.

**24** The wisdom of the scribe comes through the opportunity of leisure,  
and whoever has little business will become wise.

**25** How can he become wise who holds the plow  
and boasts in the shaft of the goad,  
who drives oxen and is busy with their labors,  
and whose talk is about bullocks?

**26** He sets his heart on turning furrows,  
and he lies awake over fodder for the heifers.

**27** So it is with every craftsman and master builder,  
who works by night as by day;  
those who engrave seals,  
whose patience goes into varying the design:  
he sets his heart on making the likeness true,  
and he lies awake to finish the work.

**28** So it is with the smith sitting by the anvil,  
intent on the iron he works;  
the breath of the fire withers his flesh,  
and he struggles with the heat of the furnace;  
the sound of the hammer is always new in his ear,  
and his eyes are on the pattern of the vessel;  
he sets his heart on finishing his works,  
and he lies awake to adorn them at the end.

**29** So it is with the potter sitting at his work  
and turning the wheel with his feet,  
always anxious over his work,  
and all he makes is counted;

**30** with his arm he shapes the clay,  
and with his feet he bends its strength;  
he sets his heart on finishing the glaze,  
and he lies awake to clean the kiln.

**31** All these rely on their hands,  
and each is wise in his own work.

**32** Without them no city can be built,  
and no one could live there as a guest or walk its streets.

**33** In the assembly they do not rise to the top,  
and they do not ponder the covenant of judgment;  
they do not sit on the judge's seat,  
nor do they declare righteousness and judgment,  
and they are not found among the makers of proverbs.

**34** But they keep the fabric of the world standing,  
and their prayer is in the work of their craft.

## Notes

- **v15 "may he fall into the hands of the physician"** — *empesoi*, a wish: either sickness is the sinner's due, or the sinner too must be sent to the physician. The Greek leaves both.
