# Sirach 46

**1** Joshua son of Nun was mighty in war,  
and Moses' successor in prophecy;  
he became, as his name says,  
great for the salvation of His chosen,  
to take vengeance on the enemies that rose up,  
so that he might give Israel its inheritance.

**2** How glorious he was when he lifted up his hands  
and stretched out the sword against the cities!

**3** Who before him stood like that?  
For the Lord Himself brought the enemies to him.

**4** Was it not by his hand that the sun went back,  
and one day became as two?

**5** He called on the Most High, the Mighty One,  
when enemies pressed him on every side,  
and the great Lord heard them  
with hailstones of mighty power.

**6** He hurled war upon that nation,  
and at the descent He destroyed those who resisted,  
so that the nations might know their armor,  
that his war was before the Lord;  
for he followed after the Mighty One.

**7** In the days of Moses he did a loyal deed,  
he and Caleb son of Jephunneh:  
they stood against the enemy,  
kept the people from sin,  
and silenced the wicked grumbling.

**8** These two alone were saved  
out of six hundred thousand men on foot,  
to bring them into their inheritance,  
into a land flowing with milk and honey.

**9** The Lord gave Caleb strength,  
and it stayed with him into old age,  
so that he went up to the heights of the land,  
and his offspring kept it as an inheritance,

**10** so that all the sons of Israel might see  
that it is good to follow the Lord.

**11** The judges also, each by his own name,  
whose hearts did not give themselves away,  
and who did not turn away from the Lord —  
may their memory be blessed!

**12** May their bones flourish again from their place,  
and their name be passed on  
to the sons of those who were honored!

**13** Beloved by his Lord,  
the prophet of the Lord set up the kingdom,  
and anointed rulers over His people.

**14** By the law of the Lord he judged the congregation,  
and the Lord watched over Jacob.

**15** By his faithfulness he was proved a prophet,  
and by his faithfulness he was known to be trustworthy in vision.

**16** He called on the Lord, the Mighty One,  
when his enemies pressed him on every side,  
with the offering of a suckling lamb;

**17** and the Lord thundered from heaven,  
and made His voice heard with a great noise,

**18** and He crushed the leaders of the Tyrians  
and all the rulers of the Philistines.

**19** Before the time of his everlasting sleep  
he bore witness before the Lord and His anointed:  
"I have taken no one's property, not even a sandal."  
No one accused him.

**20** Even after he had fallen asleep he prophesied,  
and showed the king his end;  
he lifted up his voice from the earth in prophecy,  
to blot out the lawlessness of the people.

## Notes

- **v1 "Joshua son of Nun… as his name says, great for the salvation of His chosen"** — *Iēsous Nauē*: Joshua's name in Greek is *Iēsous*, the name Jesus, and it carries the same meaning: the angel told Joseph, *you shall call His name Jesus, for He will save His people from their sins* (Matthew 1:21). On the same name at 43:23, see the note there.
- **v2 "stretched out the sword against the cities"** — Joshua 8:18, *Joshua stretched out the javelin that was in his hand toward the city*.
- **vv4–6 "one day became as two… hailstones… at the descent"** — Joshua 10:11–13: the hailstones *on the descent of Beth-horon*, and *the sun stood still, and the moon stopped*.
- **v6 "he followed after the Mighty One"** — the words used of Caleb and Joshua alone: *they have followed the LORD fully* (Numbers 32:12; Greek *synepēkolouthēsen opisō Kyriou*).
- **vv7–8 "they stood against the enemy… These two alone were saved"** — Numbers 14:6–10, 30. Swete prints *echthrou*, "the enemy"; in Numbers 14:10 it was *the whole congregation* that wanted to stone them. "Six hundred thousand men on foot" — Numbers 11:21.
- **v9 "The Lord gave Caleb strength, and it stayed with him into old age"** — Joshua 14:11, *I am still as strong today as I was on the day Moses sent me*.
- **v11 "whose hearts did not give themselves away"** — *exeporneusen*, the word used of Israel in the days of the judges: *they gave themselves to other gods* (Judges 2:17).
- **v13 "the prophet of the Lord"** — Swete's Greek does not name him here; vv14–20 show who he is, Samuel, who anointed Saul and David (1 Samuel 10:1; 16:13) and spoke to Saul after his death (v20; 1 Samuel 28:19).
- **v15 "he was known to be trustworthy"** — *pistos*, the word of the Greek of 1 Samuel 3:20 (*pistos Samouēl eis prophētēn*), *all Israel… knew that Samuel was established as a prophet of the LORD*.
- **v19 "I have taken no one's property, not even a sandal"** — Samuel's farewell, *Testify against me before the LORD and before His anointed: Whose ox have I taken?* (1 Samuel 12:3).
