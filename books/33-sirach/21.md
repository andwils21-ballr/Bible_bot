# Sirach 21

**1** My child, have you sinned? Do not do it again,  
and pray about your former sins.

**2** Flee from sin as from a snake;  
for if you go near it, it will bite you.  
Its teeth are a lion's teeth,  
destroying the souls of men.

**3** All lawlessness is like a two-edged sword;  
there is no healing for its wound.

**4** Terror and violence will lay waste wealth;  
so the house of the proud will be laid waste.

**5** The prayer of a poor man goes from his mouth to His ears,  
and His judgment comes quickly.

**6** Whoever hates reproof walks in the steps of a sinner,  
but he who fears the Lord will turn back in his heart.

**7** The man mighty in speech is known far and wide,  
but the man of understanding knows when he slips.

**8** Whoever builds his house with other people's money  
is like one who gathers his stones for the winter.

**9** An assembly of the lawless is tow gathered together,  
and their end is a flame of fire.

**10** The way of sinners is smoothly paved with stones,  
but at its end is the pit of Hades.

**11** Whoever keeps the law masters his thoughts,  
and the fulfillment of the fear of the Lord is wisdom.

**12** One who is not shrewd cannot be taught,  
but there is a shrewdness that multiplies bitterness.

**13** The knowledge of a wise man will increase like a flood,  
and his counsel like a fountain of life.

**14** The inside of a fool is like a broken jar;  
it will hold no knowledge.

**15** When a man of knowledge hears a wise saying,  
he will praise it and add to it;  
when a self-indulgent man hears it, it displeases him,  
and he throws it behind his back.

**16** A fool's talk is like a burden on a journey,  
but grace will be found on the lips of the intelligent.

**17** The mouth of a sensible man will be sought in the assembly,  
and they will ponder his words in their heart.

**18** Like a house in ruins is wisdom to a fool,  
and the knowledge of the senseless is talk without sense.

**19** To the senseless, discipline is fetters on the feet,  
and like handcuffs on the right hand.

**20** A fool raises his voice when he laughs,  
but a shrewd man will barely smile, and quietly.

**21** To a sensible man discipline is like a golden ornament,  
and like a bracelet on the right arm.

**22** The foot of a fool is quick to enter a house,  
but a man of experience holds back out of respect.

**23** A foolish man peers into the house from the door,  
but a well-taught man will stand outside.

**24** It is bad manners for a man to listen at a door,  
and the sensible man would be weighed down by the disgrace.

**25** The lips of strangers will be heavy with these things,  
but the words of the sensible will be weighed in the balance.

**26** The heart of fools is in their mouth,  
but the mouth of the wise is in their heart.

**27** When the ungodly man curses the adversary,  
he curses his own soul.

**28** The whisperer defiles his own soul,  
and is hated wherever he lives.

## Notes

- **v1 "have you sinned? Do not do it again"** — compare Jesus to the woman: *go, and from now on sin no more* (John 8:11).
- **v3 "a two-edged sword"** — *rhomphaia distomos*, the sword that Revelation sees coming from the mouth of the Son of Man (Revelation 1:16); here, the wound sin leaves.
- **v5 "from his mouth to His ears"** — the poor man's prayer goes straight to God (Exodus 22:23).
- **v8 "for the winter"** — *eis cheimōna*, as Swete prints it; the line is obscure: perhaps stones gathered in a season when no one can build.
- **v9 "tow"** — the fiber of flax, which flares up and is gone (Isaiah 1:31).
- **v10 "smoothly paved… the pit of Hades"** — compare Proverbs 14:12, *there is a way that seems right to a man, but its end is the ways of death*, and the broad way of Matthew 7:13.
- **v15 "a self-indulgent man"** — *spatalōn*, the word Paul uses of the widow *who is self-indulgent* (1 Timothy 5:6).
- **vv22–24** — manners at a door, the reverse of the seeker of 14:23 who peers in at wisdom's window.
- **v27 "When the ungodly man curses the adversary"** — *ton satanan*, "the adversary" or "Satan": he blames the devil, or his own opponent, and the curse falls on himself, since the enemy is within. The Greek uses the same word for the accuser of Job 1–2.
