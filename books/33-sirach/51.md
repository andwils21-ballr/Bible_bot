# Sirach 51

**1** *Prayer of Jesus son of Sirach.*  
I will give You thanks, Lord and King,  
and praise You, God my Savior;  
I give thanks to Your name.

**2** For You have been my shelter and my helper,  
and redeemed my body from destruction,  
from the snare of the slandering tongue,  
from lips that work lies;  
and before those who stood by  
You were my helper,

**3** and redeemed me, by the greatness of Your mercy and of Your name,  
from the gnashing of teeth, when I was ready to be devoured,  
from the hand of those who sought my life,  
from the many afflictions I had,

**4** from the choking fire all around me,  
and from the middle of a fire I had not kindled,

**5** from the depths of the belly of Hades,  
from an unclean tongue and a lying word.

**6** There was slander to the king from an unjust tongue;  
My soul gave praise even to death,  
and my life was close to Hades below.

**7** They surrounded me on every side, and there was no one to help;  
I looked for help from men, and there was none.

**8** Then I remembered Your mercy, Lord,  
and Your works from of old,  
that You rescue those who wait for You,  
and save them from the hand of the nations.

**9** I lifted up my plea from the earth,  
and prayed for rescue from death.

**10** I called on the Lord, the Father of my lord,  
not to forsake me in the days of affliction,  
in the time of the proud, when there was no help.

**11** I will praise Your name continually,  
and sing hymns with thanksgiving;  
and my prayer was heard.

**12** For You saved me from destruction,  
and rescued me from an evil time;  
therefore I will give thanks and praise You,  
and bless the name of the Lord.

**13** While I was still young, before I went wandering,  
I sought wisdom openly in my prayer.

**14** Before the temple I asked for her,  
and to the very end I will seek her out.

**15** From the blossom to the ripening grape  
my heart rejoiced in her;  
my foot walked in uprightness;  
from my youth I followed her tracks.

**16** I bent my ear a little and received her,  
and found much discipline for myself.

**17** I made progress in her;  
to the One who gives me wisdom I will give glory.

**18** For I resolved to live by her,  
and I was zealous for the good,  
and I will never be put to shame.

**19** My soul has wrestled for her,  
and in the practice of fasting I was strict;  
I spread out my hands to the heights,  
and mourned my ignorance of her.

**20** I directed my soul to her,  
and gained a heart with them from the beginning,  
and in purity I found her;  
therefore I will never be forsaken.

**21** My heart was stirred to seek her out;  
therefore I gained a good possession.

**22** The Lord gave me a tongue as my reward,  
and with it I will praise Him.

**23** Come near to me, you who are untaught,  
and lodge in the house of discipline.

**24** Why do you say you lack these things,  
while your souls are very thirsty?

**25** I opened my mouth and said:  
"Get her for yourselves without silver.

**26** Put your neck under the yoke,  
and let your soul receive discipline;  
she is near to be found.

**27** See with your eyes that I labored little,  
and found for myself much rest.

**28** Get a share of discipline with a great sum of silver,  
and you will gain much gold by her."

**29** May your soul rejoice in His mercy,  
and may you never be ashamed of praising Him.

**30** Do your work before the time,  
and in His time He will give you your reward.

## Notes

- **v1 "*Prayer of Jesus son of Sirach*"** — a heading in the Greek text. The book closes with a thanksgiving (vv1–12) and a poem on the search for wisdom (vv13–30).
- **vv2–6 "from the snare of the slandering tongue… Slander from an unjust tongue reached the king"** — the writer was accused before a king and came close to death; the prayer does not say more.
- **v4 "from the middle of a fire I had not kindled"** — rescue from the fire, as in Isaiah's promise, *when you walk through fire you shall not be burned* (Greek of Isaiah 43:2, *ou mē katakauthēs*). He had done nothing to start the blaze.
- **v6 "My soul gave praise even to death"** — *ēnesen heōs thanatou hē psychē mou*, "my soul praised to death," as Swete prints it: brought to the edge of Hades, he praised.
- **v10 "the Lord, the Father of my lord"** — *Kyrion patera kyriou mou*, as Swete prints it. The pair of words is the pair of Psalm 110:1, *The LORD says to my lord*.
- **vv13–15 "While I was still young… From the blossom to the ripening grape"** — the writer's search for wisdom from youth on, like a vine from flower to harvest.
- **v19 "in the practice of fasting I was strict"** — *en poiēsei limou*, "in the doing of hunger," as Swete prints it.
- **vv23–26 "Come near to me… Get her for yourselves without silver… Put your neck under the yoke"** — the call of Isaiah 55:1 in the Greek, *buy… without silver* (*aneu argyriou*), and the yoke of wisdom, which Jesus takes up: *Take My yoke upon you and learn from Me… and you will find rest for your souls* (Matthew 11:29). Here too, *I labored little, and found for myself much rest* (v27).
- **v28 "with a great sum of silver… you will gain much gold by her"** — the free gift of v25 turns out to be the best bargain: spend silver on discipline, and it repays in gold.
