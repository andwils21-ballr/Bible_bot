# Sirach 33

**1** No evil will meet the one who fears the Lord;  
but in testing he will be rescued again.

**2** A wise man will not hate the law,  
but one who is a hypocrite about it is like a boat in a storm.

**3** A man of understanding will trust in the law,  
and the law is as trustworthy to him as the inquiry of the righteous.

**4** Prepare what you will say, and so you will be heard;  
bind your learning together, and then answer.

**5** The heart of a fool is like a cartwheel,  
and his reasoning like a turning axle.

**6** A stallion at stud is like a mocking friend:  
he neighs under every rider.

**7** Why is one day better than another,  
when all the daylight of the year is from the sun?

**8** By the Lord's knowledge they were made distinct,  
and He made the seasons and feasts different.

**9** Some of them He exalted and set apart,  
and some of them He counted among the ordinary days.

**10** All men too are from the ground,  
and Adam was created out of earth.

**11** In the fullness of His knowledge the Lord made them distinct,  
and made their ways different.

**12** Some of them He blessed and exalted,  
and some He set apart and brought near to Himself;  
some He cursed and brought low,  
and turned them out of their place.

**13** Like clay in the potter's hand,  
all its ways as he pleases,  
so are men in the hand of Him who made them,  
to repay them as He judges.

**14** Good is set against evil,  
and life against death;  
so the sinner is set against the godly.

**15** Look in this way at all the works of the Most High:  
two by two, one against the other.

**16** I was the last to keep watch,  
like one gleaning after the grape pickers;  
by the blessing of the Lord I came in ahead,  
and like a grape picker I filled my winepress.

**17** Consider that I have not labored for myself alone,  
but for all who seek discipline.

**18** Listen to me, you great men of the people,  
and you leaders of the assembly, give ear.

**19** To son or wife, to brother or friend,  
do not give power over you while you live;  
and do not give your property to another,  
or you may change your mind and have to ask for it back.

**20** While you still live and have breath in you,  
do not trade yourself away to anyone.

**21** It is better that your children ask of you  
than that you look to the hands of your sons.

**22** In all your works keep the upper hand;  
do not let a blemish mar your honor.

**23** When the days of your life come to an end,  
at the time of your death, distribute your inheritance.

**24** Fodder and a stick and burdens for a donkey;  
bread and discipline and work for a servant.

**25** Put your servant to work, and you will find rest;  
leave his hands idle, and he will look for freedom.

**26** Yoke and strap will bow the neck,  
and for a wicked servant there are racks and tortures.

**27** Put him to work so that he is not idle,  
for idleness has taught much evil.

**28** Set him to tasks that fit him,  
and if he does not obey, make his fetters heavier.

**29** But do not go to excess with anyone,  
and do nothing without justice.

**30** If you have a servant, let him be as yourself,  
because you acquired him with blood.

**31** If you have a servant, treat him as yourself,  
for you need him as you need your own soul.  
If you mistreat him and he sets off and runs away,  
on which road will you look for him?

## Notes

- **vv1–31** — vv1–15 and the first words of v16 are Swete's 36:1–16; the rest of v16 and vv17–31 are his 30:25–40, divided into verses differently (see the note on 30:25).
- **vv2–3 "will not hate the law… will trust in the law, and the law is as trustworthy to him"** — *enpisteusei nomō, kai ho nomos autō pistos*: he trusts the law, and the law keeps faith with him. The chapter before ends on the same word, *Whoever trusts the law* (32:24). "The inquiry of the righteous" — *erōtēma dikaiōn*, as Swete prints it: a question put to God and answered.
- **vv7–12 "exalted and set apart… blessed and exalted… set apart"** — *hēgiasen* and *eulogēsen*, the two verbs of Genesis 2:3, *God blessed the seventh day and set it apart*. As God set some days above others, He sets some men above others; *Adam was created out of earth* recalls Genesis 2:7, *The LORD God shaped the human of dust from the ground*.
- **vv12–13 "Like clay in the potter's hand"** — Paul asks the same of the potter and the clay: *Does the potter not have authority over the clay, to make from the same lump one vessel for honor and another for dishonor?* (Romans 9:21).
- **vv14–15 "two by two, one against the other"** — *dyo dyo, hen katenanti tou henos*; the thought returns in 42:24, *All things are in pairs, one against the other*.
- **v16 "I was the last to keep watch… I came in ahead"** — *eschatos ēgrypnēsa… ephthasa*: he came last, a gleaner after the harvest, and yet arrived first and filled the press.
- **v17 "I have not labored for myself alone"** — the line he wrote at the end of wisdom's speech (24:34), *See that I have not labored for myself alone, but for all who seek her*; here, *all who seek discipline*.
- **vv30–31 "let him be as yourself… treat him as yourself"** — *hōs sy… hōs seauton*, the words of Leviticus 19:34 for the guest: *you shall love him as yourself*. "With blood" — *en haimati ektēsō auton*: what he cost you, paid as dearly as blood.
