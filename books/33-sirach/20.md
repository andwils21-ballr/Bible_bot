# Sirach 20

**1** There is a reproof that is untimely,  
and there is a man who keeps silent, and he is wise.

**2** How much better it is to reprove than to fume!  
The one who confesses will be kept from loss.

**3** *(This verse is not in the Greek text followed here; see the note.)*

**4** Like a eunuch's desire to violate a girl  
is the man who does justice by force.

**5** There is one who keeps silent and is found wise,  
and another who is hated for his much talk.

**6** There is one who keeps silent because he has no answer,  
and another who keeps silent because he knows the right time.

**7** A wise man will keep silent until the right time,  
but a braggart and fool will let the time go by.

**8** Whoever uses many words will be abhorred,  
and whoever takes authority upon himself will be hated.

**9** There is success that a man finds in misfortune,  
and there is a windfall that turns into loss.

**10** There is a gift that will profit you nothing,  
and there is a gift that brings a double return.

**11** There is loss because of glory,  
and there is one who has lifted his head from humiliation.

**12** There is one who buys much for little,  
and pays for it seven times over.

**13** The wise man makes himself loved by his words,  
but the courtesies of fools are poured out for nothing.

**14** A fool's gift will profit you nothing,  
for his eyes are many instead of one.

**15** He gives little and reproaches much,  
and opens his mouth like a herald;  
today he lends, and tomorrow he asks it back;  
such a man is hateful.

**16** The fool says, "I have no friend,  
and there is no thanks for my good deeds;  
those who eat my bread have evil tongues."

**17** How often, and by how many, will he be laughed at!

**18** A slip on the pavement is better than a slip of the tongue;  
so the fall of the wicked will come quickly.

**19** A graceless man is like a story told at the wrong time;  
it will be always on the lips of the undisciplined.

**20** A proverb from a fool's lips will be rejected,  
for he does not tell it at its proper time.

**21** There is one who is kept from sinning by his want,  
and when he rests, his conscience does not trouble him.

**22** There is one who destroys his own soul through shame,  
and destroys it out of regard for a fool.

**23** There is one who out of shame makes promises to a friend,  
and gains him as an enemy for nothing.

**24** A lie is an ugly blemish on a man;  
it will be always on the lips of the undisciplined.

**25** A thief is preferable to a habitual liar,  
but both will inherit destruction.

**26** The way of a liar is disgrace,  
and his shame is always with him.  
*Sayings in proverbs.*

**27** The wise man will advance himself by his words,  
and a sensible man will please great men.

**28** Whoever tills the soil will heap up his harvest,  
and whoever pleases great men will win pardon for wrongdoing.

**29** Gifts and presents blind the eyes of the wise;  
like a muzzle on the mouth they turn away reproofs.

**30** Hidden wisdom and unseen treasure —  
what use is there in either?

**31** Better is a man who hides his folly  
than a man who hides his wisdom.

## Notes

- **v3** — Swete's Greek gives this verse number and no words (see the note on 1:5).
- **v4 "Like a eunuch's desire to violate a girl"** — the desire that cannot be fulfilled, and is wrong besides: so is justice done by force. The picture returns in 30:20.
- **vv5–7 "There is one who keeps silent… because he knows the right time"** — *a time to keep silent, and a time to speak* (Ecclesiastes 3:7); *Even a fool who keeps silent is considered wise* (Proverbs 17:28).
- **v14 "his eyes are many instead of one"** — he gives one gift and looks for many in return.
- **v20 "A proverb from a fool's lips will be rejected"** — Reproof 2:7 (Proverbs 26:7), *Like the legs of the lame, which hang useless, is a proverb in the mouth of fools*.
- **v26 "Sayings in proverbs."** — a heading written into the Greek text, as at 18:30, over the sayings that follow.
- **v28 "will win pardon for wrongdoing"** — *exilasetai adikian*, "will atone for wrong": the favor of the great covers a man's faults.
- **v29 "Gifts and presents blind the eyes of the wise"** — the words of the Law, *a bribe blinds the eyes of the wise* (Deuteronomy 16:19).
- **vv30–31 "Hidden wisdom and unseen treasure"** — compare the servant who hid his talent in the ground (Matthew 25:25).
