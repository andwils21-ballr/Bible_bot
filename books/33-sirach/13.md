# Sirach 13

**1** Whoever touches pitch will be defiled,  
and whoever keeps company with a proud man will become like him.

**2** Do not lift a weight beyond your strength,  
and do not keep company with one stronger and richer than you.  
What has the clay pot in common with the kettle?  
The one will strike against it, and the pot will be smashed.

**3** A rich man does wrong, and he himself snorts with indignation;  
a poor man is wronged, and he himself must beg pardon.

**4** If you are of use to him, he will work you;  
and if you are in need, he will forsake you.

**5** If you have something, he will live with you and drain you dry,  
and he himself will feel no pain.

**6** When he needs you, he will deceive you,  
and smile at you and give you hope;  
he will speak kindly to you and say, "What do you need?"

**7** He will shame you with his food,  
until he has drained you two or three times,  
and at last he will mock you.  
Afterward, when he sees you, he will pass you by,  
and shake his head at you.

**8** Take care not to be led astray,  
and not to be humbled in your merrymaking.

**9** When a powerful man invites you, hold back,  
and he will invite you all the more.

**10** Do not push forward, or you may be pushed away;  
and do not stand too far off, or you may be forgotten.

**11** Do not presume to speak with him as an equal,  
nor trust his many words;  
for by much talk he will test you,  
and while smiling he will examine you.

**12** Merciless is he who does not keep your words to himself;  
he will not spare you harm or chains.

**13** Keep them to yourself and be very careful,  
for you are walking with your own downfall.

**14** *(This verse is not in the Greek text followed here; see the note.)*

**15** Every creature loves its like,  
and every man his neighbor;

**16** all flesh gathers by kind,  
and a man clings to one like himself.

**17** What fellowship has a wolf with a lamb?  
No more has a sinner with the godly.

**18** What peace is there between a hyena and a dog?  
What peace between the rich and the poor?

**19** Wild donkeys in the wilderness are the prey of lions;  
so the poor are the pasture of the rich.

**20** Humility is detestable to a proud man;  
so a poor man is detestable to a rich one.

**21** When a rich man totters, he is held up by his friends,  
but when a humble man falls, he is pushed away by his friends as well.

**22** When a rich man slips, many come to his aid;  
he says what should not be said, and they justify him.  
A humble man slips, and they rebuke him besides;  
he speaks sense, and no place is given him.

**23** The rich man speaks, and all are silent,  
and they extol his word to the clouds;  
the poor man speaks, and they say, "Who is this?"  
and if he stumbles, they push him down.

**24** Riches are good if there is no sin in them,  
and poverty is evil in the mouth of the godly.

**25** A man's heart changes his face,  
whether for good or for evil.

**26** The sign of a heart in good spirits is a cheerful face,  
but the devising of proverbs is wearisome thinking.

## Notes

- **v2 "the clay pot… the kettle"** — earthenware against bronze: when they meet, only one breaks.
- **vv3–7** — how a rich man uses a poor one: he works him, drains him, feeds him to shame him, and shakes his head at him.
- **vv9–10 "When a powerful man invites you, hold back, and he will invite you all the more"** — compare Jesus' counsel to take the lowest place at a feast (Luke 14:8–10).
- **v14** — Swete's Greek gives this verse number and no words (see the note on 1:5).
- **v17 "What fellowship has a wolf with a lamb?"** — compare Paul, *what fellowship has light with darkness?* (2 Corinthians 6:14); only in the age to come will *the wolf dwell with the lamb* (Isaiah 11:6).
- **v21 "When a rich man totters, he is held up by his friends"** — Proverbs 19:4, *Wealth adds many friends, but the poor man is separated from his friend*.
- **v23 "the poor man speaks, and they say, 'Who is this?'"** — as in Ecclesiastes 9:16, *the poor man's wisdom is despised and his words are not heard*.
- **v24 "poverty is evil in the mouth of the godly"** — *eusebous*, "godly," as Swete prints it: even the godly call poverty an evil. The line reads more easily with *asebous*, "ungodly."
- **v26 "the devising of proverbs is wearisome thinking"** — compare Ecclesiastes 12:12, *much study is a weariness of the flesh*.
