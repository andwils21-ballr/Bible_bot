# Sirach 22

**1** The sluggard is like a filthy stone,  
and everyone hisses at his disgrace.

**2** The sluggard is like a lump of dung;  
anyone who picks it up shakes it off his hand.

**3** It is a father's disgrace to have fathered an undisciplined son,  
and a daughter is born to his loss.

**4** A sensible daughter will gain a husband of her own,  
but one who brings shame is a grief to her father.

**5** A bold daughter brings shame on father and husband,  
and will be despised by both.

**6** Like music in mourning is a story told at the wrong time,  
but whips and discipline are wisdom at every time.

**7** Whoever teaches a fool glues potsherds together,  
or rouses a sleeper from deep sleep.

**8** Whoever tells a story to a fool tells it to a man half asleep,  
and at the end he will say, "What is it?"

**11** Weep for the dead, for he has lost the light;  
and weep for the fool, for he has lost understanding.  
Weep less bitterly for the dead, for he has found rest;  
but the life of the fool is worse than death.

**12** Mourning for the dead lasts seven days,  
but for a fool and an ungodly man, all the days of his life.

**13** Do not talk much with a fool,  
and do not visit a senseless man;  
guard yourself from him, so that you may not have trouble,  
nor be spattered when he shakes himself;  
turn away from him and you will find rest,  
and you will not be worn out by his madness.

**14** What is heavier than lead?  
What is its name but "Fool"?

**15** Sand and salt and a lump of iron  
are easier to bear than a senseless man.

**16** Timber bound firmly into a building  
will not come loose in an earthquake;  
so a heart set firm on a well-considered plan  
will not be afraid in a crisis.

**17** A heart fixed on sensible thinking  
is like the sanded plaster on a smooth wall.

**18** Stakes set on a height  
will not stand against the wind;  
so a timid heart with a fool's thoughts  
will not stand against any fear.

**19** Whoever pricks the eye brings tears,  
and whoever pricks the heart brings feelings to light.

**20** One who throws a stone at birds scares them away,  
and one who reviles a friend breaks off the friendship.

**21** If you have drawn a sword against a friend,  
do not despair, for there is a way back.

**22** If you have opened your mouth against a friend,  
do not worry, for there can be reconciliation;  
but reviling, arrogance, the telling of a secret, and a treacherous blow —  
from these any friend will flee.

**23** Win your neighbor's trust in his poverty,  
so that you may share in his good things with him;  
stand by him in time of trouble,  
so that you may share his inheritance with him.

**24** Before the fire come the vapor and smoke of the furnace;  
so before bloodshed come insults.

**25** I will not be ashamed to shelter a friend,  
and I will not hide from him;

**26** and if harm comes to me because of him,  
everyone who hears of it will beware of him.

**27** Who will set a guard over my mouth,  
and a shrewd seal upon my lips,  
so that I may not fall because of them,  
and my tongue may not destroy me?

## Notes

- **vv1–2** — the sluggard, as in Proverbs 6:6–11 and 24:30–34, here in two pictures nobody wants to touch.
- **vv3–5** — the line as it stands calls the birth of a daughter a loss to her father; vv4–5 then set the sensible daughter, who gains a husband, beside the bold one, who shames both father and husband.
- **v6 "Like music in mourning"** — compare Reproof 1:20 (Proverbs 25:20), *is one who sings songs to a heavy heart*.
- **vv9–10** — Swete's Greek gives these verse numbers and no words (see the note on 1:5).
- **v12 "Mourning for the dead lasts seven days"** — as Joseph *made mourning for his father seven days* (Genesis 50:10).
- **v13 "nor be spattered when he shakes himself"** — *entinagmos*, a shaking off, as a wet animal shakes itself over those nearby.
- **v15 "Sand and salt and a lump of iron are easier to bear"** — Reproof 3:3 (Proverbs 27:3), *A stone is heavy, and sand is weighty, but a fool's vexation is heavier than both*.
- **v22 "the telling of a secret"** — Reproof 1:9 (Proverbs 25:9), *do not reveal another's secret*.
- **v27 "Who will set a guard over my mouth"** — Psalm 141:3, *Set a guard, LORD, over my mouth*. The question opens a prayer that runs into the next chapter.
