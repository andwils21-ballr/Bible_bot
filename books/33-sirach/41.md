# Sirach 41

**1** O death, how bitter is the thought of you  
to a man at peace among his possessions,  
to a man with nothing to distract him, who prospers in everything,  
and is still strong enough to enjoy his food!

**2** O death, your sentence is welcome  
to a man in need whose strength is failing,  
in extreme old age and distracted by everything,  
who is contrary and has lost patience!

**3** Do not fear the sentence of death;  
remember those who came before you and those who come after;  
this is the sentence from the Lord on all flesh.

**4** Why do you reject the good pleasure of the Most High?  
Whether ten or a hundred or a thousand years,  
in Hades there is no inquiry into life.

**5** The children of sinners become detestable children,  
and they keep company where the ungodly live as guests.

**6** The inheritance of sinners' children will perish,  
and disgrace will stay with their offspring.

**7** Children will blame an ungodly father,  
because they suffer disgrace on his account.

**8** Woe to you, ungodly men,  
who have forsaken the law of God Most High!

**9** If you are born, you will be born for a curse,  
and if you die, a curse will be your portion.

**10** All that is from the earth will go back to the earth;  
so the ungodly go from a curse to destruction.

**11** Men mourn over their bodies,  
but the name of sinners, not being good, will be blotted out.

**12** Take care for your name,  
for it will stay with you longer than a thousand great treasures of gold.

**13** A good life has its number of days,  
but a good name lasts forever.

**14** Keep discipline in peace, my children.  
Hidden wisdom and unseen treasure —  
what use is there in either?

**15** Better is a man who hides his folly  
than a man who hides his wisdom.

**16** So feel shame as my word directs;  
for it is not good to keep every kind of shame,  
and not everything is approved by everyone in good faith.

**17** Be ashamed of unchastity before father and mother,  
and of a lie before a ruler and a prince;

**18** of an offense before a judge and a magistrate,  
of lawlessness before the congregation and the people,  
of injustice before a partner and a friend;

**19** of theft before the place where you live as a guest,  
and before the truth of God and the covenant;  
of leaning your elbow on the bread,  
of contempt in receiving and giving;

**20** of silence before those who greet you,  
of looking at a courtesan;

**21** of turning your face away from a relative,  
of taking away a portion or a gift,  
of gazing at another man's wife;

**22** of meddling with his maidservant —  
do not go near her bed —  
of insulting words before friends —  
after you have given, do not insult —

**23** of repeating what you have heard,  
and of revealing secrets.

**24** So you will be truly modest,  
and find favor before every man.

## Notes

- **vv1–24** — vv1–22 are Swete's 41:1–22, with his v4 divided between vv3 and 4; vv23–24 are the opening of his 42:1, whose last words begin English chapter 42.
- **vv1–2 "with nothing to distract him… distracted by everything"** — *aperispastō… perispōmenō*, the same word with and without its *not*: death is bitter to the man undistracted by trouble, and welcome to the man distracted by everything.
- **v4 "in Hades there is no inquiry into life"** — *elegmos*, "examining": in the grave no one asks whether a man lived ten years or a thousand. Ecclesiastes 9:10, *there is no work or reckoning or knowledge or wisdom in Sheol*.
- **vv11–13 "a good name lasts forever"** — Ecclesiastes 7:1, *A good name is better than precious oil, and the day of death than the day of one's birth*: the name outlives the body that is mourned (v11).
- **vv14–15** — the same two lines stand at 20:30–31, word for word in the Greek.
- **v16 "it is not good to keep every kind of shame"** — as in 4:21, *there is a shame that brings sin, and there is a shame that is glory and favor*. Here the two lists follow: what to be ashamed of (41:17–23), and what not to be ashamed of (42:1–8).
- **v19 "the place where you live as a guest"** — *hou paroikeis*: theft is a disgrace to the host who took you in.
