# Sirach 26

**1** Happy is the husband of a good wife;  
the number of his days will be doubled.

**2** A capable wife gladdens her husband,  
and he will complete his years in peace.

**3** A good wife is a good portion;  
she will be given as a portion to those who fear the Lord.

**4** Whether rich or poor, his heart is glad,  
and his face is always cheerful.

**5** Of three things my heart is afraid,  
and over a fourth I have pleaded:  
the slander of a city, the gathering of a mob,  
and false accusation — all these are worse than death.

**6** A wife jealous of another wife is grief of heart and sorrow,  
and a scourge of the tongue that shares it with all.

**7** An evil wife is an ox yoke that slips;  
whoever takes hold of her is like one who grasps a scorpion.

**8** A drunken wife arouses great anger;  
she will not hide her shame.

**9** A wife's unchastity shows in her bold glances,  
and is known by her eyelids.

**10** Over a daughter keep a firm and unyielding watch,  
or, finding freedom, she will use it for herself.

**11** Be on guard against an impudent eye,  
and do not be surprised if she wrongs you;

**12** as a thirsty traveler opens his mouth  
and drinks from any water near him,  
so she will sit in front of every tent peg  
and open her quiver to the arrow.

**13** A wife's charm delights her husband,  
and her skill puts fat on his bones.

**14** A silent wife is a gift of the Lord,  
and nothing can be given in exchange for a well-disciplined soul.

**15** A modest wife adds grace upon grace,  
and no scale can weigh the worth of a self-controlled soul.

**16** Like the sun rising in the heights of the Lord,  
so is the beauty of a good wife in his well-ordered home.

**17** Like the lamp shining on the holy lampstand,  
so is the beauty of a face on a stately figure.

**18** Like golden pillars on a base of silver,  
so are shapely feet with a steady heart.

## Notes

- **v2 "A capable wife"** — *gynē andreia*, the "capable wife" whom Proverbs praises at its close (Reproof 7:10; Proverbs 31:10).
- **vv5–6 "Of three things my heart is afraid, and over a fourth I have pleaded"** — the fourth is v6, a wife set against a rival wife in the same house, as Peninnah provoked Hannah (*Her rival used to provoke her severely*, 1 Samuel 1:6). "I have pleaded" — *edeēthēn*, as Swete prints it.
- **vv10–12** — counsel to a father, put in plain pictures; the subject is taken up again in 42:9–11.
- **vv16–18 "Like the sun… like the lamp on the holy lampstand… like golden pillars on a base of silver"** — the good wife's beauty likened to the sanctuary: the lampstand (Exodus 25:31–37) and the pillars overlaid with gold on sockets of silver (Exodus 26:32).
- **v18 "with a steady heart"** — *epi sternois*, "on the chest" of one who stands firm, as Swete prints it.
- **vv19–27** — Swete's Greek gives these nine verse numbers and no words (see the note on 1:5).
- **v28 "treated as refuse"** — *skybalisthōsin*, from *skybalon*, "refuse," the word Paul uses of all he gave up for Christ (Philippians 3:8).
