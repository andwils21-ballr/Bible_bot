# Sirach 18

**1** He who lives forever created the whole universe together;

**2** the Lord alone will be declared righteous.

**3** *(This verse is not in the Greek text followed here; see the note.)*

**4** To no one has He given power to proclaim His works;  
and who can search out His mighty deeds?

**5** Who can measure the might of His majesty?  
Who can go on to recount His mercies?

**6** It is not possible to take from them or add to them,  
nor is it possible to search out the wonders of the Lord.

**7** When a man has finished, then he is only beginning;  
and when he stops, he is left at a loss.

**8** What is man, and of what use is he?  
What is his good, and what is his evil?

**9** The number of a man's days is great if it reaches a hundred years.

**10** Like a drop of water from the sea and a grain of sand,  
so are a few years in the day of eternity.

**11** Therefore the Lord is patient with them,  
and pours out His mercy upon them.

**12** He sees and recognizes that their end is evil;  
therefore He has multiplied His forgiveness.

**13** The mercy of a man is for his neighbor,  
but the mercy of the Lord is for all flesh.  
He rebukes and disciplines and teaches,  
and brings them back as a shepherd his flock.

**14** He has mercy on those who accept discipline,  
and who are eager for His judgments.

**15** My child, do not mix blame with your good deeds,  
nor bring grief by your words with any gift you give.

**16** Does not the dew relieve the scorching heat?  
So a word is better than a gift.

**17** Is not a word better than a good gift?  
Both are found in a gracious man.

**18** A fool will reproach ungraciously,  
and the gift of a grudging man makes the eyes waste away.

**19** Learn before you speak,  
and take care of your health before you fall sick.

**20** Before judgment, examine yourself,  
and in the hour of visitation you will find forgiveness.

**21** Before you fall ill, humble yourself,  
and when you have sinned, show that you have turned back.

**22** Let nothing hinder you from paying a vow in good time,  
and do not wait until death to be set right.

**23** Before you make a vow, prepare yourself,  
and do not be like a man who tests the Lord.

**24** Think of His wrath in the days of your end,  
and of the time of vengeance, when He turns away His face.

**25** In the time of plenty, think of the time of hunger;  
in the days of wealth, think of poverty and want.

**26** From morning to evening the time changes,  
and all things move swiftly before the Lord.

**27** A wise man is cautious in everything,  
and in days of sin he guards against wrongdoing.

**28** Every intelligent man knows wisdom,  
and he gives thanks to the one who has found her.

**29** Those who are intelligent in words have also become wise themselves,  
and have poured out exact proverbs.

**30** *Self-control of the soul.*  
Do not follow your desires,  
but hold back from your appetites.

**31** If you grant your soul what its desire pleases,  
it will make you the laughingstock of your enemies.

**32** Do not delight in much luxury,  
nor be tied to the cost of it.

**33** Do not become a beggar by feasting on borrowed money,  
when there is nothing in your purse.

## Notes

- **v3** — Swete's Greek gives this verse number and no words (see the note on 1:5).
- **v8 "What is man, and of what use is he?"** — compare Psalm 8:4, *what is man, that You remember him*, and Job 7:17.
- **vv9–10 "a hundred years… a drop of water from the sea"** — set against eternity, even a long life is a drop; Psalm 90:10 gives seventy or eighty years.
- **v13 "as a shepherd his flock"** — Isaiah 40:11, *He will feed His flock like a shepherd*. A man's mercy reaches his neighbor; God's reaches all flesh.
- **v17 "a gracious man"** — *kecharitōmenos*, "one who has been graced," the word the messenger uses to greet Mary (Luke 1:28, *kecharitōmenē*).
- **v20 "Before judgment, examine yourself"** — compare Paul, *let a man examine himself* (1 Corinthians 11:28).
- **vv22–23 "paying a vow in good time… do not be like a man who tests the Lord"** — Ecclesiastes 5:4, *When you make a vow to God, do not delay to pay it*; Deuteronomy 6:16, *You shall not test the LORD your God*.
- **v30 "Self-control of the soul."** — a heading written into the Greek text itself, over the sayings that follow; another stands at 20:26.
