# Sirach 19

**1** A worker who is a drunkard will not become rich;  
whoever despises small things will fall little by little.

**2** Wine and women lead men of understanding astray,  
and whoever clings to prostitutes grows reckless.

**3** Decay and worms will inherit him,  
and the reckless soul will be cut off.

**4** Whoever trusts too quickly is light-headed,  
and whoever sins wrongs his own soul.

**5** Whoever takes delight in his heart will be condemned,

**6** and whoever hates gossip has less evil.

**7** Never repeat what is said,  
and you will lose nothing at all.

**8** Do not tell it to friend or foe,  
and unless it would be a sin for you, do not reveal it;

**9** for he has heard you and will watch you,  
and when the time comes he will hate you.

**10** Have you heard something? Let it die with you.  
Take heart: it will not make you burst.

**11** Because of one word a fool goes into labor,  
like a woman giving birth to a child.

**12** Like an arrow stuck in the flesh of the thigh,  
so is a word inside a fool.

**13** Question a friend; perhaps he did not do it;  
and if he did anything, so that he may not do it again.

**14** Question your friend; perhaps he did not say it;  
and if he said it, so that he may not say it again.

**15** Question a friend, for often it is slander,  
and do not believe everything you hear.

**16** A man may slip without meaning it;  
and who has not sinned with his tongue?

**17** Question your neighbor before you threaten him,  
and give the law of the Most High its place.

**20** All wisdom is the fear of the Lord,

**22** The knowledge of wickedness is not wisdom,  
nor is there prudence where sinners take counsel.

**23** There is a wickedness that is detestable,  
and there is a fool who merely lacks wisdom.

**24** Better is the God-fearing man who has little understanding  
than one rich in intelligence who breaks the law.

**25** There is a cleverness that is exact and unjust,  
and there is one who twists a kindness to win a verdict.

**26** There is a scoundrel who goes bowed down in black,  
but inside he is full of deceit;

**27** he hangs his head and pretends not to hear,  
but where he is not noticed he will get the better of you.

**28** If lack of strength keeps him from sinning,  
when he finds an opportunity he will do harm.

**29** A man is known by his appearance,  
and a sensible man is known by his face when you meet him.

**30** A man's clothing, and the laugh on his teeth,  
and the way he walks show what he is.

## Notes

- **v2 "Wine and women lead men of understanding astray"** — the counsel of King Lemuel's mother, *Do not give your strength to women* (Reproof 7:3–4; Proverbs 31:3–4).
- **v5 "Whoever takes delight in his heart"** — so Swete's Greek (*kardia*); the line reads more easily as "whoever takes delight in wickedness" (*kakia*).
- **vv7–12** — on gossip: *Have you heard something? Let it die with you.* The fool who hears a secret is in labor until he delivers it (v11).
- **vv13–17 "Question a friend… before you threaten him"** — the command of Leviticus 19:17, *You shall be sure to correct your neighbor*; Jesus, *go and tell him his fault between you and him alone* (Matthew 18:15).
- **v16 "who has not sinned with his tongue?"** — James 3:2, *we all stumble in many ways; if anyone does not stumble in what he says, he is a perfect man*.
- **vv18, 19, 21** — Swete's Greek gives these verse numbers and no words (see the note on 1:5).
- **vv26–27 "bowed down in black"** — dressed and bent as a mourner, a show of grief over a heart of deceit; "pretends not to hear" — *heterokōphōn*, "deaf in one ear."
