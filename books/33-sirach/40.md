# Sirach 40

**1** Great toil was created for every man,  
and a heavy yoke is on the sons of Adam,  
from the day they come out of their mother's womb  
until the day they are buried in the mother of all:

**2** their thoughts and the fear of their heart,  
the anticipation of what is coming, the day of death;

**3** from the one who sits on a throne in glory  
to the one brought low in dust and ashes,

**4** from the one who wears purple and a crown  
to the one clothed in rough linen —

**5** anger and envy, trouble and unrest,  
fear of death and wrath and strife.  
At the time of rest upon his bed,  
the sleep of night confuses his mind.

**6** He gets little or nothing of rest,  
and after that, in his sleep, as in a day of keeping watch,  
he is troubled by the vision of his heart,  
like one who has escaped from the front of the battle.

**7** At the moment of his rescue he wakes,  
and marvels that the fear was nothing.

**8** So it is with all flesh, from man to beast,  
and on sinners seven times more than these:

**9** death and bloodshed, strife and sword;

**10** all these were created for the lawless,  
and because of them the flood came.

**11** All that is from the earth returns to the earth,  
and what is from the waters turns back to the sea.

**12** Every bribe and injustice will be blotted out,  
but good faith will stand forever.

**13** The wealth of the unjust will dry up like a river,  
and roar away like a great thunderclap in a rainstorm.

**14** As one who opens his hands will rejoice,  
so transgressors will utterly fail.

**15** The offspring of the ungodly will not put out many branches;  
they are unclean roots on a sheer rock.

**16** The reeds by every water and on the bank of a river  
will be plucked up before any grass.

**17** Kindness is like a garden of blessings,  
and charity endures forever.

**18** The life of the contented man and the worker is sweet,  
but better than both is he who finds a treasure.

**19** Children and the building of a city establish a name,  
but better than both is counted a wife without blemish.

**20** Wine and music gladden the heart,  
but better than both is the love of wisdom.

**21** The flute and the harp make melody sweet,  
but better than both is a pleasant tongue.

**22** Your eye will desire grace and beauty,  
but better than both the green shoots of the field.

**23** A friend and a companion meet one in season,  
but better than both is a wife with her husband.

**24** Brothers and help are for a time of trouble,  
but better than both is charity, which will rescue.

**25** Gold and silver make the foot stand firm,  
but better than both a wife is esteemed.

**26** Wealth and strength lift up the heart,  
but better than both is the fear of the Lord;  
in the fear of the Lord there is no lack,  
and with it there is no need to seek help.

**27** The fear of the Lord is like a garden of blessing,  
and above all glory they have covered him.

**28** My child, do not live the life of a beggar;  
it is better to die than to beg.

**29** A man who looks to another man's table —  
his life is not to be counted as a life;  
he defiles himself with another man's food;  
but a man of knowledge and discipline will guard against it.

**30** In the mouth of the shameless begging is sweet,  
but in his belly a fire will burn.

## Notes

- **v1 "until the day they are buried in the mother of all"** — the earth, the mother every man returns to: Job 1:21, *Naked I came out of my mother's womb, and naked I will return there*.
- **v3 "dust and ashes"** — *gē kai spodos*, Abraham's words of himself before God, *I am dust and ashes* (Genesis 18:27), the same pair in the Greek.
- **vv5–7 "the sleep of night confuses his mind"** — even rest is no rest: the man flees battles all night in his dreams and wakes to find there was nothing to fear.
- **v10 "because of them the flood came"** — Genesis 6:13, *the earth is filled with violence because of them*.
- **v16 "The reeds by every water"** — *achei*, the word for the reeds where Pharaoh's cows *grazed in the reeds* (Genesis 41:2, Greek *en tō achei*). Job 8:12 says the same of the reed: *it withers before any other grass*.
- **vv17, 27 "like a garden of blessings"** — *paradeisos*, the word of the Greek of Genesis 2:8 for the garden God planted in Eden.
- **vv18–27 "but better than both"** — ten times the line names two good things and then one better. Three times (vv19, 23, 25) the better thing is a wife.
- **v25 "but better than both a wife is esteemed"** — *gynē eudokimeitai*, as Swete prints it.
- **v27 "above all glory they have covered him"** — *ekalypsan*, plural with no subject named, as Swete prints it: the one who fears the Lord is clothed with glory above all glory.
- **v29 "he defiles himself with another man's food"** — *alisgēsei*, the verb of Daniel 1:8 in both Greek forms: Daniel resolved *not to defile himself* (*mē alisgēthē*) with the king's food.
