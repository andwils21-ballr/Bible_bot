# Sirach 34

**1** Empty and false hopes are for a man without understanding,  
and dreams give wings to fools.

**2** Like one who clutches at a shadow and chases the wind  
is the one who pays attention to dreams.

**3** What one sees in dreams is one thing set against another,  
the likeness of a face facing a face.

**4** From what is unclean, what can be made clean?  
From what is false, what can come true?

**5** Divinations and omens and dreams are empty,  
and the heart imagines things like a woman in labor.

**6** Unless they are sent from the Most High as a visitation,  
do not give your heart to them.

**7** Dreams have led many astray,  
and those who hoped in them have fallen.

**8** Without lies the law will be fulfilled,  
and wisdom is made complete in a faithful mouth.

**9** A well-disciplined man knows many things,  
and a man of much experience will speak with understanding.

**10** One who has not been tested knows little,  
but one who has wandered will grow in shrewdness.

**11** I have seen many things in my wanderings,  
and I understand more than I can say.

**12** Often I have been in danger even of death,  
and I came through because of these things.

**13** The spirit of those who fear the Lord will live,  
for their hope is in the One who saves them.

**14** Whoever fears the Lord will not be afraid,  
and will not lose heart, for He is his hope.

**15** Happy is the soul of the one who fears the Lord.  
On whom does he lean? Who is his support?

**16** The eyes of the Lord are on those who love Him,  
a mighty shield and a strong support,  
a shelter from the scorching wind and a shade from the noonday sun,  
a guard against stumbling and a help against falling.

**17** He lifts up the soul and gives light to the eyes;  
He gives healing, life and blessing.

**18** Whoever sacrifices from what was wrongly gained makes a mockery of an offering,  
and the mockeries of the lawless do not win favor.

**19** The Most High takes no pleasure in the offerings of the ungodly,  
nor does He atone for sins for a multitude of sacrifices.

**20** Like one who sacrifices a son in front of his father  
is the one who brings a sacrifice from the property of the poor.

**21** The bread of the needy is the life of the poor;  
whoever robs them of it is a man of blood.

**22** To take away a neighbor's living is to murder him,  
and to rob a hired man of his wages is to shed blood.

**23** One builds and another tears down;  
what do they gain but toil?

**24** One prays and another curses;  
whose voice will the Master hear?

**25** One who is washed after touching a dead body and touches it again,  
what has he gained by his washing?

**26** So it is with a man who fasts for his sins,  
and goes again and does the same:  
who will listen to his prayer?  
What has he gained by humbling himself?

## Notes

- **vv1–26** — Swete's 31:1–31. The English joins five pairs of his verses into one (here vv10, 13, 15, 18 and 22); see the note on 30:25.
- **vv1–8 "Divinations and omens and dreams are empty"** — *manteiai kai oiōnismoi*, the words of the Greek of Deuteronomy 18:10, *one who reads omens, one who practices divination*. One door is left open: *unless they are sent from the Most High* (v6), as the LORD said, *I speak with him in a dream* (Numbers 12:6).
- **v3 "the likeness of a face facing a face"** — *katenanti prosōpou homoiōma prosōpou*: a dream is a mirror, and the dreamer sees his own face looking back.
- **vv9–10 "A well-disciplined man… one who has wandered"** — *pepaideumenos* and *peplanēmenos*, two words a few letters apart: the man trained at home and the man taught by the road. The writer claims both (vv11–12).
- **v16 "The eyes of the Lord are on those who love Him"** — Psalm 33:18, *The eye of the LORD is on those who fear Him*, and in its Greek the same words, *hoi ophthalmoi Kyriou epi*. "A shade from the noonday sun" — Psalm 121:5–6, *the LORD is your shade… The sun will not strike you by day*.
- **v18 "makes a mockery of an offering… the mockeries of the lawless"** — *memōkēmenē*, *mōkēmata*: the same word twice. Proverbs 21:27, *The sacrifice of the wicked is detestable*.
- **v20 "Like one who sacrifices a son in front of his father"** — *thyōn*, the verb of the altar: a gift taken from the poor is offered up to God as though their own child were the victim.
- **vv21–22 "to rob a hired man of his wages is to shed blood"** — Leviticus 19:13, *The wages of a hired man shall not stay with you overnight until morning*; Deuteronomy 24:15, *Otherwise he will cry to the LORD against you*.
- **v25 "One who is washed after touching a dead body"** — the cleansing of Numbers 19:11–12. *Baptizomenos*, "washed," is the verb of John's baptism in the Jordan (Matthew 3:6); here it is the old washing from a corpse, undone by touching it again.
