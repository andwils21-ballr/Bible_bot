# Sirach 32

**1** Have they made you master of the feast? Do not exalt yourself;  
be among them as one of them;  
look after them, and then take your seat.

**2** When you have done all your duty, recline,  
so that you may be glad because of them,  
and receive a crown for your good conduct.

**3** Speak, elder, for it is fitting for you,  
but with exact knowledge,  
and do not interrupt the music.

**4** Where there is a performance, do not pour out talk,  
and do not show off your wisdom at the wrong time.

**5** A seal of ruby in a setting of gold  
is a concert of music at a banquet of wine.

**6** A seal of emerald in a work of gold  
is the melody of music with pleasant wine.

**7** Speak, young man, if you are needed,  
but scarcely twice, and only if you are asked.

**8** Sum up what you say, much in few words;  
be like one who knows and yet keeps silent.

**9** Among the great do not make yourself their equal,  
and when another is speaking, do not chatter on.

**10** Lightning hurries ahead of thunder,  
and favor goes ahead of a modest man.

**11** Rise at the right time and do not be the last;  
hurry home and do not linger.

**12** There amuse yourself and do what you have in mind,  
but do not sin with a proud word.

**13** For all this, bless Him who made you  
and who gives you your fill of His good things.

**14** Whoever fears the Lord will accept discipline,  
and those who rise early will find favor.

**15** Whoever seeks the law will be filled with it,  
but the hypocrite will stumble over it.

**16** Those who fear the Lord will find what is right,  
and will kindle righteous deeds like a light.

**17** A sinful man turns aside from reproof,  
and finds a ruling to suit his own will.

**18** A man of counsel will not overlook a thought;  
the stranger and the proud man do not shrink in fear,  
even after acting without counsel.

**19** Do nothing without counsel,  
and when you have acted, do not regret it.

**20** Do not walk on a road of stumbling,  
and do not trip among the stones.

**21** Do not trust a smooth road,

**22** and guard yourself against your own children.

**23** In every work trust your own soul,  
for this too is keeping the commandments.

**24** Whoever trusts the law pays attention to the commandments,  
and whoever relies on the Lord will suffer no loss.

## Notes

- **vv1–2 "master of the feast… receive a crown"** — *hēgoumenon*, the one chosen to preside over a banquet, who sees to the guests before he takes his own place; the crown is the garland worn at a feast (*Let us crown ourselves with rosebuds*, Wisdom 2:8), here given for *eukosmia*, good order.
- **vv5–6 "A seal of ruby… a seal of emerald"** — a signet stone set in gold: music is the jewel in the setting of the feast.
- **v10 "a modest man"** — *aischyntērou*, the word of 26:15, where a *modest wife* adds *grace upon grace*; here too modesty wins favor before a word is said.
- **v13 "gives you your fill"** — *methyskonta*, "making you drunk," the banquet's own word turned to God's gifts. The Greek of Psalm 36:8 uses it the same way: *They are filled with the abundance of Your house* (*methysthēsontai*).
- **v14 "those who rise early"** — to seek the Lord, as those who rise early for wisdom in 4:12, *those who rise early for her will be filled with joy*.
- **v15 "the hypocrite"** — *ho hypokrinomenos*, "the one playing a part," an actor's word; from the same verb comes the noun Jesus uses, *hypokritai*, "hypocrites" (Matthew 6:2).
- **v18 "even after acting without counsel"** — *kai meta to poiēsai met' autou aneu boulēs*, "and after doing with him without counsel": the line is that compressed in the Greek.
- **v22 "guard yourself against your own children"** — *apo tōn teknōn sou phylaxai*, as Swete prints it.
- **vv21–24 "Do not trust… trust your own soul… Whoever trusts the law"** — *pisteuō* three times: not the smooth road, but your own soul and the law. The last line turns to a second verb, *pepoithōs*, "relying on," for the Lord.
