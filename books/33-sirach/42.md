# Sirach 42

**1** Do not be ashamed of these things,  
and do not show partiality so as to sin:

**2** of the law of the Most High and the covenant,  
and of a judgment that acquits the ungodly;

**3** of reckoning with a partner and with fellow travelers,  
of giving out an inheritance among companions;

**4** of accuracy in scales and weights,  
of acquiring much or little;

**5** of ordinary buying and selling with merchants,  
of much discipline of children,  
and of drawing blood from the side of a wicked servant.

**6** With an evil wife a seal is good,  
and where there are many hands, lock up.

**7** Whatever you hand over, let it be by number and weight,  
and put all giving and receiving in writing;

**8** of correcting the senseless and the fool,  
and the very old man who quarrels with the young.  
So you will be truly disciplined,  
and approved before every living person.

**9** A daughter is a secret cause of sleeplessness to her father,  
and worry over her drives away sleep:  
in her youth, that she may not pass her prime;  
and when married, that she may not be hated;

**10** in her virginity, that she may not be defiled  
and become pregnant in her father's house;  
when with a husband, that she may not go astray;  
and when married, that she may not be barren.

**11** Over a headstrong daughter keep a firm watch,  
or she may make you a laughingstock to your enemies,  
the talk of the city, called out before the people,  
and shame you before the crowd.

**12** Do not look at anyone for beauty,  
and do not sit among women;

**13** for from clothes comes the moth,  
and from a woman, a woman's wickedness.

**14** Better is a man's wickedness than a woman's doing good;  
a woman brings shame and disgrace.

**15** I will now call to mind the works of the Lord,  
and tell what I have seen:  
by the words of the Lord His works were made.

**16** The shining sun looks down on everything,  
and the work of the Lord is full of His glory.

**17** The Lord has not enabled His holy ones  
to tell all His wonders,  
which the Lord Almighty has established,  
so that the universe may stand firm in His glory.

**18** He searches out the abyss and the heart,  
and I have pondered their devices;  
for the Lord knows all knowledge,  
and looks into the signs of the ages,

**19** declaring what is past and what is to be,  
and revealing the traces of hidden things.

**20** No thought escapes Him,  
and not one word is hidden from Him.

**21** He has set in order the great works of His wisdom;  
He is before the ages and to the ages;  
nothing has been added and nothing taken away,  
and He has needed no counselor.

**22** How desirable are all His works,  
and like a spark to look upon!

**23** All these live and remain forever for every need,  
and all obey.

**24** All things are in pairs, one against the other,  
and He has made nothing incomplete.

**25** One confirms the good of the other,  
and who can have his fill of seeing His glory?

## Notes

- **v1** — the last words of Swete's 42:1; vv2–25 are his 42:2–25 (see the note on 41:1).
- **v2 "a judgment that acquits the ungodly"** — *krimatos dikaiōsai ton asebē*. The Greek of Exodus 23:7 forbids it, *ou dikaiōseis ton asebē heneken dōrōn*, "you shall not acquit the ungodly for gifts" (*I will not acquit the guilty*). Read here as a fair verdict given to an ungodly man when he is in the right, which a judge may be ashamed to give; the Greek says no more.
- **v4 "accuracy in scales and weights"** — Leviticus 19:36, *You shall have honest scales, honest weights*.
- **v11 "Over a headstrong daughter keep a firm watch"** — the words of 26:10 again, where Swete prints the adverb (*adiatreptōs*, "keep an unyielding watch"); here it is the adjective, *adiatreptō*, "a headstrong daughter."
- **v14 "Better is a man's wickedness than a woman's doing good"** — *kreissōn ponēria andros ē agathopoios gynē*, as the Greek has it. The same book praises the good wife (26:1–4, 13–18; 36:22–24).
- **v15 "by the words of the Lord His works were made"** — *en logois Kyriou ta erga autou*: creation by speech, as in Psalm 33:6, *By the word of the LORD the heavens were made*.
- **v18 "and I have pondered their devices"** — *dienoēthēn*, "I pondered," as Swete prints it, between two lines about the Lord.
- **v21 "nothing has been added and nothing taken away, and He has needed no counselor"** — Ecclesiastes 3:14, *nothing can be added to it, nor anything taken from it*; and Paul, *who has been His counselor?* (*tis symboulos autou egeneto*, Romans 11:34).
- **v24 "All things are in pairs, one against the other"** — the thought of 33:14–15, *two by two, one against the other*.
