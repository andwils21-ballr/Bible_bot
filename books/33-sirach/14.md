# Sirach 14

**1** Blessed is the man who has not slipped with his mouth,  
and is not stung by grief for sin;

**2** blessed is he whose soul does not condemn him,  
and who has not fallen from his hope.

**3** Riches are not fitting for a stingy man,  
and what use is money to a grudging man?

**4** Whoever gathers by depriving himself gathers for others,  
and others will live in luxury on his goods.

**5** If a man is mean to himself, to whom will he be good?  
He will not enjoy his own riches.

**6** No one is meaner than the man who is grudging to himself,  
and this is the reward of his wickedness;

**7** even if he does good, he does it by forgetfulness,  
and in the end he shows his wickedness.

**8** Evil is the man with a grudging eye;  
he turns away his face and disregards souls.

**9** The eye of the greedy man is not satisfied with his portion,  
and wicked injustice withers the soul.

**10** An evil eye begrudges bread,  
and there is nothing on his own table.

**11** My child, treat yourself well according to your means,  
and bring offerings to the Lord as is fitting.

**12** Remember that death will not delay,  
and the decree of Hades has not been shown to you.

**13** Before you die, do good to a friend,  
and as far as you are able, reach out and give to him.

**14** Do not deprive yourself of a good day;  
do not let your share of good desire pass you by.

**15** Will you not leave the fruit of your labors to another,  
and what you toiled for to be divided by lot?

**16** Give and take, and treat yourself well,  
for in Hades one cannot look for luxury.

**17** All flesh grows old like a garment,  
for the decree from of old is, "Dying you shall die."

**18** Like leaves growing on a thick tree,  
which sheds some and grows others,  
so are the generations of flesh and blood:  
one comes to an end, and another is born.

**19** Every work decays and comes to an end,  
and the one who made it will go with it.

**20** Blessed is the man who will end his days in wisdom,  
and who reasons with his understanding;

**21** who ponders her ways in his heart,  
and will come to understand her secrets.

**22** Go after her like a hunter,  
and lie in wait at her entrances.

**23** He who peers through her windows  
will also listen at her doors;

**24** he who lodges close to her house  
will also fasten a tent peg in her walls;

**25** he will pitch his tent beside her,  
and lodge in a good lodging place;

**26** he will set his children under her shelter,  
and will dwell under her branches;

**27** he will be sheltered by her from the heat,  
and will lodge in her glory.

## Notes

- **vv1–2 "Blessed is the man… whose soul does not condemn him"** — compare 1 John 3:21, *if our heart does not condemn us, we have confidence before God*.
- **vv3–10 "a grudging man… a grudging eye"** — *baskanos*, the evil eye that begrudges: Proverbs 23:6, *Do not eat the bread of a stingy man*; Jesus, *is your eye evil because I am good?* (Matthew 20:15).
- **v9 "The eye of the greedy man is not satisfied"** — Ecclesiastes 4:8, *his eyes are never satisfied with riches*.
- **v12 "the decree of Hades"** — *diathēkē*, "covenant" or "decree": the appointed terms of death, which no one is shown in advance.
- **vv14–16 "Give and take, and treat yourself well, for in Hades one cannot look for luxury"** — Ecclesiastes 2:18–21 and 9:10, *there is no work… in Sheol*. "Treat yourself well" — *apatēson tēn psychēn sou*, literally "beguile your soul," that is, give it its pleasure.
- **v17 "Dying you shall die"** — *thanatō apothanē*, the words of the Greek of Genesis 2:17, the first decree, rendered here as Genesis has them. "Like a garment" — Psalm 102:26, *they will all wear out like a garment*.
- **v20 "who will end his days in wisdom"** — *teleutēsei*, "will die," as Swete prints it; the parallel lines (vv20–21) speak of reasoning and pondering, and "meditate" would fit them more closely.
- **vv22–27** — the seeker of wisdom pictured as a lover: he peers through her windows (compare Song of Songs 2:9, *gazing through the windows, peering through the lattice*), pitches his tent against her walls, and raises his children in her shade.
