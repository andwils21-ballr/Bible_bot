# Sirach 31

**1** Lying awake over wealth wastes away the flesh,  
and the anxiety it brings drives away sleep.

**2** Wakeful anxiety keeps slumber away,  
and a grave illness drives off sleep.

**3** The rich man toils to gather money,  
and when he rests he fills himself with his delicacies.

**4** The poor man toils on a meager living,  
and when he rests he is in want.

**5** Whoever loves gold will not be declared righteous,  
and whoever pursues ruin will have his fill of it.

**6** Many have been brought to their fall because of gold,  
and their destruction met them face to face.

**7** It is a stumbling block to those who worship it,  
and every fool will be caught by it.

**8** Happy is the rich man who is found without blemish,  
and who has not gone after gold.

**9** Who is he? We will call him happy,  
for he has done wonders among his people.

**10** Who has been tested by it and found perfect?  
Let that be his boast.  
Who could have transgressed and did not transgress,  
and could have done evil and did not do it?

**11** His goods will be made secure,  
and the assembly will tell of his acts of charity.

**12** Are you seated at a great table?  
Do not open your throat over it,  
and do not say, "How much there is on it!"

**13** Remember that an evil eye is a bad thing.  
What has been created more evil than the eye?  
That is why it weeps at everything.

**14** Where he looks, do not reach out your hand,  
and do not crowd him at the dish.

**15** Judge your neighbor's feelings by your own,  
and be thoughtful in everything.

**16** Eat what is set before you as a human being should,  
and do not gobble, or you will be hated.

**17** Be the first to stop, for discipline's sake,  
and do not be greedy, or you will give offense.

**18** If you sit among many,  
do not reach out your hand before they do.

**19** How little is enough for a well-disciplined man!  
On his bed he does not gasp for breath.

**20** Healthy sleep comes with a moderate stomach;  
he rises early and has his wits about him.  
Sleeplessness and vomiting and colic  
are with the greedy man.

**21** If you have been pressed to eat too much,  
get up and leave in the middle, and you will have relief.

**22** Listen to me, my child, and do not despise me,  
and in the end you will find my words.  
In all your work be diligent,  
and no sickness will come upon you.

**23** Lips will bless a man who is generous with food,  
and the testimony to his goodness can be trusted.

**24** The city will grumble at a man who is stingy with food,  
and the testimony to his stinginess is accurate.

**25** Do not play the hero over wine,  
for wine has destroyed many.

**26** As the furnace tests the temper of steel in the quenching,  
so wine tests hearts when the proud quarrel.

**27** Wine is as good as life to men,  
if you drink it in its measure.  
What life is there for one who lacks wine?  
It was created to make men glad.

**28** Wine drunk at the right time and in moderation  
is rejoicing of heart and gladness of soul.

**29** Wine drunk to excess is bitterness of soul,  
with provocation and stumbling.

**30** Drunkenness increases a fool's rage until he stumbles,  
lessening his strength and adding wounds.

**31** At a banquet of wine do not rebuke your neighbor,  
and do not despise him in his merriment;  
speak no word of reproach to him,  
and do not press him by demanding repayment.

## Notes

- **v7 "those who worship it"** — *enthysiazousin*, as Swete prints it. Spelled so, the word is built on *thysia*, "sacrifice": those who sacrifice to it. Spelled *enthousiazousin*, it is those possessed by it, as by a god. Either way gold has become an idol.
