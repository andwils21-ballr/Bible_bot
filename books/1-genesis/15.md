# Genesis 15

**1** After these things the word of the LORD came to Abram in a vision, saying, "Do not fear, Abram. I am a shield to you. Your reward is very great."

**2** And Abram said, "Lord GOD, what will You give me, and I am going stripped? And the son of the possession of my house — he is Eliezer of Damascus."

**3** And Abram said, "Look — to me You have given no seed, and here, a son of my house is inheriting me."

**4** And here, the word of the LORD came to him, saying, "This one shall not inherit you. Rather, one who comes out of your own inward parts — he shall inherit you."

**5** And He brought him outside and said, "Look now toward the heavens and count the stars, if you are able to count them." And He said to him, "So shall your seed be."

**6** And he trusted in the LORD, and He reckoned it to him as righteousness.

**7** And He said to him, "I am the LORD, who brought you out from Ur of the Chaldeans, to give you this land to inherit it."

**8** And he said, "Lord GOD, by what shall I know that I will inherit it?"

## Between the Pieces

**9** And He said to him, "Take for Me a heifer of three, and a she-goat of three, and a ram of three, and a turtledove, and a young pigeon."

**10** And he took for Him all of these, and he cut them in two through the middle, and he set each piece opposite its fellow. But the bird he did not cut.

**11** And the birds of prey came down upon the carcasses, and Abram drove them back.

**12** And it came to pass, as the sun was going down, that a deep sleep fell upon Abram; and here — a dread, a darkness, a great one, falling upon him.

**13** And He said to Abram, "Know, you must know, that your seed will live as guests in a land not theirs, and they will serve them, and they will afflict them, four hundred years.

**14** And also the nation that they serve, I am judging. And after that they will come out with great possessions.

**15** And you — you will come to your fathers in peace. You will be buried at a good gray age.

**16** And a fourth generation will return here, for the guilt of the Amorite is not yet complete."

**17** And it came to pass — the sun had gone down, and it was thick darkness — and here: a smoking oven and a torch of fire, which passed between those pieces.

**18** On that day the LORD cut a covenant with Abram, saying, "To your seed I have given this land, from the river of Egypt to the great river, the river Euphrates:

**19** the Kenite, and the Kenizzite, and the Kadmonite,

**20** and the Hittite, and the Perizzite, and the Rephaim,

**21** and the Amorite, and the Canaanite, and the Girgashite, and the Jebusite."

## Notes

- **v1 "I am a shield to you"** — *anokhi magen lakh*. In 14:20 Melchizedek blessed God as the one who *miggen* — delivered — Abram's foes into his hand, a verb built on this same noun. The first words God speaks after that scene pick the word up and make it a title: not the one who shields you from something, but *I am the shield*.
- **v1 "Your reward is very great"** — the Hebrew can also be read as a single clause with the preceding one: *I am your shield, your very great reward*. Both parsings are available and English Bibles divide on it.
- **v2 "I am going stripped"** — *ariri*, a rare word for childless, with the sense of being stripped bare or left exposed. Not the ordinary term for having no children. He is describing a condition, not a statistic.
- **v2 "the son of the possession of my house — he is Eliezer of Damascus"** — *u-ven meshek beiti hu Dammeseq Eliezer*. This is one of the genuinely obscure sentences in Genesis. *Meshek* occurs nowhere else in the Hebrew Bible and nobody knows what it means; it resembles the name *Dammeseq*, Damascus, in a way that may be wordplay or may be coincidence. Every English version here is a reconstruction. Rendered word by word, opacity included, rather than smoothed into a sentence the Hebrew does not have.
- **v5 "count the stars"** — in 13:16 the promise was *like the dust of the earth*, a thing you look down at and walk on. Here he is taken outside at night and told to look up. Same promise, two chapters apart, aimed in opposite directions — and the dust is what a body is made of, while the stars are what he is told he cannot number.
- **v6 "he trusted"** — *he'emin*, from the root *aman*, which means to be firm, steady, reliable. The word *amen* comes from it. It is not primarily an assertion about facts; it is leaning your weight on something and finding it holds.
- **v6 "He reckoned it to him as righteousness"** — *va-yachshevehah lo tsedaqah*. *Chashav* is an accounting verb: to count something into a column, to enter it in a reckoning. The sentence does not name either party — *and he reckoned it to him* — though the flow makes God the one doing the reckoning. What is striking in Hebrew is how commercial the verb is. Something is being credited to an account.
- **v9 "a heifer of three"** — *meshulleshet*, from the number three. It may mean three years old, or it may mean three of each. The word is a form built on the number and does not specify. Left as "of three."
- **v10 "he cut them in two… but the bird he did not cut"** — *batar*, to cut in pieces. Abram is not told what to do with the animals in v9; he is only told to take them. He cuts them himself, without instruction, because he knows what this is for.
- **v11 "Abram drove them back"** — the one action he performs in the entire ceremony, and it is chasing scavengers off the meat while nothing happens. Then the sun goes down.
- **v12 "a deep sleep"** — *tardemah*, the same word used in 2:21 when God put the man under to build the woman. It occurs rarely, and both times something is done to a sleeping man that he cannot participate in.
- **v12 "a dread, a darkness, a great one"** — three words in apposition, *eimah chashekhah gedolah*, stacked without conjunctions. English wants "a great and dreadful darkness"; the Hebrew lays them side by side and lets them accumulate.
- **v17 "a smoking oven and a torch of fire, which passed between those pieces"** — this is the point of the chapter, and it depends on knowing what cutting animals in half was for. Two parties to an oath walked between the pieces together, and the meaning was: *let this be done to me if I break it*. Here only one party walks. Abram is asleep, held down by darkness, and God passes through alone. The covenant is made with nobody on the other side of it.
- **v18 "cut a covenant"** — *karat berit*, the standing Hebrew idiom for making an agreement: you *cut* a covenant. The metaphor is dead in ordinary usage, the way English "strike a bargain" is dead. This chapter wakes it up — the animals really are cut, the pieces really are laid out — and then uses the idiom in the summary verse, so the phrase lands on its literal sense.
- **v16 "the guilt of the Amorite is not yet complete"** — *lo shalem avon ha-Emori*. *Shalem* is the word for whole, full, at peace — the name of Melchizedek's city two chapters ago. The delay of four hundred years is explained as waiting for something to fill up. *Avon* is the same word Cain used in 4:13, which means both the wrongdoing and the punishment owed for it.
