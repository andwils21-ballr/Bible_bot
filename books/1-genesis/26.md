# Genesis 26

**1** There was a famine in the land, besides the first famine that was in the days of Abraham. Isaac went to Abimelech king of the Philistines, to Gerar.

**2** The LORD appeared to him and said, "Do not go down to Egypt. Settle in the land that I tell you of.

**3** Live as a guest in this land, and I will be with you and bless you. For to you and to your seed I will give all these lands, and I will raise up the oath that I swore to Abraham your father.

**4** I will multiply your seed as the stars of the heavens, and I will give to your seed all these lands. In your seed all the nations of the earth shall bless themselves —

**5** because Abraham listened to My voice, and kept My charge: My commands, My statutes, and My instructions."

**6** Isaac settled in Gerar.

**7** The men of the place asked about his wife, and he said, "She is my sister" — for he was afraid to say "my wife" — the men of the place might kill me over Rebekah, for she was good to look at.

**8** And it came to pass, when his days there had grown long, that Abimelech king of the Philistines looked down through the window, and saw — and here was Isaac laughing with Rebekah his wife.

**9** Abimelech called Isaac and said, "So — here, she is your wife! How did you say, 'She is my sister'?" Isaac said to him, "Because I said to myself, I might die over her."

**10** Abimelech said, "What is this you have done to us? One of the people might easily have lain with your wife, and you would have brought guilt upon us."

**11** Abimelech charged all the people, saying, "Whoever touches this man or his wife shall surely be put to death."

## The Wells

**12** Isaac sowed in that land, and found in that year a hundredfold. The LORD blessed him.

**13** The man became great, and he kept growing greater and greater, until he was very great.

**14** He had livestock of flocks and livestock of cattle, and a great household of servants. The Philistines envied him.

**15** All the wells that his father's servants had dug in the days of Abraham his father, the Philistines stopped up, and filled them with dust.

**16** Abimelech said to Isaac, "Go from us, for you have become much mightier than we."

**17** Isaac went from there and camped in the streambed of Gerar, and settled there.

**18** Isaac went back and dug out the wells of water that they had dug in the days of Abraham his father, and the Philistines had stopped them up after Abraham's death. He called them by the names his father had called them.

**19** Isaac's servants dug in the streambed, and found there a well of living water.

**20** The herdsmen of Gerar quarreled with Isaac's herdsmen, saying, "The water is ours." He called the name of the well Esek, for they had contended with him.

**21** They dug another well, and they quarreled over that one too, and he called its name Sitnah.

**22** He moved on from there and dug another well, and they did not quarrel over it, and he called its name Rehoboth, and said, "For now the LORD has made room for us, and we shall be fruitful in the land."

**23** He went up from there to Beersheba.

**24** The LORD appeared to him that night and said, "I am the God of Abraham your father. Do not fear, for I am with you, and I will bless you and multiply your seed, for the sake of Abraham My servant."

**25** He built an altar there and called on the name of the LORD, and pitched his tent there. Isaac's servants dug a well there.

**26** Abimelech went to him from Gerar, with Ahuzzath his companion and Phicol the commander of his army.

**27** Isaac said to them, "Why have you come to me, and you hated me and sent me away from you?"

**28** They said, "We have plainly seen that the LORD has been with you, and we said: let there be an oath between us — between us and you — and let us cut a covenant with you,

**29** that you will not do evil with us, just as we have not touched you, and just as we have done with you only good, and sent you away in peace. You now are blessed of the LORD."

**30** He made them a feast, and they ate and drank.

**31** They rose early in the morning and swore, each to his brother. Isaac sent them off, and they went from him in peace.

**32** And it came to pass on that same day that Isaac's servants came and told him about the well they had dug, and said to him, "We have found water."

**33** He called it Shibah. Therefore the name of the city is Beersheba to this day.

**34** Esau was forty years old, and he took as wives Judith daughter of Beeri the Hittite, and Basemath daughter of Elon the Hittite.

**35** They were a bitterness of spirit to Isaac and to Rebekah.

## Notes

- **v1 "besides the first famine"** — the narrator says it outright: this has happened before. Genesis 12:10 sent Abraham down to Egypt in a famine, where he passed his wife off as his sister and was paid to leave. Isaac is about to do all of it again, and the text tells you so in the first verse.
- **v2 "Do not go down to Egypt"** — the one thing his father did. Isaac is the only patriarch never to leave Canaan, and this is why.
- **v5 "My charge: My commands, My statutes, and My instructions"** — *mishmarti, mitsvotai, chuqqotai, ve-torotai*. Four terms of legal vocabulary, and they belong to the law given at Sinai, centuries after Abraham. The verse says Abraham kept them. That is not an oversight; it is the narrator describing Abraham in the language of the covenant his descendants will receive. And *torotai* is the plural of *torah*, which here simply means instructions.
- **v8 "Isaac laughing with Rebekah his wife"** — *Yitschaq metsacheq*. The man's name is the verb: *he laughs*, **laughing**. And this is the same participle as 21:9, where Sarah saw Ishmael *metsacheq* and demanded he be driven out — the word whose meaning the whole Hagar episode turns on, and which no translation admits is undecidable.

  Here the context decides it. Whatever Isaac is doing to Rebekah through that window, a king sees it and concludes instantly that they are married, not siblings. In this verse the word plainly means something between a husband and a wife. That is evidence about 21:9 which the text itself supplies, five chapters later, using the same form — and it is why "mocking" there is a choice and not a translation.
- **v10 "might easily have lain with your wife"** — *shakhav*, the lying-down verb, in the mouth of a foreign king describing what nearly happened. Two verses after the knowing-word appeared as a pun on Isaac's name, the other verb arrives for the act stripped of any bond. The book keeps them apart even here.
- **v13 "went on going and growing great"** — *va-yelekh halokh ve-gadel*, the doubled infinitive Hebrew uses for a process that will not stop, the same construction as the waters *going and returning* in 8:3. Three forms of *gadol* in one verse.
- **v15, 18 "stopped up… filled them with dust"** — *aphar*, the dust the human was made from and returns to. Filling a well with it is the most complete insult available: you do not take the water, you make the place forget it was ever water.
- **v18 "he called them by the names his father had called them"** — the whole chapter in one clause. Isaac does nothing new. He re-digs his father's wells, restores his father's names, receives his father's promise, repeats his father's lie to his father's Abimelech, and makes a treaty at his father's Beersheba. He is the only patriarch whose story is mostly someone else's story, told again.
- **vv20–22 "Esek… Sitnah… Rehoboth"** — three wells, three names, and each is the argument it came from. *Esek* is contention, from the verb in the same verse, *hit'assequ*, they contended. *Sitnah* is hostility, from a root meaning to oppose or accuse — the root behind the word *satan*, which in Hebrew is a common noun for an adversary. *Rehoboth* is wide places, from *rachav*, broad — and Isaac explains it himself: *the LORD has made room*, *hirchiv*. Three names, three verbs, each planted in its own verse.
- **v28 "We have seen, seeing"** — *ra'o ra'inu*, the doubled verb for emphasis. They are conceding something.
- **v29 "just as we have not touched you"** — Abimelech's men had stopped his wells (v15) and driven him out (v16). The treaty is written as though none of that happened, and Isaac signs it and throws them a feast.
- **v33 "Shibah… Beersheba"** — *Shiv'ah* is seven; *shava* is to swear. In 21:31 Abraham named this same place from the same pun, after a treaty with a man called Abimelech and a commander called Phicol. It is named twice, by father and son, for the same reason.
- **vv34–35 "a bitterness of spirit"** — *morat ruach*, literally a bitterness of breath or spirit. The chapter of Isaac quietly repeating his father ends with his son marrying outside the family, which is the one thing Abraham sent a servant a thousand miles to prevent. The next chapter is the stolen blessing.
