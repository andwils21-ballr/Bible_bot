# Genesis 38

**1** And it came to pass at that time that Judah went down from his brothers and turned aside to an Adullamite man whose name was Hirah.

**2** And Judah saw there the daughter of a Canaanite man whose name was Shua, and he took her and came to her.

**3** And she conceived and bore a son, and he called his name Er.

**4** And she conceived again and bore a son, and she called his name Onan.

**5** And she bore yet again, a son, and she called his name Shelah. And he was at Chezib when she bore him.

**6** And Judah took a wife for Er his firstborn, and her name was Tamar.

**7** And Er, Judah's firstborn, was evil in the eyes of the LORD, and the LORD put him to death.

**8** And Judah said to Onan, "Come to your brother's wife and do the brother's duty by her, and raise up seed for your brother."

**9** And Onan knew that the seed would not be his, and it came about that when he came to his brother's wife he ruined it to the ground, so as not to give seed to his brother.

**10** And what he did was evil in the eyes of the LORD, and He put him to death also.

**11** And Judah said to Tamar his daughter-in-law, "Sit as a widow in your father's house until Shelah my son grows up" — for he said, "He might die too, like his brothers." And Tamar went and sat in her father's house.

## At the Opening of Enaim

**12** And the days multiplied, and the daughter of Shua, Judah's wife, died. And Judah was comforted, and he went up to his sheepshearers, he and Hirah his friend the Adullamite, to Timnah.

**13** And it was told to Tamar, saying, "Here — your father-in-law is going up to Timnah to shear his flock."

**14** And she took off her widow's clothes from upon her and covered herself with a veil and wrapped herself, and sat at the opening of Enaim, which is on the road to Timnah — for she saw that Shelah had grown up, and she had not been given to him as a wife.

**15** And Judah saw her and reckoned her a prostitute, because she had covered her face.

**16** And he turned aside to her by the road and said, "Come now, let me come to you" — for he did not know that she was his daughter-in-law. And she said, "What will you give me, that you may come to me?"

**17** And he said, "I will send a kid of the goats from the flock." And she said, "If you give a pledge until you send it."

**18** And he said, "What is the pledge that I shall give you?" And she said, "Your seal and your cord, and your staff that is in your hand." And he gave them to her, and came to her, and she conceived by him.

**19** And she rose and went, and took off her veil from upon her, and put on her widow's clothes.

**20** And Judah sent the kid of the goats by the hand of his friend the Adullamite, to take the pledge from the woman's hand, and he did not find her.

**21** And he asked the men of her place, saying, "Where is the consecrated woman, the one at Enaim by the road?" And they said, "There has been no consecrated woman here."

**22** And he returned to Judah and said, "I did not find her. And the men of the place also said, 'There has been no consecrated woman here.'"

**23** And Judah said, "Let her take them for herself, or we will become a laughingstock. Here, I sent this kid, and you did not find her."

## Identify, Please

**24** And it came to pass, about three months later, that it was told to Judah, saying, "Tamar your daughter-in-law has played the whore, and here, she is also pregnant by whoring." And Judah said, "Bring her out, and let her be burned."

**25** She was being brought out, and she sent to her father-in-law, saying, "By the man to whom these belong I am pregnant." And she said, "Identify, please — whose are this seal and these cords and this staff?"

**26** And Judah identified them and said, "She is more in the right than I, because I did not give her to Shelah my son." And he did not know her again.

**27** And it came to pass at the time of her giving birth, and here — twins in her womb.

**28** And it came to pass, as she gave birth, that one put out a hand, and the midwife took and tied a scarlet thread on his hand, saying, "This one came out first."

**29** And it came to pass, as he drew back his hand, that here — his brother came out. And she said, "How you have breached — a breach upon you!" And he called his name Perez.

**30** And afterward his brother came out, the one with the scarlet thread on his hand. And he called his name Zerah.

## Notes

- **v1 "went down from his brothers"** — *va-yered*, he went **down**. The previous chapter ended with Joseph brought down to Egypt; this one opens with Judah going down from his family, and the two departures are set side by side. The chapter interrupts the Joseph story completely and does not return to it, and the man it follows is the brother who said in 37:27 *let us sell him*. Whatever else it is doing, the book chooses this moment to leave Joseph in Potiphar's house and spend thirty verses on what became of Judah.
- **v8 "do the brother's duty"** — *yabbem*, a verb made directly from *yavam*, brother-in-law: to act the brother-in-law. English has no such word and has to spell the custom out. The obligation is codified much later in Deuteronomy 25:5 — *her brother-in-law shall come to her... and do the brother's duty by her*, the same verb — but here it is already simply assumed, with no law cited and no one surprised by it.
- **v9 "he ruined it to the ground"** — *ve-shichet artsah*, from *shachat*, to spoil or destroy. The English word *onanism* comes from this verse, and it names the wrong thing. The text states the offence in the same sentence: *so as not to give seed to his brother*. Onan takes the marriage and withholds what it was for — the son who would carry his dead brother's name and inherit in his place.
- **v11 "Lest he die also, like his brothers"** — the narrator hands us Judah's private reason for sending Tamar home, and it is not care for her. He has buried two sons married to this woman and he thinks she is the cause. He tells her to wait for Shelah; verse 14 says Shelah grew up and she was never sent for.
- **v14 "the opening of Enaim"** — *petach Einayim*. It is a place, and the name is the ordinary Hebrew word for **eyes**, so the sentence reads as *the opening of the eyes* as much as it reads as a location. She sits at the eye-opening on the Timnah road, and the whole chapter is about who sees what: Judah sees her and reckons wrongly (v15), and is made to identify his own belongings (v25).
- **v15 "because she had covered her face"** — the covering is given as the *reason* he took her for a prostitute, which runs opposite to a modern reader's instinct. The veil is not what hides an ordinary woman here; it is what marks this one as available. Tamar has read the customs of the road better than her father-in-law reads them.
- **vv15, 21 two different words for what she is** — the narrator says Judah reckoned her a *zonah*, the plain word for a prostitute. But when Hirah asks after her in public he says *qedeshah* (twice, vv21–22), a word built on *q-d-sh*, **holy, set apart**, the root behind *qadosh* and *Qodesh ha-Qodashim*, the Holy of Holies. Asking for a *zonah* by name in a strange town would say one thing; asking for a *qedeshah* says another. The messenger changes the word.
- **v18 "your seal and your cord, and your staff"** — *chotam*, *petil*, *matteh*. A seal is what a man presses into clay to make a document his; the cord hangs it at the neck; the staff is carried and recognized. Tamar does not ask for silver. She asks for the three objects that prove who he is, and keeps them for three months.
- **v24 "let her be burned"** — Judah passes a death sentence on his daughter-in-law for an act he paid a goat for on the Timnah road twelve verses earlier. The narrator makes no comment and needs none: both scenes are in the same chapter, and the reader has just read the first one.
- **v25 "Identify, please"** — *hakker na*, and these are the two words Judah's own brothers used in 37:32, sending their father the bloodied coat: *identify, please — is it your son's coat or not?* Judah was part of that. One chapter later the same two words come back at him with his own seal and staff in the woman's hand, and the answering verb is the same one his father used: *va-yakker*, **and he identified**. The man who helped fake a son's death with a goat is exposed by a goat he never managed to deliver.
- **v26 "She is more in the right than I"** — *tsadeqah mimmenni*, from *tsadaq*, to be in the right, the root behind *tsaddiq*, a righteous person. It can be read as a comparison — she is more righteous than I — or as a verdict: **she is right, and I am not**. Either way it is the first time anyone in Genesis says such a sentence about himself, and Judah says it in public, about a woman he has just condemned to death.
- **v29 "Perez"** — *parats*, to break out, breach a wall, burst through. The midwife's line is *mah paratsta alekha parets*, the verb and the noun together. It is the same root Jacob used of his own wealth in 30:30 and 30:43, and here it names a baby who shoves past his brother in the birth canal. Once more in this book the younger comes out ahead of the elder — and this time it happens physically, in front of a witness, with a scarlet thread already tied on the other one's wrist.
- **v30 "Zerah"** — *zarach*, to rise or shine, the verb used of the sun coming up on Jacob at Penuel in 32:31. The hand with the scarlet thread on it comes out first and the name means *dawning*, and he is the one who loses.
- **the chapter's point arrives outside the chapter** — Ruth 4:18–22 runs the line: *Perez fathered Hezron*, then Ram, Amminadab, Nahshon, Salmon, Boaz, Obed, Jesse — *and Jesse fathered **David***. Ten generations from this birth to the king. And the Greek of Matthew 1:3 keeps the mother in: *Judah fathered Perez and Zerah **by Tamar***, one of four women named in that genealogy. The son who mattered came from the daughter-in-law Judah refused to provide for and then sentenced to death.
