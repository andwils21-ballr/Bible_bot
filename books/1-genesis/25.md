# Genesis 25

**1** And Abraham went on and took a wife, and her name was Keturah.

**2** And she bore him Zimran and Jokshan and Medan and Midian and Ishbak and Shuah.

**3** And Jokshan fathered Sheba and Dedan. And the sons of Dedan were Asshurim and Letushim and Leummim.

**4** And the sons of Midian: Ephah and Epher and Hanoch and Abida and Eldaah. All these were the sons of Keturah.

**5** And Abraham gave all that was his to Isaac.

**6** And to the sons of the secondary wives that Abraham had, Abraham gave gifts, and he sent them away from Isaac his son, while he was still alive, eastward, to the land of the east.

**7** And these are the days of the years of Abraham's life that he lived: a hundred years and seventy years and five years.

**8** And Abraham expired and died at a good gray age, old and satisfied, and he was gathered to his people.

**9** And Isaac and Ishmael his sons buried him in the cave of Machpelah, in the field of Ephron son of Zohar the Hittite, which faces Mamre,

**10** the field that Abraham bought from the sons of Heth. There Abraham was buried, and Sarah his wife.

**11** And it came to pass after Abraham's death that God blessed Isaac his son. And Isaac settled by Beer-lahai-roi.

## The Begettings of Ishmael

**12** And these are the begettings of Ishmael son of Abraham, whom Hagar the Egyptian, Sarah's servant girl, bore to Abraham.

**13** And these are the names of the sons of Ishmael, by their names, by their begettings: the firstborn of Ishmael, Nebaioth; and Kedar and Adbeel and Mibsam,

**14** and Mishma and Dumah and Massa,

**15** Hadad and Tema, Jetur, Naphish and Kedemah.

**16** These are the sons of Ishmael, and these are their names, by their villages and by their encampments — twelve chiefs by their peoples.

**17** And these are the years of Ishmael's life: a hundred years and thirty years and seven years. And he expired and died, and was gathered to his people.

**18** And they settled from Havilah as far as Shur, which faces Egypt, as you come toward Asshur. In the face of all his brothers he fell.

## The Begettings of Isaac

**19** And these are the begettings of Isaac son of Abraham. Abraham fathered Isaac.

**20** And Isaac was forty years old when he took Rebekah, daughter of Bethuel the Aramean of Paddan-aram, sister of Laban the Aramean, for himself as a wife.

**21** And Isaac pleaded with the LORD on behalf of his wife, for she was barren. And the LORD let Himself be pleaded with, and Rebekah his wife conceived.

**22** And the sons crushed each other inside her, and she said, "If it is to be like this, why am I alive?" And she went to inquire of the LORD.

**23** And the LORD said to her,  
"Two nations are in your belly,  
and two peoples from your inward parts shall be separated.  
And one people will be stronger than the other,  
and the elder shall serve the younger."

**24** And her days were filled to give birth, and here — twins in her belly.

**25** And the first came out ruddy, all of him like a hairy mantle, and they called his name Esau.

**26** And after that his brother came out, and his hand holding the heel of Esau, and he called his name Jacob. And Isaac was sixty years old when she bore them.

**27** And the boys grew, and Esau was a man who knew hunting, a man of the field; and Jacob was a whole man, dwelling in tents.

**28** And Isaac loved Esau, because hunted game was in his mouth. And Rebekah loves Jacob.

## The Red Stuff

**29** And Jacob was cooking a stew, and Esau came in from the field, and he was exhausted.

**30** And Esau said to Jacob, "Let me gulp down some of that red, that red there — for I am exhausted." Therefore his name was called Edom.

**31** And Jacob said, "Sell me, as of today, your birthright."

**32** And Esau said, "Here I am, going to die. And what is this to me, a birthright?"

**33** And Jacob said, "Swear to me as of today." And he swore to him, and he sold his birthright to Jacob.

**34** And Jacob gave Esau bread and a boiling of lentils, and he ate and drank and rose and went. And Esau despised the birthright.

## Notes

- **v6 "eastward, to the land of the east"** — the direction of leaving in this book, as at 3:24, 4:16, 11:2 and 13:11. Every son who is not the chosen one goes east, and the text says it plainly each time.
- **v8 "old and satisfied"** — *zaqen ve-savea*, the word for having eaten enough. He dies full. It is not a stock phrase; only a handful of people in the Hebrew Bible get it.
- **v8 "gathered to his people"** — *va-ye'asef el ammav*, an idiom distinct from both dying and being buried — the verse names all three separately, in order. Whatever it pictures, it is not the cave, since his people are not in it.
- **v9 "Isaac and Ishmael his sons buried him"** — the last time these two were in the same sentence, one was being sent into the wilderness with a waterskin because of the other. They meet again at their father's grave, in that order, and nothing is said about the years between.
- **v11 "Isaac settled by Beer-lahai-roi"** — Hagar's well again (16:14), where Isaac was also coming from in 24:62. The son who stayed lives at the place named by the woman who was driven out.
- **v17 "and was gathered to his people"** — Ishmael gets the same formula as Abraham, and his twelve sons are called *nesi'im*, chieftains, exactly as God promised in 17:20. The blessing given to the son who was sent away is recorded as kept.
- **v18 "In the face of all his brothers he fell"** — *al penei kol echav naphal*. The same phrase was used of him before he was born, in 16:12, where it was *he shall dwell*. Here the verb is *naphal*, fell — which can mean to settle, to encamp, or to fall upon. The Hebrew closes his story with the same words it opened it with, and one of them has changed.
- **v21 "pleaded… let Himself be pleaded with"** — *va-ye'tar… va-ye'ater*, the same rare verb, active then passive, in one verse. Isaac entreats and God is entreated. And the whole of Isaac's twenty childless years is in that single verse; his wife is barren like his mother, and the text spends nine words on it.
- **v22 "the sons crushed each other inside her"** — *va-yitrotsatsu*, a violent reciprocal verb from a root meaning to crush or smash. Not "struggled." And her response is four words that do not finish: *im ken lammah zeh anokhi*, literally **if so, why this I**. There is no verb. The proposals all supply one and each changes her — *why am I alive*, *why am I like this*, *why is this happening to me*, plain *why me*. This rendering supplies *alive*, because *anokhi* is the bare pronoun standing where existence would be; the honest position is that the sentence breaks off and every English version finishes it for her.
- **v23 "the elder shall serve the younger"** — *ve-rav ya'avod tsa'ir*. The line has no preposition and no object marker, which in Hebrew poetry means the word order alone decides who serves whom — and word order in poetry is flexible. It can be read *the elder shall serve the younger* or, with equal grammar, *the younger shall serve the elder*. The oracle that governs the rest of Genesis is, strictly, reversible.
- **v23 "two nations are in your belly"** — the division is announced **before either child is born**, and no reason is given. Genesis never disqualifies Esau for anything: he sells the birthright in this chapter and loses the blessing in the next, but the oracle that separates the two of them arrives while they are still in the womb, unborn and having done nothing. Paul builds an argument on exactly that in Greek — *before they were born or had done anything good or bad… it was said to her, the greater shall serve the lesser* (Romans 9:11–12, quoting this verse from the Septuagint). And Esau is not left with nothing: he becomes Edom, a nation with kings before Israel has any (36:31). What he does not get is the covenant, and the text puts that outside his conduct altogether.
- **v25 "ruddy… like a hairy mantle"** — *admoni* and *se'ar*. The first chimes with *Edom*, red, which v30 will make explicit; the second chimes with *Se'ir*, the country Esau settles. His body is a map of the nations he will father.
- **v26 "holding the heel… Jacob"** — *aqev*, heel, and *Ya'aqov*. The name is the heel made into a verb: heel-grabber, and by extension one who supplants or trips. It is a name given by what he was doing when he arrived, and he spends the rest of the book doing it.
- **v27 "a whole man"** — *ish tam*. This is the word used of Noah in 6:9 and commanded to Abraham in 17:1: *be whole*, unblemished, sound, entire. English prints "quiet," "plain," "mild," none of which is what the word means anywhere else. The book calls the deceiver by the same adjective it uses for the man who walked with God, and does not explain itself.
- **v28 "And Rebekah loves Jacob"** — the verbs change tense. Isaac *loved* Esau — a completed statement with a reason attached, and the reason is food. Rebekah *loves* Jacob, participle, ongoing, no reason given. Two kinds of love in one verse, and the Hebrew marks the difference by grammar alone.
- **v29 "was cooking a stew"** — *va-yazed nazid*, the verb and its own noun, the construction from 11:3: *he boiled a boiling*. Flat on purpose, and the root is *zid*, which also means to **boil over** in the sense of acting presumptuously — the word Jethro uses of the Egyptians at Exodus 18:11. Pride in this language is a pot left on the fire, and here it is a literal pot.
- **vv29, 30 "exhausted"** — *ayef*, a word for being physically spent rather than sleepy: it is what Gideon's three hundred are when they cross the Jordan still chasing an army. Then in v32 Esau says *here I am, going to die.* Nothing in the narration says he is dying. The text gives him *ayef* and gives the dying to his own mouth, and the difference between the two is the whole transaction.
- **v30 "some of that red, that red there"** — *hal'iteni na min ha-adom ha-adom ha-zeh*. *Hal'it* is a coarse verb used of stuffing food down an animal's throat, and it occurs only here in the Bible. He does not name the food. He says *the red, the red*, twice, because he is too spent to find the word for lentils — and the narrator takes that grunt and makes it the name of a nation: *therefore his name was called Edom*, red.
- **v34 "he ate and drank and rose and went"** — four verbs, no objects, no pause. Then one more clause: *and Esau despised the birthright*. It is the only judgment the narrator passes in the chapter, and it falls on him rather than on the brother who asked for an oath before handing over soup.
