# Genesis 9

**1** And God blessed Noah and his sons, and said to them, "Be fruitful and multiply and fill the earth.

**2** And the fear of you and the dread of you shall be upon every living thing of the earth, and upon every bird of the heavens, upon everything that the ground creeps with, and upon all the fish of the sea. Into your hand they are given.

**3** Every creeping thing that is alive shall be food for you. Like the green plants, I have given you everything.

**4** But flesh with its soul — its blood — you shall not eat.

**5** And surely your blood, for your souls, I will require. From the hand of every living thing I will require it, and from the hand of the human — from the hand of a man's brother — I will require the soul of the human.

**6** One who sheds the blood of the human,  
by the human shall his blood be shed,  
for in the image of God He made the human.

**7** And you, be fruitful and multiply; swarm in the earth and multiply in it."

## The Bow

**8** And God said to Noah and to his sons with him, saying,

**9** "And I — look, I am raising up My covenant with you, and with your seed after you,

**10** and with every living soul that is with you: in the bird, in the beasts, and in every living thing of the earth with you — from all that came out of the ark, to every living thing of the earth.

**11** And I raise up My covenant with you: never again shall all flesh be cut off by the waters of the flood, and never again shall there be a flood to ruin the earth."

**12** And God said, "This is the sign of the covenant that I am giving between Me and you and every living soul that is with you, for everlasting generations:

**13** My bow I have set in the cloud, and it shall be for a sign of covenant between Me and the earth.

**14** And it shall be, when I cloud a cloud over the earth, that the bow shall be seen in the cloud.

**15** And I will remember My covenant that is between Me and you and every living soul in all flesh, and never again shall the waters become a flood to ruin all flesh.

**16** And the bow shall be in the cloud, and I will see it, to remember the everlasting covenant between God and every living soul in all flesh that is upon the earth."

**17** And God said to Noah, "This is the sign of the covenant that I have raised up between Me and all flesh that is upon the earth."

## The Vineyard

**18** And the sons of Noah who came out of the ark were Shem and Ham and Japheth. And Ham, he is the father of Canaan.

**19** These three were the sons of Noah, and from these the whole earth was scattered.

**20** And Noah began — a man of the ground — and he planted a vineyard.

**21** And he drank from the wine and became drunk, and he uncovered himself inside his tent.

**22** And Ham, the father of Canaan, saw the nakedness of his father, and he told his two brothers outside.

**23** And Shem and Japheth took the garment and laid it on the shoulder of the two of them, and they walked backward and covered the nakedness of their father. And their faces were backward, and they did not see the nakedness of their father.

**24** And Noah woke from his wine, and he knew what his youngest son had done to him.

**25** And he said,  
"Cursed be Canaan.  
A servant of servants shall he be to his brothers."

**26** And he said,  
"Blessed be the LORD, the God of Shem,  
and let Canaan be a servant to them.

**27** May God make wide for Japheth,  
and let him dwell in the tents of Shem,  
and let Canaan be a servant to them."

**28** And Noah lived after the flood three hundred and fifty years.

**29** And all the days of Noah were nine hundred and fifty years. And he died.

## Notes

- **vv1–7 the first chapter, run again** — the flood does not simply end; creation is redone in order, using its own words. The wind that hovered over the waters in 1:2 passes over the earth again in 8:1 and the waters sink. The mountaintops appear as the dry land did on day three. The animals are told in 8:17 to **swarm** and be fruitful, which is the day-five blessing of 1:22. Humanity is blessed in 9:1 in the words of 1:28, letter for letter. And 9:6 grounds the new law in *the image of God*, quoting 1:27. Whatever else the ark is carrying, the text treats its cargo as the stock of a second creation.

  The differences are where the weight is. See the notes at v1 on what 1:28 said that this does not, at v2 on what replaces it, and at v3 on meat.
- **v1 "Be fruitful and multiply and fill the earth"** — word for word from 1:28, the blessing on the first humans. But read what is *not* repeated: 1:28 continued *and subdue it, and hold sway*. Those two hard verbs, *kavash* and *radah*, are gone. What replaces them in v2 is fear and dread. The first humans were given rule; these are given terror. It is a colder arrangement, and the omission is the argument.
- **v2 "your fear and your dread"** — *mora'akhem ve-chittekhem*, and this is what stands in the place where 1:28 had *hold sway* and *subdue*. Those were words of rule. These are words of terror: *mora* is the noun of the verb **to be afraid**, and *chat* is the shattering fear of 35:5, where a dread of God fell on the cities so they would not pursue Jacob's sons. In the first world the animals were under authority. In this one they are afraid, and the next verse says why.
- **v3 "Every creeping thing that is alive shall be food for you"** — in 1:29–30, both humans and animals were given plants. Eating meat is permitted here for the first time, and the sentence that grants it points straight back: *like the green plants, I have given you everything*.
- **v4 "flesh with its soul — its blood"** — *basar be-nafsho damo*. The *nephesh* — the same word rendered "soul" throughout chapters 1 and 2, whose root sense is throat — is identified with the blood. The prohibition is not hygiene. It is that the life belongs to someone else, and you may have the animal but not that.
- **v5 "I will require"** — *edrosh*, three times in one verse. The verb means to seek out, to inquire after, to go looking for. Blood is not merely avenged; it is *sought*, the way you search for something owed to you. The same picture as 4:10, where Abel's blood cried out from the ground.
- **v6** — set as lines because the Hebrew is a perfect mirror. Word by word: *shofekh / dam / ha-adam / ba-adam / damo / yishaphekh* — shedder, blood, the human, by the human, his blood, shall be shed. The sentence turns around at its middle and walks back out the way it came in, so the punishment is the crime reversed, in the shape of the line itself. No English word order can reproduce it; the lineation is an attempt to let the turn be felt.
- **v6 "by the human"** — *ba-adam*, which can be "by a human," "in the human," or "for the human." The preposition is doing real work and is not decidable here. Whether this verse establishes human courts or states a principle about God's own requiring depends on which sense is chosen, and the Hebrew does not choose.
- **v6 "in the image of God"** — *be-tselem Elohim*, quoting 1:27. The reason murder is intolerable is not the value of life in general; it is that the thing destroyed was a likeness.
- **v13 "My bow"** — *qashti*. This is not a word for a rainbow. *Qeshet* is the **war bow**, the weapon — it is the word used when a soldier draws a bow throughout the Hebrew Bible, and Hebrew has no separate term for the arc in the sky. So the sentence is: *My weapon I have set in the cloud.* A bow hung in the clouds is a bow that has been put down, and one hung with its curve upward is a bow pointed away from the earth. English "rainbow" turns a laid-down weapon into a weather phenomenon.
- **v14 "when I cloud a cloud"** — *be-'anni 'anan*, the verb and its noun from one root, the way Hebrew likes to do it. Kept literal because the smoothing to "when I bring clouds" loses the doubling.
- **vv15–16 "I will remember… to remember"** — *zakhar* again, the verb from 8:1 where God remembered Noah. The sign in the sky is not for the humans to see and take comfort; both verses say it is so that **God** will see it and remember. The covenant's reminder points upward.
- **v20 "a man of the ground"** — *ish ha-adamah*. Cain in 4:2 was an *oved adamah*, a worker of the ground. Noah, who was named for relief from the ground's curse in 5:29, becomes a man of the ground — and the first thing the restarted world produces is a drunk man naked in a tent.
- **v21 "he uncovered himself"** — *va-yitgal*, reflexive: he uncovered himself. Not "was uncovered." What follows is his own doing first.
- **v22, 23 "nakedness"** — *'ervah*. The word covers ordinary nakedness and also functions in legal contexts as a term for sexual transgression, which is why v24's "what his youngest son had **done** to him" has been read as more than looking. The Hebrew says *saw* and then says *done*. Both words are there, and the gap between them is not filled in.
- **v25 "Cursed be Canaan"** — Ham commits the act; Canaan, Ham's son, who has not appeared in the scene, receives the curse. The text states this without explaining it, and v18 and v22 both go out of their way to remind the reader whose father Ham is. Reported as it stands.
- **v27 "May God make wide for Japheth"** — *yapht Elohim le-Yephet*, a pun: the verb *yapht* (make wide, make room) chimes with the name *Yephet*. Like Noah's own name in 5:29, the blessing is built out of the sound of the name rather than its derivation.
