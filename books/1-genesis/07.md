# Genesis 7

**1** And the LORD said to Noah, "Come, you and all your household, into the ark, for you I have seen righteous before Me in this generation.

**2** From all the clean beasts you shall take for yourself seven and seven, each with its mate; and from the beasts that are not clean, two, each with its mate.

**3** Also from the bird of the heavens, seven and seven, male and female, to keep seed alive upon the face of all the earth.

**4** For in seven more days I will send rain upon the earth forty days and forty nights, and I will wipe away every standing thing that I have made from upon the face of the ground."

**5** And Noah did according to all that the LORD commanded him.

**6** And Noah was six hundred years old, and the flood came — waters upon the earth.

**7** And Noah came, and his sons, and his wife, and his sons' wives with him, into the ark, from before the waters of the flood.

**8** From the clean beasts, and from the beasts that are not clean, and from the bird, and everything that creeps upon the ground,

**9** two by two they came to Noah, into the ark, male and female, as God had commanded Noah.

## The Day the Deep Broke Open

**10** And it came to pass after the seven days that the waters of the flood were upon the earth.

**11** In the six hundredth year of Noah's life, in the second month, on the seventeenth day of the month — on that day all the fountains of the great deep were split open, and the lattices of the heavens were opened.

**12** And the rain was upon the earth forty days and forty nights.

**13** On that very day Noah came, and Shem and Ham and Japheth the sons of Noah, and Noah's wife and the three wives of his sons with them, into the ark —

**14** they, and every living thing of every kind, and every beast of every kind, and every creeping thing that creeps upon the earth of every kind, and every bird of every kind, every winged thing of every wing.

**15** And they came to Noah, into the ark, two by two from all flesh in which is the breath of life.

**16** And those that came, male and female from all flesh they came, as God had commanded him. And the LORD shut him in.

**17** And the flood was forty days upon the earth, and the waters multiplied and lifted the ark, and it rose high above the earth.

**18** And the waters overpowered and multiplied greatly upon the earth, and the ark went upon the face of the waters.

**19** And the waters overpowered exceedingly, exceedingly upon the earth, and all the high mountains under all the heavens were covered.

**20** Fifteen cubits above them the waters overpowered, and the mountains were covered.

**21** And all flesh expired that creeps upon the earth — in the bird, and in the beasts, and in the living thing, and in every swarming thing that swarms upon the earth, and every human.

**22** Everything in whose nostrils was the breath of the spirit of life, of all that was on the dry ground, died.

**23** And He wiped away every standing thing that was upon the face of the ground, from human to beast, to creeping thing, and to the bird of the heavens — and they were wiped away from the earth. And only Noah was left, and those with him in the ark.

**24** And the waters overpowered upon the earth a hundred and fifty days.

## Notes

- **v2 "seven and seven… two"** — the text says seven pairs of clean animals and two of unclean. In 6:19 the instruction was two of every kind without distinction. Both stand in the Hebrew, four verses apart, and neither is a translation artifact. Reported rather than harmonized.

  The extra ones have a use. In 8:20 Noah builds an altar and offers burnt offerings *from all the clean beasts and from all the clean birds*. Two of a kind is exactly enough to breed and nothing to spare; seven leaves a surplus that can be killed.
- **v2 "clean… not clean"** — Genesis never says what makes an animal clean. The words are used of animals three times in the book, all three in this flood (7:2, 7:8, 8:20), and the categories arrive already known, with no list and no explanation. The definitions come much later, in Leviticus 11 and Deuteronomy 14, and there they are framed as a question of **eating**: *this is the living thing you may eat*.

  Which is what they cannot be here. Nobody eats meat yet. In 1:29 God gives humanity plants for food, and permission to eat animals is granted only after the flood, in 9:3. So the clean and unclean of chapter 7 are not a diet. They are about what may be put on an altar — a distinction that exists, in this book, for no other purpose.
- **v2 "each with its mate"** — the Hebrew is *ish ve-ishto*, word for word **a man and his woman**, and one verse later it switches to *zakhar u-neqevah*, male and female, for the birds. No marriage is implied. Hebrew regularly uses *ish* and *ishah* to mean *each one* and *its counterpart*, including for things with no sex at all: in 15:10 Abram lays *ish bitro* against its fellow, **each** its carcass-half, and in Exodus 26:3 the tabernacle curtains are joined *ishah el achotah*, a woman to her sister. Isaiah 34:15 says it of birds outright — the kites gather *ishah re'utah*, each with her mate.

  So the sense is a mated pair, and the phrase is rendered that way here. It is worth knowing the letters say *a man and his woman*, because a translation that prints it literally makes the verse look like it is claiming something it is not.
- **v3 "male and female"** — *zakhar u-neqevah*, which is what 1:27 says about the making of humanity, and 5:2 after it. The phrase belongs to creation; outside Genesis the Bible never joins these two words at all (see the note at 1:27). Its only other home is this flood, where it appears four times. The ark is being loaded in the vocabulary of the first chapter — which is what makes the previous verse's different wording worth noticing, and also why nothing certain can be built on it: Hebrew varies its formulas, and one verse is not a pattern.
- **v4 "every standing thing"** — *ha-yequm*, from the root *qum*, to stand up or arise. A rare word meaning roughly "all that stands," everything that has risen into existence. English usually prints "living substance" or "living thing," which is close but loses the picture of things standing that are about to be flattened.
- **v11 "all the fountains of the great deep were split open, and the lattices of the heavens were opened"** — the single most important sentence in the chapter, and English translations generally soften it. *Tehom*, the deep, is the same word as 1:2, the dark water God divided on the second day of creation by hammering out an expanse between the waters below and the waters above. Here that expanse fails from both directions at once: the deep below is *split open* — *nivqe'u*, a violent verb, used of cracking wood and splitting a rock — and the heavens above are opened. This is not weather. It is day two running backwards, and the created order coming apart along the seam where it was made.
- **v11 "lattices"** — *arubbot*, the word for a latticed opening, a smoke-hole, a dovecote. "Floodgates" is an interpretation; "windows" is closer. The sky is a screen with holes in it, and they have been unstopped.
- **v13 "on that very day"** — *be-'etsem ha-yom ha-zeh*, literally "in the bone of this day." Hebrew idiom for *on that very day*, and it is kept literal here because the image is exactly right: the day's own bone, its hard center.
- **vv17–20 "overpowered"** — *gavar*, four times, a verb of military dominance: to prevail, to be mighty, to overcome. English prints "increased" and "prevailed," but the word is the same each time and it is a word about strength, not volume. The waters are not rising; they are winning.
- **v19 "exceedingly, exceedingly"** — *me'od me'od*, the word doubled. The last time *me'od* appeared in this book was 1:31, where God looked at everything He had made and it was *tov me'od*, very good. The intensifier returns, twice over, for the water.
- **v16 "And the LORD shut him in"** — *va-yisgor YHWH ba'ado*, four words, and they are the last thing said before the rain. Noah does not close the door. The verse also quietly changes the divine name: God, *Elohim*, gave the command in the first half of the same sentence; it is the LORD, YHWH, who shuts the door.
- **v22 "the breath of the spirit of life"** — *nishmat ruach chayyim*, stacking the word for breath from 2:7 with the word for spirit/wind from 1:2. The two words that made the human alive are named together at the moment everything with them dies.
- **v23 "wiped away… wiped away"** — *va-yimach… va-yimmachu*, active then passive, the verb God promised in 6:7 and 7:4 now carried out. *Machah* is the word for wiping a dish or blotting writing off a scroll. The earth is being erased, not drowned.
