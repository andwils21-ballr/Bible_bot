# Genesis 31

**1** And he heard the words of Laban's sons, saying, "Jacob has taken everything that was our father's, and from what was our father's he has made all this weight."

**2** And Jacob saw Laban's face, and here — it was not toward him as yesterday and the day before.

**3** And the LORD said to Jacob, "Return to the land of your fathers and to your kindred, and I will be with you."

**4** And Jacob sent and called Rachel and Leah to the field, to his flock.

**5** And he said to them, "I see your father's face, that it is not toward me as yesterday and the day before. But the God of my father has been with me.

**6** And you yourselves know that with all my strength I served your father.

**7** And your father has mocked me and changed my wages ten times over — and God did not let him do me harm.

**8** If he said this, 'The speckled shall be your wages,' then all the flock bore speckled; and if he said this, 'The striped shall be your wages,' then all the flock bore striped.

**9** So God has stripped away your father's livestock and given it to me.

**10** And it came to pass, at the time the flock went into heat, that I lifted my eyes and saw in a dream, and here — the he-goats mounting the flock were striped, speckled, and dappled.

**11** And the angel of God said to me in the dream, 'Jacob.' And I said, 'Here I am.'

**12** And he said, 'Lift your eyes now and see: all the he-goats mounting the flock are striped, speckled, and dappled — for I have seen everything Laban is doing to you.

**13** I am the God of Bethel, where you anointed a pillar, where you made a vow to me. Now rise, go out from this land, and return to the land of your kindred.'"

## Two Daughters Answer

**14** And Rachel and Leah answered and said to him, "Do we still have a portion or an inheritance in our father's house?

**15** Are we not reckoned to him as foreigners? For he sold us, and he has devoured our money outright.

**16** For all the wealth that God has stripped from our father — it is ours and our children's. So now, everything God has said to you, do."

**17** And Jacob rose and lifted his sons and his wives onto the camels.

**18** And he drove all his livestock and all his goods that he had gotten, the livestock of his acquiring that he had gotten in Paddan-aram, to come to Isaac his father, to the land of Canaan.

**19** And Laban had gone to shear his flock, and Rachel stole the household gods that were her father's.

**20** And Jacob stole the heart of Laban the Aramean, in not telling him that he was running.

**21** And he ran, he and all that was his, and he rose and crossed the River and set his face toward the hill country of Gilead.

## The Search

**22** And it was told to Laban on the third day that Jacob had run.

**23** And he took his kinsmen with him and pursued after him a journey of seven days, and overtook him in the hill country of Gilead.

**24** And God came to Laban the Aramean in a dream by night and said to him, "Guard yourself, that you do not speak with Jacob from good to bad."

**25** And Laban overtook Jacob. And Jacob had pitched his tent in the hill country, and Laban pitched his kinsmen in the hill country of Gilead.

**26** And Laban said to Jacob, "What have you done, that you stole my heart and drove my daughters off like captives of the sword?

**27** Why did you hide yourself to run, and steal from me, and not tell me? I would have sent you off with joy and with songs, with timbrel and with lyre.

**28** And you did not let me kiss my sons and my daughters. Now you have acted as the fool.

**29** My hand has the power to do you harm — but the God of your father spoke to me last night, saying, 'Guard yourself from speaking with Jacob from good to bad.'

**30** And now, you have indeed gone, because you deeply longed for your father's house. Why did you steal my gods?"

**31** And Jacob answered and said to Laban, "Because I was afraid — for I said to myself, you might tear your daughters away from me.

**32** With whomever you find your gods, he shall not live. In front of our kinsmen, identify what is mine with me and take it for yourself." And Jacob did not know that Rachel had stolen them.

**33** And Laban went into Jacob's tent and into Leah's tent and into the tent of the two maidservants, and found nothing. And he came out of Leah's tent and went into Rachel's tent.

**34** And Rachel had taken the household gods and put them in the camel's saddle-basket and sat on them. And Laban felt through the whole tent, and did not find.

**35** And she said to her father, "Let it not burn in my lord's eyes that I cannot rise before you, for the way of women is upon me." And he searched, and did not find the household gods.

## Twenty Years

**36** And it burned in Jacob, and he quarreled with Laban. And Jacob answered and said to Laban, "What is my crime? What is my sin, that you have burned after me?

**37** For you have felt through all my things — what have you found of all your household things? Set it here, in front of my kinsmen and your kinsmen, and let them decide between the two of us.

**38** These twenty years I have been with you. Your ewes and your she-goats have not miscarried, and the rams of your flock I have not eaten.

**39** Torn flesh I did not bring to you — I made it good myself. From my hand you sought it, stolen by day or stolen by night.

**40** I was — by day the heat consumed me, and the frost by night, and my sleep fled from my eyes.

**41** This is twenty years for me in your house. I served you fourteen years for your two daughters and six years for your flock, and you changed my wages ten times over.

**42** Had not the God of my father, the God of Abraham and the Fear of Isaac, been mine, you would have sent me away empty now. God has seen my affliction and the labour of my palms, and He decided last night."

## The Heap of Witness

**43** And Laban answered and said to Jacob, "The daughters are my daughters, and the sons are my sons, and the flock is my flock, and everything you see is mine. And to my daughters — what can I do for these today, or for their sons whom they have borne?

**44** And now, come, let us cut a covenant, I and you, and let it be a witness between me and you."

**45** And Jacob took a stone and raised it as a pillar.

**46** And Jacob said to his kinsmen, "Gather stones." And they took stones and made a heap, and they ate there on the heap.

**47** And Laban called it *Yegar Sahaduta*, and Jacob called it Galeed.

**48** And Laban said, "This heap is a witness between me and you today." Therefore its name was called Galeed —

**49** and Mizpah, because he said, "May the LORD keep watch between me and you when we are hidden from one another.

**50** If you afflict my daughters, and if you take wives beside my daughters — no man is with us. See, God is witness between me and you."

**51** And Laban said to Jacob, "Here is this heap, and here is the pillar I have set between me and you.

**52** This heap is witness, and the pillar is witness, that I will not cross this heap to you, and that you will not cross this heap and this pillar to me, for harm.

**53** The God of Abraham and the God of Nahor judge between us — the God of their father." And Jacob swore by the Fear of his father Isaac.

**54** And Jacob sacrificed a sacrifice in the hill country and called his kinsmen to eat bread. And they ate bread and spent the night in the hill country.

**55** And Laban rose early in the morning and kissed his sons and his daughters and blessed them. And Laban went, and returned to his place.

## Notes

- **v1 "all this weight"** — *kol ha-kavod ha-zeh*. *Kavod* is the noun English Bibles render **glory**, and its root *kaved* means **heavy**. Wealth is the oldest sense: what you own is what weighs. Laban's sons say Jacob took their father's substance and made it into all this *kavod*, and the word will still be the word for God's glory on Sinai. Weight, honour, and property are one idea in Hebrew, and English splits them into three unrelated words.
- **v2 "as yesterday and the day before"** — *kitmol shilshom*, the standard Hebrew way of saying *as it used to be*. What English can't carry is how physical the sentence is around it: Jacob **sees Laban's face**, and the face is not *toward him*. Nothing is said. The whole break with Laban starts with a change of expression across a field.
- **v7 "changed my wages ten times over"** — *hechelif et maskurti aseret monim*. *Monim* is a counting word, rare, and "ten" here is the round number Hebrew uses for *repeatedly* rather than a tally. The verb before it is *hetel bi*, from *talal*, to trifle with, to make a fool of — Laban did not merely underpay him, he played with him.
- **vv9, 16 "stripped away"** — *va-yatsel*, the hiphil of *natsal*, to snatch out, rescue, tear away. It is the word for a lamb pulled from a lion's mouth and the word for God delivering Israel from Egypt. Jacob uses it of the flocks, and then his wives repeat it back to him in v16 about their father's wealth. In the mouths of all three, God's rescuing is what happened to Laban's property.
- **v13 "I am the God of Bethel"** — *anokhi ha-El Beit-El*, awkward in Hebrew: *I am the God, Bethel*, the place-name sitting where a description should be. And *Beit-El* is itself *house of God*, so the sentence stutters — **I am the God of God's-house**. The angel is reaching twenty years back to 28:18, to the stone Jacob anointed on the night he left with nothing, and answering the vow Jacob made there by quoting it.
- **vv14–16 the only time they speak together** — *va-ta'an Rachel ve-Leah*, one verb, and the two sisters who have been set against each other for three chapters say the same thing. Their case is a property case: a bride-price was normally paid to the father and held for the daughter, and Jacob paid his in fourteen years of labour. *He sold us, and he has also devoured, devoured our money* — *akhal gam akhol*, eaten and eaten. They say they were *reckoned as foreigners*, *nokhriyyot*, the word for an outsider woman with no claim in the house. They are not choosing their husband over their father. They are telling him there is nothing left to stay for.
- **vv19, 20, 26, 27, 30, 32 "stole"** — *ganav*, six times in the chapter, and this is the spine of it. Rachel **steals** the household gods (19). Jacob **steals** Laban's heart (20) — *ganav et lev*, an idiom for taking someone in, deceiving; English has no verb for it. Laban accuses Jacob of stealing his heart (26) and then of stealing *him* (27), and finally asks *why did you steal my gods* (30). Jacob, who does not know, pronounces death on the thief (32). Then in v39 he uses the word for the sixth time about animals **stolen** from his watch, which he paid for out of his own pocket. One root carries the deception, the theft, the curse, and the twenty years of honest loss.
- **v19 "household gods"** — *terafim*. The word is never explained in the Bible and its etymology is not recoverable; what can be said is what the text shows. They are portable, they fit under a saddle, they belong to a household, and Laban calls them *elohai*, **my gods** (v30). This is Abraham's brother's family, the people Rebekah and Rachel and Leah came from, and they keep gods in the house and read omens (30:27).
- **v24 "from good to bad"** — *mi-tov ad ra*, an idiom that means *anything whatever*: God tells Laban not to say a word to Jacob, good or bad. Laban obeys the letter of it by repeating the warning back to Jacob in v29 while delivering a speech of some length. He tells Jacob he has the power to hurt him, and then explains why he isn't going to.
- **vv34–35 what Laban does not find** — *va-yemashesh Laban et kol ha-ohel*, he **felt through** the whole tent, a groping, hands-on search. The gods of his house are under his daughter, who is sitting on a camel's saddle-basket claiming *derekh nashim li*, **the way of women is upon me**. In the purity terms this family lived by, that puts the household gods under the one thing that would make them unclean, and the text reports it in a flat sentence and moves on. The only comment it allows itself is the repetition: *and did not find* three times in three verses.
- **v32 "he shall not live"** — Jacob says it in confidence, in front of witnesses, and the narrator immediately tells us he was wrong about who took them. Rachel dies in childbirth on the road in 35:19, four chapters later. The text never connects the two sentences. It only puts them in the same book, and leaves the reader to notice that the man who cursed the thief was married to her.
- **v36 "it burned"** — *va-yichar le-Ya'aqov*. The same verb that opened 30:2, *va-yichar af Ya'aqov*, his anger burned at Rachel, and the same verb Rachel uses to her father in v35, *al yichar*, **let it not burn**. Jacob is angry twice in his whole story, and both times in these two chapters: once at his wife for asking him for children, and once here, at the man who took twenty years from him. It is the longest speech he makes in Genesis.
- **vv38–40 the shepherd's defence** — this is a legal brief, in the technical vocabulary of herding contracts. *Terefah*, torn flesh, is the standing excuse of a hired shepherd: an animal killed by a predator was the owner's loss, not the herdsman's, provided the carcass was produced. Jacob says he never once used it — *anokhi achattenna*, from *chata*, the ordinary verb **to sin**, here in the sense of *make good the shortfall*, to bear the loss yourself. He absorbed the predators, the thefts by day, the thefts by night, the heat, the frost, and the sleeplessness, and he says so item by item like a man reading an invoice.
- **v42 "the Fear of Isaac"** — *pachad Yitzchaq*, a title for God that occurs here and in v53 and nowhere else. *Pachad* is ordinary Hebrew for dread. What it means as a divine name is not settled and the word does not settle it: it may be *the One Isaac fears*, or the fear-inspiring God of Isaac. Whatever it is, Jacob deliberately does not name his God the way Laban has just named his, and in v53 the distinction is the whole point.
- **v47 the only Aramaic in Genesis** — Laban names the heap *Yegar Sahaduta*; Jacob names it *Gal-ed*. The two phrases mean the same thing — **heap of witness** — in two different languages, and Laban's two words are the only Aramaic in the book. The narrator has been calling him *Laban the Aramean* since v20. This verse is where the family splits in two on the page: the branch that stayed in the north speaks Aramaic, and the branch going to Canaan speaks Hebrew, and the last thing they do together is give one pile of rocks two names.
- **v49 "Mizpah"** — *ha-mitspah*, from *tsafah*, to keep watch, and the sentence that explains it is *yitsef YHWH beini u-veinekha*, **may the LORD keep watch between me and you when we are hidden from one another**. This line is printed on rings and read at weddings as a blessing for people who miss each other. In the chapter it is a surveillance clause. Two men who have just finished accusing each other of theft are agreeing that since neither will be able to see what the other does, God will have to watch — and the next verse spells out what he is watching for.
- **v53 "the God of Abraham and the God of Nahor judge between us"** — *yishpetu*, a **plural** verb, which in Hebrew is what you use when the subject is more than one. Laban names two gods, one for each grandfather, and lets the verb agree with two. Then Jacob swears — and does not swear by that pair. He swears by *the Fear of his father Isaac*, one God, his own line only. The covenant is sealed by two men taking two different oaths on the same heap of stones.
