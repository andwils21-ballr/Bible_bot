# Genesis 48

**1** And it came to pass after these things that it was said to Joseph, "Here, your father is ill." He took his two sons with him, Manasseh and Ephraim.

**2** It was told to Jacob, and he said, "Here, your son Joseph has come to you." Israel gathered his strength and sat up on the bed.

**3** Jacob said to Joseph, "God Almighty appeared to me at Luz in the land of Canaan, and blessed me,

**4** and said to me, 'Here I am making you fruitful and multiplying you, and I will make you an assembly of peoples, and I will give this land to your seed after you as an everlasting holding.'

**5** Now, your two sons born to you in the land of Egypt before I came to you in Egypt — they are mine. Ephraim and Manasseh, like Reuben and Simeon, shall be mine.

**6** Your offspring whom you father after them shall be yours. By the name of their brothers they shall be called in their inheritance.

**7** I — when I came from Paddan, Rachel died on me in the land of Canaan, on the road, with still a stretch of ground to come to Ephrath. I buried her there, on the road to Ephrath — that is Bethlehem."

## The Crossing of the Hands

**8** Israel saw Joseph's sons and said, "Who are these?"

**9** Joseph said to his father, "They are my sons, whom God has given me here." He said, "Take them, please, to me, and let me bless them."

**10** Israel's eyes were heavy from age; he could not see. He brought them near to him, and he kissed them and embraced them.

**11** Israel said to Joseph, "I did not expect to see your face — and here, God has shown me your seed also."

**12** Joseph took them out from between his knees, and bowed with his face to the ground.

**13** Joseph took the two of them — Ephraim at his right, from Israel's left, and Manasseh at his left, from Israel's right — and brought them near to him.

**14** Israel put out his right hand and set it on Ephraim's head, and he was the younger, and his left hand on Manasseh's head. He crossed his hands, for Manasseh was the firstborn.

**15** He blessed Joseph and said, "The God before whom my fathers Abraham and Isaac walked, the God who has shepherded me from my beginning to this day,

**16** the angel who redeems me from all evil — may He bless the boys. Let my name be called in them, and the name of my fathers Abraham and Isaac. Let them teem into a multitude in the middle of the earth."

**17** Joseph saw that his father was setting his right hand on Ephraim's head, and it was bad in his eyes, and he took hold of his father's hand to move it from Ephraim's head to Manasseh's head.

**18** Joseph said to his father, "Not so, my father — for this one is the firstborn. Put your right hand on his head."

**19** His father refused and said, "I know, my son, I know. He too will become a people, and he too will be great. But his younger brother will be greater than he, and his seed will be a fullness of nations."

**20** He blessed them that day, saying, "By you Israel will bless, saying, 'May God make you like Ephraim and like Manasseh.'" And he set Ephraim before Manasseh.

**21** Israel said to Joseph, "Here, I am dying. God will be with you, and will bring you back to the land of your fathers.

**22** I have given you one shoulder above your brothers, which I took from the hand of the Amorite with my sword and with my bow."

## Notes

- **v3 "at Luz"** — the place he is thinking of on his deathbed is the one he slept at with a stone under his head on the night he ran from Esau, which he renamed Bethel (28:19). Not the ford, not Shechem, not the twenty years. The stone, the ladder, and the promise.
- **v5 "like Reuben and Simeon, shall be mine"** — he adopts Joseph's two Egyptian-born sons and names his own first and second sons as the measure. Reuben slept with his father's secondary wife (35:22) and Simeon emptied Shechem with a sword (34:25), and chapter 49 will take both of them apart for it. The two he names as the standard are the two he is about to disinherit.
- **v5 he says Ephraim first** — before the boys are brought in, before anyone's hands are anywhere, the sentence reads *Ephraim and Manasseh*, younger before elder. What happens at verse 14 was decided nine verses earlier.
- **v7 "Rachel died on me"** — *metah alai Rachel*, and it arrives with no connection to anything around it. He is in the middle of a legal adoption and he breaks off to say his wife died on the road, and he buried her there, and he gives the distance. The text offers no reason. It sits beside a sentence giving her grandsons a double share, and says nothing about why.
- **v8 "Who are these?"** — the same two words Esau said to him at the reunion in 33:5, *mi elleh lakh*. And an old man who cannot see, asking who is standing in front of him, is chapter 27 again.
- **v10 "Israel's eyes were heavy from age; he could not see"** — Isaac in 27:1: *his eyes were too dim to see*. Different verb, identical situation, and the same scene follows in both — the younger son ends up with the blessing meant for the elder. The difference is in verse 19.
- **v14 "He crossed his hands"** — *sikkel et yadav*, a rare verb for laying them crosswise. He does not ask the boys to swap places, which would have been simpler. He leaves them where their father put them and crosses his own arms over them, so that everyone in the room can see what he is doing.
- **vv15–16 two names for God that begin here** — *ha-Elohim **ha-ro'eh** oti*, the God who has **shepherded** me, and *ha-mal'akh **ha-go'el** oti*, the angel who **redeems** me. Both are first in the Bible in these two verses. The redeem-root, *ga'al*, occurs exactly once in all of Genesis, right here; it is the word for the kinsman who buys back a relative's land or freedom, and it becomes one of the largest words in the Bible for what God does. The Shepherd and the Redeemer both enter Scripture out of the mouth of a dying man describing his own life.
- **v16 "let them teem into a multitude"** — *ve-yidgu la-rov*, and the verb is built on *dag*, **fish**. It occurs nowhere else. He asks that his grandsons multiply the way fish do — the one creature the first chapter of the book says God blessed and told to fill the waters.
- **v19 "I know, my son, I know"** — *yada'ti veni yada'ti*, and this is the hinge of the chapter and the answer to chapter 27. Isaac was blind and deceived, and gave the blessing to the younger son because he could not tell them apart. Jacob is blind and not deceived. He is told plainly which boy is which, by the boys' own father, and he says he knows, and does it anyway. The same outcome, arrived at with open eyes.
- **v19 "a fullness of nations"** — *melo ha-goyim*, and 48:4 has just quoted God's promise of *an assembly of peoples*. The blessing keeps widening past the family while the family is sitting in Egypt with seventy people in it.
- **v20 "May God make you like Ephraim and like Manasseh"** — Jacob says this is the form the blessing will take in Israel's mouth, and it is still the blessing spoken over Jewish sons. Two boys born in Egypt to an Egyptian mother, named by a man who spoke of forgetting his father's house, become the words a father says over his children.
- **v22 "one shoulder above your brothers"** — *shekhem achad*, and *shekhem* is the ordinary word for a shoulder or a portion, and also the name of the town Shechem. The pun is unavoidable in Hebrew and invisible in English.

  And the claim raises a question the book does not answer. Jacob says he took it *from the hand of the Amorite with my sword and with my bow*. In 33:19 he **bought** that ground for a hundred qesitah. In chapter 34 Simeon and Levi took the city by sword while he was elsewhere, and he condemned them for it and condemns them again in 49:5–7. Three accounts of how the family came to hold Shechem, and this is the only one in which Jacob fought for it.
