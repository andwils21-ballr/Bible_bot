# Genesis 44

**1** He commanded his steward, saying, "Fill the men's bags with food, as much as they can carry, and put each man's silver in the mouth of his bag.

**2** My goblet, the silver goblet, put in the mouth of the youngest one's bag, with his grain silver." He did according to Joseph's word that he spoke.

**3** The morning was light, and the men were sent off, they and their donkeys.

**4** They had gone out of the city, not far, and Joseph said to his steward, "Rise, pursue after the men, and overtake them, and say to them, 'Why have you repaid evil for good?

**5** Is this not the cup my lord drinks from — the very one he reads the omens with? What you have done is evil.'"

**6** He overtook them and spoke these words to them.

**7** They said to him, "Why does my lord speak such words? Far be it from your servants to do such a thing.

**8** Look — the silver we found in the mouth of our bags, we brought back to you from the land of Canaan. How then could we steal silver or gold from your lord's house?

**9** The one of your servants it is found with shall die, and we also will be slaves to my lord."

**10** He said, "Now also, let it be according to your words: the one it is found with shall be my slave, and you shall be clear."

**11** They hurried and each man lowered his bag to the ground, and each man opened his bag.

**12** He searched — he began with the eldest and finished with the youngest — and the goblet was found in Benjamin's bag.

**13** They tore their clothes, and each man loaded his donkey, and they returned to the city.

**14** Judah came, and his brothers, to Joseph's house — and he was still there — and they fell before him to the ground.

**15** Joseph said to them, "What is this deed you have done? Did you not know that a man like me certainly divines?"

**16** Judah said, "What shall we say to my lord? What shall we speak, and how shall we justify ourselves? God has found out the guilt of your servants. Here we are, slaves to my lord — we, and also the one in whose hand the goblet was found."

**17** He said, "Far be it from me to do this. The man in whose hand the goblet was found, he shall be my slave, and you — go up in peace to your father."

## Judah Comes Near

**18** Judah came near to him and said, "Please, my lord — let your servant speak a word in my lord's ears, and let your anger not burn against your servant, for you are like Pharaoh.

**19** You asked us, my lord: 'Do you have a father, or a brother?'

**20** We said to my lord, 'We have an old father, and a child of his old age, a little one. His brother is dead, and he alone is left of his mother, and his father loves him.'

**21** You said to your servants, 'Bring him down to me, and let me set my eye on him.'

**22** We said to my lord, 'The boy cannot leave his father. If he leaves his father, he will die.'

**23** You said to your servants, 'If your youngest brother does not come down with you, you shall not see my face again.'

**24** And it came to pass, when we went up to your servant my father, that we told him my lord's words.

**25** Our father said, 'Go back, buy us a little food.'

**26** We said, 'We cannot go down. If our youngest brother is with us, then we will go down — for we cannot see the man's face unless our youngest brother is with us.'

**27** Your servant my father said to us, 'You know that my wife bore me two.

**28** The one went out from me, and I said, Surely he is torn, torn — and I have not seen him since.

**29** If you take this one also from my face, and harm befalls him, you will bring my gray head down in evil to Sheol.'

**30** Now, when I come to your servant my father and the boy is not with us — and his life is bound up with the boy's life —

**31** it will come to pass, when he sees that the boy is not, that he will die. Your servants will bring the gray head of your servant our father down in sorrow to Sheol.

**32** For I pledged myself to my father for the boy, saying, 'If I do not bring him to you, then I will have sinned against my father all my days.'

**33** Now, let your servant stay instead of the boy as a slave to my lord, and let the boy go up with his brothers.

**34** For how can I go up to my father and the boy not with me — and see the evil that would find my father."

## Notes

- **v2 "the silver goblet"** — *gevia ha-kesef*, and silver is what this family does. Twenty pieces of it bought Joseph in 37:28; it turned up in every sack in 42:35 and they were terrified of it; they carried it back double in 43:12. Now a silver cup is planted on the one brother who was not there when the first silver changed hands.
- **vv5, 15 "he certainly divines by it"** — *nachesh yenachesh*, from *nachash*, reading omens. The only other person in Genesis who says this word is **Laban**, in 30:27: *nichashti*, I have read the omens. Joseph, dressed as an Egyptian, now has the practice of Jacob's father-in-law attributed to him — and in v15 he says it of himself, *a man like me certainly divines*. Whether he does or is bluffing, the text does not say. It is part of the costume either way.
- **v8 "How then could we steal"** — *ve-eikh nignov*, the verb *ganav* that ran six times through chapter 31 and that Joseph used of himself in 40:15: *I was stolen, stolen*. These ten men are protesting, with complete sincerity, that theft is not the sort of thing they do.
- **v9 "shall die"** — they pronounce sentence on themselves unprompted, and it has happened in this family before. In 31:32 Jacob told Laban *with whomever you find your gods, he shall not live*, not knowing Rachel had taken them. Here Rachel's other son has the cup. Both times a man swears blind about something hidden in the baggage, and both times the person he has condemned is the one he would least choose.
- **v12 "he began with the eldest and finished with the youngest"** — the steward could have gone straight to the bag he packed. Instead he works down the line in birth order while ten men watch their brother's turn come. It is the same knowledge of their ages that astonished them at the table in 43:33, used this time to draw the thing out.
- **v13 "They tore their clothes"** — *va-yiqre'u simlotam*. Their father did this in 37:34, over a coat they had dipped in goat's blood and sent him. They are now doing it themselves, over a brother they have not harmed, in a trouble they did not arrange.
- **v14 "Judah came, and his brothers"** — not Reuben, who is the firstborn and who offered his own two sons as security in 42:37. From here to the end of the chapter, Judah speaks for all of them.
- **v16 "How shall we justify ourselves?"** — *ma nitstaddaq*, from *tsadaq*, to be in the right. Judah is the one man in Genesis who uses this root about himself, and both times it is a surrender: in 38:26 *she is more in the right than I*, and here *what shall we say... God has found out the **guilt** of your servants*. They are not guilty of the cup. He confesses anyway, to something he does not name, in front of the brother it was done to.
- **vv18–34 seventeen verses, uninterrupted** — Judah speaks and nobody answers him until the next chapter. And he argues nothing. He cites no law, denies no charge, offers no evidence about the cup; he simply retells the story from his father's side and then asks to take the punishment. It works, and chapter 45 begins with Joseph unable to keep it up any longer.
- **v20 "a child of his old age"** — *yeled zequnim*, and that is the narrator's phrase for **Joseph** in 37:3: *Israel loved Joseph more than all his sons, for he was a son of his old age*. Judah is describing Benjamin to a stranger in the exact words the book used for the brother he sold.
- **v20 "and his father loves him"** — Judah says this out loud, as an argument, to the man whose being loved is the reason he was thrown in a pit. The sentence that made Joseph hated in 37:4 is now the one being used to plead for Benjamin's life, by the brother who proposed the sale.
- **v27 "my wife bore me two"** — Jacob's words, relayed by Judah, who is **Leah's** son. Four of the men standing there are Leah's, and their father speaks of Rachel as *my wife* and counts his children as two. Judah repeats it to a foreign official without a flicker, as though it were simply how things are.
- **v28 "Surely he is torn, torn"** — *tarof toraf*, precisely what Jacob said in 37:33 when he identified the coat. Judah is quoting his father quoting a lie that Judah helped stage, to the man the lie was about, who is listening.
- **v29 "harm befalls him"** — *ason*, the third and last time the word appears in Genesis. All three are Jacob, all three about Benjamin (42:4, 42:38, and here). Judah has heard it often enough to quote it.
- **v30 "his life is bound up with the boy's life"** — *ve-nafsho qeshurah be-nafsho*, from *qashar*, to tie or knot. *Nefesh* is the breath and the life, not a spirit in the later sense. Judah's case is not that Benjamin will be hurt; it is that the two lives are knotted together and cutting one cuts both.
- **v33 "let your servant stay instead of the boy"** — *tachat ha-na'ar*, in his place, *eved*, a slave. This is Judah, who said in 37:27 *let us sell him to the Ishmaelites*, offering to be sold himself so that Rachel's remaining son can go home to the father who loves him more. Nothing in the chapter marks it as the turning point. It simply is one.
- **v34 the last word** — he does not finish on Benjamin. *Lest I see the evil that would find my father.* Twenty-two years earlier this man watched his father shown a bloodied coat and said nothing.
