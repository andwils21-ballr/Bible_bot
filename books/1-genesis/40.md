# Genesis 40

**1** And it came to pass after these things that the cupbearer of the king of Egypt and the baker offended their lord, the king of Egypt.

**2** And Pharaoh was furious at his two officers, at the chief of the cupbearers and at the chief of the bakers.

**3** And he put them in custody in the house of the chief of the slaughterers, into the round house, the place where Joseph was bound.

**4** And the chief of the slaughterers appointed Joseph to be with them, and he served them. And they were in custody for some days.

**5** And the two of them dreamed, each his own dream on the same night, and each dream with its own meaning — the cupbearer and the baker of the king of Egypt, who were being held in the round house.

**6** And Joseph came to them in the morning and saw them, and here — they were downcast.

**7** And he asked Pharaoh's officers who were with him in custody in his master's house, saying, "Why are your faces bad today?"

**8** And they said to him, "We have dreamed a dream, and there is no one to interpret it." And Joseph said to them, "Do not interpretations belong to God? Recount them to me, please."

## The Vine

**9** And the chief of the cupbearers recounted his dream to Joseph, and said to him, "In my dream — and here, a vine before me.

**10** And on the vine three tendrils, and it was as though budding; its blossom went up, its clusters ripened into grapes.

**11** And Pharaoh's cup was in my hand, and I took the grapes and pressed them into Pharaoh's cup, and I put the cup on Pharaoh's palm."

**12** And Joseph said to him, "This is its interpretation. The three tendrils are three days.

**13** In three more days Pharaoh will lift your head and restore you to your place, and you will put Pharaoh's cup in his hand, according to the former custom, when you were his cupbearer.

**14** Only remember me when it goes well with you, and do kindness with me, please, and mention me to Pharaoh, and bring me out of this house.

**15** For I was stolen outright from the land of the Hebrews, and here too I have done nothing that they should put me in the pit."

## The Baskets

**16** And the chief of the bakers saw that he had interpreted well, and he said to Joseph, "I also — in my dream, and here, three baskets of white bread on my head.

**17** And in the topmost basket, from all Pharaoh's food, the work of a baker. And the birds were eating them out of the basket from off my head."

**18** And Joseph answered and said, "This is its interpretation. The three baskets are three days.

**19** In three more days Pharaoh will lift your head from off you and hang you on a tree, and the birds will eat your flesh from off you."

**20** And it came to pass on the third day, the day of Pharaoh's birth, that he made a feast for all his servants. And he lifted the head of the chief of the cupbearers and the head of the chief of the bakers, among his servants.

**21** And he restored the chief of the cupbearers to his cupbearing, and he put the cup on Pharaoh's palm.

**22** And the chief of the bakers he hanged, just as Joseph had interpreted to them.

**23** And the chief of the cupbearers did not remember Joseph, and he forgot him.

## Notes

- **v3 "the house of the chief of the slaughterers"** — that is Potiphar's title, given in 37:36 and 39:1. The prison Joseph was thrown into in 39:20 sits inside the household he was thrown out of, and in v7 Joseph calls it *my master's house*. He has been demoted from running the estate to serving prisoners in its lockup, without changing address.
- **v5 "each man according to the interpretation of his dream"** — *ish ke-fitron chalomo*, said before anyone has interpreted anything. The sentence assumes the meaning is already in the dream, waiting, and the only question is who can read it. That assumption is what the next two chapters are built on.
- **v8 "Do not interpretations belong to God?"** — *ha-lo le-Elohim pitronim*, and the first half of their complaint is the setup: *u-foter ein oto*, **there is no interpreter for it**. Egypt was full of professional dream-readers; what these two lack is not expertise but access, because they are locked up. Joseph's answer moves the whole business out of the profession and hands it to God — and then he says *recount them to me, please*.
- **v11 "pressed them into Pharaoh's cup"** — *va-eschat*, from *sachat*, a verb that appears nowhere else in the Hebrew Bible. Whatever its precise force, the action in the dream is immediate: the grapes go from the vine to the cup to the king's hand with nothing in between, which is not how wine is made and is exactly how a dream works.
- **vv13, 19, 20 "Pharaoh will lift your head"** — *yissa Par'oh et roshekha*, and Joseph says the same four words to both men. To the cupbearer it is an idiom for being summoned and restored — the raised head of a man brought back into favour. To the baker it is the same phrase with two words added, *me-alekha*, **from off you**.

  Then v20 uses it once for both at the same party: Pharaoh *lifted the head of the chief of the cupbearers and the head of the chief of the bakers*. One idiom, one sentence, two men, and opposite outcomes. The Hebrew does it without a word of comment.
- **v14 "remember me... mention me"** — *zekhartani* and *ve-hizkartani*, the same root *zakhar* twice, the verb used of God remembering Noah in 8:1 and Rachel in 30:22. And *asita immadi chased*, **do kindness with me** — *chesed*, the loyalty word Jacob used in 32:10. Joseph asks a fellow prisoner for what the book has so far only ever said about God.
- **v15 "I was stolen, stolen"** — *gunnov gunnavti*, the verb doubled for emphasis, and it is *ganav*, the word that ran six times through chapter 31 when Rachel took the household gods and Jacob stole Laban's heart. Joseph says he was **stolen**. He was sold, by his brothers, and he does not say so — not here, and not to Potiphar, and not to Pharaoh.
- **v15 "the pit"** — *ba-bor*, the same word as the dry cistern his brothers threw him into in 37:24. He uses it of an Egyptian prison. Whatever the building was, the word he reaches for is the one from the field at Dothan.
- **v16 "three baskets of white bread"** — *sallei chori*, and *chori* is not recoverable. It has been read as white, and as wicker or holed, and nothing in the language settles it. What is clear is the position: the baskets are stacked **on his head**, the topmost holds the king's own food, and the birds are already eating out of it while he stands there.
- **v19 "hang you on a tree"** — *ve-talah otekha al ets*. The Hebrew says hung on wood and does not specify how; impaling and hanging are both within the word. The detail the dream supplies is the one Joseph repeats back: the birds eat the flesh, which means the body stays up.
- **v16 "he saw that he had interpreted well"** — the baker asks only after hearing a good answer. He would not have asked otherwise, and he gets three days' notice of his own execution from a man who owes him nothing and does not soften it. Joseph gives the reading as it is, to a frightened man, with no preamble.
- **v23 "did not remember... and he forgot him"** — *ve-lo zakhar... va-yishkachehu*. Two verbs for one failure, the second one making sure of the first, and they answer Joseph's *remember me* in v14 exactly. It is the last sentence of the chapter, and the next one begins *at the end of two full years*.
