# Genesis 22

**1** And it came to pass after these things that God tested Abraham. And He said to him, "Abraham." And he said, "Here I am."

**2** And He said, "Take, please, your son — your beloved one — whom you have loved — Isaac. And go, for yourself, to the land of Moriah, and bring him up there as a burnt offering, upon one of the mountains that I will say to you."

**3** And Abraham rose early in the morning and saddled his donkey, and took his two young men with him, and Isaac his son. And he split the wood for the burnt offering, and he rose and went to the place that God had said to him.

**4** On the third day Abraham lifted his eyes and saw the place from far off.

**5** And Abraham said to his young men, "Sit yourselves here with the donkey, and I and the boy will go over there. And we will bow down, and we will return to you."

**6** And Abraham took the wood of the burnt offering and set it on Isaac his son, and he took in his hand the fire and the knife. And the two of them went together.

**7** And Isaac said to Abraham his father, and he said, "My father." And he said, "Here I am, my son." And he said, "Here is the fire and the wood — and where is the lamb for a burnt offering?"

**8** And Abraham said, "God will see to the lamb for a burnt offering for Himself, my son." And the two of them went together.

**9** And they came to the place that God had said to him, and Abraham built there the altar and arranged the wood. And he bound Isaac his son and set him on the altar, on top of the wood.

**10** And Abraham put out his hand and took the knife, to slaughter his son.

**11** And the messenger of the LORD called to him from the heavens and said, "Abraham! Abraham!" And he said, "Here I am."

**12** And He said, "Do not put out your hand toward the boy, and do not do anything to him. For now I know that you fear God, and you have not withheld your son, your beloved one, from Me."

**13** And Abraham lifted his eyes and saw — and here, a ram, behind, caught in the thicket by its horns. And Abraham went and took the ram and brought it up as a burnt offering in place of his son.

**14** And Abraham called the name of that place "The LORD Will See," of which it is said today, "On the mountain of the LORD it will be seen."

**15** And the messenger of the LORD called to Abraham a second time from the heavens,

**16** and said, "By Myself I have sworn — the utterance of the LORD — that because you have done this thing, and have not withheld your son, your beloved one,

**17** that blessing, I will bless you; and multiplying, I will multiply your seed as the stars of the heavens and as the sand that is on the lip of the sea. And your seed shall possess the gate of his enemies.

**18** And in your seed all the nations of the earth shall bless themselves — because you listened to My voice."

**19** And Abraham returned to his young men, and they rose and went together to Beersheba. And Abraham settled in Beersheba.

## News from Home

**20** And it came to pass after these things that it was told to Abraham, saying, "Here — Milcah has borne sons, she also, to Nahor your brother:

**21** Uz his firstborn, and Buz his brother, and Kemuel father of Aram,

**22** and Chesed and Hazo and Pildash and Jidlaph, and Bethuel."

**23** And Bethuel fathered Rebekah. These eight Milcah bore to Nahor, Abraham's brother.

**24** And his secondary wife, whose name was Reumah — she also bore Tebah and Gaham and Tahash and Maacah.

## Notes

- **v1 "Here I am"** — *hinneni*, one word. It is said three times in this chapter and nowhere else in it: to God in v1, to Isaac in v7, to the messenger in v11. The same word to the one commanding, to the one being taken, and to the one who stops it. The chapter is built on those three.
- **v2 "Take, please, your son — your beloved one — whom you have loved — Isaac"** — four phrases, each introduced by the object marker, and they narrow exactly as 12:1 narrowed: *your land, your kindred, your father's house*. Same technique, same speaker, same man. The tightening ends on the name.
- **v2 "And go, for yourself"** — *lekh lekha*. These two words occur together **only twice in the Hebrew Bible**, and both times God says them to Abraham: here, and in 12:1. Both times he is sent to a place that is not named — *the land that I will show you*, *one of the mountains that I will say to you*. The call and the test are written as the same sentence.
- **v2 "your beloved one"** — *yechidcha*, from *yachid*. The word occurs **nowhere else in Genesis** except here and at vv12 and 16, always of Isaac. Its range runs from *only* and *solitary* through *one of a kind* to — because an only child is irreplaceable — **precious, beloved**; elsewhere in the Hebrew Bible a man calls his own life his *yechidah*. It is not simply a counting word, which matters here, because Abraham has another son. The Greek translators did not read it as a number at all: all three times they wrote *ton agapēton*, **the beloved**, with no "only" anywhere. And *agapētos* is the word the voice from heaven uses at the Jordan — *this is my beloved son* (Matthew 3:17, Mark 1:11). A Greek reader had heard that sentence before, on a mountain in Moriah.
- **v2 "your beloved one" — what the other son was given** — Abraham has two sons, and the book has already divided between them. Of Ishmael: *I have blessed him, and I will make him fruitful, and I will multiply him beyond measure. Twelve chiefs he shall father, and I will make him into a great nation* (17:20), repeated to Abraham at 21:13 and to Hagar in the wilderness at 21:18. Of Isaac, in the very next verse: *But My covenant I will raise up with Isaac* (17:21). One son is promised a bloodline; the other is promised the covenant. Genesis 25:16 records the first half paid in full — twelve sons, named, twelve chiefs, by their villages. So what is at risk on this mountain is not Abraham's line, which is secured twice over. It is the covenant.
- **v2 "whom you have loved"** — *ahavta*. This is the **first occurrence of the word love in the Bible**. Not between man and woman, not between God and anyone: it appears for the first time in the sentence commanding a father to give up the son he loves.
- **v2 "as a burnt offering"** — *olah*, from *alah*, **to go up**: the offering is named for the smoke that rises, not for the fire (see 8:20). The verb beside it in the same verse is *ha'alehu*, **bring him up**. He is told to bring his son up as a going-up, on a mountain — three motions upward in one command, and English carries none of them.
- **vv3, 5 "his two young men"** — they are never named, and they do not go up. Abraham leaves them at the bottom with the donkey (v5) and comes back down to them (v19); only two people are on that mountain. Nothing in Genesis links either of them to Ishmael, who was sent away one chapter earlier. The word is *na'ar*, and it is the same word the chapter uses for **Isaac** — *I and the na'ar will go over there* (v5), *do not put out your hand toward the na'ar* (v12). Abraham leaves two young men behind and takes a third up the hill.
- **v3 "And he split the wood"** — he does this before setting out, at home, in the dark, for a fire three days away. Nothing required it then. The verse lists the saddling, the men, the son, and the wood in one breath and does not pause on any of them.
- **v4 "on the third day"** — the text gives no word for those three days. Whatever was said on the road is not recorded.
- **v5 "we will bow down, and we will return to you"** — both verbs are first person **plural**. He tells the servants that two will come back.
- **vv6, 8 "And the two of them went together"** — *va-yelekhu shneihem yachdav*, identical, twice, framing the only conversation between father and son in the Bible. It is said once before the question and once after the answer, and the second time it means something different.
- **v7 "Here is the fire and the wood — and where is the lamb?"** — Isaac carries the wood; his father carries the fire and the knife, the two things that could hurt him. The boy counts what is present and names what is missing.
- **v8 "God will see to the lamb"** — *Elohim yir'eh lo ha-seh*. The verb is *ra'ah*, to **see**. Hebrew says *see to it* where English says *provide*, and this chapter runs on that verb: Abraham *saw* the place from far off (v4), God will *see* the lamb (v8), Abraham lifted his eyes and *saw* the ram (v13), and the place is named for it twice in v14. The word for the whole chapter's mercy is an eye.
- **v9 "And he bound"** — *va-ya'aqod*. This verb, in this sense, occurs **once** in the entire Hebrew Bible. The same three letters appear elsewhere, but as an adjective for streaked animals (30:35–31:12) and in a place name (2 Kings 10:12). The act has no other name. This is why Jewish tradition calls the chapter *the Akedah*, the Binding — the whole episode is known by the one word that exists only inside it.
- **v12 "you have not withheld"** — *lo chasakhta*. Two chapters back, God said to Abimelech *I also **held you back** from sinning against Me* — *va-echsokh*, the same verb. God withheld a pagan king from a sin; Abraham did not withhold his son.
- **v13 "a ram, behind"** — *ayil achar*, and the second word is a problem. *Achar* means behind or after; some readers take it as *echad*, one, differing by a single letter. As it stands the ram is *behind* — which is why Abraham has to lift his eyes and turn.
- **v14 "The LORD Will See… it will be seen"** — *YHWH yir'eh*, then *be-har YHWH yera'eh*. The same verb, active and then passive. He names the place *the LORD sees*; the saying that survives is *on the mountain, He is seen*. One letter separates being the one who looks from being the one who is looked at.
- **v16 "By Myself I have sworn"** — the only time God swears by Himself to Abraham. In 15:17 He passed alone between the pieces; here there is nothing left to swear by that is not Himself.
- **v17 "as the stars… and as the sand"** — the promise has been made three times with three images: *dust of the earth* (13:16), *stars of the heavens* (15:5), and now both stars and sand together. Each time it is given after Abraham has let something go.
- **v19 "And Abraham returned to his young men"** — Abraham returns. Isaac is not mentioned. Two went up together, twice; one is named coming down. The text does not say Isaac stayed, and it does not say he came. It simply stops counting him, and the next time he appears is in 24:62, arriving from the well called *Lahai-roi* — Hagar's well.
- **v24 "his secondary wife"** — *pilegesh*, and she is not a mistress. She is a member of the household with standing: her name is recorded, her four sons are counted in the family list beside the eight born to the wife, and the same word covers Bilhah, whose sons become two of the twelve tribes. What the word marks is **rank**, not irregularity — a wife of the second order, usually without the dowry and the standing a full marriage brought. English has called her a *concubine* since the Latin, and that word now suggests something the Hebrew does not say.
- **vv20–24 twelve sons, and Abraham has one** — the knife comes down off Isaac, Abraham walks back to Beersheba, and the very next sentence is news from home: his brother Nahor has eight sons by his wife and four more by a secondary wife. The list is dropped in without comment, immediately after a chapter in which Abraham nearly lost the only son he has. Twelve is also the number Jacob's household will end up with, three generations later, and it is Nahor's line that gets there first.
- **v23 "And Bethuel fathered Rebekah"** — the point of the whole list, and the only woman named in it. She arrives here, in a genealogy, two chapters before Abraham's servant is sent north to find a wife for Isaac and finds her at a well. The reader is given her before the servant is.
- **vv21–22 Uz and Chesed** — two of these names go somewhere. *Uts* is the land Job lives in: *a man was in the land of **Uz**, Job his name* (Job 1:1). And *Kesed* is the singular behind *Kasdim*, the **Chaldeans** — the people of *Ur of the Chaldees*, where this family started and where Haran died (11:28). The genealogy quietly ties Abraham's relatives to the country he left and to the one book of the Bible set outside Israel altogether.
