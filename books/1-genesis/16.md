# Genesis 16

**1** And Sarai, Abram's wife, had borne him nothing. And she had an Egyptian servant girl, and her name was Hagar.

**2** And Sarai said to Abram, "Look now — the LORD has held me back from bearing. Come, please, to my servant girl. Perhaps I will be built up from her." And Abram listened to the voice of Sarai.

**3** And Sarai, Abram's wife, took Hagar the Egyptian, her servant girl — at the end of ten years of Abram's dwelling in the land of Canaan — and gave her to Abram her husband, to him as a wife.

**4** And he came to Hagar, and she conceived. And she saw that she had conceived, and her mistress counted for little in her eyes.

**5** And Sarai said to Abram, "My violence is upon you. I myself gave my servant girl into your lap, and she saw that she had conceived, and I counted for little in her eyes. Let the LORD judge between me and you."

**6** And Abram said to Sarai, "Look — your servant girl is in your hand. Do to her what is good in your eyes." And Sarai afflicted her, and she fled from her face.

## The Spring on the Way

**7** And the messenger of the LORD found her by a spring of water in the wilderness, by the spring on the way to Shur.

**8** And he said, "Hagar, servant girl of Sarai, where have you come from, and where are you going?" And she said, "From the face of Sarai my mistress I am fleeing."

**9** And the messenger of the LORD said to her, "Return to your mistress, and submit yourself under her hands."

**10** And the messenger of the LORD said to her, "I will greatly multiply your seed — too many to be counted."

**11** And the messenger of the LORD said to her, "Look — you are with child, and you shall bear a son, and you shall call his name Ishmael, for the LORD has heard your affliction.

**12** And he shall be a wild donkey of a man. His hand against all, and the hand of all against him; and in the face of all his brothers he shall dwell."

**13** And she called the name of the LORD who spoke to her, "You are the God who sees me," for she said, "Have I really seen here the One who sees me?"

**14** That is why the well was called Beer-lahai-roi — the Well of the Living One who sees me. It lies between Kadesh and Bered.

**15** And Hagar bore Abram a son, and Abram called the name of his son whom Hagar bore, Ishmael.

**16** And Abram was eighty-six years old when Hagar bore Ishmael to Abram.

## Notes

- **v2 "Perhaps I will be built up from her"** — *ulai ibbaneh mimmennah*, from *banah*, to build — the verb used of the woman in 2:22, where God *built* the side into a woman. Hebrew hears *ben*, son, inside it: to be built is to be given a son. English can keep "built up" but cannot keep the son inside the word.
- **v2 "And Abram listened to the voice of Sarai"** — *va-yishma Avram le-qol Sarai*. This is the sentence God spoke in 3:17, word for word in its construction: *because you listened to the voice of your wife*. It has not appeared in between. The book saves it for here, and it is the only comment the narrator makes on the whole arrangement.
- **v4 "her mistress counted for little in her eyes"** — *va-teqal*, from *qalal*, to be light, slight, of no weight. It is the verb from 12:3 — *the one who treats you lightly I will curse* — and it has now happened inside Abram's own tent, in both directions: Hagar makes Sarai light in v4, and Sarai says *I became light in her eyes* in v5. The promise of 12:3 has a household problem.
- **v5 "My violence is upon you"** — *chamasi alekha*. *Chamas* is the word that filled the earth before the flood in 6:11 and 6:13 — lawless seizure, the taking of what is not yours. Sarai uses the flood-word for what has been done to her, three verses after she arranged it herself.
- **v5 "between me and you"** — the Masoretes placed a dot over the final letter of *u-veinekha*, "and you." These marks — a handful of them across the Hebrew Bible — are scribal flags on something the copyists found odd, and the tradition preserved the dot without ever explaining it or changing the text. The dot is still there, on this word, in the manuscripts we read from.
- **v6 "And Sarai afflicted her"** — *va-te'anneha*, from *anah*. Fourteen verses earlier, in 15:13, God told Abram that his seed would be guests in a land not theirs, and that the people there *would serve them and afflict them* — *ve-innu otam*, the same verb. The word for what Egypt will do to Abram's descendants is used here for what Abram's household does to an Egyptian, in the next chapter but one. And the name Ishmael is given in v11 *because the LORD has heard your affliction* — *onyekh*, the same root a third time.
- **v6 "and she fled from her face"** — *va-tivrach*. She is the first person in the Bible to run away.
- **v7 "the messenger of the LORD"** — *mal'akh YHWH*, the first occurrence in the Bible. *Mal'akh* means a sent one, a courier; "angel" comes to English through Greek *angelos*, which is the same word. It is a job, not a species. And the first person he is sent to is a runaway foreign slave.
- **v8 "where have you come from, and where are you going?"** — the first question anyone is asked in this book since *Where are you?* in 3:9 and *Where is Abel your brother?* in 4:9. God's questions in Genesis are addressed to people who are hiding.
- **v9 "submit yourself under her hands"** — *ve-hit'anni*, the reflexive of the same *anah* that Sarai did to her in v6. She is told to go back and put herself under the thing she ran from. The text records the instruction and offers no softening of it.
- **v12 "a wild donkey of a man"** — *pere adam*. The *pere* is the untamed onager of the desert, and the phrase is not obviously an insult; Job 39 describes the wild donkey as a creature that scorns the city and answers to no driver. Freedom and unmanageability in one animal.
- **v13 "she called the name of the LORD who spoke to her"** — Hagar **names God**. No one else in the Hebrew Bible does this. Abram builds altars and calls on the name; she gives one. And she is an Egyptian slave woman, running, pregnant, with no standing in anyone's household.
- **v13 "the God who sees me… Have I really seen here the One who sees me?"** — she names Him *El Ro'i*, God of my seeing, and then comes a sentence that has defeated everyone: *ha-gam halom ra'iti acharei ro'i*. The words are all common and the sense will not resolve. Has she seen and lived? Has she seen the back of the one who sees her? Is *halom*, "here," pointing at the place or at her survival? The verse is built entirely out of the root *ra'ah*, to see, four times in two lines, and the naming is certain even where the sentence is not.

  One word sits between "seen" and "the One who sees me" and is the reason the sentence will not resolve: *acharei*, **after** or **behind**. No rendering of it produces English that reads.
