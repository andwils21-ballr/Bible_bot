# Genesis 30

**1** Rachel saw that she had borne Jacob no children, and Rachel was jealous of her sister. She said to Jacob, "Give me sons — and if not, I am dead."

**2** Jacob's anger burned against Rachel, and he said, "Am I in the place of God, who has withheld from you the fruit of the womb?"

**3** She said, "Here is my maidservant Bilhah. Come to her, and let her bear upon my knees, and I too will be built up from her."

**4** She gave him Bilhah her servant girl as a wife, and Jacob came to her.

**5** Bilhah conceived and bore Jacob a son.

**6** Rachel said, "God has judged me, and He has also heard my voice and given me a son." Therefore she called his name Dan.

**7** Bilhah, Rachel's servant girl, conceived again and bore a second son to Jacob.

**8** Rachel said, "Wrestlings of God I have wrestled with my sister, and I have prevailed." She called his name Naphtali.

**9** Leah saw that she had stopped bearing, and she took Zilpah her servant girl and gave her to Jacob as a wife.

**10** Zilpah, Leah's servant girl, bore Jacob a son.

**11** Leah said, "Fortune has come." She called his name Gad.

**12** Zilpah, Leah's servant girl, bore a second son to Jacob.

**13** Leah said, "In my happiness — for the daughters will call me happy." She called his name Asher.

## The Mandrakes

**14** Reuben went out in the days of the wheat harvest and found mandrakes in the field, and brought them to Leah his mother. Rachel said to Leah, "Give me, please, some of your son's mandrakes."

**15** She said to her, "Is it not enough that you took my husband — and now you would take my son's mandrakes as well?" Rachel said, "Then let him lie with you tonight, for your son's mandrakes."

**16** Jacob came in from the field in the evening, and Leah went out to meet him and said, "You will come to me, for I have surely hired you with my son's mandrakes." He lay with her that night.

**17** God heard Leah, and she conceived and bore Jacob a fifth son.

**18** Leah said, "God has given me my wages, because I gave my servant girl to my husband." She called his name Issachar.

**19** Leah conceived again and bore a sixth son to Jacob.

**20** Leah said, "God has given me a good endowment. This time my husband will dwell with me, for I have borne him six sons." She called his name Zebulun.

**21** Afterward she bore a daughter, and she called her name Dinah.

**22** God remembered Rachel, and God heard her, and He opened her womb.

**23** She conceived and bore a son, and she said, "God has gathered up my disgrace."

**24** She called his name Joseph, saying, "May the LORD add to me another son."

## Name Your Wages

**25** And it came to pass, when Rachel had borne Joseph, that Jacob said to Laban, "Send me away, and let me go to my own place and to my land.

**26** Give me my wives and my children, for whom I served you, and let me go — for you yourself know my service, how I served you."

**27** Laban said to him, "If now I have found favor in your eyes — I have read the omens: the LORD has blessed me because of you."

**28** He said, "Name your wages upon me, and I will give them."

**29** He said to him, "You yourself know how I served you, and what your livestock became with me.

**30** For the little that was yours before me has broken out into a multitude, and the LORD has blessed you at my foot. And now — when shall I do something for my own house, I also?"

**31** He said, "What shall I give you?" Jacob said, "You shall give me nothing. If you will do this thing for me, I will go back, I will graze your flock, I will keep it.

**32** Let me pass through all your flock today, removing from it every speckled and spotted sheep, and every dark one among the lambs, and the spotted and speckled among the goats. That shall be my wages.

**33** My righteousness will answer for me on a later day, when you come to look over my wages: every one among the goats that is not speckled and spotted, and among the lambs that is not dark — if it is with me, it is stolen."

**34** Laban said, "Here — let it be according to your word."

**35** On that day he removed the striped and spotted he-goats, and all the speckled and spotted she-goats, every one that had white in it, and every dark one among the lambs, and he gave them into the hand of his sons.

**36** He set a distance of three days' journey between himself and Jacob. Jacob was grazing the rest of Laban's flock.

**37** Jacob took for himself fresh branches of poplar and almond and plane, and peeled white strips in them, exposing the white that was underneath.

**38** He set the branches that he had peeled in the troughs, in the watering channels where the flocks came to drink, right in front of the flocks — and they went into heat when they came to drink.

**39** The flocks went into heat in front of the branches, and the flocks bore striped, speckled, and spotted.

**40** The lambs Jacob separated out, and he set the faces of the flock toward the striped and toward every dark one in Laban's flock. He made himself droves of his own, and did not put them with Laban's flock.

**41** And it came to pass, whenever the vigorous of the flock went into heat, that Jacob would set the rods before the eyes of the flock, in the troughs, to make them heat at the rods.

**42** When the flock was feeble he would not set them. So the feeble went to Laban, and the vigorous to Jacob.

**43** The man broke out beyond measure, and he had many flocks, and maidservants and menservants, and camels and donkeys.

## Notes

- **v1 "Give me sons — and if not, I am dead"** — seven words in Hebrew: *havah li vanim, ve-im ayin, metah anokhi*. The last two are not "I will die." *Metah* is a participle and *anokhi* is the emphatic "I" — *dead, I am*. She is not predicting; she is describing her present condition. And she says it to Jacob, having just been told by the narrator in 29:31 who it is that opens wombs.
- **v2 "Am I in the place of God"** — *ha-tachat Elohim anokhi*. He answers her *anokhi* with his own. Two people in two verses each saying **I**, and the argument is about which of them is not God. This is also the first time in the book that Jacob is angry — *va-yichar af*, his nose burned, which is what Hebrew anger does.
- **v3 "let her bear upon my knees, and I too will be built up from her"** — *ve-ibbaneh*, from *banah*, to build. *Ben*, son, comes from the same root: in Hebrew, to get a child is to be **built**. The sentence is not new. Sarai said it in 16:2 — *bo na el shifchati, ulai ibbaneh mimmennah*, come to my servant girl, perhaps I will be built from her — the identical verb, the identical arrangement, and that one produced Ishmael, Hagar's contempt, and a woman driven into the desert twice. Rachel repeats it word for word two generations later.

  She also changes the word for Bilhah between verses. In her own mouth Bilhah is *amati*, my maidservant; when the narrator reports the handover in v4 she is *shifchatah*, her servant girl. Both are slave words. The one Rachel uses is the slightly higher of the two.
- **v6 "God has judged me"** — *dananni Elohim*, root *din*: to judge, and specifically to decide a case in someone's favor. Not "God punished me" — *vindicated me*. She has won something. And it is **Rachel** who names him, not Bilhah, who bore him; the woman who gave birth does not speak in this chapter at all.
- **v8 "Wrestlings of God I have wrestled"** — *naftulei Elohim niftalti*. The root is *patal*, to twist — used of a twisted cord and of crooked, devious speech. The noun *naftulim* occurs nowhere else in the Bible, so what the phrase weighs is genuinely open: *Elohim* may be doing the work Hebrew often gives it of an intensifier, **mighty** wrestlings, or she may mean the struggle was with God and her sister was only where it happened.

  What is not open is the last word. *Gam yakholti*, **and I have prevailed** — and in 32:28 the man at the ford tells Jacob *you have struggled with God and with men, and you have prevailed*, **va-tukhal**, the same verb. The wrestling words differ; the prevailing word is the same. Rachel says of her sister, twenty years early, what the angel will say of her husband — and Jacob gets a new name for it while she gets a son she did not carry.
- **v11 "Fortune has come"** — this verse preserves a scribal split that is visible on the page. The consonants written are **בגד**, one word; what is read aloud is **בָּא גָד**, two words, *ba gad*, *fortune has come*. The scribes kept a written form they did not pronounce and marked the reading beside it rather than correcting the text — the same conservatism that left Mehujael spelled two ways in one verse at 4:18.

  And *gad* is not only a common noun. Isaiah 65:11 condemns those *who set a table for **Gad***, where the word is the name of a god of luck. Leah, in the middle of a fertility contest, names a son Luck.
- **v13 "In my happiness — for the daughters will call me happy"** — *be-oshri... ishsheruni banot*, root *ashar*, to be happy, to be called fortunate. Two sons in a row named for good luck, from a woman who was hated in the previous chapter. And she says **daughters** will call her happy. She has no daughter yet; one arrives eight verses later and is not named for happiness or for anything else.
- **v14 "mandrakes"** — *duda'im*. The consonants are those of *dod*, **beloved**, the word that runs through the Song of Songs on every page: the mandrake is the love-plant, a forked root vaguely shaped like a body, held to bring on conception. Then the chapter does what it does with everything: Rachel buys them and stays barren for eight more verses; Leah, who sells them, conceives that night.
- **v15 "Is it not enough that you took my husband"** — *ha-me'at qachtekh et ishi*. This is the only sentence Leah is ever recorded saying to Rachel, and the only line in Genesis spoken between the two sisters. From Leah's side of chapter 29, Jacob was hers first: she was married to him, and Rachel arrived a week later.
- **v16 "I have surely hired you"** — *sakhor sekhartikha*, the infinitive doubled for emphasis, and it is the vocabulary of wage labor, the same root Jacob and Laban use for his contract in vv28–33. Leah goes out to the field to tell her husband she has paid for him. The verb for what follows is *va-yishkav immah*, **he lay with her** — not the *yada*, to know, of 4:1. Jacob's night is a transaction on both ends.
- **v18 "Issachar"** — *natan Elohim sekhari*, God has given my **wages**, root *sakhar*, the word from the verse before. And the name has an oddity you can see: it is written **יִשָּׂשכָר**, with two sibilants where only one is pronounced. The written form carries a letter the reading drops, and the scribes preserved it rather than tidy the spelling.
- **v20 "God has given me a good endowment. This time my husband will dwell with me"** — one sentence, two different roots, and the name matches the second. *Zevadani... zeved tov* is *zabad*, to bestow a gift. *Yizbeleni* is *zabal*, a rare verb whose noun *zevul* means an exalted dwelling — it is used of the temple. So the sixth name is explained twice over, and neither explanation is quite the name: it is a sentence about a gift wrapped around a sentence about finally being lived with. Six sons in, that is still what she is asking for.
- **v21 "Afterward she bore a daughter, and she called her name Dinah"** — fifteen words of explanation for every son in this chapter, and for her, nothing. She is the only child here whose name is given without a reason, and she is the only daughter. *Dinah* is the feminine of *Dan*, from *din*, to judge — the root that got Rachel a whole sentence at v6 and gets Leah none here. Chapter 34 is hers, and it is about what is done to her rather than anything she says.
- **v22 "God remembered Rachel"** — *va-yizkor Elohim*, the verb used of Noah in the ark at 8:1 and of Abraham at 19:29. In these verses it never means God had forgotten. *Zakhar* is remembering as an **act**: the moment God moves on something owed.
- **vv23–24 "Joseph"** — named twice in two verses, off two different roots. *Asaf Elohim et cherpati*, God has **gathered up** my disgrace — *asaf*, to gather in, to take away. Then *yosef YHWH li ben acher*, may the LORD **add** me another son — *yasaf*, to add. The name as spelled is the second. But her first sentence is the one that says what happened, and her second is a request made in the same breath as the answer: at the exact moment of getting what she said she would die without, she asks for one more. She gets him in 35:18, and it kills her.
- **v27 "I have read the omens"** — *nichashti*, root *nachash*, the word for divination by signs. Laban says it plainly, in the middle of a compliment, and he is the man who in 31:30 will accuse Jacob of stealing **his gods**. The house Jacob married into keeps omens and idols, and nobody in the chapter treats this as remarkable.
- **vv35, 37 "everything Laban has is white"** — the trick is built on his name. *Lavan* means **white**. In v35 Laban removes *kol asher lavan bo*, which reads word for word as *every one that has white in it* and also as *every one that Laban is in*. In v37 Jacob cuts rods of *livneh* — poplar, a tree named for its whiteness, the same three consonants — and peels *petsalot levanot*, white peelings, *machsof ha-lavan*, the exposing of the white. Four white words in three verses, aimed at a man named White. The Hebrew is doing out loud what the story is doing quietly: Jacob is stripping Laban.
- **v43 "And the man broke out exceedingly"** — *va-yifrots ha-ish me'od me'od*. In v30 Jacob complained to Laban that *the little that was yours before me has **broken out** into a multitude* — *va-yifrots la-rov*, the same verb, about Laban's wealth increasing on Jacob's labor. Thirteen verses later it happens to Jacob, off Laban's flock. The chapter closes by using the accusation as the verdict.
- **v18 Leah's reasoning, and whose it is** — the text does not say God wanted the servant girl given away. It says **Leah** read the fifth son as payment for having done it. That distinction matters, because every one of her sons is named with a sentence arguing her case: Reuben, *the LORD has seen my affliction, now my husband will love me*; Simeon, *the LORD has heard that I am hated*; Levi, *now my husband will be joined to me*; then Issachar here, and Zebulun, *this time my husband will dwell with me*. She reads each birth as a verdict in a case she keeps losing. And she is matching Rachel move for move — Rachel handed over Bilhah in v3, so Leah handed over Zilpah in v9. The surrogate arrangement was an ordinary legal one, used by Sarai in 16:2 and by Rachel here; what is unusual is not the act but that Leah counts it as a debt God then settled with her.
