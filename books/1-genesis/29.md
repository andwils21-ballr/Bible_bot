# Genesis 29

**1** Jacob lifted his feet and went to the land of the sons of the east.

**2** He looked, and here — a well in the field, and here, three flocks of sheep lying beside it, for from that well they watered the flocks. The stone on the mouth of the well was large.

**3** All the flocks would gather there, and they would roll the stone from the mouth of the well and water the sheep, and return the stone to its place on the mouth of the well.

**4** Jacob said to them, "My brothers, where are you from?" They said, "We are from Harran."

**5** He said to them, "Do you know Laban son of Nahor?" They said, "We know him."

**6** He said to them, "Is it well with him?" They said, "Well. And here — Rachel his daughter, coming with the sheep."

**7** He said, "Look, the day is still long. It is not time for the livestock to be gathered. Water the sheep and go, graze them."

**8** They said, "We cannot, until all the flocks are gathered and they roll the stone from the mouth of the well. Then we water the sheep."

**9** He was still speaking with them, and Rachel came with the sheep that were her father's, for she was a shepherd.

**10** And it came to pass, when Jacob saw Rachel daughter of Laban his mother's brother, and the sheep of Laban his mother's brother, that Jacob came near and rolled the stone from the mouth of the well, and watered the sheep of Laban his mother's brother.

**11** Jacob kissed Rachel, and he lifted his voice and wept.

**12** Jacob told Rachel that he was her father's brother, and that he was Rebekah's son. She ran and told her father.

**13** And it came to pass, when Laban heard the report of Jacob his sister's son, that he ran to meet him and embraced him and kissed him, and brought him to his house. He recounted to Laban all these things.

**14** Laban said to him, "Surely you are my bone and my flesh." He stayed with him a full month.

## Seven Years

**15** Laban said to Jacob, "Because you are my brother, should you serve me for nothing? Tell me — what shall your wages be?"

**16** Laban had two daughters. The name of the elder was Leah, and the name of the younger was Rachel.

**17** Leah's eyes were soft, and Rachel was beautiful in form and beautiful to look at.

**18** Jacob loved Rachel, and he said, "I will serve you seven years for Rachel your younger daughter."

**19** Laban said, "Better that I give her to you than that I give her to another man. Stay with me."

**20** Jacob served seven years for Rachel, and they were in his eyes like a few days, because of his love for her.

**21** Jacob said to Laban, "Give me my wife, for my days are filled, and let me come to her."

**22** Laban gathered all the men of the place and made a feast.

**23** And it came to pass in the evening that he took Leah his daughter and brought her to him, and he came to her.

**24** Laban gave her Zilpah his servant girl — to Leah his daughter, a servant girl.

**25** And it came to pass in the morning — and here, it was Leah. He said to Laban, "What is this you have done to me? Was it not for Rachel that I served with you? Why have you deceived me?"

**26** Laban said, "It is not done so in our place, to give the younger before the firstborn.

**27** Finish out the wedding week for this one, and we will give you the other one as well, for the service you will serve with me another seven years."

**28** Jacob did so, and finished out her wedding week, and he gave him Rachel his daughter as a wife.

**29** Laban gave to Rachel his daughter Bilhah his servant girl, to her as a servant girl.

**30** He came also to Rachel, and he loved Rachel also, more than Leah. He served with him yet another seven years.

## The Sons of the Hated One

**31** The LORD saw that Leah was hated, and He opened her womb. Rachel was barren.

**32** Leah conceived and bore a son, and she called his name Reuben, for she said, "Because the LORD has seen my affliction — for now my husband will love me."

**33** She conceived again and bore a son, and she said, "Because the LORD has heard that I am hated, He has given me this one also." She called his name Simeon.

**34** She conceived again and bore a son, and she said, "Now, this time, my husband will be joined to me, for I have borne him three sons." Therefore he called his name Levi.

**35** She conceived again and bore a son, and she said, "This time I will give thanks to the LORD." Therefore she called his name Judah. She stopped bearing.

## Notes

- **v1 "lifted his feet"** — *va-yissa Ya'aqov raglav*, an idiom found only here. Everywhere else in these chapters people lift their **eyes** — Lot in 13:10, Abraham in 22:4 and 22:13, Rebekah in 24:64. Jacob, leaving home with nothing after the night at Bethel, lifts his feet.
- **vv2–10 the well scene** — this is the third betrothal at a well in the book: Abraham's servant meets Rebekah at one (24), and Moses will meet Zipporah at one later. The shape repeats — a traveler, a well, a woman with a flock, water drawn, a run home with news, a welcome. Here the roles are reversed from chapter 24: there, the woman drew the water for the man and his camels; here, the man rolls the stone away and waters her flock.
- **v2, 3, 8, 10 "the stone… the stone… the stone"** — four times, and the shepherds explain that it takes all the flocks gathered to move it. Then Jacob moves it alone, on sight of Rachel, and the text does not comment. The man who has so far achieved everything by cunning does the one purely physical thing in his story the moment he sees her.
- **v11 "Jacob kissed Rachel, and he lifted his voice and wept"** — in that order. He weeps before he explains who he is.
- **v13 "he ran to meet him and embraced him and kissed him"** — Laban runs, exactly as he ran in 24:29 — where the narrator noted that he ran **after seeing the gold**. Here Jacob arrives with nothing, and the greeting is the same. The reader who remembers the earlier verse is meant to notice that this time there is no jewelry.
- **v14 "my bone and my flesh"** — *atsmi u-vesari*, the phrase Adam used of the woman in 2:23. Laban uses the language of the first marriage to open a labor negotiation, and the very next verse is about wages.
- **v17 "Leah's eyes were soft"** — *rakkot*. The word means tender, soft, delicate, weak. It is used of soft ground, of a tender child, of a faint heart. Whether it is a compliment or a defect is not decidable from the word, and the sentence sets it against Rachel's beauty with a plain *and*, which may be a contrast or may not. Translations print "weak," "dull," "lovely," "delicate" — all of them choosing. This is one of the places where a single adjective decides how a reader sees a woman for the rest of the book, and the Hebrew declines to help.
- **v20 "like a few days, in his loving her"** — *ke-yamim achadim*. And *yamim achadim* is exactly what his mother said in 27:44: *stay with him a few days*. She meant until Esau cooled. He serves fourteen years and stays twenty.
- **v21 "Give me my wife, for my days are filled"** — blunt, and the second clause is a contract term. He is calling in a debt.
- **v23 "And it came to pass in the evening"** — and the whole deception fits in one verse. The text does not explain the veil, the dark, the wine at the feast, or how it was possible. It simply reports the evening and then the morning.
- **v25 "And it came to pass in the morning — and here, it was Leah"** — *ve-hinneh hi Leah*. Four words in Hebrew. It is the finest sentence in the chapter and one of the great sentences in the book: the *hinneh*, **look**, that Hebrew uses to put the reader inside someone's eyes at the moment of seeing.

  And it is the exact reversal of chapter 27. There, a **younger** son put on the **elder** brother's clothing and stood before a father who **could not see**, and was asked *are you this, my son Esau?* — and said *I am*. Here the **elder** daughter is put in the **younger** sister's place in a dark tent, and the deceiver wakes to find he has been given the elder instead of the younger, exactly as his father was given the younger instead of the elder. He asks Laban *why have you deceived me* — *rimmitani*, from the root *ramah*, and in 27:35 Isaac told Esau *your brother came with **deceit***, *be-mirmah*, the same root. The word Jacob's fraud was named with is the word he now uses as an accusation.
- **v26 "to give the younger before the firstborn"** — *ha-tse'irah lifnei ha-bekhirah*. Laban's excuse names the two categories the previous two chapters were fought over: the younger and the firstborn. He says it as though it were simply how things are done, and it is the one sentence in the chapter that answers Genesis 25:23.
- **v31 "hated"** — *senu'ah*, the ordinary passive of *sane*, to hate. It is a hard word, and v30 has just said Jacob loved Rachel **more than** Leah — a comparative. Hebrew legal language uses loved/hated for the preferred and unpreferred wife, so the word may be technical rather than emotional. It may also be exactly what it says. The text uses it three times about her and never softens it.
- **vv32–35 the four names** — each is a sentence Leah says, and each is built on a verb:
  *Re'uven* — *ra'ah*, see: **the LORD has seen** my affliction, and *re'u ben*, see, a son.
  *Shim'on* — *shama*, hear: the LORD has **heard** that I am hated.
  *Levi* — *lavah*, to join: now my husband will be **joined** to me.
  *Yehudah* — *yadah*, to give thanks: this time I will **give thanks**.
  The first three are about her husband. Only the fourth is not — she stops explaining herself by what he might do, and the naming stops with it. That fourth name is the one the kingdom of Judah, and the word Jew, come from.
- **v34 "Therefore **he** called his name Levi"** — the verb shifts. She names the first two and the fourth; this one the text gives to him, in the only verse where she says he will be joined to her.
- **v27 "the wedding week"** — the Hebrew says only *malle shevua zot*, **fill out the week of this one**. There is no word for wedding in it; the seven-day feast is assumed, because everyone in the story knows what week is meant. What the verse does say plainly is that Jacob gets Rachel at the end of that week, not at the end of the second seven years — he is married to both sisters inside a fortnight and then works fourteen years, having already been paid.
