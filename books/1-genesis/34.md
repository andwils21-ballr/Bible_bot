# Genesis 34

**1** And Dinah, daughter of Leah, whom she had borne to Jacob, went out to see among the daughters of the land.

**2** And Shechem son of Hamor the Hivite, prince of the land, saw her. And he took her and lay her and violated her.

**3** And his soul clung to Dinah daughter of Jacob, and he loved the girl, and he spoke to the girl's heart.

**4** And Shechem said to Hamor his father, saying, "Take me this child for a wife."

**5** And Jacob heard that he had defiled Dinah his daughter — and his sons were with his livestock in the field, and Jacob kept silent until they came.

**6** And Hamor, Shechem's father, went out to Jacob to speak with him.

**7** And Jacob's sons came in from the field when they heard, and the men were cut to the quick, and it burned in them exceedingly — for he had done an outrage in Israel, to lie with Jacob's daughter, and so it is not done.

## The Offer

**8** And Hamor spoke with them, saying, "Shechem my son — his soul is bound to your daughter. Give her to him, please, for a wife.

**9** And make marriages with us: your daughters you shall give to us, and our daughters you shall take for yourselves.

**10** And with us you shall settle, and the land will be before you. Settle, and trade in it, and take holdings in it."

**11** And Shechem said to her father and to her brothers, "Let me find favor in your eyes, and whatever you say to me I will give.

**12** Set the bride-price and gift as high as you like, and I will pay whatever you ask — only give me the girl for a wife."

**13** And Jacob's sons answered Shechem and Hamor his father with deceit, and spoke — because he had defiled Dinah their sister.

**14** And they said to them, "We cannot do this thing, to give our sister to a man who has a foreskin, for that is a disgrace to us.

**15** Only on this will we consent to you: if you become like us, that every male of you be circumcised.

**16** Then we will give our daughters to you and take your daughters for ourselves, and we will settle with you, and we will become one people.

**17** And if you do not listen to us, to be circumcised — then we will take our daughter and go."

**18** And their words were good in the eyes of Hamor and in the eyes of Shechem son of Hamor.

**19** And the young man did not delay to do the thing, for he delighted in Jacob's daughter. And he was the most honored of all his father's house.

**20** And Hamor came, and Shechem his son, to the gate of their city, and they spoke to the men of their city, saying,

**21** "These men are at peace with us. Let them settle in the land and trade in it — and the land, look, is wide enough before them. Their daughters we will take for ourselves as wives, and our daughters we will give to them.

**22** Only on this will the men consent to us, to settle with us, to become one people: that every male among us be circumcised, as they are circumcised.

**23** Their livestock and their property and all their beasts — will they not be ours? Only let us consent to them, and they will settle with us."

**24** And all who went out of the gate of his city listened to Hamor and to Shechem his son, and every male was circumcised, all who went out of the gate of his city.

## The Third Day

**25** And it came to pass on the third day, while they were in pain, that two of Jacob's sons, Simeon and Levi, Dinah's brothers, each took his sword and came against the city unopposed, and killed every male.

**26** And Hamor and Shechem his son they killed by the mouth of the sword, and they took Dinah out of Shechem's house and went out.

**27** Jacob's sons came in over the slain and plundered the city, because they had defiled their sister.

**28** Their flocks and their herds and their donkeys, and what was in the city and what was in the field, they took.

**29** And all their wealth, and all their little ones and their wives, they carried off, and plundered everything that was in the house.

**30** And Jacob said to Simeon and to Levi, "You have muddied me, to make me stink among those who dwell in the land, among the Canaanite and the Perizzite — and I am men of a countable number. They will gather against me and strike me, and I will be wiped out, I and my house."

**31** And they said, "Should he treat our sister like a whore?"

## Notes

- **v1 "went out to see"** — *lir'ot*, to see. The next verse begins *va-yar otah*, **and he saw her**. She goes out to look at the daughters of the land, and the sentence turns her into what is looked at. Two verses, one verb, in both voices.

  And she is introduced as *Dinah, daughter of Leah, whom she bore to Jacob* — through her mother first. In v3 she becomes *Dinah daughter of Jacob*, and in v5 *Dinah his daughter*, and by v13 *Dinah their sister*. Whose she is keeps being restated, by a different relative each time, and the whole chapter will be fought over that question. She herself does not speak once, here or anywhere else in the Bible.
- **v2 "he took her and lay her and violated her"** — three verbs, and the middle one is not ordinary Hebrew. *Shakhav*, to lie down, normally takes a preposition: *shakhav **im**, shakhav **et*** — to lie *with* someone. Here it is *va-yishkav **otah***, with the direct-object marker, the construction you use for a thing you do *to* something: he lay her. The grammar is doing the violence before the third verb arrives.

  That third verb is *va-ye'anneha*, from *anah*: to afflict, to press down, to humble by force. It is the verb used of Egypt's treatment of Israel in slavery. English translations reach for "humbled," "dishonoured," "defiled," "violated," "raped," and the Hebrew supports the harder end: what is in the word is the forcing down of someone weaker.
- **v3 "his soul clung to Dinah"** — *va-tidbaq nafsho*, and *davaq* is the marriage verb of 2:24, *a man shall leave his father and his mother and **cling** to his wife*. The chapter puts that verb in the sentence immediately after the rape. Then *va-ye'ehav*, he loved her, and *va-yedabber al lev ha-na'ara*, he **spoke upon the girl's heart**, the idiom for tender or reassuring speech.

  The text does not reconcile these verses with verse 2 and does not tell the reader what to do with them. It reports both, in order, and gives Shechem more interior life in one verse than it gives Dinah in thirty-one.
- **v3, 12 "the girl"** — *ha-na'ara*, and in the Hebrew it is written **הַֽנַּעֲרָ**, without the final letter that marks it feminine — spelled, that is, like the masculine *na'ar*, a young man. The Torah does this almost every time the word appears, and the reason is not recorded anywhere in the text; the reading tradition says *na'ara* regardless. It is one more place where what is written and what is read have been kept side by side rather than harmonized.
- **v4 "this child"** — *ha-yaldah hazot*. Shechem, speaking to his father about the woman he wants to marry, does not use the word he used in v3. *Yaldah* is from *yalad*, to give birth: a girl-child. It is a colder and younger word than *na'ara*, and it is the one he uses when he is arranging a purchase.
- **v5 "Jacob kept silent"** — *ve-hecherish Ya'aqov*, from *charash*, to be deaf, to hold one's peace. Jacob hears what happened to his daughter and says nothing until his sons get home from work. It is the same man who, told of Joseph's dream in 37:11, *kept the matter*; and the same silence will be pointed at in 49:5–7, when he finally speaks about this day — on his deathbed, fifteen chapters later, to curse the anger of the two sons who did act.
- **v7 "cut to the quick"** — *va-yit'atsevu*, from *atsav*, the word for pain that is grief rather than injury. It is the word of 3:16 and 3:17, the pain the man and the woman are sentenced to, and of 6:6, where God's heart is **grieved** that he made humanity. The sons' reaction is given in that word and then in *va-yichar lahem me'od*, and it burned in them — the anger verb that has run through these chapters since 30:2.
- **v7 "an outrage in Israel... and so it is not done"** — *nevalah asah be-Yisra'el... ve-khen lo ye'aseh*. *Nevalah* is from *naval*, the word for a contemptible fool; the noun means an act so far outside the bounds that it disgraces everyone near it. And this is the first time in the Bible that **Israel** means a people rather than a man — Jacob got the name two chapters ago, and here his sons invoke it as a community with standards, in the middle of planning a massacre. The last clause, *so it is not done*, is impersonal: not *we do not do this* but *this is not done*, a rule with no one behind it.
- **v12 "bride-price and gift"** — *mohar u-mattan*. *Mohar* is the technical term for the payment a groom's family makes to the bride's; it occurs three times in the whole Hebrew Bible. Shechem offers to let them name any figure. The negotiation is between men about the price of a woman, and the text records it in the correct legal vocabulary without a word of comment.
- **v13 "with deceit"** — *be-mirmah*, and this is the family's word. In 27:35 Isaac told Esau *your brother came **with deceit*** and took your blessing, *be-mirmah*. In 29:25 Jacob shouted at Laban *why have you **deceived** me*, from the same root. The sons of Jacob now answer Shechem the way their father was answered and the way their father acted, and the narrator names it as they open their mouths, before the reader can wonder.
- **v14 "a disgrace to us"** — *cherpah*, the same word Rachel used at 30:23 when God took away her **disgrace** by giving her a son. And the thing the brothers name as the disgrace is not what was done to their sister. It is the foreskin.
- **v21 "These men are at peace with us"** — *shelemim hem ittanu*, and *shalem* is the word from 33:18, *Jacob came **whole** to the city of Shechem*. Hamor picks it up to reassure his townsmen. One chapter after the narrator used it as the verdict on Jacob's whole life, it is on a salesman's tongue at the city gate, and it is about to be false.
- **v23 "will they not be ours?"** — Hamor's public argument to the men of the city is not about a marriage. He tells them the newcomers' livestock and property will end up theirs. Both sides in this chapter are running the same play: an alliance offered in good-sounding words, with an acquisition underneath it. The difference is who gets to the third day first.
- **v25 "came against the city unopposed"** — *va-yavo'u al ha-ir betach*, and *betach* is genuinely two-sided. It means security, confidence, unsuspectingness — and the grammar leaves it hanging between the attackers and the town. Either the brothers came **boldly**, or they came upon a city that felt **safe**. Both readings are in the word, and nothing in the sentence decides it. The ambiguity is the most exact thing about the verse.
- **v25 "Simeon and Levi, Dinah's brothers"** — the text specifies. All eleven are her brothers; these two are Leah's sons, as she is. Of the four who could claim that, the two who act are the second and third. And they are the two Jacob will single out on his deathbed in 49:5–7 — *Simeon and Levi are brothers, weapons of violence are their trade... in their anger they killed a man* — which is the only answer he ever gives to the question at the end of this chapter, and it comes fifteen chapters late.
- **v27 "because they had defiled their sister"** — the verb is plural, *timme'u*. Verse 5 and verse 13 both said **he** defiled her, singular, meaning Shechem. Here it is *they*. Whether the brothers are charging the whole city or the narrator is reporting their charge is not marked, and it is the sentence that turns one man's crime into a warrant against every male in the town.
- **v29 "their little ones and their wives"** — *tappam*, the word for small children. The chapter that began with a woman taken ends with the women and children of a city carried off as goods, listed in the same sentence as the donkeys. No one comments.
- **v30 "You have muddied me"** — *akhartem oti*, from *akhar*, to stir up water until it is undrinkable, hence to bring ruin. And *le-hav'isheni*, to **make me stink**, from the root of rotting and stench. Jacob's whole complaint is about his position: he is *metei mispar*, **men of a number**, few enough to be counted, and they will gather and kill him. He does not mention Dinah. He does not say the killing was wrong. He says it was dangerous.
- **v31 "Should he treat our sister like a whore?"** — *ha-khe-zonah ya'aseh et achotenu*. The chapter ends here. Jacob does not reply — not in this verse, not in this chapter, not at any point before his deathbed. The brothers get the last word, in the form of a question, and the text lets it stand unanswered on the page, which is a decision the narrator made and not an accident.
