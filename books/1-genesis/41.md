# Genesis 41

**1** And it came to pass at the end of two full years that Pharaoh was dreaming, and here — he was standing by the Nile.

**2** And here, coming up out of the Nile, seven cows, beautiful of appearance and fat of flesh, and they grazed in the reeds.

**3** And here, seven other cows coming up after them out of the Nile, bad of appearance and thin of flesh, and they stood beside the cows on the bank of the Nile.

**4** And the cows bad of appearance and thin of flesh ate the seven cows beautiful of appearance and fat. And Pharaoh awoke.

**5** And he slept and dreamed a second time, and here — seven ears of grain coming up on one stalk, fat and good.

**6** And here, seven ears thin and scorched by the east wind, sprouting after them.

**7** And the thin ears swallowed the seven fat and full ears. And Pharaoh awoke, and here — it was a dream.

**8** And it came to pass in the morning that his spirit was beaten, and he sent and called all the magicians of Egypt and all her wise men. And Pharaoh recounted his dream to them, and there was no one to interpret them for Pharaoh.

## My Offenses I Mention Today

**9** And the chief of the cupbearers spoke to Pharaoh, saying, "My offenses I mention today.

**10** Pharaoh was furious at his servants, and he put me in custody in the house of the chief of the slaughterers, me and the chief of the bakers.

**11** And we dreamed, he and I, on the same night — and each dream had a meaning of its own.

**12** And there with us was a Hebrew lad, a slave of the chief of the slaughterers, and we told him, and he interpreted our dreams for us — he gave each of us the meaning of his own dream.

**13** And it came to pass, just as he interpreted for us, so it was: I was restored to my place, and he was hanged."

**14** And Pharaoh sent and called Joseph, and they rushed him out of the pit. And he shaved and changed his clothes, and came to Pharaoh.

**15** And Pharaoh said to Joseph, "I have dreamed a dream, and there is no one to interpret it. And I have heard it said of you that you hear a dream, to interpret it."

**16** And Joseph answered Pharaoh, saying, "Not I. God will answer Pharaoh's welfare."

**17** And Pharaoh spoke to Joseph: "In my dream — here I was, standing on the bank of the Nile.

**18** And here, coming up out of the Nile, seven cows fat of flesh and beautiful of form, and they grazed in the reeds.

**19** And here, seven other cows coming up after them, poor and very bad of form and lean of flesh — I have never seen their like in all the land of Egypt for badness.

**20** And the lean and bad cows ate the first seven fat cows.

**21** And they came into their innards, and it was not known that they had come into their innards, and their appearance was as bad as at the beginning. And I awoke.

**22** And I saw in my dream, and here — seven ears coming up on one stalk, full and good.

**23** And here, seven ears shriveled, thin, scorched by the east wind, sprouting after them.

**24** And the thin ears swallowed the seven good ears. And I said it to the magicians, and there was no one telling me."

## Pharaoh's Dream Is One

**25** And Joseph said to Pharaoh, "Pharaoh's dream is one. What God is doing, He has told Pharaoh.

**26** The seven good cows are seven years, and the seven good ears are seven years. It is one dream.

**27** And the seven lean and bad cows coming up after them are seven years, and the seven empty ears scorched by the east wind — they will be seven years of famine.

**28** That is what I told Pharaoh: what God is doing, He has shown Pharaoh.

**29** Here — seven years are coming, great plenty in all the land of Egypt.

**30** And seven years of famine will rise after them, and all the plenty in the land of Egypt will be forgotten, and the famine will finish off the land.

**31** And the plenty will not be known in the land, because of that famine afterward, for it will be very heavy.

**32** And as for the dream being repeated to Pharaoh twice — it is because the thing is fixed from God, and God is hurrying to do it.

**33** And now, let Pharaoh look out a man discerning and wise, and set him over the land of Egypt.

**34** Let Pharaoh act, and appoint overseers over the land, and take a fifth of the land of Egypt in the seven years of plenty.

**35** And let them gather all the food of these good years that are coming, and pile up grain under Pharaoh's hand — food in the cities — and keep it.

**36** And the food shall be a deposit for the land, for the seven years of famine that will be in the land of Egypt, and the land will not be cut off by the famine."

## Only the Throne

**37** And the thing was good in the eyes of Pharaoh and in the eyes of all his servants.

**38** And Pharaoh said to his servants, "Shall we find one like this, a man in whom is the spirit of God?"

**39** And Pharaoh said to Joseph, "Since God has made all this known to you, there is none discerning and wise like you.

**40** You shall be over my house, and by your mouth all my people shall order themselves. Only by the throne will I be greater than you."

**41** And Pharaoh said to Joseph, "See, I have set you over all the land of Egypt."

**42** And Pharaoh took off his signet ring from his hand and put it on Joseph's hand, and clothed him in garments of fine linen, and put the gold collar on his neck.

**43** And he had him ride in the second chariot that was his, and they called out before him, "Abrek!" And he set him over all the land of Egypt.

**44** And Pharaoh said to Joseph, "I am Pharaoh. And without you no man shall lift his hand or his foot in all the land of Egypt."

**45** And Pharaoh called Joseph's name Zaphenath-paneah, and gave him Asenath daughter of Poti-phera, priest of On, as a wife. And Joseph went out over the land of Egypt.

**46** And Joseph was a son of thirty years when he stood before Pharaoh king of Egypt. And Joseph went out from before Pharaoh and passed through all the land of Egypt.

**47** And in the seven years of plenty the land produced by handfuls.

**48** And he gathered all the food of the seven years that were in the land of Egypt, and put food in the cities — the food of the field around each city he put inside it.

**49** And Joseph piled up grain like the sand of the sea, very much, until he stopped counting, for there was no number.

**50** And to Joseph were born two sons before the year of famine came, whom Asenath daughter of Poti-phera, priest of On, bore to him.

**51** And Joseph called the name of the firstborn Manasseh — "for God has made me forget all my toil, and all my father's house."

**52** And the name of the second he called Ephraim — "for God has made me fruitful in the land of my affliction."

**53** And the seven years of plenty that were in the land of Egypt came to an end.

**54** And the seven years of famine began to come, just as Joseph had said. And there was famine in all the lands, but in all the land of Egypt there was bread.

**55** And all the land of Egypt was hungry, and the people cried out to Pharaoh for bread. And Pharaoh said to all Egypt, "Go to Joseph. What he says to you, do."

**56** And the famine was over all the face of the earth, and Joseph opened all that was in them and sold grain to Egypt. And the famine grew strong in the land of Egypt.

**57** And all the earth came to Egypt to buy grain, to Joseph, because the famine was strong in all the earth.

## Notes

- **v1 "at the end of two years of days"** — *mi-qets shnatayim yamim*, and the last sentence of the previous chapter was *he forgot him*. The text counts the forgetting in years. Nothing happens in them; they are named only to be measured.
- **v1 "the Nile"** — *ha-ye'or*, not the ordinary Hebrew word for a river. It is an Egyptian word taken into Hebrew, and it is used almost exclusively of the Nile. The narrator has moved into Egyptian vocabulary along with the story, and the same happens with *chartummim*, the magicians of v8.
- **v8 "his spirit was beaten"** — *va-tippa'em rucho*, from *pa'am*, to strike or beat, the root behind the word for a footstep and for a pulse. Something in him was knocking. Then comes *ve-ein poter otam*, **and there was no one to interpret them** — the identical complaint the two prisoners made in 40:8. Pharaoh has every magician in Egypt and is in exactly the position of two convicts in a cell.
- **v9 "My offenses I mention today"** — *et chata'ai ani mazkir*, and *mazkir* is from *zakhar*, the very verb 40:23 used twice to say he **did not remember** and forgot. Joseph had asked him to *mention me to Pharaoh* (40:14), same root. Two years later he finally mentions — and the first thing he mentions is his own guilt, to save himself in front of a king who has just executed a man over bread.
- **v12 "a Hebrew lad, a slave"** — three words, three demotions: *na'ar*, young; *Ivri*, foreign; *eved*, owned. He does not give Joseph's name. He remembers the interpretation exactly and the interpreter not at all.
- **v14 "they rushed him out of the pit"** — *min ha-bor*, the same word Joseph used in 40:15 and the same word for the cistern at Dothan in 37:24. And then *va-yegallach*, **he shaved**, and changed his clothes. Egyptians were clean-shaven and Semites were not. Before he may stand in front of the king, the Hebrew is made to look Egyptian — which is why nobody recognizes him in chapter 42.
- **v16 "Not I"** — *bil'adai*, one word, and it is the first thing Joseph says to the most powerful man on earth. He has been in a hole for years and he opens by disclaiming the only skill that can get him out. Pharaoh had said *you hear a dream, to interpret it*; Joseph gives the credit away before he has been offered anything.
- **vv17–24 Pharaoh changes the dream in the retelling** — set his account beside the narrator's in vv2–7 and he has added things nobody told him. Verse 19: *I have never seen their like in all the land of Egypt for badness*. Verse 21: *they came into their innards, and it was not known that they had come into their innards, and their appearance was as bad as at the beginning*. Neither sentence is in the dream as reported. The horror of the thing is the king's own, and it is the part he cannot stop saying.
- **v25 "Pharaoh's dream is one"** — *chalom Par'oh echad hu*, and it is Joseph's first move: two dreams, one meaning. Every magician in Egypt had two problems. He tells the king he has one.
- **v32 "the dream being repeated twice"** — Joseph interprets not only what the dream shows but the fact that there were two of them. The doubling is itself information: *the thing is fixed from God, and God is hurrying to do it*. It is the most technically impressive sentence he says, and it is what turns a reading into advice.
- **vv33–36 nobody asked him for a plan** — Pharaoh asked for an interpretation. Joseph gives it in eight verses and then keeps going: appoint overseers, take a fifth, store it in the cities under central control, hold it as a *piqqadon*, a **deposit** or trust, against the seven years. He is a foreign convict, mid-audience, telling the king how to run the country.
- **v38 "a man in whom is the spirit of God"** — *ish asher ruach Elohim bo*, said by Pharaoh, about a Hebrew slave he met minutes ago. The phrase is God-language in the mouth of a man who is himself worshipped, and the narrator lets it stand without any comment about whose God is meant.
- **v40 "by your mouth all my people shall order themselves"** — *ve-al pikha yishaq kol ammi*. The verb is the problem: *yishaq* is the ordinary word for **kiss**, which gives *all my people shall kiss upon your mouth*, and something in the sense of being ordered or arrayed is the other possibility. Neither reading is secure. What is secure is the next clause — *only by the throne will I be greater than you* — which fixes how much authority is being handed over even if the idiom is lost.
- **vv42, 45 made Egyptian** — a signet ring, linen, a gold collar, a chariot, an Egyptian name (*Zaphenath-paneah*, whose meaning Hebrew cannot recover), and an Egyptian wife who is a priest's daughter. In v43 the crowd shouts *avrekh*, a word that appears nowhere else in the Bible and has never been explained. Joseph disappears into Egypt so completely that the chapter can no longer say what his own title means.
- **v46 "a son of thirty years"** — he was seventeen in 37:2. Thirteen years between the pit and the chariot, and the text gives them in two numbers and no description.
- **v49 "like the sand of the sea... there was no number"** — the phrase God used for the promise to Abraham in 22:17 and Jacob quoted back in 32:12, *your seed like the sand of the sea which cannot be counted*. Here it is grain. The covenant's own words turn up in a warehouse inventory, and the family it was made to is about to be kept alive by what is in there.
- **vv51–52 the names of his sons** — *Menasheh* from *nashah*, to forget: *God has made me forget all my toil, and all my father's house*. He puts his father's house in the same clause as his suffering and names his firstborn after being rid of both. *Efrayim* from *parah*, to be fruitful: *God has made me fruitful in the land of my **affliction***. He is vizier of Egypt with a wife and two sons, and he still calls it that.
