# Genesis 39

**1** And Joseph was brought down to Egypt, and Potiphar, an officer of Pharaoh, chief of the slaughterers, an Egyptian man, bought him from the hand of the Ishmaelites who had brought him down there.

**2** And the LORD was with Joseph, and he was a man who prospered, and he was in the house of his Egyptian master.

**3** And his master saw that the LORD was with him, and that all he did the LORD made prosper in his hand.

**4** And Joseph found favor in his eyes and served him, and he appointed him over his house, and all he had he gave into his hand.

**5** And it came to pass, from the time he appointed him over his house and over all he had, that the LORD blessed the Egyptian's house on account of Joseph. And the blessing of the LORD was on all he had, in the house and in the field.

**6** And he left all he had in Joseph's hand, and he knew nothing of what was with him except the bread he ate. And Joseph was beautiful of form and beautiful of appearance.

## Lie With Me

**7** And it came to pass after these things that his master's wife lifted her eyes to Joseph, and she said, "Lie with me."

**8** And he refused, and said to his master's wife, "Look — my master does not know what is with me in the house, and all he has he has given into my hand.

**9** He is no greater in this house than I, and he has withheld nothing from me except you, since you are his wife. And how could I do this great evil, and sin against God?"

**10** And it came to pass, as she spoke to Joseph day by day, that he did not listen to her, to lie beside her, to be with her.

**11** And it came to pass on a day like this that he came into the house to do his work, and none of the men of the house was there in the house.

**12** And she caught him by his garment, saying, "Lie with me." And he left his garment in her hand and fled, and went outside.

**13** And it came to pass, when she saw that he had left his garment in her hand and fled outside,

**14** that she called to the men of her house and said to them, saying, "See — he has brought us a Hebrew man to make sport of us. He came to me, to lie with me, and I called out in a loud voice.

**15** And it came to pass, when he heard that I raised my voice and called, that he left his garment beside me and fled, and went outside."

**16** And she laid his garment beside her until his master came to his house.

**17** And she spoke to him according to these words, saying, "The Hebrew slave whom you brought to us came to me, to make sport of me.

**18** And it came to pass, as I raised my voice and called, that he left his garment beside me and fled outside."

**19** And it came to pass, when his master heard the words of his wife which she spoke to him, saying, "According to these words your slave did to me," that his anger burned.

**20** And Joseph's master took him and put him into the round house, the place where the king's prisoners were bound. And he was there in the round house.

**21** And the LORD was with Joseph, and extended kindness to him, and gave him favor in the eyes of the keeper of the round house.

**22** And the keeper of the round house gave into Joseph's hand all the prisoners who were in the round house, and everything that was done there, he was the one doing it.

**23** The keeper of the round house did not look at anything in his hand, because the LORD was with him, and what he did the LORD made prosper.

## Notes

- **v1 "was brought down"** — *hurad*, a passive: Joseph does not go, he **is taken down**. It is the third descent in three chapters. Joseph is brought down to Egypt (37:36), Judah goes down from his brothers (38:1), and now the word comes back twice in one verse, *hurad* and *horiduhu*. Everything in this part of the book moves downward before it moves anywhere else.
- **vv2, 3, 5, 21, 23 "the LORD"** — the divine name YHWH appears eight times in this chapter, and outside it, in the whole fourteen-chapter Joseph story, it appears four more times in total. Everywhere else God is *Elohim* — in Joseph's own mouth, in Pharaoh's, in the dream interpretations. This chapter is the exception, and it says the same sentence at both ends: **the LORD was with Joseph** in the house he runs (v2) and in the prison he is thrown into (v21). The frame is the point.
- **v6 "beautiful of form and beautiful of appearance"** — *yefeh to'ar vi-yfeh mar'eh*, and the phrase is his mother's. 29:17 describes Rachel with the identical words in the feminine: *yefat to'ar vi-yfat mar'eh*. It is used of no one else in Genesis. Joseph is the only man in the book whose looks are reported, and the sentence used to report them was written for the woman Jacob worked fourteen years to marry.
- **v6 "except the bread he ate"** — *lo yada itto me'umah ki im ha-lechem asher hu okhel*. It may be plain: Potiphar handed over everything but his own meals. It may be a discreet way of saying what a master keeps to himself. Nothing in the sentence settles it, and 43:32 will say that Egyptians could not eat bread with Hebrews, which makes the literal reading do real work.
- **v7 "Lie with me"** — *shikhvah immi*. Two words in Hebrew, and the verb is *shakhav*, to lie down — the one the book keeps for acts that sit outside the bond they belong in, against *yada*, to know, which it keeps for the ones inside it (see 4:1). She says the same two words again in v12 while holding his clothes.
- **v8 "And he refused"** — *va-yema'en*, a rare verb, and the whole chapter turns on it. It is the same word used of Jacob in 37:35, who *refused to be comforted* over this boy's supposed death.
- **v9 "and sin against God"** — Joseph's argument runs through Potiphar's trust for two verses and then lands somewhere else entirely. He does not say *against my master*; he says *ve-chatati le-Elohim*, and sin against God. It is the first time anyone in Genesis names an act as a sin against God before committing it rather than after.
- **v12 "he left his garment in her hand"** — the second time a garment of Joseph's is taken off him and used to tell a lie about him. In 37:23 his brothers stripped his long coat and dipped it in goat's blood so their father would draw the wrong conclusion; here his master's wife keeps a garment in her hand and does the same thing to her husband. Both times the cloth is produced as evidence, and both times what it proves did not happen.
- **vv14, 17 "to make sport of"** — *le-tsacheq*, from *tsachaq*, to laugh — the root of **Isaac's** name. The verb turns up in exactly this suggestive register in 26:8, where Abimelech looks out a window and sees *Isaac **playing** with Rebekah his wife* and knows at once she is not his sister. It is the same form Sarah saw Ishmael doing in 21:9. She chooses a word that can mean anything from mocking to what the window showed Abimelech.
- **vv14, 17 who she blames, and to whom** — to the household she says *he has brought **us** a Hebrew man to make sport of **us***, which makes the servants fellow-victims and leaves the *he* unnamed. To her husband the same story becomes *the Hebrew **slave** whom **you** brought to us... to make sport of **me***. Between the two tellings the man is demoted from *ish* to *eved*, the household drops out, and the blame lands on Potiphar.
- **v19 "his anger burned"** — *va-yichar appo*, and the Hebrew does not say at whom. It says his anger burned, and the next thing he does is put Joseph in the prison that holds the king's own prisoners rather than execute a slave who has been accused of assaulting his wife. The text reports both facts and draws no conclusion from them.
- **v20 "the round house"** — *beit ha-sohar*, and *sohar* occurs nowhere in the Bible outside this story. Its meaning is not recoverable from Hebrew; the word may not be Hebrew at all. Round is one old guess and a fortress or tower is another, and nothing in the language decides it. This same verse also carries a written-and-read pair the scribes preserved rather than tidy: the consonants **אסורי** stand on the page while the reading is *asirei*, prisoners.
- **vv22–23 it happens twice** — read verses 4–6 and verses 21–23 side by side and they are the same paragraph. Master sees the LORD is with him; Joseph finds favor; everything is given **into his hand**; the man over him stops looking at the accounts. Potiphar's house, then the king's prison. The story of Joseph rising is told once, then told again after he has lost everything, in the same words — and the second time he is a convict.
