# Genesis 36

**1** And these are the generations of Esau — he is Edom.

**2** Esau took his wives from the daughters of Canaan: Adah daughter of Elon the Hittite, and Oholibamah daughter of Anah daughter of Zibeon the Hivite,

**3** and Basemath daughter of Ishmael, sister of Nebaioth.

**4** And Adah bore Eliphaz to Esau, and Basemath bore Reuel,

**5** and Oholibamah bore Jeush and Jalam and Korah. These are the sons of Esau who were born to him in the land of Canaan.

**6** And Esau took his wives and his sons and his daughters and all the souls of his house, and his livestock and all his beasts and all his property that he had gotten in the land of Canaan, and went to a land away from the face of Jacob his brother.

**7** For their goods were too many for them to settle together, and the land where they were living as foreigners could not carry them, because of their livestock.

**8** And Esau settled in the hill country of Seir. Esau — he is Edom.

## The Chiefs

**9** And these are the generations of Esau, father of Edom, in the hill country of Seir.

**10** These are the names of Esau's sons: Eliphaz son of Adah, Esau's wife; Reuel son of Basemath, Esau's wife.

**11** And the sons of Eliphaz were Teman, Omar, Zepho, and Gatam, and Kenaz.

**12** And Timna was a concubine to Eliphaz son of Esau, and she bore Amalek to Eliphaz. These are the sons of Adah, Esau's wife.

**13** And these are the sons of Reuel: Nahath and Zerah, Shammah and Mizzah. These were the sons of Basemath, Esau's wife.

**14** And these were the sons of Oholibamah daughter of Anah daughter of Zibeon, Esau's wife: she bore to Esau Jeush and Jalam and Korah.

**15** These are the chiefs of the sons of Esau. The sons of Eliphaz, Esau's firstborn: chief Teman, chief Omar, chief Zepho, chief Kenaz,

**16** chief Korah, chief Gatam, chief Amalek. These are the chiefs of Eliphaz in the land of Edom. These are the sons of Adah.

**17** And these are the sons of Reuel son of Esau: chief Nahath, chief Zerah, chief Shammah, chief Mizzah. These are the chiefs of Reuel in the land of Edom. These are the sons of Basemath, Esau's wife.

**18** And these are the sons of Oholibamah, Esau's wife: chief Jeush, chief Jalam, chief Korah. These are the chiefs of Oholibamah daughter of Anah, Esau's wife.

**19** These are the sons of Esau, and these are their chiefs. He is Edom.

## The Sons of Seir

**20** These are the sons of Seir the Horite, the inhabitants of the land: Lotan and Shobal and Zibeon and Anah,

**21** and Dishon and Ezer and Dishan. These are the chiefs of the Horite, the sons of Seir in the land of Edom.

**22** And the sons of Lotan were Hori and Hemam, and Lotan's sister was Timna.

**23** And these are the sons of Shobal: Alvan and Manahath and Ebal, Shepho and Onam.

**24** And these are the sons of Zibeon: both Aiah and Anah. That is the Anah who found the yemim in the wilderness while he was pasturing the donkeys of Zibeon his father.

**25** And these are the children of Anah: Dishon, and Oholibamah daughter of Anah.

**26** And these are the sons of Dishan: Hemdan and Eshban and Ithran and Cheran.

**27** These are the sons of Ezer: Bilhan and Zaavan and Akan.

**28** These are the sons of Dishan: Uz and Aran.

**29** These are the chiefs of the Horite: chief Lotan, chief Shobal, chief Zibeon, chief Anah,

**30** chief Dishon, chief Ezer, chief Dishan. These are the chiefs of the Horite, by their chieftainships, in the land of Seir.

## The Kings

**31** And these are the kings who reigned in the land of Edom before any king reigned over the sons of Israel.

**32** And Bela son of Beor reigned in Edom, and the name of his city was Dinhabah.

**33** And Bela died, and Jobab son of Zerah of Bozrah reigned in his place.

**34** And Jobab died, and Husham of the land of the Temanite reigned in his place.

**35** And Husham died, and Hadad son of Bedad, who struck Midian in the field of Moab, reigned in his place, and the name of his city was Avith.

**36** And Hadad died, and Samlah of Masrekah reigned in his place.

**37** And Samlah died, and Shaul of Rehoboth-on-the-River reigned in his place.

**38** And Shaul died, and Baal-hanan son of Achbor reigned in his place.

**39** And Baal-hanan son of Achbor died, and Hadar reigned in his place, and the name of his city was Pau, and his wife's name was Mehetabel daughter of Matred daughter of Me-zahab.

**40** And these are the names of the chiefs of Esau, by their clans, by their places, by their names: chief Timna, chief Alvah, chief Jetheth,

**41** chief Oholibamah, chief Elah, chief Pinon,

**42** chief Kenaz, chief Teman, chief Mibzar,

**43** chief Magdiel, chief Iram. These are the chiefs of Edom, by their settlements in the land of their holding. He is Esau, father of Edom.

## Notes

- **vv1, 8, 19, 43 "he is Edom"** — the chapter says it four times, at the top, at the end of the first section, at the end of the second, and in the last words of the last verse. *Hu Edom. Esav hu Edom. Hu Edom. Hu Esav avi Edom.* The book has been building to this since 25:30, where Esau asked for *ha-adom ha-adom ha-zeh*, **that red red stuff**, and the narrator added *therefore his name was called Edom*. A whole nation is named after a bowl of lentils and one man's hunger, and this chapter is the receipt.
- **v2–3 the wives do not match** — Esau's wives were named twice before this, and the names are different. In 26:34 he married **Judith** daughter of Beeri the Hittite, and **Basemath daughter of Elon the Hittite**; in 28:9 he added **Mahalath** daughter of Ishmael, sister of Nebaioth. Here in v2–3 they are **Adah daughter of Elon the Hittite**, Oholibamah daughter of Anah, and **Basemath daughter of Ishmael, sister of Nebaioth**.

  The pieces are the same pieces, reattached differently: Elon's daughter is Basemath in one list and Adah in the other; Ishmael's daughter, identified both times as Nebaioth's sister, is Mahalath in one and Basemath in the other. It is not a vague discrepancy — it is specific and checkable, and the text preserves both lists without adjusting either. This rendering does the same. What caused it is not recorded, and guessing would be inventing.
- **vv6–7 the second peaceful separation** — *for their goods were too many for them to settle together, and the land where they were living as foreigners could not carry them*. This is chapter 13 word for word. When Abram and Lot separated, 13:6 read *the land could not **carry** them to settle **together**, for their goods were **many*** — the same verb *nasa*, the same *lashevet yachdav*, the same *rekhusham rav*. The book has one formula for brothers who part without bloodshed, and it uses it exactly twice: for Abram and Lot, and here.

  And note who moves. Esau — cheated of the birthright, cheated of the blessing — is the one who packs up his household and goes, *away from the face of Jacob his brother*. He does it in one sentence, and the narrator supplies an economic reason and no grievance at all.
- **v12 "Timna was a concubine"** — she is named again in v22 as **Lotan's sister**, and Lotan is the firstborn of Seir the Horite, a chief. So a chief's sister is a concubine in Esau's grandson's house. The chapter states both facts, ten verses apart, and does not connect them.

  And what she bore was **Amalek** — the nation that attacks Israel in the wilderness in Exodus 17 and that Israel is commanded in Deuteronomy 25 never to forget. The Bible's most implacable enemy is introduced here, in a subordinate clause, in a list, as the son of a concubine. He is Esau's great-grandson, which makes the feud between Jacob's line and Amalek the same feud, four generations on.
- **v15 "chiefs"** — *alluf*, and the word is almost this chapter's private property. It occurs **42 times in Genesis, and all 42 are in chapter 36**; the only comparable block anywhere else is in 1 Chronicles, where the Chronicler copies this same list. It is built on the consonants of *eleph*, a thousand, and behind that a clan-unit — a chief is the head of a thousand. The English "duke" of the older Bibles came in by way of the Latin *dux*, and it is not wrong so much as too aristocratic: this is a tribal head, not a nobleman.
- **v20 "the sons of Seir the Horite, the inhabitants of the land"** — the chapter stops listing Esau's descendants and lists the people who were there first. *Yoshvei ha-arets*, the ones sitting in the land. Their chiefs get the same word, *alluf*, as Esau's.

  And the two lists are joined: Oholibamah, Esau's wife (v2), is *daughter of Anah daughter of Zibeon* — and Anah and Zibeon are Horites, sons of Seir (v20, v24–25). Esau's line married into the people it displaced. The genealogy makes Edom a mixture and shows the seam rather than hiding it, which is the opposite of what a national origin list usually does.
- **v24 "found the yemim in the wilderness"** — *ha-yemim* occurs in the Bible only here, and what it names cannot be recovered. Nothing in the surrounding language constrains it. The old translations went in different directions — hot springs, mules, water — and every one of them is a guess dressed as a reading. What the verse establishes is that Anah was out pasturing his father's donkeys, that he found something, and that whoever preserved this list thought it was the one thing about him worth recording.
- **vv31–39 eight kings and not one son** — *before any king reigned over the sons of Israel*. Read the list: Bela, then Jobab, then Husham, then Hadad, then Samlah, then Shaul, then Baal-hanan, then Hadar — and not once does the formula say *his son*. Each is *ben* somebody else, and most are tagged with a different home town: Bozrah, the land of the Temanite, Avith, Masrekah, Rehoboth-on-the-River, Pau. This is not a dynasty. It is eight kingships in a row that did not pass from father to son, recorded by people who counted that as the succession.

  And the verse that introduces them is looking backward from somewhere: *before any king reigned over the sons of Israel* is a sentence written by someone for whom Israel having kings is the known fact against which Edom's kings are dated.
- **v39 "Mehetabel daughter of Matred daughter of Me-zahab"** — the last king in the list is also the only one whose death is not recorded, and he is the only one whose wife is named — named, and then traced back two generations through women, which the chapter does for no one else. *Mehetav'el* is one of those sentence-names, **God does good**. And the grandmother's name, *Me-zahav*, is two ordinary words: **waters of gold**.
- **vv5, 14 "Jeush"** — the name is written **יעיש**, *Ye'ish*, and read *Ye'ush*, both times it appears. Another of the places, like Gad at 30:11, where the consonants on the page and the word said aloud were preserved side by side rather than reconciled.
- **On reading this chapter at all** — it is a list, and it is meant to be. It has no story, no speech, and no comment; the only narrative sentence in forty-three verses is the one about Anah and the donkeys. What it is doing is giving Esau a full and honourable accounting — wives, sons, chiefs, the people he married into, eight kings with their cities — immediately before the book turns to Joseph and never comes back to him. Ishmael got the same treatment in 25:12–18 and was then dropped. Genesis closes out the brother it is not going to follow, at length, and with respect, and then lets him go.
