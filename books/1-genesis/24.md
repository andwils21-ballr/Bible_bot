# Genesis 24

**1** And Abraham was old, coming on in days, and the LORD had blessed Abraham in everything.

**2** And Abraham said to his servant, the elder of his house, the one ruling over all that was his, "Put, please, your hand under my loin.

**3** And I will put you under oath by the LORD, God of the heavens and God of the earth, that you will not take a wife for my son from the daughters of the Canaanite, among whom I am dwelling.

**4** But to my land and to my kindred you shall go, and take a wife for my son, for Isaac."

**5** And the servant said to him, "Perhaps the woman will not be willing to follow me to this land. Shall I really take your son back to the land you came out from?"

**6** And Abraham said to him, "Make sure you do not take my son back there.

**7** The LORD, God of the heavens, who took me from my father's house and from the land of my kindred, and who spoke to me and who swore to me, saying, 'To your seed I will give this land' — He will send His messenger before you, and you shall take a wife for my son from there.

**8** And if the woman is not willing to go after you, then you are clear from this oath of mine. Only do not bring my son back there."

**9** And the servant put his hand under the loin of Abraham his master, and swore to him concerning this matter.

## The Sign

**10** And the servant took ten camels from his master's camels and went, and all his master's good things in his hand. And he rose and went to Aram-naharaim, to the city of Nahor.

**11** And he made the camels kneel outside the city, by the well of water, at evening time, at the time the water-drawers go out.

**12** And he said, "LORD, God of my master Abraham — let it happen, please, before me today, and do kindness with my master Abraham.

**13** Here I am, standing by the spring of water, and the daughters of the men of the city are coming out to draw water.

**14** Let it be that the girl to whom I say, 'Tip, please, your jar, and let me drink,' and she says, 'Drink — and your camels also I will water' — her You have appointed for Your servant, for Isaac. And by her I shall know that You have done kindness with my master."

**15** And it came to pass, before he had finished speaking, that here — Rebekah was coming out, who was born to Bethuel son of Milcah, wife of Nahor, Abraham's brother — and her jar on her shoulder.

**16** And the girl was very good to look at, a virgin, and no man had known her. And she went down to the spring and filled her jar and came up.

**17** And the servant ran to meet her and said, "Let me sip, please, a little water from your jar."

**18** And she said, "Drink, my lord." And she hurried and lowered her jar onto her hand and gave him drink.

**19** And she finished giving him drink, and she said, "For your camels also I will draw, until they have finished drinking."

**20** And she hurried and emptied her jar into the trough, and ran again to the well to draw, and drew for all his camels.

**21** And the man was gazing at her, silent, to know whether the LORD had given success to his journey or not.

**22** And it came to pass, when the camels had finished drinking, that the man took a gold nose-ring, a beka in weight, and two bracelets for her hands, ten of gold in weight,

**23** and said, "Whose daughter are you? Tell me, please. Is there room in your father's house for us to spend the night?"

**24** And she said to him, "I am the daughter of Bethuel, son of Milcah, whom she bore to Nahor."

**25** And she said to him, "There is both straw and much fodder with us, and also room to spend the night."

**26** And the man bowed low and bowed down to the LORD.

**27** And he said, "Blessed be the LORD, God of my master Abraham, who has not forsaken His kindness and His truth from my master. I — on the way the LORD has led me — to the house of my master's brothers."

## Laban

**28** And the girl ran and told her mother's household these things.

**29** And Rebekah had a brother, and his name was Laban. And Laban ran out to the man, to the spring.

**30** And it came to pass, when he saw the ring and the bracelets on his sister's hands, and when he heard the words of Rebekah his sister, saying, "Thus the man spoke to me" — that he came to the man, and here he was, standing by the camels at the spring.

**31** And he said, "Come, blessed of the LORD. Why do you stand outside? And I have cleared the house, and a place for the camels."

**32** And the man came to the house, and he unharnessed the camels, and gave straw and fodder to the camels, and water to wash his feet and the feet of the men who were with him.

**33** And food was set before him to eat. And the servant said, "I will not eat until I have spoken my words." And his host said, "Speak."

**34** And he said, "I am Abraham's servant.

**35** And the LORD has blessed my master greatly, and he has become great. And He has given him flocks and cattle, and silver and gold, and male servants and female servants, and camels and donkeys.

**36** And Sarah, my master's wife, bore a son to my master after her old age, and he has given him all that is his.

**37** And my master made me swear, saying, 'You shall not take a wife for my son from the daughters of the Canaanite, in whose land I am dwelling.

**38** But to my father's house you shall go, and to my family, and take a wife for my son.'

**39** And I said to my master, 'Perhaps the woman will not go after me.'

**40** And he said to me, 'The LORD, before whom I have walked about, will send His messenger with you and give success to your journey. And you shall take a wife for my son from my family and from my father's house.

**41** Then you will be clear from my oath, when you come to my family. And if they do not give her to you, then you are clear from my oath.'

**42** And I came today to the spring, and I said, 'LORD, God of my master Abraham — if now You are giving success to the journey I am on:

**43** here I am, standing by the spring of water. Let it be that the young woman who comes out to draw, and I say to her, Give me a little water to drink, please, from your jar —

**44** and she says to me, You drink, and for your camels also I will draw — she is the woman the LORD has appointed for my master's son.'

**45** I had not yet finished speaking to my heart, and here — Rebekah was coming out, with her jar on her shoulder. And she went down to the spring and drew, and I said to her, 'Give me drink, please.'

**46** And she hurried and lowered her jar from upon her, and said, 'Drink — and your camels also I will water.' And I drank, and she watered the camels also.

**47** And I asked her and said, 'Whose daughter are you?' And she said, 'The daughter of Bethuel son of Nahor, whom Milcah bore to him.' And I put the ring in her nose and the bracelets on her hands.

**48** And I bowed low and bowed down to the LORD, and I blessed the LORD, God of my master Abraham, who led me in a true way to take the daughter of my master's brother for his son.

**49** And now, if you are doing kindness and truth with my master, tell me. And if not, tell me, and I will turn to the right or to the left."

**50** And Laban and Bethuel answered and said, "The matter has come out from the LORD. We cannot speak to you evil or good.

**51** Here is Rebekah before you. Take her and go, and let her be a wife for your master's son, as the LORD has spoken."

**52** And it came to pass, when Abraham's servant heard their words, that he bowed to the ground to the LORD.

**53** And the servant brought out objects of silver and objects of gold, and garments, and gave them to Rebekah. And he gave precious things to her brother and to her mother.

**54** And they ate and drank, he and the men who were with him, and they spent the night. And they rose in the morning, and he said, "Send me off to my master."

**55** And her brother and her mother said, "Let the girl stay with us days, or ten. After that she may go."

**56** And he said to them, "Do not delay me, and the LORD has given success to my journey. Send me off, and let me go to my master."

**57** And they said, "Let us call the girl and ask her mouth."

**58** And they called Rebekah and said to her, "Will you go with this man?" And she said, "I will go."

**59** And they sent off Rebekah their sister, and her nurse, and Abraham's servant, and his men.

**60** And they blessed Rebekah and said to her,  
"Our sister, may you become thousands of ten thousands,  
and may your seed seize the gate of those who hate them."

**61** And Rebekah rose, and her young women, and they rode on the camels and went after the man. And the servant took Rebekah and went.

## The Field at Evening

**62** And Isaac had come back from the way to Beer-lahai-roi, and he was living in the land of the Negev.

**63** And Isaac went out to walk in the field toward evening. And he lifted his eyes and saw — and here, camels coming.

**64** And Rebekah lifted her eyes and saw Isaac, and she fell from upon the camel.

**65** And she said to the servant, "Who is that man, walking in the field to meet us?" And the servant said, "He is my master." And she took the veil and covered herself.

**66** And the servant recounted to Isaac everything he had done.

**67** And Isaac brought her into the tent of Sarah his mother, and he took Rebekah, and she became his wife, and he loved her. And Isaac was comforted after his mother.

## Notes

- **The servant has no name.** Sixty-seven verses, the longest chapter in Genesis, and the man at the center of it is called *ha-eved*, the servant, throughout. He is the most reliable character in the book so far and the text never says who he is.
- **v22 "a gold nose-ring"** — *nezem*, a ring worn through the nose or the ear; the Hebrew does not say which until v47, where the servant reports putting it **in her nose**. Read straight through, the chapter hands out the ornament first and only later says where it went. The bracelets are for her hands, the ring is not.
- **v33 "and his host said"** — the Hebrew says only *and he said*, twice in a row, with no names: the first speaker is the servant (v34 has him introduce himself), the second is whoever is hosting him — Laban, by v31, though the verse does not say so. The Greek has **they** said, making it the household answering rather than one man.
- **v2 "under my loin"** — *tachat yerekhi*. The *yarekh* runs from the upper thigh to the loin, and Hebrew uses it for the place offspring come from: 46:26 counts Jacob's household as those who *came out of his yerekh*. So an oath about a man's descendants is sworn with a hand laid on the part of the body they come from, and the next verse names it an oath. Most English Bibles print *thigh*, which keeps the word and hides the reason; the same Hebrew noun means the plain hip joint at 32:25, where it is a wrestling injury and nothing else. The gesture belongs to a world that is gone, and no English word will make it feel ordinary — it occurs twice in the Bible, here and at 47:29, both times a dying man binding someone about where the family belongs.
- **v7 "He will send His messenger before you"** — Abraham says this. The messenger is never mentioned again, never appears, and is never credited. Whatever happens at the well happens through a young woman's ordinary decency.
- **v12 "let it happen, please, before me"** — *haqreh na*, from *qarah*, to happen, to befall, to chance upon. He is asking God to arrange a coincidence, and the word he uses is the word for accident.
- **vv12, 14, 27, 49 "kindness"** — *chesed*, four times. There is no good English word: it is loyalty inside a relationship, the thing you do because of who you are to someone, not because you are obliged. Not mercy, not love, not kindness quite. In v27 and v49 it is paired with *emet*, truth or faithfulness, and the pair means something like *steady, reliable good faith*.
- **v14 "your camels also I will water"** — the servant's test. It is worth understanding what he is asking for. Ten camels, at the end of a long desert journey, drink an enormous quantity of water, and it comes up a jar at a time, by hand, from a well a woman has to walk down into and back up out of. The sign he chooses is not a word or a gesture. It is hours of heavy labor, offered freely by someone who has no reason to.
- **vv18–20 "she hurried… she hurried… ran"** — three verbs of speed in three verses, matching Abraham's four in 18:6–7 when he fed the three men. Hospitality in this book is measured in how fast someone moves.
- **v21 "gazing at her, silent"** — *mishta'eh… macharish*. The first word is rare and means staring in astonishment; the second is the ordinary word for keeping quiet. He watches her do it and says nothing until the last camel finishes.
- **v22 "a gold ring… two bracelets"** — he gives the jewelry **before** he asks who she is. He has decided.
- **vv30, 31 "when he saw the ring and the bracelets… Come, blessed of the LORD"** — the narrator puts Laban's running and Laban's piety in that order, after the gold. Nothing is said about it. This is the reader's first look at a man Jacob will spend twenty years working for.
- **v33 "And food was set before him"** — the Masoretes record two forms here: the text as written, and the text as it is to be read. The written consonants and the pronounced word differ, and the scribes preserved both rather than choosing. It is a small thing about eating, and they kept the discrepancy anyway.
- **vv34–49** — the servant retells the whole story, at length, to the family. Hebrew narrative rarely repeats itself like this, and the repetition is not identical: in v45 he says *I had not yet finished speaking **to my heart***, which tells the reader that his prayer in v12 was silent. He also drops the detail that he gave the jewelry before asking her name, and reverses the order so that it comes after.
- **vv50–51 "And Laban and Bethuel answered"** — the brother is named before the father, and the father says nothing afterward. In v55 it is again *her brother and her mother* who negotiate. Whatever the household arrangement is, the text shows it rather than explaining it.
- **vv57–58 "Let us call the girl and ask her mouth"** — *ve-nishalah et piha*. After sixty verses of men swearing oaths and arranging a marriage, someone asks her. And her answer is one word in Hebrew — *elekh*, **I will go** — which is the same verb Abram was given in 12:1, *lekh lekha*. She does what he did, without being told to, and nobody has promised her anything.
- **v60 "seize the gate of those who hate them"** — her family's blessing is nearly word for word God's oath to Abraham in 22:17, *your seed shall seize the gate of their enemies*. They have never heard that oath. The same promise arrives twice from opposite directions.
- **v60 "the gate"** — *sha'ar*, and the idiom is not about a door. A city's gate is where its defense stands and where its elders sit to judge — Abraham buys the cave in 23:10 at the gate, in front of everyone. To hold a city's gate is to hold the city, its army and its court together, which is why the blessing names the gate rather than the walls or the fields.
- **v62 "come back from the way to Beer-lahai-roi"** — the Hebrew is *ba mi-bo*, **came from the coming of**, and it is awkward enough that its sense has to be reasoned out rather than read: either he was returning from that place or he was coming by way of it. Beer-lahai-roi is where **Hagar** met the messenger and named God *the One who sees me* (16:14). Isaac is walking in from his brother's mother's well at the hour his wife arrives.
- **v62 "from coming to Beer-lahai-roi"** — Isaac's first appearance since the mountain in chapter 22, and he is arriving from **Hagar's well** — the place she named *the Living One who sees me* in 16:14. The text does not say why he was there.
- **v63 "to walk in the field"** — *lasuach*, a verb that occurs only here in this form, and its meaning is not established. To meditate, to complain, to wander, to talk to oneself. Something a man does alone in a field at dusk, and Hebrew has one word for it that it never uses again.
- **v64 "she fell from upon the camel"** — *va-tippol*, the plain verb for falling. Most English versions print "alighted" or "dismounted." The Hebrew says she fell.
- **v67 "and he loved her"** — *va-ye'ehaveha*. This is the **second** occurrence of love in the Bible. The first was 22:2, the son Abraham loved, on the way to the mountain. The word appears in this book first as what is asked for and then as what is found.
- **v67 "And Isaac was comforted after his mother"** — *va-yinnachem*, from *nacham*, the root of Noah's name and of God's relenting in 6:6. The chapter that begins with an old man arranging his succession ends with his son being consoled for a death the reader was told about a chapter ago and that Isaac was never shown being told.
