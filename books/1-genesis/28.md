# Genesis 28

**1** And Isaac called Jacob and blessed him, and charged him and said to him, "You shall not take a wife from the daughters of Canaan.

**2** Rise, go to Paddan-aram, to the house of Bethuel your mother's father, and take yourself a wife from there, from the daughters of Laban your mother's brother.

**3** And may God Almighty bless you, and make you fruitful and multiply you, and you shall become an assembly of peoples.

**4** And may He give you the blessing of Abraham — to you and to your seed with you — that you may possess the land where you have lived as a foreigner, which God gave to Abraham."

**5** And Isaac sent Jacob off, and he went to Paddan-aram, to Laban son of Bethuel the Aramean, brother of Rebekah, mother of Jacob and Esau.

**6** And Esau saw that Isaac had blessed Jacob and sent him to Paddan-aram to take himself a wife from there — that in blessing him he charged him, saying, "You shall not take a wife from the daughters of Canaan" —

**7** and that Jacob had listened to his father and his mother and had gone to Paddan-aram.

**8** And Esau saw that the daughters of Canaan were evil in the eyes of Isaac his father.

**9** And Esau went to Ishmael, and took Mahalath daughter of Ishmael son of Abraham, sister of Nebaioth, in addition to his wives, for himself as a wife.

## The Place

**10** And Jacob went out from Beersheba and went toward Harran.

**11** And he came upon the place, and spent the night there, for the sun had set. And he took from the stones of the place and set them at his head, and lay down in that place.

**12** And he dreamed — and here, a stairway set on the earth, and its head reaching to the heavens. And here, messengers of God going up and coming down on it.

**13** And here, the LORD standing over him, and He said, "I am the LORD, God of Abraham your father and God of Isaac. The land on which you are lying — to you I will give it, and to your seed.

**14** And your seed shall be as the dust of the earth, and you shall burst out to the west and to the east and to the north and to the south. And in you all the families of the ground shall bless themselves, and in your seed.

**15** And here — I am with you, and I will keep you wherever you go, and I will bring you back to this ground. For I will not leave you until I have done what I have spoken to you."

**16** And Jacob woke from his sleep and said, "Surely the LORD is in this place, and I — I did not know."

**17** And he was afraid and said, "How fearful is this place! This is nothing but the house of God, and this is the gate of the heavens."

**18** And Jacob rose early in the morning and took the stone that he had set at his head, and set it up as a standing-stone, and poured oil on its head.

**19** And he called the name of that place Bethel — but Luz was the name of the city at first.

**20** And Jacob made a vow, saying, "If God will be with me, and keep me on this way that I am going, and give me bread to eat and a garment to wear,

**21** and I return in peace to my father's house — then the LORD shall be my God.

**22** And this stone, which I have set as a standing-stone, shall be the house of God. And all that You give me, I will surely give a tenth of it to You."

## Notes

- **vv1–5 against 27:46** — Rebekah told Isaac that Jacob must leave because of Hittite wives. Isaac now sends him away for exactly that reason, and never mentions Esau, the blessing, or the deceit. Either he has not been told, or he is taking the reason offered. The text does not say which, and the silence is the whole shape of the scene.
- **v9 "in addition to his wives"** — Esau, seeing that Canaanite women displease his father, marries a granddaughter of Abraham. He has correctly identified the rule and is trying to satisfy it after the fact, having already married two Hittites. It is the most sympathetic thing he does, and it changes nothing.
- **v11 "he came upon the place"** — *va-yifga ba-maqom*. *Paga* is to strike against, to encounter by collision — it is used of meeting someone by accident and of falling upon someone in violence. He does not choose the spot; he runs into it. And *ha-maqom*, **the** place, with the definite article, three times in the verse, before anything has happened there.
- **v11 "for the sun had come in"** — *ki va ha-shemesh*, the ordinary idiom for sunset, kept literal because the chapter is about what is above and below: the sun goes in, a stairway goes up, and Jacob is on the ground.
- **v11 "set them at his head"** — *mera'ashotav*. Not "for a pillow," which is an English softening; the word means the place at the head, and stones are what is there. He is a man sleeping on the ground with rocks by his head, which is the point of what happens next.
- **v12 "a stairway"** — *sullam*, and the word occurs **once in the Hebrew Bible**, here. "Ladder" comes through the Greek and Latin. The root is probably *salal*, to heap up or build a raised way — which suits a ramp or a flight of steps, like the stairways built up the sides of Mesopotamian temple towers, better than it suits a ladder. Nobody can settle it, and the note is that the most famous object in the chapter is named by a word used nowhere else.
- **v12 "going up and coming down"** — in that order. They start on the ground. Whatever the messengers are doing, they were already here.
- **v12 "its head reaching to the heavens"** — *ve-rosho maggia ha-shamaymah*. In 11:4 the builders at Babel said *let us build a tower with its head in the heavens* — *ve-rosho va-shamayim*. The same phrase, and this time it is not built and not climbed; it is dreamed, and it is already there. Babel's tower was a project; this one is a given.
- **v13 "standing over him"** — *nitsav alav*. The preposition can mean *over him*, *beside him*, or *on it* — that is, on the stairway. Hebrew leaves it open whether God is at the top of the stairs or standing next to a sleeping man.
- **v13 "The land on which you are lying"** — *shokhev*, the lying-down verb. He owns nothing, he is running for his life, and the promise is measured by the patch of ground his body covers.
- **v15 "I am with you"** — the promise to Jacob is different from the ones to Abraham and Isaac. They were promised land, seed, and blessing. He is promised **company**, protection, and return — the things a man running away at night needs, in that order.
- **v16 "Surely the LORD is in this place, and I — I did not know"** — *ve-anokhi lo yadati*. The *anokhi* is emphatic and unnecessary; Hebrew has already marked the person in the verb. He puts it in anyway. *And I — I did not know.*
- **v17 "How fearful is this place"** — *mah nora ha-maqom ha-zeh*. *Nora* is the passive of the verb to fear: fear-inducing, awe-bearing. Not "awesome," which English has spent.
- **v17 "the gate of the heavens"** — *sha'ar ha-shamayim*. Worth knowing that Babylon's own name for itself, *Bab-ilu*, means **gate of god**, the etymology set against the Hebrew pun in 11:9. The exile from that city called itself the gate of heaven; here a man sleeping on stones in an empty field says the phrase about a place that has no city in it at all.
- **v18 "a standing-stone"** — *matsevah*, an upright stone set as a marker or witness. The same root as *nitsav* in v13, *standing*, used of the LORD. What he saw standing over him, he answers with a stone stood up.
- **v19 "Bethel — but Luz was the name of the city at first"** — *Beit-El* is house of God, which is what he called it in v17. The narrator immediately notes that it already had a name. Jacob renames a place that was not his, on the strength of one night.
- **vv20–22 "If God will be with me…"** — the first vow in the Bible, and it is conditional. God has just promised, unprompted and without terms, to be with him, keep him, and bring him back. Jacob's reply repeats those three items back as conditions — *if* He will be with me, *and* keep me, *and* I return — and adds bread and clothing. He has been offered everything and responds by negotiating. It is entirely in character for the man who asked his starving brother for an oath before handing over soup.
- **v22 "I will surely give a tenth"** — *asser a'asrennu*, the verb doubled with its own noun. The second time in the book someone gives a tenth; the first was Abraham to Melchizedek in 14:20, given freely after a victory. This one is offered in advance, in exchange.
