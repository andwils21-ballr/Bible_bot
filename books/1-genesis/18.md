# Genesis 18

**1** And the LORD appeared to him by the oaks of Mamre, and he was sitting at the opening of the tent in the heat of the day.

**2** And he lifted his eyes and saw, and here — three men standing over him. And he saw, and he ran to meet them from the opening of the tent, and he bowed to the ground.

**3** And he said, "My lord, if now I have found favor in your eyes, do not pass by from over your servant.

**4** Let a little water be taken, and wash your feet, and lean back under the tree.

**5** And let me take a morsel of bread, and sustain your heart; after that you may pass on — for this is why you have passed by your servant." And they said, "So do, as you have spoken."

**6** And Abraham hurried to the tent, to Sarah, and said, "Hurry — three measures of fine flour! Knead it and make loaves."

**7** And to the herd Abraham ran, and he took a calf, tender and good, and gave it to the young man, and he hurried to make it ready.

**8** And he took curds and milk and the calf that he had made ready, and he set it before them. And he was standing over them under the tree, and they ate.

**9** And they said to him, "Where is Sarah your wife?" And he said, "Here, in the tent."

**10** And He said, "Returning, I will return to you at the time of life — and here, a son for Sarah your wife." And Sarah was listening at the opening of the tent, and it was behind him.

**11** And Abraham and Sarah were old, coming on in days. The way of women had ceased to be for Sarah.

**12** And Sarah laughed within herself, saying, "After I am worn out, shall I have pleasure — and my lord is old?"

**13** And the LORD said to Abraham, "Why is this — Sarah laughed, saying, 'Shall I truly bear, and I am old?'

**14** Is any word too wondrous for the LORD? At the appointed time I will return to you, at the time of life, and Sarah shall have a son."

**15** And Sarah denied it, saying, "I did not laugh" — for she was afraid. And He said, "No. You did laugh."

## Shall the Judge of All the Earth Not Do Justice?

**16** And the men rose from there and looked down over the face of Sodom. And Abraham was walking with them, to send them off.

**17** And the LORD said, "Shall I cover from Abraham what I am doing?

**18** And Abraham — becoming, he shall become a great and mighty nation, and all the nations of the earth shall be blessed in him.

**19** For I have known him, so that he may charge his sons and his house after him, and they shall keep the way of the LORD, to do righteousness and judgment — so that the LORD may bring upon Abraham what He has spoken concerning him."

**20** And the LORD said, "The outcry of Sodom and Gomorrah — how great it is; and their sin — how very heavy.

**21** Let Me go down now and see whether they have done altogether according to her outcry that has come to Me. And if not, I will know."

**22** And the men turned from there and went toward Sodom. And Abraham was still standing before the LORD.

**23** And Abraham drew near and said, "Will You really sweep away the righteous with the wicked?

**24** Perhaps there are fifty righteous within the city. Will You really sweep away and not lift the place for the sake of the fifty righteous within it?

**25** Far be it from You to do a thing like this — to put the righteous to death with the wicked, so that the righteous should be as the wicked. Far be it from You. Shall the Judge of all the earth not do judgment?"

**26** And the LORD said, "If I find in Sodom fifty righteous within the city, then I will lift the whole place for their sake."

**27** And Abraham answered and said, "Look now — I have undertaken to speak to my Lord, and I am dust and ashes.

**28** Perhaps the fifty righteous will lack five. Will You ruin the whole city for five?" And He said, "I will not ruin, if I find there forty-five."

**29** And he spoke to Him yet again and said, "Perhaps forty will be found there." And He said, "I will not do it, for the sake of the forty."

**30** And he said, "Let it not burn in my Lord, and let me speak. Perhaps thirty will be found there." And He said, "I will not do it, if I find thirty there."

**31** And he said, "Look now — I have undertaken to speak to my Lord. Perhaps twenty will be found there." And He said, "I will not ruin, for the sake of the twenty."

**32** And he said, "Let it not burn in my Lord, and let me speak only this once. Perhaps ten will be found there." And He said, "I will not ruin, for the sake of the ten."

**33** And the LORD went, as He finished speaking to Abraham. And Abraham returned to his place.

## Notes

- **vv1–2 "the LORD appeared to him… three men"** — the chapter opens by saying the LORD appeared, and then what Abraham sees is three men. The text never reconciles the two. Through the scene the verbs slide between plural and singular: *they said* in v9, *He said* in v10, *they ate* in v8. The Hebrew does not explain the arithmetic and neither does this rendering.
- **vv4–8 "a little water… a morsel of bread"** — and then he produces three *se'ahs* of fine flour, which is something over twenty liters, a whole calf, curds and milk. He offers almost nothing and delivers a feast. Hospitality in this world is understated in the offering and extravagant in the doing.
- **vv6–7 "hurried… Hurry… ran… hurried"** — four verbs of haste in two verses, plus *ran* in v2. A ninety-nine-year-old man, three days after being circumcised, is sprinting.
- **v9 "And they said to him"** — the Masoretes put a dot over **every letter** of the word *elav*, "to him," here. These marks are rare — a handful across the whole Hebrew Bible — and the scribes who made them left the text untouched and simply flagged it. What they meant by it was never written down. This is the second one in Genesis; the other is on Sarai's accusation in 16:5.
- **v10 "at the time of life"** — *ka-et chayyah*, an idiom meaning roughly *this time next year*, when the season comes round living again. It is repeated in v14 and rendered literally both times because the phrase is about life returning and the chapter is about a womb.
- **v12 "shall I have pleasure"** — *ednah*, a rare noun from the same root as *Eden*: delight, luxuriance. A ninety-year-old woman, laughing to herself behind a tent flap, uses the word the garden is named for.
- **vv12–13 what she said, and what God repeats** — she says *and **my lord** is old*. God quotes her back to Abraham as *and **I** am old*. The words are changed in the repetition, and the change removes the part about her husband. Both verses are right here, four words apart, and any reader of the Hebrew can see the swap.
- **v14 "too wondrous"** — *ha-yippale*, from *pala*, the root for what is extraordinary, surpassing, beyond reach. Not "too hard," which suggests effort. The question is whether anything lies outside the category of things the LORD does.
- **v15** — four short clauses and the chapter's whole exchange between God and Sarah: *I did not laugh — for she was afraid — No. You did laugh.* It is the only time she is spoken to directly, and it is a contradiction.
- **v20 "the outcry"** — *za'aqat*. This noun is the technical word for the scream of someone being wronged — the cry that reaches a judge. It is what Abel's blood does from the ground in 4:10. Sodom is not described here by what its people do; it is described by the **noise coming out of it**.
- **v21 "Let Me go down now and see"** — the same descent as 11:5, where the LORD came down to see the city and the tower. Both times the sentence is flat, and both times what is being looked at is a city.
- **v22 "Abraham was still standing before the LORD"** — the men leave for Sodom; he does not move. The whole bargain happens in the space made by his not going.
- **v23 "sweep away"** — *saphah*, a violent verb for gathering something up and carrying it off entirely. Not "destroy," which is the word he uses later in v28 — *tashchit*, the flood verb from 6:13. He starts with one word and moves to the heavier one as the count comes down.
- **v25 "Far be it from You"** — *chalilah lekha*, twice in one verse. The phrase is close to *it would be a profanation for you*. He is telling God what God may not do.
- **v27 "I am dust and ashes"** — *aphar va-epher*, two words that rhyme in Hebrew as they nearly do in English. *Aphar* is the dust the man was made from in 2:7 and returns to in 3:19; *epher* is what is left after burning. He says it in the middle of negotiating.
- **The count: 50, 45, 40, 30, 20, 10** — the steps are five, five, ten, ten, ten. He makes the first cut small, then grows bolder, then stops. Nothing in the text says why he stops at ten, and he does not ask about one.
- **v33 "And the LORD went… And Abraham returned to his place"** — two clauses, no farewell, no verdict. The bargaining simply ends, and the next chapter opens in Sodom at evening.
