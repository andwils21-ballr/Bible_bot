# Genesis 6

**1** And it came to pass, when humankind began to be many upon the face of the ground, and daughters were born to them,

**2** that the sons of God saw the daughters of humankind, that they were good, and they took for themselves women from all whom they chose.

**3** And the LORD said, "My breath shall not remain in the human forever, in that he also is flesh. And his days shall be a hundred and twenty years."

**4** The fallen ones were in the earth in those days, and also afterward, when the sons of God came in to the daughters of humankind and they bore to them. These are the mighty ones who were of old, the men of the name.

## The Grief

**5** And the LORD saw that the evil of humankind was great in the earth, and that every shaping of the thoughts of his heart was only evil all the day.

**6** And the LORD was sorry that He had made humankind in the earth, and it grieved Him to His heart.

**7** And the LORD said, "I will wipe away the human whom I created from upon the face of the ground — from human to beast, to creeping thing, and to the bird of the heavens — for I am sorry that I made them."

**8** But Noah found favor in the eyes of the LORD.

## The Begettings of Noah

**9** These are the begettings of Noah. Noah was a righteous man, whole in his generations. With God Noah walked.

**10** And Noah fathered three sons: Shem, Ham, and Japheth.

**11** And the earth was ruined before God, and the earth was filled with violence.

**12** And God saw the earth, and look — it was ruined, for all flesh had ruined its way upon the earth.

**13** And God said to Noah, "The end of all flesh has come before Me, for the earth is filled with violence because of them. And look — I am ruining them, along with the earth.

**14** Make yourself an ark of gopher wood. Rooms you shall make in the ark, and you shall cover it inside and out with the covering.

**15** And this is how you shall make it: three hundred cubits the length of the ark, fifty cubits its width, and thirty cubits its height.

**16** A light you shall make for the ark, and to a cubit you shall finish it from above. And the opening of the ark you shall set in its side. Lower, second, and third decks you shall make it.

**17** And I — look, I am bringing the flood, waters upon the earth, to ruin all flesh in which is the breath of life from under the heavens. Everything that is in the earth shall expire.

**18** But I will raise up My covenant with you, and you shall come into the ark — you, and your sons, and your wife, and your sons' wives with you.

**19** And from every living thing, from all flesh, two of each you shall bring into the ark to keep alive with you; male and female they shall be.

**20** From the bird after its kind, and from the beasts after its kind, from every creeping thing of the ground after its kind — two of each shall come to you, to be kept alive.

**21** And you, take for yourself from every food that is eaten, and gather it to yourself, and it shall be food for you and for them."

**22** And Noah did according to all that God commanded him. So he did.

## Notes

- **v2, 4 "the sons of God"** — *benei ha-Elohim*, exactly that, twice. The text does not say who they are. It does not explain them, condemn them by name, or return to them. Four verses, and then the subject changes and never comes back. Every account of who they were is supplied from somewhere other than these four verses.
- **v3 "My breath shall not abide"** — *lo yadon ruchi*. Nobody knows what *yadon* is. The root could be *din*, to judge or contend, or *dun*, to remain or abide; neither is certain and both are argued. And the next word, *beshaggam*, rendered here "in that he also," is one of the genuinely opaque words in Genesis. This verse is unclear in Hebrew and the English should not pretend otherwise.
- **v4 "the fallen ones"** — *ha-nephilim*. Most English Bibles transliterate it, which turns an ordinary-looking Hebrew word into a proper noun and a mystery. The form looks like it belongs to the root *naphal*, to fall, so "fallen ones" — but that is what the word looks like, not a translation anyone can prove. Rendered as what it appears to say, with the uncertainty stated here rather than hidden behind a capital letter.
- **v4 "the men of the name"** — *anshei ha-shem*. Usually "men of renown," which is what it means. Worth hearing literally anyway, because five chapters later a group of men will try to build a tower and *make themselves a name*.
- **v6 "was sorry… it grieved Him"** — two words that both reach backward. *Va-yinnachem*, from *nacham*, is the root Lamech used when he named Noah in 5:29, saying *this one will bring us relief*. The father named his son for comfort; now God is "comforted" in the sense of relenting, and the flood follows. And *va-yit'atsev*, "it grieved Him," is from *'atsav* — the same root as *'itsavon*, the pain given to the woman and the man in 3:16–17. The word for the curse laid on humankind is now the word for what humankind does to God. That is not a coincidence available to an English reader, and it is the center of the chapter.
- **v8 "Noah found favor"** — *Noach matsa chen*. In Hebrew, *Noach* is written nun-chet and *chen*, favor, is written chet-nun. The same two letters, reversed. The sentence that saves him is his own name turned around.
- **v9 "whole in his generations"** — *tamim*, the word used of an animal fit for sacrifice: unblemished, entire, without defect. Not "morally perfect" — sound, complete, all there.
- **v9 "With God Noah walked"** — *et ha-Elohim hithhallekh Noach*, the same construction used of Enoch in 5:22 and 5:24, the man who did not die. Two men in the book are described this way, and this is the second.
- **vv11–13 "ruined… ruined… ruining"** — the root *shachat* four times in three verses: the earth *was ruined*, God saw it *was ruined*, all flesh *had ruined* its way, and God says *I am ruining them*. English prints "corrupt" for the crime and "destroy" for the punishment, which conceals that they are the same word. The judgment is not a response to the ruin; it is the ruin, handed back.
- **v11 "violence"** — *chamas*. Not warfare specifically — lawlessness, the violent seizure of what is not yours. It is the word the prophets use for what the strong do to the poor.
- **v14 "an ark"** — *tevah*, and it is not a ship. Hebrew has ship words — *oniyyah*, the kind Jonah boards; *sefinah* — and Genesis uses neither, in twenty-six chances. A *tevah* is a chest or container, and the only other object in the Bible called one is the reed basket Moses' mother waterproofs and floats down the Nile. Two containers, two rescues through water, one rare word used nowhere else.
- **v14–16 it has no way to steer and nothing to drive it** — God specifies dimensions, compartments, pitch inside and out, three decks, a door in the side, one opening for light. No sail, no oar, no rudder, no mast, no prow. And the verbs match: the waters **lift** it (7:17), it **goes** on the face of the waters (7:18) — just the ordinary word for *went* — and it **rests** (8:4). The survival is not seamanship.

  The closest literal English is **box**, which is what the Hebrew word means — but in English that word is small, and this one is four hundred and fifty feet long. *Boat* goes wrong the other way: a boat is a thing you drive. English settled on **ark**, from Latin *arca*, a chest, and it holds up because it is used for nothing else — *container* suggests a shipping crate, *box* suggests something liftable, *boat* suggests steering, and *ark* suggests only this.
- **v14 "ark" covers two unrelated Hebrew words** — Noah's is *tevah*. The Ark of the Covenant is *aron*, which shares nothing with it and appears more than two hundred other times in the Hebrew Bible. Any sense that the two arks are a matched pair comes from English alone.

  And *aron* is in Genesis exactly once, in its last chapter: *and they embalmed him, and he was put in the **aron** in Egypt* (50:26). It is Joseph's coffin. Genesis opens with a container that carries life through water and closes with a container holding a dead man in Egypt — and English gives the first two "ark" and the third "coffin," so the shape is invisible to us and was not to them.
- **v14 "gopher wood"** — *atsei gopher*. The word *gopher* occurs here and nowhere else in Hebrew. No one knows what tree it was. Transliterating it is the honest option.
- **v14 "cover it… with the covering"** — *ve-khapharta… ba-kopher*, the verb and the noun from the same root. *Kopher* is pitch, the black tar you seal a hull with. It is also the root of *kippur* — atonement, as in the Day of Atonement. The verb means to cover over, and what it covers is either a boat's seams or a people's guilt. Here, at its first appearance in the Bible, it is literal tar on literal wood.
- **v16 "a light"** — *tsohar*, another word whose meaning is not settled: possibly a window, possibly a roof, possibly something that gives light. And "to a cubit you shall finish it from above" is obscure in the Hebrew as it stands. The building instructions are less clear than English translations make them.
- **v17 "the flood"** — *mabbul*, a rare word used almost exclusively of this event. Not the ordinary word for a flood or an overflowing river. Whatever this is, Hebrew gives it its own name.
