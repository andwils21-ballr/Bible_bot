# Genesis 47

**1** And Joseph came and told Pharaoh, and said, "My father and my brothers, and their flocks and their herds and all that is theirs, have come from the land of Canaan. And here they are in the land of Goshen."

**2** And from the whole of his brothers he took five men and set them before Pharaoh.

**3** And Pharaoh said to his brothers, "What is your work?" And they said to Pharaoh, "Your servants are shepherds of flocks — both we and our fathers."

**4** And they said to Pharaoh, "We have come to live in the land as foreigners, for there is no pasture for the flocks that belong to your servants, since the famine is heavy in the land of Canaan. And now, let your servants please dwell in the land of Goshen."

**5** And Pharaoh said to Joseph, saying, "Your father and your brothers have come to you.

**6** The land of Egypt is before you. In the best of the land settle your father and your brothers — let them dwell in the land of Goshen. And if you know there are capable men among them, then set them as chiefs of livestock over what is mine."

**7** And Joseph brought Jacob his father and stood him before Pharaoh, and Jacob blessed Pharaoh.

**8** And Pharaoh said to Jacob, "How many are the days of the years of your life?"

**9** And Jacob said to Pharaoh, "The years I have lived as a foreigner are a hundred and thirty. Few and evil have been the days of the years of my life, and they have not reached the days of the years of the lives of my fathers in the days of their time as a foreigner."

**10** And Jacob blessed Pharaoh, and went out from before Pharaoh.

**11** And Joseph settled his father and his brothers, and gave them a holding in the land of Egypt, in the best of the land, in the land of Rameses, as Pharaoh had commanded.

**12** And Joseph sustained his father and his brothers and all his father's house with bread, according to the little ones.

## The Buying of Egypt

**13** And there was no bread in all the land, for the famine was very heavy, and the land of Egypt and the land of Canaan languished before the famine.

**14** And Joseph gathered up all the silver found in the land of Egypt and in the land of Canaan, for the grain they were buying, and Joseph brought the silver to Pharaoh's house.

**15** And the silver ran out from the land of Egypt and from the land of Canaan, and all Egypt came to Joseph, saying, "Give us bread — why should we die in front of you? For the silver is gone."

**16** And Joseph said, "Give your livestock, and I will give you for your livestock, if the silver is gone."

**17** And they brought their livestock to Joseph, and Joseph gave them bread for the horses, and for the livestock of the flocks, and for the livestock of the herds, and for the donkeys. And he led them along with bread that year, for all their livestock.

**18** And that year ran out, and they came to him in the second year and said to him, "We will not hide from my lord that the silver is spent and the herds of beasts belong to my lord. Nothing is left before my lord but our bodies and our ground.

**19** Why should we die before your eyes, both we and our ground? Buy us and our ground for bread, and we and our ground will be slaves to Pharaoh. And give seed, that we may live and not die, and the ground not be laid waste."

**20** And Joseph bought all the ground of Egypt for Pharaoh, for every Egyptian sold his field, because the famine was strong upon them. And the land became Pharaoh's.

**21** And the people — he moved them into the cities, from one end of the border of Egypt to the other.

**22** Only the ground of the priests he did not buy, for the priests had an allotment from Pharaoh, and they ate their allotment that Pharaoh gave them. Therefore they did not sell their ground.

**23** And Joseph said to the people, "Look — I have bought you today, and your ground, for Pharaoh. Here is seed for you, and you shall sow the ground.

**24** And it will come to pass at the harvests that you shall give a fifth to Pharaoh, and four parts shall be yours, for seed of the field and for your food, and for those in your houses, and for your little ones to eat."

**25** And they said, "You have kept us alive. Let us find favor in the eyes of my lord, and we will be slaves to Pharaoh."

**26** And Joseph made it a statute to this day over the ground of Egypt — a fifth for Pharaoh. Only the ground of the priests alone did not become Pharaoh's.

## Do Not Bury Me in Egypt

**27** And Israel dwelt in the land of Egypt, in the land of Goshen, and they took holding in it, and were fruitful and multiplied greatly.

**28** And Jacob lived in the land of Egypt seventeen years. And the days of Jacob, the years of his life, were a hundred and forty-seven years.

**29** And the days of Israel drew near to die, and he called his son Joseph and said to him, "If I have found favor in your eyes, put your hand, please, under my loin, and do kindness and truth with me: do not bury me, please, in Egypt.

**30** And I will lie with my fathers, and you shall carry me out of Egypt and bury me in their burial place." And he said, "I myself will do according to your word."

**31** And he said, "Swear to me." And he swore to him. And Israel bowed himself at the head of the bed.

## Notes

- **vv7, 10 "Jacob blessed Pharaoh"** — twice, once entering and once leaving, and the two sentences frame the audience. A hundred-and-thirty-year-old shepherd from a famine country, whose trade the Egyptians find detestable, blesses the man Egypt worshipped, and the narrator reports it without a syllable of comment on either side.
- **v9 "Few and evil"** — *me'at ve-ra'im*. He is a hundred and thirty and calls it few, and he is right by the family's own arithmetic: Abraham reached 175, Isaac 180. He is also the only one of the three to say a word about how his life went. And he calls them *yemei shnei **megurai***, the years of my **time as a foreigner** — *magur*, from *gur*, to reside as an outsider. Asked how old he is, he answers with how long he has been a stranger.
- **v11 "in the land of Rameses"** — the best land in Egypt, given as a *holding*. And Exodus 1:11 names the two store-cities the Israelites are later forced to build under the whip: *Pithom and **Rameses***. The gift and the slave-labor site carry one name. Nothing in Genesis points this out; the word simply sits there and Exodus picks it up.
- **v13 "languished"** — *va-telah*, from a root so rare its force is hard to fix. The sense required is exhaustion, the land wrung out. What the verb does carry is that Egypt and Canaan are named together, suffering the same thing — which is the sentence that brings Jacob's family and Pharaoh's kingdom into one economy.
- **vv14–21 how Egypt was bought** — read it as a sequence and it is a foreclosure in three steps. First the **silver** goes to Pharaoh's house (v14). When the silver runs out, the **livestock** (vv16–17). When that year ends, the people themselves offer their **bodies and their ground** (vv18–19). Joseph accepts, buys the whole country, and moves the population into the cities. He is not accused of anything and he does not defend himself. The text lays out the ledger and moves on.
- **v22 "Only the ground of the priests"** — one class keeps its land, because Pharaoh already feeds them from a state allotment. Joseph's own wife is the daughter of a priest of On (41:45). The exemption is reported as a fact of administration, with no remark attached.
- **v25 "we will be slaves to Pharaoh"** — *avadim*, and they say it as thanks: *you have kept us alive*. It is the word the whole Joseph story has been circling. He was sold as an *eved* (39:17); his brothers were terrified of being made *avadim* (43:18); Judah offered to become one in Benjamin's place (44:33). Here a whole nation asks for the status, and the man who grants it is the one who was sold into it.
- **v26 "a fifth... to this day"** — the twenty percent is the same figure Joseph proposed to Pharaoh in 41:34 for the years of plenty. What was an emergency levy becomes permanent law, and the narrator says it is still in force at the time of writing.
- **v27 "were fruitful and multiplied greatly"** — *va-yifru va-yirbu*, which is the blessing of 1:28 and 9:1, the words spoken over humanity at creation and again after the flood. Israel receives it here, in the same chapter where Egypt is reduced to tenancy, and *va-ye'achazu bah*, **they took holding in it** — *achuzzah*, the word for the cave Abraham bought in chapter 23 and for the land Canaan was promised as. They put down roots, and they put them down in the wrong country. Exodus 1:7 opens by repeating this sentence and expanding it, and Exodus 1:11 is four verses later.
- **v29 "put your hand under my loin"** — the oath gesture from 24:2, where Abraham made his servant swear this way about not taking a wife for Isaac from Canaan. It is used twice in the Bible, both times by a dying patriarch, both times about where the family belongs. And *chesed ve-emet*, **kindness and truth**, is the pair Jacob used of God in 32:11 when he was too small for all of it.
- **v30 "I will lie with my fathers"** — *ve-shakhavti im avotai*, and *shakhav* is the ordinary verb to lie down. Hebrew's phrase for dying is lying down with the people you came from, which is why the burial place matters so much in the same breath. He is not asking for a grave. He is asking not to be left.
- **v31 "bowed himself at the head of the bed"** — *al rosh ha-mittah*. The consonants are ה-מ-ט-ה, and vowelled the other way they read *ha-matteh*, **the staff** — bowing over the top of his staff. The Masoretic reading tradition points it *mittah*, a bed, and the Greek Old Testament read it as the staff. Both are in the letters; nothing else in the verse decides between them. A dying man propped in bed and an old man leaning on a stick are the same four consonants.
