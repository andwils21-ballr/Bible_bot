# Genesis 43

**1** The famine was heavy in the land.

**2** And it came to pass, when they had finished eating the grain they had brought from Egypt, that their father said to them, "Go back, buy us a little food."

**3** Judah said to him, saying, "The man solemnly warned us, saying, 'You shall not see my face unless your brother is with you.'

**4** If you will send our brother with us, we will go down and buy you food.

**5** If you will not send him, we will not go down — for the man said to us, 'You shall not see my face unless your brother is with you.'"

**6** Israel said, "Why did you do me this harm, telling the man you had another brother?"

**7** They said, "The man asked closely about us and about our kindred, saying, 'Is your father still alive? Do you have a brother?' We told him according to these words. Could we possibly know that he would say, 'Bring your brother down'?"

**8** Judah said to Israel his father, "Send the boy with me, and let us rise and go, and let us live and not die — we, and you, and our little ones.

**9** I myself will stand as the pledge for him. Hold me answerable for him. If I do not bring him to you and set him before you, then I will have sinned against you all my days.

**10** For if we had not delayed, we would have been back twice by now."

**11** Israel their father said to them, "If it must be so, then do this. Take some of the land's choice produce in your vessels and bring the man down a gift: a little balm and a little honey, gum and ladanum, pistachios and almonds.

**12** Take double silver in your hand, and the silver that was returned in the mouth of your bags — take it back in your hand. Perhaps it was an oversight.

**13** Take your brother, and rise, go back to the man.

**14** May God Almighty give you compassion before the man, so he sends away with you your other brother, and Benjamin. As for me — if I am bereaved, I am bereaved."

## Bring the Men to the House

**15** The men took this gift, and they took double silver in their hand, and Benjamin. They rose and went down to Egypt and stood before Joseph.

**16** Joseph saw Benjamin with them, and said to his steward, "Bring the men to the house, and slaughter an animal and prepare it, for the men will eat with me at noon."

**17** The man did as Joseph said, and the man brought the men to Joseph's house.

**18** The men were afraid because they had been brought to Joseph's house, and they said, "It is over the matter of the silver returned in our bags the first time that we are being brought in — to roll himself upon us and fall upon us, and take us as slaves, and our donkeys."

**19** They came up to Joseph's steward and spoke to him at the door of the house,

**20** and said, "Please, my lord — we came down the first time to buy food.

**21** And it came to pass, when we came to the lodging place and opened our bags, that here — each man's silver was in the mouth of his bag, our silver by its weight. We have brought it back in our hand.

**22** We have brought down other silver in our hand to buy food. We do not know who put our silver in our bags."

**23** He said, "Peace to you. Do not be afraid. Your God and the God of your father gave you treasure in your bags. Your silver came to me." He brought Simeon out to them.

**24** The man brought the men into Joseph's house and gave water, and they washed their feet, and he gave fodder to their donkeys.

**25** They prepared the gift for Joseph's coming at noon, for they had heard that they would eat bread there.

## Is It Well With Your Father?

**26** Joseph came to the house, and they brought him the gift that was in their hand, into the house, and bowed down to him to the ground.

**27** He asked them about their peace, and said, "Is it well with your father, the old man you spoke of? Is he still alive?"

**28** They said, "It is well with your servant our father. He is still alive." They knelt and bowed down.

**29** He lifted his eyes and saw Benjamin his brother, his mother's son, and said, "Is this your youngest brother, whom you spoke of to me?" He said, "God be gracious to you, my son."

**30** Joseph hurried, for his compassion grew hot toward his brother, and he sought to weep. He came into the chamber and wept there.

**31** He washed his face and came out, and he held himself in, and said, "Set out bread."

**32** They set it out for him by himself, and for them by themselves, and for the Egyptians eating with him by themselves — because Egyptians could not eat bread with Hebrews, for that is detestable to Egypt.

**33** They sat before him, the firstborn according to his birthright and the youngest according to his youth, and the men looked at one another in astonishment.

**34** He had portions carried to them from before his face, and Benjamin's portion was five times more than the portions of all of them. They drank and became drunk with him.

## Notes

- **vv3, 5 "You shall not see my face"** — *lo tir'u fanai*, and Judah quotes it twice in three verses. Joseph did not say this in chapter 42; what he said there was *you shall not go out from here* (42:15). The brothers have been carrying the demand around in terms of a **face** they may not look at, and the word *panim* is the one this whole reunion turns on: they bow before his face (v26), he sends portions *from before his face* (v34), and in 45:3 he will finally say *I am Joseph*.
- **v7 "The man asked closely about us"** — *sha'ol sha'al*, the verb doubled: he questioned and questioned. Except that he did not. Read 42:13 again and the brothers volunteer the whole family — twelve of us, one gone, the youngest at home — with no question asked. Under their father's anger they remember an interrogation instead. It is the second time in two chapters that the brothers narrate an earlier scene differently from how it was written, and the narrator corrects neither.
- **v9 "I myself will stand as the pledge for him"** — *anokhi e'ervennu*, from *arav*, to pledge, to go bail. Its noun is *eravon*, and the last time this root appeared was 38:17–18, when Judah handed a woman on the road his seal and staff as a **pledge** and lost them. He now offers himself as the pledge, for the brother of the boy he sold. And *mi-yadi tevaqshennu*, **from my hand you shall require him**, is what Reuben's *his blood is being required* (42:22) was pointing at.
- **v9 against 37:26** — the last plan Judah proposed about a brother of Joseph's was *what profit is it that we kill our brother... let us sell him*. This is the same man, to the same father, about the same mother's other son, offering to answer for him with everything he has. The book puts the two sentences six chapters apart and says nothing.
- **v11 "the land's choice produce"** — *mi-zimrat ha-arets*, and *zimrah* elsewhere means **music, a song**. What it is doing here is not settled; the sense required is something like the land's best or its yield, and no other passage pins it down. The old translations reach for *the best fruits*, which is a guess with a long pedigree.
- **v11 the gift is the cargo that carried Joseph away** — Jacob's list includes *tsori*, *nekhot* and *lot* — balm, gum, ladanum. In 37:25 the Ishmaelite caravan his sons sold Joseph to was carrying *nekhot u-tsori va-lot*, the same three commodities, down to Egypt. Jacob, who does not know, sends after his lost son the exact goods that were on the camels that took him.
- **v14 "give you compassion"** — *rachamim*, a plural noun built on *rechem*, **womb**. Hebrew's word for mercy is the organ a mother carries a child in. Jacob asks *El Shaddai* for womb-feeling from an Egyptian official, on behalf of Rachel's last son.
- **v14 "as I have been bereaved, I am bereaved"** — *ka'asher shakholti shakhalti*. The same verb twice, perfect tense both times, with nothing between them. He is not saying *if I lose him I lose him*; he is speaking as a man already robbed, agreeing to be robbed again.
- **v16 "slaughter a slaughter"** — *u-tvoach tevach*, verb and its own noun. The root is *tabach*, and it is the root of Potiphar's title, *sar ha-tabbachim*, chief of the slaughterers (37:36). The word that named the man who owned Joseph is now an order Joseph gives about lunch.
- **v18 "take us as slaves"** — *la-avadim*, and this is what the ten of them are afraid of, standing in Egypt. They are the men who took twenty pieces of silver for a brother and let him be taken there as one. The narrator reports the fear without a flicker.
- **v23 "Your God and the God of your father gave you treasure"** — the steward, an Egyptian, answers a question about missing silver by naming their God, tells them their money reached him, and produces Simeon. Every word of it is a cover story, and it is the kindest speech anyone makes to them in three chapters.
- **vv27–28 "peace"** — *shalom*, four times in two verses. He asks after their *shalom*, then after their father's; they answer *shalom*. In 37:4 these men *could not speak to him le-shalom*, could not say a peaceable word. In 37:14 their father sent him to *see the shalom of your brothers*, which is the errand that ended in the pit. He is now standing in front of them asking the question he was sent to ask, and they answer it.
- **v30 "his compassion grew hot"** — *nikhmeru rachamav*, from *kamar*, to be heated or stirred up; *rachamim* is the womb-word again, the one his father used in v14. Jacob asked God to give the Egyptian *rachamim* toward his sons. Sixteen verses later the Egyptian's *rachamim* boil over and he has to leave the room.
- **v31 "he held himself in"** — *va-yit'appaq*, a rare verb for forcing back what is rising. He washes his face first, then does it, then gives an order about food. The same verb comes back in 45:1, where he *could not* do it any more.
- **v32 three tables** — Egyptians will not eat bread with Hebrews, *ki to'evah hi le-Mitsrayim*, because it is detestable. And Joseph eats alone, at neither table. It is also the sentence that makes 39:6 literal: Potiphar left Joseph everything *except the bread he ate*.
- **v33 seated in birth order** — *ha-bekhor ki-vkhorato ve-ha-tsa'ir ki-ts'irato*, the firstborn by his birthright, the youngest by his youth, and *va-yitmehu ha-anashim*, the men were astonished at one another. An Egyptian official has just seated eleven strangers in the exact order they were born. They have no explanation and the text offers them none.
- **v34 "five times more"** — *chamesh yadot*, five hands. He has the youngest son of his own mother served five portions in front of ten older brothers, and then they drink together. It is the arrangement that started all of this — one favored boy, ten watching — rebuilt deliberately by the boy, who sits where the father used to sit.
