# Genesis 42

**1** Jacob saw that there was grain in Egypt, and Jacob said to his sons, "Why do you look at one another?"

**2** He said, "Here, I have heard that there is grain in Egypt. Go down there and buy grain for us from there, and let us live and not die."

**3** Ten of Joseph's brothers went down to buy grain from Egypt.

**4** But Benjamin, Joseph's brother, Jacob did not send with his brothers, for he said, "Harm might come to him."

**5** The sons of Israel came to buy among those who were coming, for the famine was in the land of Canaan.

**6** Joseph — he was the ruler over the land, he was the one who sold grain to all the people of the land. Joseph's brothers came and bowed down to him, faces to the ground.

**7** Joseph saw his brothers and recognized them, and he made himself a stranger to them and spoke harshly with them. He said to them, "Where have you come from?" They said, "From the land of Canaan, to buy food."

**8** Joseph recognized his brothers, and they did not recognize him.

**9** Joseph remembered the dreams he had dreamed about them, and he said to them, "You are spies. You have come to see the nakedness of the land."

**10** They said to him, "No, my lord. Your servants have come to buy food.

**11** All of us are sons of one man. We are honest men. Your servants have never been spies."

**12** He said to them, "No — you have come to see the nakedness of the land."

**13** They said, "Your servants are twelve brothers, sons of one man in the land of Canaan. Here, the youngest is with our father today, and the one is no more."

**14** Joseph said to them, "That is what I said to you: you are spies.

**15** By this you shall be tested: by the life of Pharaoh, you shall not go out from here unless your youngest brother comes here.

**16** Send one of you, and let him take your brother, and you be bound, and let your words be tested, whether there is truth with you. If not — by the life of Pharaoh, you are spies."

**17** He gathered them into custody for three days.

## We Are Guilty Concerning Our Brother

**18** Joseph said to them on the third day, "Do this and live — I fear God.

**19** If you are honest men, let one of your brothers be bound in your house of custody, and you go, bring grain for the hunger of your houses.

**20** Bring your youngest brother to me, so your words will be confirmed and you will not die." They did so.

**21** They said, each man to his brother, "Truly we are guilty concerning our brother, whose soul's distress we saw when he pleaded with us, and we did not listen. That is why this distress has come to us."

**22** Reuben answered them, saying, "Did I not say to you, 'Do not sin against the boy,' and you did not listen? Now, look, his blood is being required."

**23** They did not know that Joseph was listening, because the interpreter was between them.

**24** He turned away from them and wept, and returned to them and spoke to them. He took Simeon from among them and bound him before their eyes.

**25** Joseph commanded, and they filled their vessels with grain, and returned each man's silver to his sack, and gave them provisions for the road. It was done for them so.

**26** They loaded their grain on their donkeys and went from there.

**27** One of them opened his sack to give fodder to his donkey at the lodging place, and he saw his silver — and here, it was in the mouth of his bag.

**28** He said to his brothers, "My silver has been returned, and here — it is in my bag." Their heart went out, and they trembled, each man to his brother, saying, "What is this God has done to us?"

## All of Them Are Against Me

**29** They came to Jacob their father, to the land of Canaan, and told him all that had befallen them, saying,

**30** "The man, the lord of the land, spoke harshly with us, and made us out to be spies of the land.

**31** We said to him, 'We are honest men. We have never been spies.

**32** We are twelve brothers, sons of our father. The one is no more, and the youngest is with our father today in the land of Canaan.'

**33** The man, the lord of the land, said to us, 'By this I will know that you are honest men: leave one of your brothers with me, and take grain for the hunger of your houses and go.

**34** Bring your youngest brother to me, and I will know that you are not spies, that you are honest men. I will give you your brother, and you may trade in the land.'"

**35** And it came to pass, as they were emptying their sacks, that here — each man's bundle of silver was in his sack. They saw their bundles of silver, they and their father, and they were afraid.

**36** Jacob their father said to them, "You have bereaved me. Joseph is no more, and Simeon is no more, and you would take Benjamin. All of them are against me."

**37** Reuben said to his father, saying, "You may put my two sons to death if I do not bring him back to you. Give him into my hand, and I will return him to you."

**38** He said, "My son shall not go down with you, for his brother is dead and he alone is left. If harm befalls him on the road you go, you will bring my gray head down in sorrow to Sheol."

## Notes

- **vv1, 2 "grain"** — *shever*, and the verb for buying it is *lishbor*, from *shavar*, **to break**. The same noun in the same form means a fracture or a smashing. Hebrew's word for the rations that keep a family alive is its word for something broken, and the chapter uses it eight times without ever needing to explain why.
- **v1 "Why do you look at one another?"** — *lammah titra'u*, an odd form of *ra'ah*, to see: why are you eyeing each other. It is the first thing Jacob says in the famine, and it is aimed at ten grown men standing in a circle doing nothing.
- **vv4, 38 "lest harm befall him"** — *ason*, a rare word for disaster. It occurs three times in Genesis and all three are Jacob, about Benjamin — here, in v38, and again in 44:29. He has one word for what he is afraid of, and he uses it only about this one son.
- **vv6, 9 the dream, and then the remembering** — *va-yishtachavu lo appayim artsah*, **they bowed down to him, faces to the ground**, which is 37:7 happening. Three verses later: *and Joseph **remembered** the dreams he had dreamed about them*. The text puts the bowing first and the remembering second. He does not recognize the moment as it arrives; he recognizes it afterward, and what he does next he does knowing.
- **vv7, 8 recognized, and made himself a stranger** — *va-yakkirem va-yitnakker aleihem*: two verbs from the same root, *nakar*, back to back, meaning **to recognize** and **to act the stranger**. Then v8 says it again from both sides — *Joseph recognized his brothers, and they did not recognize him*. This is the fourth chapter running to turn on this root: the bloodied coat in 37:32, Judah's seal in 38:25, and now a man using one half of the word to hide behind the other.
- **v9 "the nakedness of the land"** — *ervat ha-arets*. *Ervah* is the word for exposed genitals; it is the term Leviticus uses for forbidden sexual uncovering. Applied to a country it means the undefended places, where the walls are weak — but the word he picks is a shame word, and he is saying it to his own brothers.
- **vv11, 19, 31, 33, 34 "honest men"** — *kenim*, from *ken*, true, upright, so. They say it about themselves five times in one chapter, and Joseph feeds it back to them twice. Everything they say about their family is accurate except one clause, and they have been saying that clause for twenty years.
- **vv13, 32 "and the one is no more"** — *ve-ha-echad einennu*, twice, and it is the family's settled formula. The Hebrew is barer than any English can be: not *died*, not *was killed*, but flatly **he is not**. Reuben used the same word at the empty pit in 37:30, *ha-yeled einennu*, the boy is not. Jacob uses it of two sons at once in v36. The man they are standing in front of has been *einennu* since they made him so.
- **vv15, 18 two oaths in four verses** — Joseph swears *chei Far'oh*, **by the life of Pharaoh**, twice, in the voice of an Egyptian official. Then on the third day he tells them *et ha-Elohim ani yare*, **I fear God**. The reader knows which one is his. The brothers hear an Egyptian who has mentioned a god.
- **v21 something chapter 37 never told us** — *whose soul's distress we saw when he **pleaded** with us, and we did not listen*. Go back to 37:23–24 and there is none of this: the coat is stripped, he is thrown in the pit, they sit down to eat. Not one word about Joseph begging, and not one word about them hearing him. Twenty years later his brothers supply it themselves, in front of him, and the narrator lets the earlier silence stand uncorrected. The pleading was always in the story; it was just never written down.

  And the word they use for his suffering is *tsarah* — *tsarat nafsho* — and then they use it again for their own: *that is why this **tsarah** has come to us*. Same word, his and theirs.
- **v23 "because the interpreter was between them"** — *ha-melits beinotam*, a word that appears in only one other place in the Hebrew Bible. It explains the entire scene: they have been talking freely in Hebrew in front of an Egyptian official, and confessing to his face because they are certain he cannot understand. He can. And the sentence that tells us is placed immediately after their confession, not before it — so the reader hears it the way Joseph did.
- **v24 "he turned away from them and wept"** — the first of the times he cries in this story, and he goes out of the room to do it. Then he comes back and continues the interrogation.
- **v24 he takes Simeon** — not Reuben, the firstborn, who has just been heard reminding them he tried to stop it (v22). Simeon is the next in line, and he is one of the two who emptied Shechem in chapter 34. The text gives the choice and no reason for it.
- **vv28, 35 the silver** — planted by Joseph (v25), found at the lodging place, and found again in every sack when they get home. Their response is *what is this **God** has done to us?* Silver has been in this family's story before: twenty pieces of it, in chapter 37, for a brother. It is the one thing they cannot get rid of.
- **v36 "All of them are against me"** — *alai hayu khullanah*, and the sentence before it names his losses in order: *Joseph is not, and Simeon is not*. He does not know that Simeon is with Joseph and that neither is lost. He is the only person in the chapter who is wrong about everything, and he is the only one who has not done anything.
- **v38 "down in sorrow to Sheol"** — *ve-horadtem et seivati be-yagon she'olah*, and he said almost this in 37:35 when they brought him the coat: *I will go down to my son, mourning, to Sheol*. The two are the only times Sheol appears in Genesis, and both are Jacob, and both are about Rachel's sons. He also says *his brother is **dead***, plainly, for the first time.
