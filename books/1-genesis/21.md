# Genesis 21

**1** And the LORD attended to Sarah as He had said, and the LORD did for Sarah as He had spoken.

**2** And Sarah conceived and bore Abraham a son in his old age, at the appointed time of which God had spoken to him.

**3** And Abraham called the name of his son who was born to him, whom Sarah bore to him, Isaac.

**4** And Abraham circumcised Isaac his son at eight days old, as God had commanded him.

**5** And Abraham was a hundred years old when Isaac his son was born to him.

**6** And Sarah said, "Laughter God has made for me. Everyone who hears will laugh for me."

**7** And she said, "Who would have spoken it to Abraham — *Sarah has nursed sons*? For I have borne a son in his old age."

## The Sending Away

**8** And the child grew and was weaned, and Abraham made a great feast on the day Isaac was weaned.

**9** And Sarah saw the son of Hagar the Egyptian, whom she had borne to Abraham — laughing.

**10** And she said to Abraham, "Drive out this slave woman and her son, for the son of this slave woman shall not inherit with my son, with Isaac."

**11** And the thing was very evil in Abraham's eyes, on account of his son.

**12** And God said to Abraham, "Let it not be evil in your eyes over the boy and over your slave woman. All that Sarah says to you — listen to her voice, for through Isaac shall seed be called for you.

**13** And also the son of the slave woman I will make into a nation, for he is your seed."

**14** And Abraham rose early in the morning and took bread and a skin of water, and gave them to Hagar — setting them on her shoulder — and the child, and he sent her away. And she went, and she wandered in the wilderness of Beersheba.

**15** And the water was finished from the skin, and she cast the child under one of the bushes.

**16** And she went and sat down opposite him, far off, about a bowshot, for she said, "Let me not see the child die." And she sat opposite, and she lifted her voice and wept.

**17** And God heard the voice of the boy, and the messenger of God called to Hagar from the heavens and said to her, "What is it with you, Hagar? Do not fear, for God has heard the voice of the boy where he is.

**18** Rise, lift up the boy, and hold your hand strong on him, for I will make him into a great nation."

**19** And God opened her eyes, and she saw a well of water. And she went and filled the skin with water, and gave the boy drink.

**20** And God was with the boy, and he grew, and he settled in the wilderness, and he became an archer.

**21** And he settled in the wilderness of Paran, and his mother took a wife for him from the land of Egypt.

## The Well of the Oath

**22** And it came to pass at that time that Abimelech, and Phicol the commander of his army, said to Abraham, "God is with you in all that you do.

**23** And now, swear to me here by God that you will not deal falsely with me, nor with my offspring, nor with my posterity. According to the kindness I have done with you, you shall do with me, and with the land in which you have lived as a foreigner."

**24** And Abraham said, "I will swear."

**25** And Abraham reproved Abimelech over the matter of a well of water that Abimelech's servants had seized.

**26** And Abimelech said, "I do not know who did this thing. And you also did not tell me, and I also have not heard of it until today."

**27** And Abraham took flocks and cattle and gave them to Abimelech, and the two of them cut a covenant.

**28** And Abraham set seven ewe lambs of the flock by themselves.

**29** And Abimelech said to Abraham, "What are these seven ewe lambs that you have set by themselves?"

**30** And he said, "For you shall take the seven ewe lambs from my hand, so that it may be a witness for me that I dug this well."

**31** Therefore that place was called Beersheba, for there the two of them swore.

**32** And they cut a covenant at Beersheba. And Abimelech rose, and Phicol the commander of his army, and they returned to the land of the Philistines.

**33** And he planted a tamarisk at Beersheba, and there he called on the name of the LORD, God of Everlasting.

**34** And Abraham lived as a foreigner in the land of the Philistines many days.

## Notes

- **v1 "attended to"** — *paqad*, a wide verb: to visit, to muster, to number, to take account of, to appoint an overseer. It is what a commander does when he counts his troops and what a creditor does when he calls in what is owed. Rendered "attended to" because "visited" has gone soft in English, and the verb means action was taken.
- **vv6–7 "Laughter… will laugh"** — *tsechoq* and *yitschaq*. The second of those is the boy's name, written out as an ordinary verb. She says *God has made laughter for me*, and *everyone who hears will Isaac for me* — and the preposition *li* can mean *for me*, *with me*, or *at me*. Whether the neighbors are laughing with her or at her is left open in the Hebrew, from a woman who denied laughing in 18:15.
- **v7 "Sarah has nursed sons"** — she quotes a sentence nobody ever said, as the thing nobody could have said. And *banim* is plural: *sons*, not *a son*.
- **v9 "laughing"** — *metsacheq*, the participle of the same root as Isaac's name. What Ishmael was doing is one word, and it can mean laughing, playing, joking, mocking, or fondling — and this verse gives no context at all. English Bibles supply one: "mocking," "playing," "laughing." The Hebrew says he was **Isaac-ing**, and the whole chapter turns on a sentence that does not say what he did.

  The book uses the same form twice more, and both are worth setting beside this one. In 19:14, Lot warning his sons-in-law of the city's destruction seems to them *ki-metsacheq*, like a man making a joke. In 26:8, Abimelech looks through a window and sees Isaac *metsacheq* with Rebekah his wife — and concludes at once that they are married, not siblings. There the word plainly means something between a husband and a wife.

  So the range is real and wide, and the text itself demonstrates it: a joke in one place, intimacy in another, and here, whatever Sarah saw. What cannot be said is that the word means *mocking*. That is a translator's decision about a boy's guilt, and it is the decision on which his expulsion is usually justified.
- **v10 "Drive out"** — *garesh*, the verb used in 3:24 when God **drove out** the man from the garden, and in 4:14 when Cain says *you have driven me out*. It is expulsion, not dismissal.
- **v11 "very evil in Abraham's eyes"** — *va-yera… me'od*. The only time in these chapters that something is said to grieve him. Not when Sarai afflicted Hagar in 16:6; not when he was asked for Isaac in the next chapter. Here.
- **v12 "listen to her voice"** — *shema be-qolah*. In 3:17 the man was condemned *because you listened to the voice of your wife*; in 16:2 Abram *listened to the voice of Sarai* and it produced this crisis. Here God commands the same phrase. The book uses the identical expression for the fall, for the error, and for the instruction, and does not explain the difference.
- **v14 "bread and a skin of water"** — he had just been given flocks, cattle, and servants by Abimelech in 20:14, and had a household large enough to arm 318 men in 14:14. She leaves with what she can carry on one shoulder.
- **v14 "she wandered"** — *va-teta*, the verb Abraham used of himself in 20:13, *when God made me wander*. He was made to wander and came out rich; she is sent to wander with a waterskin.
- **v16 "she lifted her voice and wept"** — and then in v17 **God heard the voice of the boy**. She is the one weeping; the voice that is heard is his. The text does not say the boy made a sound.
- **v17 "where he is"** — *ba-asher hu sham*, three words, no explanation. Heard in the place he is in, as he is.
- **v17 Ishmael, again** — the name means *God hears*, and the verse says *God heard* twice without ever using the boy's name. The same wordplay was made to Hagar in 16:11 and to Abraham in 17:20. Three times, always about hearing, always about this son.
- **v19 "God opened her eyes, and she saw a well"** — the well was there. Nothing is created; her eyes are opened. This is the second time she has been met at water in a wilderness (16:7), and the second time she is the one who sees — she is the woman who named God *El Ro'i*, God of my seeing.
- **v25–30 "a well… seven ewe lambs"** — the naming turns on a pun that works only in Hebrew: *sheva* is seven, and *shava* is to swear an oath; *be'er* is a well. *Be'er-sheva* is therefore Well of Seven and Well of the Oath at the same time, and v31 gives the oath explanation while vv28–30 give the seven. Both are in the text, and they do not compete.
- **v33 "a tamarisk"** — *eshel*. He plants a tree in a land he does not own, at a well he had to prove he dug, having just sworn not to deal falsely with the people who took it.
- **v33 "God of Everlasting"** — *El Olam*. *Olam* is not eternity as a philosophical category; it is the vanishing point — the farthest back and the farthest ahead that anyone can see. Another *El* title, like *El Elyon* from Melchizedek and *El Shaddai* in 17:1 and *El Ro'i* from Hagar. Four of them now, each given at a particular place by a particular person.
