# Genesis 32

**1** And Laban rose early in the morning and kissed his sons and his daughters and blessed them. And Laban went, and returned to his place.

**2** And Jacob went on his way, and the messengers of God met him.

**3** And Jacob said when he saw them, "This is God's camp." And he called the name of that place Mahanaim.

## Four Hundred Men

**4** And Jacob sent messengers ahead of him to Esau his brother, to the land of Seir, the field of Edom.

**5** And he commanded them, saying, "So shall you say to my lord, to Esau: 'So says your servant Jacob: I have sojourned with Laban and stayed until now.

**6** And I have come to have ox and donkey, flock and manservant and maidservant. And I have sent to tell my lord, to find favor in your eyes.'"

**7** And the messengers returned to Jacob, saying, "We came to your brother, to Esau — and he is also coming to meet you, and four hundred men with him."

**8** And Jacob was very afraid, and it was narrow for him. And he divided the people who were with him, and the flocks and the herds and the camels, into two camps.

**9** And he said, "If Esau comes to the one camp and strikes it, then the camp that is left will be an escape."

**10** And Jacob said, "God of my father Abraham and God of my father Isaac, LORD who said to me, 'Return to your land and to your kindred, and I will do well by you' —

**11** I am too small for all the kindnesses and all the faithfulness you have done for your servant. For with my staff I crossed this Jordan, and now I have become two camps.

**12** Deliver me, please, from the hand of my brother, from the hand of Esau — for I am afraid of him, lest he come and strike me, mother upon sons.

**13** And you yourself said, 'I will surely do well by you, and I will make your seed like the sand of the sea, which cannot be counted for multitude.'"

**14** And he spent the night there that night. And he took from what had come into his hand a gift for Esau his brother:

**15** two hundred she-goats and twenty he-goats, two hundred ewes and twenty rams,

**16** thirty nursing camels and their young, forty cows and ten bulls, twenty she-donkeys and ten young donkeys.

**17** And he gave them into the hand of his servants, drove by drove on its own, and said to his servants, "Cross over ahead of me, and put a space between drove and drove."

**18** And he commanded the first, saying, "When Esau my brother meets you and asks you, saying, 'Whose are you, and where are you going, and whose are these ahead of you?' —

**19** then you shall say, 'Your servant Jacob's. It is a gift sent to my lord, to Esau. And here — he is also behind us.'"

**20** And he commanded the second also, and the third also, and all those going behind the droves also, saying, "In this manner you shall speak to Esau when you find him.

**21** And you shall say also, 'Here — your servant Jacob is behind us.'" For he said, "Let me cover his face with the gift that goes ahead of me, and afterward I will see his face. Perhaps he will lift my face."

**22** And the gift crossed over ahead of his face, and he spent that night in the camp.

## The Ford of the Jabbok

**23** And he rose that night and took his two wives and his two servant girls and his eleven children, and crossed the ford of the Jabbok.

**24** And he took them and brought them across the stream, and he brought across what was his.

**25** And Jacob was left alone. And a man wrestled with him until the dawn came up.

**26** And he saw that he could not prevail against him, and he touched the socket of his thigh, and the socket of Jacob's thigh was wrenched as he wrestled with him.

**27** And he said, "Send me away, for the dawn has come up." And he said, "I will not send you away unless you bless me."

**28** And he said to him, "What is your name?" And he said, "Jacob."

**29** And he said, "Your name shall no longer be said as Jacob, but Israel — for you have struggled with God and with men, and you have prevailed."

**30** And Jacob asked and said, "Tell me, please, your name." And he said, "Why is this that you ask for my name?" And he blessed him there.

**31** And Jacob called the name of the place Peniel — "for I have seen God face to face, and my life was delivered."

**32** And the sun rose upon him as he crossed Penuel, and he was limping on his thigh.

**33** Therefore the sons of Israel do not eat the sinew of the hip that is on the socket of the thigh, to this day, because he touched the socket of Jacob's thigh, at the sinew of the hip.

## Notes

- **On the verse numbers in this chapter** — the Hebrew text this rendering follows begins chapter 32 with Laban's departure, so it has 33 verses. Most English Bibles put that verse at the end of chapter 31 and number this chapter 1–32. So from v1 on, every verse here is **one higher** than the same words in an NIV or ESV: the wrestling match is 32:25–33 here and 32:24–32 there. Chapter 33 lines up again.
- **v2 "the messengers of God met him"** — *va-yifge'u vo mal'akhei Elohim*, and the verb is the one from 28:11, where Jacob leaving home *va-yifga ba-maqom*, **met** the place, and slept there, and saw the ladder. *Paga* is not a gentle word for meeting; it means to run into, to come up against, and in other contexts to fall upon someone. Twenty years ago it happened to him on the way out and there were angels on a ladder. Now it happens on the way back, in a single sentence, with no explanation of what they wanted.
- **vv2, 4 "messengers"** — Hebrew has one word, *mal'akh*, for a messenger and for what English calls an angel. God's *mal'akhim* meet Jacob in v2; Jacob sends his own *mal'akhim* to Esau in v4. The same word, back to back, and English cannot do it — the moment you write "angels" in one verse and "messengers" in the next, the echo is gone.
- **v3 "Mahanaim"** — *machanayim*, and the ending is Hebrew's **dual**, a grammatical number for things that come in twos, used for eyes, ears, hands. The name means **two camps**. Five verses later, afraid, Jacob splits everything he owns into *shnei machanot*, two camps, and in v11 he prays *I have become two camps*. He names the place before he knows why, and the rest of the chapter is spent earning the name.
- **v6 "ox and donkey, flock and manservant and maidservant"** — every noun singular, a short and modest list from a man the previous chapter left with flocks, servants, camels and donkeys in quantity (30:43). The camels are missing. He is about to send Esau a gift of five hundred and fifty head, and the message he sends first understates what he has.
- **v8 "it was narrow for him"** — *va-yetser lo*, from *tsarar*, to be tight, to press in. Hebrew distress is spatial: the walls come in. English "distress" is from the same picture — Latin *stringere*, to draw tight — but nobody feels it any more, and the Hebrew is still literal.
- **v11 "I am too small"** — *qatonti*. One word, a verb, and no English equivalent is one word: *I have been made small by*, *I am less than*. Not modesty; a measurement. And then *with my **staff** I crossed this Jordan* — *be-maqli*, and *maqel* is the same word as the peeled rods of 30:37, the trick that made him rich. He came across the river owning one stick. He is going back across with two camps, and the word for the stick is the word for the con.
- **v11 "kindnesses... faithfulness"** — *chasadim* and *emet*, the pair that runs through the rest of the Bible as *chesed ve-emet*. *Chesed* is loyalty inside a relationship — not generosity to a stranger but keeping faith with your own; *emet* is from *aman*, to be firm, which is also where **amen** comes from. Together they mean something like *the loyalty that holds and does not shift*, and Jacob is saying he does not measure up to the amount of it he has received.
- **v12 "mother upon sons"** — *em al banim*, a set phrase for annihilation: not the fighters killed and the household taken, but the whole line ended in one place. Jacob, who has just spent two chapters counting livestock, prays about his children.
- **vv21–22 "face"** — *panim*, five times in two verses, and it is the hinge of the chapter. *Akhapperah fanav* — let me **cover his face** with the gift. *Ve-acharei khen er'eh fanav* — afterward I will see his face. *Ulai yissa fanai* — perhaps he will **lift my face**, the idiom for accepting a person, showing favor. *Va-ta'avor ha-minchah al panav* — the gift crossed over **ahead of his face**.

  And the verb in the first of them is *kaphar*, which means to cover, and which is the verb the whole sacrificial system is built on — the Day of *Kippur* is the day of covering. Its first appearance in this sense in the Bible is here, and it is not a priest and an altar. It is a frightened man sending goats ahead of him to cover his brother's face before he has to look at it.

  Ten verses later he names a place **Peniel**, *face of God*, and says *I have seen God **face to face***. The chapter spends its whole length on one word.
- **v23 "the ford of the Jabbok"** — *ma'avar Yabboq*, and three words in this chapter are built from nearly the same three letters. **Yabboq**, the river. **Ya'aqov**, Jacob. **Ye'aveq**, he wrestled. *Y-b-q*, *y-'-q-b*, *'-b-q* — the place, the man, and the act, turning over each other like the letters themselves. It is the densest piece of wordplay in Genesis, and no English rendering can show it without stopping to explain, which is what this note is for.
- **v25 "a man wrestled with him"** — *ish*, a man. Not an angel, not the LORD, not a stranger: the plainest word in the language for an adult male. The text says nothing else about him for six verses, refuses to give his name when asked, and lets Jacob be the one to conclude in v31 that he has seen God. Every reading that arrives faster than that is arriving faster than the text.
- **v26 "the socket of his thigh"** — *kaf yerekh*, the hollow or cup of the hip. *Yerekh* is also the euphemism for the loins as the source of offspring — Genesis 46:26 counts the people who came *out of Jacob's yerekh*. The man cannot beat him, so he touches him once, and the place he touches is where the sons come from, on the night before Jacob meets the brother who wanted to kill him and after the eleventh son is born.
- **v27 "Send me away"** — *shallecheni*, the exact word Jacob said to Laban in 30:25: *send me away, and let me go to my own place*. Jacob spent twenty years asking to be let go. He now has hold of someone who is asking him for it, and he says no.
- **vv28–29 "What is your name?"** — the last time Jacob was asked who he was, it was his blind father in 27:18, *mi attah beni*, **who are you, my son** — and he answered *I am Esau your firstborn*, and stole the blessing with the lie. Here he is asked again, in the dark, on the night he has to face the brother he stole it from. This time he says **Jacob**, which is the name that means heel-grabber and supplanter, and which Esau in 27:36 turned into a complaint — *is he not rightly named Jacob, for he has supplanted me twice*. He gives his real name and gets the blessing anyway, and a new name with it.
- **v29 "Israel"** — *ki sarita im Elohim ve-im anashim va-tukhal*, **you have struggled with God and with men and you have prevailed**. The verb is *sarah*, to contend, to persist — a rare root, and the name *Yisra'el* is built from it plus *El*. Two honest things. First, the name as a piece of grammar would more naturally mean *God contends* or *God rules*, since in the ordinary run of Hebrew names ending in *-el* — Daniel, Ishmael, Ezekiel — God is the one doing the verb. The verse assigns the verb to Jacob instead, which is either the narrator telling us what it means or a name being read against its grammar; the text does not say which. Second, *va-tukhal*, and you prevailed, is the word Rachel used at 30:8 about beating her sister, and the word used at v26 of this chapter for what the man **could not** do to Jacob. The name is given for the one thing about Jacob that never changes.
- **v30 "Why is this that you ask for my name?"** — the question is refused, and the blessing is given anyway, and the man leaves before the sun is up. Jacob names the place after what he concludes happened; the story never confirms it.
- **v31 "my life was delivered"** — *va-tinnatsel nafshi*, the verb *natsal* for the third time in two chapters: God **stripped away** Laban's flocks (31:9, 31:16), Jacob prayed *deliver me from the hand of my brother* (v12), and now he says his own life was pulled out. The prayer is answered in the wrong fight, before he has met Esau at all.
- **v31, 32 "Peniel... Penuel"** — the place is spelled two ways in two consecutive verses, and the text leaves both, the same way it left Mehujael spelled twice at 4:18 and the written and read forms of Gad at 30:11.
- **v32 "the sun rose upon him"** — *va-yizrach lo ha-shemesh*. When he left home in 28:11 he stopped for the night *ki va ha-shemesh*, **because the sun had gone in**, and dreamed the ladder. The sun set on him going out and rises on him coming back, and the verb is *zarach*, the same word used of dawn all through the Bible. Between the two sentences is everything that happened to him, and he comes back into the light limping.
- **v33 "the sinew of the hip"** — *gid ha-nasheh*. *Gid* is a sinew or tendon; *nasheh* occurs in the Bible only in this verse, twice, so what exactly it names is not recoverable from the language — it is defined only by where it sits, *on the socket of the thigh*. This is the one dietary law in Genesis, it is given without a command attached, and it is explained as a memorial rather than ordered: the sons of Israel **do not eat** it, not *shall not*. The whole nation's kitchens keep the record of one night's limp.
