# Genesis 1

**1** In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth.

**2** And the earth was wild and waste, and darkness lay over the face of the deep, and the breath of God was hovering over the face of the waters.

**3** And God said, "Light — be." And light was.

**4** And God saw the light, that it was good; and God made a division between the light and the darkness.

**5** And God called the light Day, and the darkness He called Night. And it was evening, and it was morning — day one.

## The Second Day

**6** And God said, "Let there be a beaten expanse in the middle of the waters, and let it divide between waters and waters."

**7** And God made the expanse, and He divided between the waters that were beneath the expanse and the waters that were above the expanse. And it was so.

**8** And God called the expanse Heaven. And it was evening, and it was morning — a second day.

## The Third Day

**9** And God said, "Let the waters beneath the heavens be gathered to one place, and let the dry ground be seen." And it was so.

**10** And God called the dry ground Earth, and the gathering of the waters He called Seas. And God saw that it was good.

**11** And God said, "Let the earth grow green with growing things: seed-bearing plants, and fruit trees making fruit after their kind, with their seed in them, upon the earth." And it was so.

**12** And the earth brought out growing things — seed-bearing plants after their kind, and trees making fruit with their seed in them after their kind. And God saw that it was good.

**13** And it was evening, and it was morning — a third day.

## The Fourth Day

**14** And God said, "Let there be lamps in the expanse of the heavens, to divide between the day and the night; and let them be for signs, and for appointed times, and for days and years.

**15** And let them be lamps in the expanse of the heavens, to give light upon the earth." And it was so.

**16** And God made the two great lamps — the greater lamp for the rule of the day, and the lesser lamp for the rule of the night — and the stars.

**17** And God set them in the expanse of the heavens, to give light upon the earth,

**18** and to rule over the day and over the night, and to divide between the light and the darkness. And God saw that it was good.

**19** And it was evening, and it was morning — a fourth day.

## The Fifth Day

**20** And God said, "Let the waters swarm with swarms of living souls, and let birds fly above the earth, across the face of the expanse of the heavens."

**21** And God created the great sea-monsters, and every living soul that creeps, with which the waters swarmed, after their kind, and every winged bird after its kind. And God saw that it was good.

**22** And God blessed them, saying, "Be fruitful and multiply, and fill the waters in the seas; and let the birds multiply in the earth."

**23** And it was evening, and it was morning — a fifth day.

## The Sixth Day

**24** And God said, "Let the earth bring out living souls after their kind — beasts, and creeping things, and the living things of the earth after their kind." And it was so.

**25** And God made the living things of the earth after their kind, and the beasts after their kind, and everything that creeps on the ground after its kind. And God saw that it was good.

**26** And God said, "Let us make humankind in our image, according to our likeness; and let them hold sway over the fish of the sea, and over the birds of the heavens, and over the beasts, and over all the earth, and over every creeping thing that creeps upon the earth."

**27** And God created the human in His image;  
in the image of God He created him;  
male and female He created them.

**28** And God blessed them, and God said to them, "Be fruitful and multiply, and fill the earth and take it under foot; and hold sway over the fish of the sea, and over the birds of the heavens, and over every living thing that creeps upon the earth."

**29** And God said, "Look — I have given you every seed-bearing plant that is upon the face of all the earth, and every tree that has in it tree-fruit bearing seed. To you it shall be for food.

**30** And to every living thing of the earth, and to every bird of the heavens, and to everything that creeps upon the earth, everything with a living soul in it — every green plant for food." And it was so.

**31** And God saw everything that He had made, and look — it was very good. And it was evening, and it was morning — the sixth day.

## Notes

- **v1 "In the beginning"** — Heb. *bereshit*. The word is grammatically a construct form, which is why some read the opening as one long sentence: "When God began to create the heavens and the earth — the earth being wild and waste — God said, 'Light, be.'" That reading makes v1 a subordinate clause rather than a standing declaration. The traditional absolute reading is kept here because Hebrew does permit it and because the sentence carries its own weight, but the reader should know the first three words of the Bible are contested.
- **v1 "created"** — Heb. *bara*. Across the whole Hebrew Bible this verb never takes a human subject. It is reserved for God. English "create" is worn down by ordinary use in a way the Hebrew word is not.
- **v1 "the heavens and the earth"** — a merism: naming the two poles to mean everything between them, the way we say "searched high and low." Not two items on a list.
- **v2 "wild and waste"** — Heb. *tohu wa-vohu*, a rhyming jingle. "Formless and void" is accurate but sounds like philosophy; the Hebrew sounds like a phrase, almost a taunt. It describes a desert waste — uninhabitable, not empty of matter.
- **v2 "the breath of God was hovering"** — Heb. *ruach* is wind, breath, and spirit in one word, and no English choice keeps all three. *Merachefet* (hovering) is rare; Deuteronomy 32:11 uses it of an eagle fluttering over its nest. The image is a bird brooding over the water, not a ghost drifting.
- **v3 "Light — be." And light was.** — Heb. *yehi 'or wa-yehi 'or*. Two words, then the same two words with a single letter added — a prefixed vav, the Hebrew "and." The command itself is three syllables. Any English longer than that is already a commentary.
- **v4 "it was good"** — Heb. *tov*. Closer to "it worked," "it was fit for what it was for." A good knife is *tov*. The moral sense we hear in English "good" is present in Hebrew elsewhere but is not what is being said about light.
- **v5 "day one"** — Heb. *yom echad*, a cardinal number, not the ordinal "first." Days two through six then take ordinals. Almost every English Bible flattens this to "the first day," losing a deliberate oddity: before there is a second day, there is no *first* — there is only one. The pattern is worth watching to the end of the chapter: day one is a cardinal, days two through five are bare ordinals with no article, and the sixth alone is *yom ha-shishi*, **the** sixth day. Only the last one gets a definite article.
- **v6 "beaten expanse"** — Heb. *raqia*, from the root *raqa*. Everywhere that verb appears it means making something flat and broad by beating or pressure: gold hammered into plates and cut into threads (Exod 39:3), bronze censers beaten into a plating for the altar (Num 17:4), a goldsmith overlaying an idol (Isa 40:19), beaten silver from Tarshish (Jer 10:9) — and also the earth **spread out** over the waters (Ps 136:6, Isa 42:5), and a foot **stamping** the ground (Ezek 6:11, 2 Sam 22:43).

  What the root does **not** say is *dome*. Beating and stamping make things flat, not curved, and when Ezekiel sees a *raqia* (1:22) the verb he uses is *natui*, stretched out, the word for pitching a tent. So the familiar English options are four different interpretations rather than four synonyms: "firmament" comes from Latin *firmamentum*, rendering Greek *stereoma*, "a solid thing" — the Septuagint translators had already decided it was solid; "vault" and "dome" supply a curve the Hebrew never mentions; "expanse" keeps the spreading and drops the beating. Only the process is certain: something thin and broad, made by being beaten or stretched out.

  Solidity is argued from elsewhere, and the arguments are from the text rather than the root: Job 37:18 asks whether you can spread out the skies **hard as a cast mirror**, and Ezekiel 1:26 sets a throne on top of one.
- **v14 "appointed times"** — Heb. *mo'adim*, the standard word for the festival calendar. The lamps are not decoration and not merely clocks; they are hung to tell Israel when to gather.
- **v16 "the two great lamps"** — the sun and the moon are never named in this chapter, though Hebrew has ordinary words for both. Every neighbouring culture worshipped them under those names. Here they are demoted to fixtures, hung up like lamps in a room, and the stars get two words tacked onto the end of a sentence.
- **vv24–25 "beasts"** — *behemah*, and it is not a word for cows. The verse sorts land animals into three groups — *behemah*, *remes* (creeping things), and *chayyat ha-arets* (the living things of the earth) — and *behemah* is the middle class: the large animals that can be kept, as against the wild ones and the small ones. English has no single word for it. The King James printed **cattle**, which was right in 1611 when the word still meant livestock of any kind, the same root that gives us *chattel*. It now means bovines, so this rendering uses **beasts** throughout, and reserves *livestock* for *miqneh*, the word for animals as property.
- **v26 "Let us make"** — Heb. *na'aseh*, first person plural. The Hebrew simply says it, and any explanation offered for it — a heavenly court, a plural of deliberation, a plural of majesty — is the reader's, not the text's.
- **v26, 28 "hold sway," "take it under foot"** — Heb. *radah* and *kavash*. Both are hard words. *Radah* is what a king does to subjects; *kavash* is used of subduing conquered land and of forcing a person down. "Have dominion" and "subdue" are not softenings, but four centuries of use have made them sound ceremonial. They are not.
- **v27** — the prose breaks into three balanced lines here and nowhere else in the chapter. Set as poetry because the Hebrew is.
- **v27 "male and female"** — *zakhar u-neqevah*, and this exact pair — male **and** female, joined — occurs six times in the Hebrew Bible and all six are in Genesis: here, at 5:2 where it is repeated word for word, and four times in the flood account, 6:19, 7:3, 7:9 and 7:16. Everywhere else in the Bible the two words appear only as alternatives, *zakhar **o** neqevah*, **male or female** — a sorting question, as in Leviticus 3:1 and 12:7 and Deuteronomy 4:16. "Or" asks which kind a thing is. "And" makes a pair. So when the animals go into the ark two by two, the text is not reporting their biology. It is using the phrase from the day humanity was made, and using it nowhere else.
- **vv20, 21, 24, 30 "living soul"** — Heb. *nephesh chayyah*. The King James renders this phrase "living creature" every time it belongs to an animal, and "living soul" in 2:7 when God breathes into the human. It is the same two Hebrew words. The split is the translator's, not the text's, and most modern English Bibles have kept the habit. Rendered identically here, because the Hebrew is identical.
- **v30 "everything with a living soul in it"** — Heb. *asher bo nephesh chayyah*, "in which there is a living soul." The *bo* ("in it") is masculine; *eretz*, earth, is feminine, as the feminine verb *hayetah* in v2 shows. So the clause cannot reach back to the earth — grammar rules it out. The soul is in the creeping thing, not in the ground it crawls on. English word order makes this ambiguous where Hebrew is not, which is why the clause is moved here.
- **"soul"** — *nephesh* is not the Greek immortal soul. The root sense is **throat**: the neck, where breath and food pass. From there it means breath, appetite, hunger, life, and finally the self. A *nephesh chayyah* is a breathing, hungry, living thing — which is why this verse, having listed everything with a throat, goes on to say what they may eat.
- **v31 "very good"** — Heb. *tov me'od*. The only time the verdict is intensified, and it is passed on the whole, not on the last item made.
