# Genesis 13

**1** And Abram went up from Egypt — he and his wife and all that was his, and Lot with him — toward the Negev.

**2** And Abram was very heavy in livestock, in silver, and in gold.

**3** And he went on his journeys from the Negev as far as Bethel, to the place where his tent had been at the beginning, between Bethel and Ai,

**4** to the place of the altar that he had made there at the first. And Abram called there on the name of the LORD.

**5** And Lot also, who went with Abram, had flocks and cattle and tents.

**6** And the land could not carry them, to settle together, for their possessions were many, and they were not able to settle together.

**7** And there was quarreling between the herdsmen of Abram's livestock and the herdsmen of Lot's livestock. And the Canaanite and the Perizzite were then settled in the land.

## The Choice

**8** And Abram said to Lot, "Let there not be quarreling, please, between me and you, and between my herdsmen and your herdsmen, for we are men, brothers.

**9** Is not all the land before you? Separate, please, from me. If to the left, then I will go right; and if to the right, then I will go left."

**10** And Lot lifted his eyes and saw all the plain of the Jordan, that all of it was well watered — before the LORD ruined Sodom and Gomorrah — like the garden of the LORD, like the land of Egypt, as you come toward Zoar.

**11** And Lot chose for himself all the plain of the Jordan, and Lot journeyed eastward, and they separated, each from his brother.

**12** Abram settled in the land of Canaan, and Lot settled in the cities of the plain, and he pitched his tent as far as Sodom.

**13** And the men of Sodom were evil, and sinners against the LORD, exceedingly.

## Lift Your Eyes

**14** And the LORD said to Abram, after Lot had separated from him, "Lift up, please, your eyes, and look from the place where you are — northward and southward and eastward and seaward.

**15** For all the land that you see, to you I will give it, and to your seed, forever.

**16** And I will make your seed like the dust of the earth, so that if a man could count the dust of the earth, then your seed also could be counted.

**17** Rise, walk about in the land, through its length and through its breadth, for to you I will give it."

**18** And Abram moved his tent, and he came and settled by the oaks of Mamre, which are in Hebron. And he built there an altar to the LORD.

## Notes

- **v2 "very heavy"** — *kaved me'od*. Three verses earlier, in 12:10, the reason he went down to Egypt was that the famine was *kaved*, heavy, in the land. He goes down because of heaviness and comes back up heavy. The same word does both jobs, and the second is the wages of the lie he told in between — the livestock and servants of 12:16, given to him for his wife.
- **v6 "the land could not carry them"** — *lo nasa otam ha-arets*, the land did not lift or bear them. The same verb, *nasa*, that Lot uses four verses later when he *lifts* his eyes, and that God uses to Abram in v14. The land could not lift them; so they lift their eyes instead, and are divided by what they see.
- **v8 "we are men, brothers"** — *anashim achim anachnu*. Lot is Abram's nephew, not his brother; *ach* in Hebrew covers the whole range of male kin. But the word choice matters here, because v11 ends *and they separated, each from his brother* — and the last two brothers in this book to be alone in a field together were Cain and Abel. Abram proposes the opposite ending, and pays for it with first choice.
- **v9 "Separate, please, from me"** — *hippared na me-alai*, from the root *parad*. It is the verb that ran through the table of nations in chapter 10: *from these the nations separated out*. What happened to the whole world in a genealogy happens here between two men over grazing.
- **v9 "If to the left, then I will go right"** — Abram, who was promised this land in 12:7, gives it away with a coin-flip. He is the elder and holds the promise, and he offers the choice to the younger man with nothing.
- **v10 "Lot lifted his eyes and saw"** — the chapter turns on two liftings of eyes. Lot lifts his own, and chooses. In v14 God tells Abram *lift up your eyes* — after the choosing is over, and what Abram sees is given rather than taken. The identical gesture, once seized and once received, three verses apart.
- **v10 "like the garden of the LORD, like the land of Egypt"** — two comparisons, and they do not sit comfortably together. One is Eden. The other is the country they have just been thrown out of. The narrator hands the reader both and does not choose between them.
- **v10 "before the LORD ruined Sodom and Gomorrah"** — *shachet*, the verb that ran four times through 6:11–13, where the earth was *ruined* and God said *I am ruining them*. The word for the flood is attached to Sodom here, thirteen verses before Lot moves his tent there, and five chapters before it happens.
- **v11 "journeyed eastward"** — *mi-qedem*, which is either *from the east* or *eastward*; the Hebrew is not decisive. But eastward is the direction of departure in this book. Cain went out east of Eden (4:16); the builders came from the east to Shinar (11:2); the cherubim were stationed east of the garden (3:24). Moving east is how people in Genesis leave.
- **v13 "the men of Sodom were evil, and sinners against the LORD, exceedingly"** — *ra'im ve-chatta'im… me'od*. The narrator says this immediately after Lot pitches toward Sodom, so the reader knows what Lot does not. And *me'od*, exceedingly, is the word from 1:31 — everything God had made was *tov me'od*. The intensifier is the same; the noun it modifies has changed.
- **v14 "northward and southward and eastward and seaward"** — the Hebrew for west is *yammah*, seaward, because the Mediterranean is what lies west of anyone standing in Canaan. The compass in this language is drawn from a body standing in one place: ahead is east, behind is west, the sea is the sunset.
- **v16 "like the dust of the earth"** — *ka-aphar ha-arets*. *Aphar* is the dust the human was shaped from in 2:7 and returns to in 3:19. The promise of descendants is made in the word for what a body is made of and comes apart into.
- **v18 "the oaks of Mamre"** — *elonei*, large trees; the word is related to *el*, and such trees mark sacred places all through the book. Abram has now built altars at Shechem (12:7), at Bethel (12:8), and here. He owns none of the land and has marked three points on it.
