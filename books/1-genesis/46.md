# Genesis 46

**1** Israel set out with all that was his, and came to Beersheba, and offered sacrifices to the God of his father Isaac.

**2** God said to Israel in the visions of the night, and He said, "Jacob, Jacob." He said, "Here I am."

**3** He said, "I am the God, the God of your father. Do not be afraid to go down to Egypt, for I will make you a great nation there.

**4** I Myself will go down with you to Egypt, and I Myself will surely bring you up again. Joseph will set his hand on your eyes."

**5** Jacob rose from Beersheba, and the sons of Israel carried Jacob their father, and their little ones and their wives, in the wagons Pharaoh had sent to carry him.

**6** They took their livestock and their goods that they had gotten in the land of Canaan, and came to Egypt — Jacob and all his seed with him,

**7** his sons and his sons' sons with him, his daughters and his sons' daughters, and all his seed he brought with him to Egypt.

## The Names

**8** These are the names of the sons of Israel coming to Egypt — Jacob and his sons. Jacob's firstborn, Reuben.

**9** The sons of Reuben: Hanoch and Pallu and Hezron and Carmi.

**10** The sons of Simeon: Jemuel and Jamin and Ohad and Jachin and Zohar, and Shaul son of the Canaanite woman.

**11** The sons of Levi: Gershon, Kohath, and Merari.

**12** The sons of Judah: Er and Onan and Shelah and Perez and Zerah — and Er and Onan died in the land of Canaan. The sons of Perez were Hezron and Hamul.

**13** The sons of Issachar: Tola and Puvah and Job and Shimron.

**14** The sons of Zebulun: Sered and Elon and Jahleel.

**15** These are the sons of Leah, whom she bore to Jacob in Paddan-aram, and Dinah his daughter. All the souls of his sons and his daughters: thirty-three.

**16** The sons of Gad: Ziphion and Haggi, Shuni and Ezbon, Eri and Arodi and Areli.

**17** The sons of Asher: Imnah and Ishvah and Ishvi and Beriah, and Serah their sister. The sons of Beriah: Heber and Malchiel.

**18** These are the sons of Zilpah, whom Laban gave to Leah his daughter, and she bore these to Jacob — sixteen souls.

**19** The sons of Rachel, Jacob's wife: Joseph and Benjamin.

**20** There were born to Joseph in the land of Egypt, whom Asenath daughter of Poti-phera, priest of On, bore to him: Manasseh and Ephraim.

**21** The sons of Benjamin: Bela and Becher and Ashbel, Gera and Naaman, Ehi and Rosh, Muppim and Huppim and Ard.

**22** These are the sons of Rachel, who were born to Jacob — all the souls, fourteen.

**23** The sons of Dan: Hushim.

**24** The sons of Naphtali: Jahzeel and Guni and Jezer and Shillem.

**25** These are the sons of Bilhah, whom Laban gave to Rachel his daughter, and she bore these to Jacob — all the souls, seven.

**26** All the souls coming with Jacob to Egypt, who came out of his loins, apart from the wives of Jacob's sons — all the souls were sixty-six.

**27** The sons of Joseph, who were born to him in Egypt, were two souls. All the souls of the house of Jacob coming to Egypt: seventy.

## Goshen

**28** He sent Judah ahead of him to Joseph, to point the way before him to Goshen. They came to the land of Goshen.

**29** Joseph harnessed his chariot and went up to meet Israel his father at Goshen. He appeared to him, and fell on his neck, and wept on his neck a long while.

**30** Israel said to Joseph, "Let me die this time, after seeing your face, since you are still alive."

**31** Joseph said to his brothers and to his father's house, "I will go up and tell Pharaoh, and say to him, 'My brothers and my father's house, who were in the land of Canaan, have come to me.

**32** The men are shepherds of flocks, for they have been men of livestock, and they have brought their flocks and their herds and all that is theirs.'

**33** It will come to pass, when Pharaoh calls you and says, 'What is your work?' —

**34** then you shall say, 'Your servants have been men of livestock from our youth until now, both we and our fathers' — so that you may dwell in the land of Goshen, for every shepherd of flocks is detestable to Egypt."

## Notes

- **v1 "to Beersheba"** — he stops where his family's story has stopped before. Abraham planted a tamarisk at Beersheba and called on the name of *El Olam* there (21:33); Isaac lived there. Jacob is on his way out of the land for good and he pulls up at the last place in it that belongs to his father and grandfather, and offers sacrifices there.
- **v2 "Jacob, Jacob"** — the doubled name, answered with *hinneni*, **here I am**. It happened once before in this book: *Abraham, Abraham* at the knife, in 22:11, answered the same way. It is the form of address for the moment before something irreversible.
- **v3 "Do not be afraid to go down to Egypt"** — and one generation earlier God told Isaac the exact opposite. In 26:2, in a famine, *the LORD appeared to him and said, **do not go down to Egypt***, same verb *yarad*. And Abram in 12:10 went down in a famine with no divine word at all, and it went badly. Three generations, three descents, three different answers — forbidden, unmentioned, and now commanded.
- **v4 "I will go down with you... and I will surely bring you up"** — *anokhi ered immekha... ve-anokhi a'alkha gam aloh*, with the emphatic **I Myself** twice. Down and up: *yarad* and *alah* are the two verbs the Bible will use for Egypt for the next four hundred years. The whole Exodus is promised here, in one sentence, to one frightened old man who will not live to see it.
- **v4 "Joseph will set his hand on your eyes"** — closing the eyes of the dead. It is the last clause of the promise and the smallest: after the nation and the going up, God tells him who will be standing there when he dies.
- **v7 "his daughters and his sons' daughters"** — plural, twice. The book names exactly two women in this generation: Dinah in v15 and Serah daughter of Asher in v17, and Serah is named for no stated reason at all. The rest are counted and not named.
- **v12 "Er and Onan died in the land of Canaan"** — they are listed among Judah's sons and then immediately reported dead, in the same verse. The list does not drop them. It carries them and then tells you.
- **v15 "thirty-three"** — count the names in verses 9–15 and there are thirty-four: six sons of Leah, twenty-five grandsons, two great-grandsons through Perez, and Dinah. The total says thirty-three. There are several ways to reach the smaller figure — subtract the two who are stated dead and add Jacob himself, or leave Dinah out of the count of *sons and daughters* she is standing in the middle of — and the text specifies none of them. The arithmetic is left showing.
- **vv26–27 "sixty-six... seventy"** — the chapter gives two totals four words apart. Sixty-six is *those who came out of his loins*, not counting the sons' wives. Then Joseph's two Egyptian-born sons are added, and Joseph, and Jacob, and the figure is seventy. Both numbers are correct under their own definitions, and the text supplies both rather than choosing.
- **v19 "the sons of Rachel, Jacob's wife"** — Rachel is the only one of the four called *Jacob's wife* in this list. Leah is *Leah* (v15); Zilpah and Bilhah are identified by which daughter Laban gave them to. Forty years after her death and two chapters after Judah quoted him saying *my wife bore me two* (44:27), the register still has only one wife in it.
- **v28 "to point the way before him"** — *le-horot lefanav*, from *yarah*, to throw, to aim, to point — and it is the root of **Torah**, which is the pointing, the direction given. Jacob sends Judah ahead to aim the family at Goshen. Reuben is the firstborn; Judah has been leading since chapter 44 and the text simply keeps handing him the job.
- **v29 "wept on his neck a long while"** — *va-yevk al tsavva'rav **od***, and *od* means *still*, *yet*, *more*. Joseph weeps; Jacob does not. The old man's line is the next verse, and it is about dying.
- **v30 "after seeing your face"** — *panim*, the word this reunion has run on since 42:6 — the face they could not see, the face he hid, the face he sent gifts ahead of. Jacob gets the one thing the phrase has been withholding for five chapters and immediately says he is ready to die.
- **vv32, 34 "detestable to Egypt"** — *to'evat Mitsrayim*, the same word used in 43:32 for Egyptians eating bread with Hebrews. Joseph coaches his family to describe themselves in the terms most offensive to the country he governs, precisely so they will be housed apart in Goshen. He is using Egyptian contempt as a wall around them, and he is the one Egyptian-named, Egyptian-married, Egyptian-dressed man in the room.
- **v27 "seventy" — and the Greek says seventy-five** — the Hebrew closes the list at *all the souls of the house of Jacob who came to Egypt were **seventy***, counting Joseph's sons in Egypt as **two**. The **Septuagint** counts them as **nine**, because at v20 it adds three further generations the Hebrew has not got: Manasseh's son Machir and his son Gilead, and Ephraim's sons Sutalaam and Taam, and Sutalaam's son Edem. Nine instead of two makes the total **seventy-five**. And when Stephen recounts this scene in **Acts 7:14** he says Joseph sent for his father and all his kindred, *ἐν ψυχαῖς ἑβδομήκοντα πέντε* — **seventy-five souls**. The New Testament counts the family by the Greek list, not the Hebrew one.
