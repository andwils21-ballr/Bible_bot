# Genesis 20

**1** And Abraham journeyed from there to the land of the Negev, and he settled between Kadesh and Shur, and he sojourned in Gerar.

**2** And Abraham said of Sarah his wife, "She is my sister." And Abimelech king of Gerar sent and took Sarah.

**3** And God came to Abimelech in a dream of the night, and said to him, "Here, you are a dead man, over the woman you have taken — and she is owned by an owner."

**4** And Abimelech had not come near her. And he said, "My Lord, will You kill even a righteous nation?

**5** Did he not say to me, 'She is my sister'? And she — she herself also said, 'He is my brother.' In the integrity of my heart and in the cleanness of my hands I have done this."

**6** And God said to him in the dream, "I also know that in the integrity of your heart you did this. And I also held you back from sinning against Me. Therefore I did not let you touch her.

**7** And now, return the man's wife, for he is a prophet, and he will pray for you, and live. And if you do not return her, know that dying you shall die — you and all that is yours."

**8** And Abimelech rose early in the morning and called all his servants, and spoke all these words in their ears, and the men were very afraid.

**9** And Abimelech called Abraham and said to him, "What have you done to us? And how have I sinned against you, that you have brought upon me and upon my kingdom a great sin? Deeds that are not done, you have done with me."

**10** And Abimelech said to Abraham, "What did you see, that you did this thing?"

**11** And Abraham said, "Because I said, 'Surely there is no fear of God in this place, and they will kill me over the matter of my wife.'

**12** And also, truly, she is my sister — the daughter of my father, but not the daughter of my mother — and she became my wife.

**13** And it came to pass, when God made me wander from my father's house, that I said to her, 'This is your kindness that you shall do with me: at every place where we come, say of me, He is my brother.'"

**14** And Abimelech took flocks and cattle and male servants and female servants, and gave them to Abraham, and he returned to him Sarah his wife.

**15** And Abimelech said, "Here is my land before you. Settle where it is good in your eyes."

**16** And to Sarah he said, "Here — I have given a thousand of silver to your brother. Here, it is for you a covering of eyes, for all who are with you; and with everyone, you are cleared."

**17** And Abraham prayed to God, and God healed Abimelech, and his wife, and his slave women, and they bore children.

**18** For the LORD had closed up, closing up, every womb of the house of Abimelech, over the matter of Sarah, Abraham's wife.

## Notes

- **v3 "you are a dead man"** — *hinnekha met*, literally *here-you-are dead*. Not a threat about the future; a statement of present condition, using the participle. And it is said to a man who has done nothing yet.
- **v3 "owned by an owner"** — *be'ulat ba'al*. *Ba'al* is the ordinary Hebrew word for a husband, and it is also the word for an owner, a master, a possessor. The phrase doubles it — she is *ba'al*-ed by a *ba'al*. English has no way to say that a wife is described with the vocabulary of property, because our word for husband does not carry it.
- **v4 "will You kill even a righteous nation?"** — *ha-goy gam tsaddiq taharog*. Two chapters ago Abraham stood before the LORD and asked whether the Judge of all the earth would sweep away the **righteous** with the wicked. Here a foreign king, with no covenant and no promise, makes the same objection in the same words — *tsaddiq*, righteous — and he is the one who has been wronged.
- **v5 "in the integrity of my heart and in the cleanness of my hands"** — *be-tom levavi u-ve-niqyon kappai*. *Tom* is the noun behind *tamim*, the word God used to Abraham in 17:1: *be whole*. The pagan king claims the quality Abraham was commanded to have, and God confirms it in v6.
- **v7 "he is a prophet"** — *navi*, the first occurrence of the word in the Bible. It is applied to Abraham, by God, in a conversation with a foreigner, to explain why the foreigner should ask Abraham to pray. A word that will carry Isaiah and Jeremiah arrives here as a reason to trust a man who has just lied.
- **v7 "dying you shall die"** — *mot tamut*, the formula from 2:17, the death sentence phrasing. It is spoken to Abimelech, not to Abraham.
- **v9 "Deeds that are not done"** — *ma'asim asher lo ye'asu*, a passive with no agent. Not *you should not have*; rather, *things one does not do*. It is the language of a shared standard, spoken by the man supposed to have no standards.
- **v11 "there is no fear of God in this place"** — Abraham's explanation, delivered to a man who has just risen early, assembled his household, feared greatly, returned the woman, and paid compensation. The chapter has spent ten verses establishing that he was wrong, and then lets him say it.
- **v13 "when God made me wander"** — *hit'u oti Elohim*, and the verb is **plural**, agreeing with *Elohim* as a plural form, which Hebrew usually does not do when the word means the one God. Abraham is speaking to a foreign king and his grammar bends toward the way that king would hear it. The verb itself, *ta'ah*, means to wander or go astray — it is what a lost animal does.
- **v16 "a covering of eyes"** — *kesut einayim*, and nobody knows what it means. A veil? Compensation that covers the offense in the sight of others? The second half of the verse, *ve-et kol ve-nokhachat*, is harder still and its grammar does not resolve. Rendered word for word; the obscurity is in the Hebrew and should not be smoothed away.
- **v16 "to your brother"** — Abimelech says it to her face, about her husband, after being told the truth. It is the one barbed word in a speech that is otherwise generous.
- **v17 "And Abraham prayed"** — *va-yitpallel*, the first prayer of intercession in the Bible. The man who deceived Abimelech is the one who has to ask God to heal him.
- **v18 "the LORD had closed up, closing up, every womb"** — *atsor atsar*, the doubled verb. This is the same verb Sarai used in 16:2 — *the LORD has held me back from bearing*. The chapter closes on wombs being shut and then opened, immediately before the chapter in which Sarah's is opened.
- **The shape** — this is the second time Abraham has passed his wife off as his sister, and the second time a foreign king has been struck for it and has paid him to leave. The first was Pharaoh in 12:10–20. The narrator does not compare them or comment; the story simply happens again, twenty-five years later, after the covenant, after the name change, after the circumcision.
