# Genesis 19

**1** And the two messengers came to Sodom in the evening, and Lot was sitting in the gate of Sodom. And Lot saw, and rose to meet them, and bowed, face to the ground.

**2** And he said, "Look now, my lords — turn aside, please, to your servant's house, and spend the night, and wash your feet. And you shall rise early and go on your way." And they said, "No. We will spend the night in the square."

**3** And he pressed them greatly, and they turned aside to him and came into his house. And he made them a feast, and baked unleavened bread, and they ate.

**4** They had not yet lain down, and the men of the city — the men of Sodom — surrounded the house, from young to old, all the people, from every quarter.

**5** And they called to Lot and said to him, "Where are the men who came to you tonight? Bring them out to us, and let us know them."

**6** And Lot went out to them, to the doorway, and shut the door behind him.

**7** And he said, "Please, my brothers, do not do evil.

**8** Look now — I have two daughters who have not known a man. Let me bring them out to you, and do to them what is good in your eyes. Only to these men do nothing, for this is why they have come under the shadow of my roof-beam."

**9** And they said, "Step aside!" And they said, "This one came to sojourn, and he judges, judging! Now we will do worse to you than to them." And they pressed hard against the man, against Lot, and drew near to break the door.

**10** And the men put out their hand and brought Lot in to them, into the house, and they shut the door.

**11** And the men who were at the opening of the house they struck with blindings, from small to great, and they wore themselves out trying to find the opening.

## Get Out

**12** And the men said to Lot, "Who else do you have here — a son-in-law, and your sons, and your daughters, and everyone you have in the city? Bring them out from the place.

**13** For we are ruining this place, because their outcry has grown great before the face of the LORD, and the LORD has sent us to ruin it."

**14** And Lot went out and spoke to his sons-in-law, the takers of his daughters, and said, "Rise, get out from this place, for the LORD is ruining the city." And he was like one making a joke in the eyes of his sons-in-law.

**15** And as the dawn came up, the messengers urged Lot, saying, "Rise, take your wife and your two daughters who are found here, lest you be swept away in the guilt of the city."

**16** And he lingered. And the men seized his hand, and the hand of his wife, and the hand of his two daughters — in the LORD's pity on him — and they brought him out and set him down outside the city.

**17** And it came to pass, as they brought them outside, that he said, "Escape, for your life! Do not look behind you, and do not stand still anywhere in the plain. Escape to the hill country, lest you be swept away."

**18** And Lot said to them, "Not so, please, my lord.

**19** Look now — your servant has found favor in your eyes, and you have made great your kindness that you did with me, to keep my soul alive. But I cannot escape to the hill country, lest the evil overtake me and I die.

**20** Look now — this city is near, to flee there, and it is a little thing. Let me escape there, please — is it not a little thing? — and my soul will live."

**21** And He said to him, "Look, I have lifted your face in this matter also: I will not overturn the city of which you have spoken.

**22** Hurry, escape there, for I cannot do anything until you arrive there." Therefore the name of the city was called Zoar.

**23** The sun had gone out over the earth, and Lot came to Zoar.

**24** And the LORD rained upon Sodom and upon Gomorrah sulfur and fire, from the LORD, from the heavens.

**25** And He overturned those cities, and all the plain, and all the inhabitants of the cities, and what grew from the ground.

**26** And his wife looked back from behind him, and she became a pillar of salt.

**27** And Abraham rose early in the morning, to the place where he had stood before the face of the LORD.

**28** And he looked down over the face of Sodom and Gomorrah, and over all the face of the land of the plain, and he saw — and here, the smoke of the land went up like the smoke of a kiln.

**29** And it came to pass, when God ruined the cities of the plain, that God remembered Abraham, and He sent Lot out from the midst of the overturning, when He overturned the cities in which Lot had settled.

## The Cave

**30** And Lot went up from Zoar and settled in the hill country, and his two daughters with him, for he was afraid to settle in Zoar. And he settled in the cave, he and his two daughters.

**31** And the firstborn said to the younger, "Our father is old, and there is no man in the earth to come to us according to the way of all the earth.

**32** Come, let us give our father wine to drink, and let us lie with him, and let us keep seed alive from our father."

**33** And they gave their father wine to drink that night, and the firstborn came and lay with her father. And he did not know when she lay down or when she rose.

**34** And it came to pass on the next day that the firstborn said to the younger, "Look — I lay last night with my father. Let us give him wine to drink tonight also, and you go in, lie with him, and let us keep seed alive from our father."

**35** And they gave their father wine to drink that night also, and the younger rose and lay with him. And he did not know when she lay down or when she rose.

**36** And the two daughters of Lot conceived by their father.

**37** And the firstborn bore a son, and she called his name Moab. He is the father of Moab to this day.

**38** And the younger, she also bore a son, and she called his name Ben-ammi. He is the father of the sons of Ammon to this day.

## Notes

- **v1 "the two messengers"** — three men came to Abraham's tent in chapter 18; two arrive here. The third is not accounted for, and the text does not remark on it.
- **vv1–3 against 18:1–8** — read the two receptions side by side. Abraham *ran*; Lot *rose*. Abraham bowed to the ground; Lot bowed face to the ground. Abraham's guests accepted at once; Lot's refuse, and he has to press them. Abraham killed a calf and baked leavened loaves; Lot bakes *matsot*, flat bread, the quick kind. The scene is the same scene, one degree colder at every step, and Lot is sitting in the city gate rather than at a tent door.
- **v5 "let us know them"** — *ve-ned'ah otam*. Everywhere else in this book, *yada* is the word for intimacy that produces a child and a name — Adam and Eve, Cain and his wife (see the note at 4:1) — while *shakhav*, to lie with, is the word for the act when it is coerced or illicit. Here a mob demanding to assault two strangers uses the **knowing** word. Lot's answer in v8, daughters *who have not known a man*, uses it in its ordinary sense and settles what they meant. Whether the mob's choice of word is the narrator's irony or simply the idiom is not something the verse says; what can be said is that it is the wrong word for what they are asking.
- **v8** — Lot offers his two virgin daughters to a mob to protect two guests. The text reports it without a word of comment, as it reported Abraham handing Sarai to Pharaoh. Judgment is withheld here in the same way, and the chapter's last scene is what his daughters do to him.
- **v9 "he judges, judging"** — *va-yishpot shaphot*, the doubled verb for emphasis. They are mocking him for playing judge, and the reader has just come from chapter 18, where Abraham asked whether the **Judge** of all the earth would do justice. The same root, in a sneer, one chapter later.
- **v11 "blindings"** — *sanverim*, a rare word that appears only here and in one other episode in the Hebrew Bible. Not the ordinary word for blind. Whatever it names, the men keep groping for the door, so they are not simply sightless — they cannot find what is in front of them.
- **vv13, 14, 29 "ruining"** — *shachat*, four times in this chapter. It is the flood verb from 6:11–13, where the earth was ruined and God said *I am ruining them*. Sodom is the second time the word is used at full strength, and 13:10 warned the reader with it before Lot ever moved there.
- **v14 "like one making a joke"** — *ki-metsacheq*, from *tsachaq*, to laugh. The same root as Abraham's laugh in 17:17, Sarah's in 18:12, and the name Isaac. Here the laughter is at a man warning of death, and the sons-in-law stay.
- **v16 "And he lingered"** — *va-yitmahmah*, one word, and it is the whole character. Then the messengers take him by the hand and carry him out. He is rescued while resisting.
- **v17, 15 "swept away"** — *tissapheh*, the same verb Abraham used in 18:23: *will you really sweep away the righteous with the wicked?* The word from the bargain comes back as a warning to the one man the bargain saved.
- **v20 "it is a little thing… is it not a little thing?"** — *mits'ar*, twice, and the city is then named *Tso'ar*, Little, in v22. He is given the mountain and negotiates his way down to a town, and the town is named for his argument.
- **vv24–25 "rained… overturned"** — *himtir*, the rain verb from the flood, and *haphakh*, to overturn, turn upside down. *Haphakh* and its noun *haphekhah* run through vv21, 25, 29 — four times. This is not burning; it is **inversion**. The word will become the standing biblical name for what happened to these cities.
- **v26 "his wife looked back"** — *va-tabbet*, the verb for gazing, looking intently — the same one used in 15:5 when Abram was told to *look* at the stars. She is not named, here or anywhere.
- **v29 "God remembered Abraham"** — *va-yizkor Elohim et Avraham*, the identical construction as 8:1, *and God remembered Noah*. Both come at the turn of a catastrophe, and both mean that something is now going to happen. Note who is remembered: not Lot. Lot is saved because of a conversation he was not present for.
- **v30 "for he was afraid to settle in Zoar"** — he argued for the town in v20 and will not live in it. Three verses of negotiation, and then he goes up the mountain he had refused.
- **vv31–36 the wine and the cave** — the last man to be made drunk and uncovered in this book was Noah in 9:21, also after a world was destroyed, also with children involved and a curse following. The pattern repeats with the roles reversed: there the son saw the father's nakedness, here the daughters take it.
- **v33, 35 "he did not know"** — the two verbs of this chapter arrive in one sentence. *Va-tishkav*, she **lay** with her father — the word Genesis reserves for the act when it is a violation. And *ve-lo yada*, he did not **know** — the word Genesis reserves for the act when it makes a family. Lot lay and did not know. The verse puts the two side by side and negates the second one.
- **v33 "when she lay down or when she rose"** — the Masoretes placed a dot over a letter in *u-ve-qumah*, "and when she rose," in this verse — and **not** in v35, where the identical phrase describes the younger daughter. Both verses sit four lines apart with the same words, and the mark is on only one of them. As with the other dotted words, the scribes flagged it and changed nothing.
- **vv37–38 "Moab… Ben-ammi"** — the names are given plainly: *Mo-av* sounds like *from father*, and *Ben-ammi* means *son of my people*. Two nations that will spend centuries at war with Israel are introduced here, with their origins stated and no verdict attached. The chapter ends *to this day*.
