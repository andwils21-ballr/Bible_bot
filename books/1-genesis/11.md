# Genesis 11

**1** And all the earth was one lip and one set of words.

**2** And it came to pass, as they journeyed from the east, that they found a valley in the land of Shinar, and they settled there.

**3** And they said, each to his neighbor, "Come, let us brick bricks, and burn them to a burning." And the brick was stone to them, and tar was their mortar.

**4** And they said, "Come, let us build for ourselves a city, and a tower with its head in the heavens. And let us make for ourselves a name, or we will be scattered over the face of all the earth."

**5** And the LORD came down to see the city and the tower that the sons of man had built.

**6** And the LORD said, "Look — one people, and one lip for all of them, and this is what they begin to do. And now nothing will be held back from them, all that they scheme to do.

**7** Come, let us go down, and let us confuse their lip there, so that they will not hear, each the lip of his neighbor."

**8** And the LORD scattered them from there over the face of all the earth, and they left off building the city.

**9** Therefore its name was called Babel, for there the LORD confused the lip of all the earth, and from there the LORD scattered them over the face of all the earth.

## The Begettings of Shem

**10** These are the begettings of Shem. Shem was a hundred years old, and he fathered Arpachshad, two years after the flood.

**11** And Shem lived after he fathered Arpachshad five hundred years, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**12** And Arpachshad lived thirty-five years, and he fathered Shelah.

**13** And Arpachshad lived after he fathered Shelah four hundred and three years, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**14** And Shelah lived thirty years, and he fathered Eber.

**15** And Shelah lived after he fathered Eber four hundred and three years, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**16** And Eber lived thirty-four years, and he fathered Peleg.

**17** And Eber lived after he fathered Peleg four hundred and thirty years, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**18** And Peleg lived thirty years, and he fathered Reu.

**19** And Peleg lived after he fathered Reu two hundred and nine years, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**20** And Reu lived thirty-two years, and he fathered Serug.

**21** And Reu lived after he fathered Serug two hundred and seven years, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**22** And Serug lived thirty years, and he fathered Nahor.

**23** And Serug lived after he fathered Nahor two hundred years, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**24** And Nahor lived twenty-nine years, and he fathered Terah.

**25** And Nahor lived after he fathered Terah a hundred and nineteen years, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**26** And Terah lived seventy years, and he fathered Abram, Nahor, and Haran.

## The Begettings of Terah

**27** And these are the begettings of Terah. Terah fathered Abram, Nahor, and Haran; and Haran fathered Lot.

**28** And Haran died before the face of Terah his father, in the land of his kindred, in Ur of the Chaldeans.

**29** And Abram and Nahor took wives for themselves. The name of Abram's wife was Sarai, and the name of Nahor's wife was Milcah, daughter of Haran, the father of Milcah and the father of Iscah.

**30** And Sarai was barren. She had no child.

**31** And Terah took Abram his son, and Lot son of Haran, his son's son, and Sarai his daughter-in-law, the wife of Abram his son, and they went out with them from Ur of the Chaldeans to go to the land of Canaan. And they came as far as Harran, and they settled there.

**32** And the days of Terah were two hundred and five years, and Terah died in Harran.

## Notes

- **v1 "one lip and one set of words"** — *saphah echat u-devarim achadim*. *Saphah* is the lip, the body part; Hebrew names a language by the thing it is spoken with. English "language" comes from Latin *lingua*, the tongue, so we do the same and have stopped hearing it. Kept literal throughout this chapter because the word recurs five times and the story is about mouths.
- **v3 "let us brick bricks, and burn them to a burning"** — *nilbenah levenim… ve-nisrephah li-srephah*. Twice the verb takes its own noun as its object, which is a construction Hebrew uses for emphasis and which English can only imitate clumsily. The clumsiness is the point: the sentence is people talking themselves into work. And then the same line puns twice more — *ha-levenah le-aven*, the brick became their stone, and *ha-chemar… la-chomer*, the bitumen their mortar. Four sound-pairs in one verse. The building project is made of wordplay before it is made of brick.
- **v4 "let us make for ourselves a name"** — *na'aseh lanu shem*. This phrase has a history in the book. In 6:4 the mighty ones before the flood were *anshei ha-shem*, the men of the name. Here men decide to make one for themselves. And at the top of the very next chapter, God says to Abram *I will make your name great* — the same thing, given rather than seized, three verses after this story ends. The tower and the call are set side by side on purpose.
- **v4 "lest we be scattered"** — *pen naphuts*. In v8, *va-yaphets YHWH otam*, the LORD scattered them. The thing they build the tower to prevent is exactly what happens, and the text uses the same root for the fear and the outcome.
- **v5 "the LORD came down to see"** — the tower's head is in the heavens, and God has to come down to look at it. Nothing in the verse mocks them; it simply reports the descent, and lets the distance speak.
- **v7 "Come, let us go down"** — *havah neredah*, answering their *havah nilbenah* (v3) and *havah nivneh* (v4). Three times someone says *havah*, "come now" — twice by the builders, once from above. The reply is in their own words.
- **v7 "so that they will not hear"** — *asher lo yishme'u*, hear. Not "understand." The confusion is at the level of sound: the noise still arrives, and nothing comes of it.
- **v9 "Babel… confused"** — *Babel… balal*. In Akkadian, the language of the city itself, *Bab-ilu* means *gate of god*, and that is what its own people understood the name to mean. Hebrew hears something else in it — *balal*, to mix, to confound — and the verse builds the whole naming on that hearing. It is not an etymology; it is a rival one.
- **vv10–26 "and he fathered sons and daughters"** — compare chapter 5, where the identical formula ran with one addition: *and he died*, eight times, like a drum. Here that clause is **gone**. Nine generations pass and the text does not say that any of them died. The first death recorded after the flood is Haran's in v28, and the first completed lifespan is Terah's in v32, the last verse before Abram is called.
- **The numbers** — Shem lives 600 years all told; by Peleg it is 239, by Nahor 148, by Terah 205. The lifespans are collapsing across the chapter, and the collapse lands exactly where the story stops being about the world and starts being about one family.
- **v28 "died before the face of Terah his father"** — *al penei Terach aviv*. The first time in the Bible a son dies while his father watches. It is stated in eight words and not commented on.
- **v30 "And Sarai was barren. She had no child."** — two short clauses saying the same thing twice, which Hebrew narrative almost never does without reason. Everything that follows for the next ten chapters is this sentence.
- **v31 "Harran"** — a different word from *Haran* the son in v27. The man is spelled with an initial *he*; the town is spelled with an initial *chet*. They are unrelated words that English collapses into one spelling, so an English reader sees Terah's dead son's name on the town where Terah stops and dies, and hears a resonance that is not in the Hebrew.
- **v31 "to go to the land of Canaan. And they came as far as Harran, and they settled there."** — Terah sets out for Canaan and does not arrive. The journey Abram is called to make in 12:1 was already begun by his father, and abandoned halfway.
- **v13 the man who is not in the Hebrew** — the **Septuagint** puts an extra generation into this list. Between Arphaxad and Shelah it inserts **Kainan**, who lived a hundred and thirty years and fathered Shelah. He is not in the Hebrew of this chapter, not in the Hebrew of 1 Chronicles 1, and he is not anywhere in the Hebrew Bible. And **Luke 3:36**, tracing the ancestry of Jesus, reads *τοῦ Καϊνὰμ τοῦ Ἀρφαξάδ* — **of Cainan, of Arphaxad**. Luke follows the Greek against the Hebrew, and the genealogy of Jesus in the New Testament contains a man the Hebrew scriptures do not have.
- **The family chart** — this chapter introduces Terah's household, and from here on Genesis keeps naming people who are related to each other in ways it never stops to explain. The whole family, all five generations of it, is drawn out at **the end of this book, in the chart called *The House of Terah***. Every name on it is taken from Genesis and nothing is supplied from elsewhere.
