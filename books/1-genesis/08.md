# Genesis 8

**1** And God remembered Noah, and every living thing and every beast that was with him in the ark. And God made a wind pass over the earth, and the waters grew quiet.

**2** And the fountains of the deep were stopped up, and the lattices of the heavens, and the rain from the heavens was shut in.

**3** And the waters returned from upon the earth, going and returning; and the waters diminished at the end of a hundred and fifty days.

**4** And the ark rested, in the seventh month, on the seventeenth day of the month, upon the mountains of Ararat.

**5** And the waters went on going and diminishing until the tenth month; in the tenth, on the first of the month, the heads of the mountains were seen.

## The Raven and the Dove

**6** And it came to pass at the end of forty days that Noah opened the window of the ark that he had made,

**7** and he sent out the raven; and it went out, going out and returning, until the waters dried up from upon the earth.

**8** And he sent out the dove from him, to see whether the waters had lightened from upon the face of the ground.

**9** But the dove found no resting place for the sole of her foot, and she returned to him, to the ark, for waters were upon the face of all the earth. And he put out his hand and took her and brought her to himself, into the ark.

**10** And he waited yet another seven days, and again he sent out the dove from the ark.

**11** And the dove came to him at evening time, and look — an olive leaf, freshly plucked, in her mouth. And Noah knew that the waters had lightened from upon the earth.

**12** And he waited yet another seven days, and he sent out the dove, and she did not return to him again.

**13** And it came to pass in the six hundred and first year, in the first month, on the first of the month, that the waters dried from upon the earth. And Noah removed the covering of the ark and looked, and here — the face of the ground was dry.

**14** And in the second month, on the twenty-seventh day of the month, the earth was dried out.

## Come Out

**15** And God spoke to Noah, saying,

**16** "Go out from the ark — you, and your wife, and your sons, and your sons' wives with you.

**17** Every living thing that is with you, from all flesh, in the bird and in the beasts and in every creeping thing that creeps upon the earth — bring out with you, and let them swarm in the earth and be fruitful and multiply upon the earth."

**18** And Noah went out, and his sons, and his wife, and his sons' wives with him.

**19** Every living thing, every creeping thing, and every bird — everything that creeps upon the earth, by their families — went out from the ark.

**20** And Noah built an altar to the LORD, and he took from every clean beast and from every clean bird, and he sent up burnt offerings on the altar.

**21** And the LORD smelled the quieting smell, and the LORD said to His heart, "I will not go on cursing the ground again on account of the human, for the shaping of the human heart is evil from his youth. And I will not go on striking every living thing again, as I have done.

**22** From now on, all the days of the earth —  
seed and harvest,  
and cold and heat,  
and summer and winter,  
and day and night —  
shall not cease."

## Notes

- **v1 "God remembered"** — *va-yizkor*. Hebrew *zakhar* is not the recovery of something forgotten; it is turning toward and acting on an obligation. When God "remembers," something happens next. The word is the hinge of the flood story, and everything after this verse runs backwards toward dry land.
- **v1 "God made a wind pass over the earth"** — *va-ya'aver Elohim ruach al ha-aretz*. Go back to 1:2: *the breath of God was hovering over the face of the waters*. The same word, *ruach*, over the same waters, at the moment the world begins again. Chapter 7 unmade the second day of creation; this verse restarts at the second verse of the book. The story is not a rescue narrative with a creation echo bolted on — it is creation, run in reverse and then forward.
- **v4 "the ark rested"** — *va-tanach ha-tevah*, from the root *nuach*, to rest. Which is Noah's name. The vessel *Noahs* onto the mountain. This is the first of four times in this chapter that his name's root surfaces in ordinary words, and no English reader can hear any of them.
- **v4 "the mountains of Ararat"** — plural, *harei Ararat*. A range or a region, not a named peak. "Mount Ararat" is a later identification; the Hebrew does not name a mountain.
- **v7 "going out and returning"** — *yatso va-shov*, the same doubled construction used of the waters in v3, *halokh va-shov*, going and returning. The raven flies the way the water moves.
- **v9 "no resting place for the sole of her foot"** — *manoach*, and there is the root again — the same *nuach* as v4 and as Noah. The bird cannot find a *manoach*; the ark has already found its rest. The dove is looking for what the man is named for.
- **v11 "freshly plucked"** — *taraph*. The verb normally means to tear, and is used of a predator tearing prey. Here it describes a leaf torn from a tree — which tells Noah what he needs to know, because a leaf that had to be torn off was growing.
- **vv13–14 "dried… was dried out"** — two different verbs. *Charvu* in v13 is the water drying off the surface; *yavshah* in v14 is the ground itself becoming dry. English tends to print "dried" twice. The Hebrew marks two stages, fifty-seven days apart.
- **v20 "sent up burnt offerings"** — *va-ya'al 'olot*. The noun *'olah* is from the verb *'alah*, to go up: a burnt offering is literally a *going-up*, named for the smoke. The English "burnt offering" names the fire; the Hebrew names the ascent.
- **v21 "the quieting smell"** — *reach ha-nichoach*. And there is the root a fourth time. *Nichoach* is from *nuach*, the same root as the ark resting, the dove's resting place, and the man's name. English prints "a pleasing aroma" or "a sweet savor," which is the sense, but the word is built from *rest*. The smoke quiets something. In a chapter where God has just remembered, the man named Rest makes a smell of resting rise, and the sentence that follows is the promise never to do this again.
- **v21 "said to His heart"** — *el libbo*. In 6:6, before the flood, *it grieved Him to His heart* — *el libbo*, the identical phrase. The same heart that was grieved into destroying now speaks to itself and stops.
- **v21 "for the shaping of the human heart is evil from his youth"** — this is the most important sentence in the flood story, and it is easy to miss. In 6:5 the reason given for the flood was that *every shaping of the thoughts of his heart was only evil all the day* — *yetser*, shaping, the same word. Here that same fact is given as the reason for **never doing it again**. Nothing about humankind has changed. The identical diagnosis appears once as grounds for destruction and once as grounds for mercy, and the only thing between them is that God remembered.
- **v22** — set as lines because the Hebrew is four balanced pairs: seed and harvest, cold and heat, summer and winter, day and night. The last pair is quoted straight out of the first chapter, where God separated them.
