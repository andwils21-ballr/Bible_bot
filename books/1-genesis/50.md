# Genesis 50

**1** Joseph fell on his father's face, and wept over him, and kissed him.

**2** Joseph commanded his servants, the physicians, to embalm his father. The physicians embalmed Israel.

**3** Forty days were filled for him, for so are the days of embalming filled. Egypt wept for him seventy days.

**4** The days of weeping for him passed, and Joseph spoke to the house of Pharaoh, saying, "If I have found favor in your eyes, speak, please, in Pharaoh's ears, saying,

**5** 'My father made me swear, saying, Here, I am dying. In my grave that I dug for myself in the land of Canaan, there you shall bury me. And now let me go up, please, and bury my father, and I will return.'"

**6** Pharaoh said, "Go up and bury your father, as he made you swear."

**7** Joseph went up to bury his father, and all Pharaoh's servants went up with him — the elders of his house, and all the elders of the land of Egypt —

**8** and all of Joseph's house, and his brothers, and his father's house. Only their little ones and their flocks and their herds they left in the land of Goshen.

**9** There went up with him both chariots and horsemen, and the camp was very heavy.

**10** They came to the threshing floor of the Bramble, which is across the Jordan, and they wailed there a great and very heavy wailing. He made mourning for his father seven days.

**11** The inhabitants of the land, the Canaanites, saw the mourning at the threshing floor of the Bramble, and they said, "This is a heavy mourning for Egypt." Therefore its name was called Abel-mizraim, which is across the Jordan.

**12** His sons did for him just as he had commanded them.

**13** His sons carried him to the land of Canaan and buried him in the cave of the field of Machpelah, which Abraham bought — the field — as a holding for burial, from Ephron the Hittite, facing Mamre.

**14** Joseph returned to Egypt, he and his brothers and all who had gone up with him to bury his father, after he had buried his father.

## What If Joseph Hates Us

**15** Joseph's brothers saw that their father was dead, and they said, "What if Joseph hates us, and pays us back in full for all the evil we dealt him?"

**16** They sent word to Joseph, saying, "Your father commanded before his death, saying,

**17** 'So you shall say to Joseph: Please — lift the offense of your brothers and their sin, for they dealt you evil.' And now, lift, please, the offense of the servants of the God of your father." Joseph wept when they spoke to him.

**18** His brothers also went and fell before him and said, "Here we are, your slaves."

**19** Joseph said to them, "Do not be afraid. Am I in the place of God?

**20** You — you planned evil against me. God planned it for good, so as to do what is being done this day, to keep a great people alive.

**21** And now, do not be afraid. I myself will sustain you and your little ones." He comforted them and spoke to their heart.

## Carry My Bones Up From Here

**22** Joseph dwelt in Egypt, he and his father's house. Joseph lived a hundred and ten years.

**23** Joseph saw Ephraim's sons to the third generation. Also the sons of Machir son of Manasseh were born on Joseph's knees.

**24** Joseph said to his brothers, "I am dying. God will surely visit you, and bring you up from this land to the land He swore to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob."

**25** Joseph made the sons of Israel swear, saying, "God will surely visit you, and you shall carry my bones up from here."

**26** Joseph died, a son of a hundred and ten years. They embalmed him, and he was put in the coffin in Egypt.

## Notes

- **vv2, 3, 26 "embalm"** — *chanat*, and the word occurs four times in Genesis, all four in this chapter. Nowhere else in the Bible is a body treated this way. Israel buries; Egypt preserves. Jacob and Joseph are the only two people in Scripture embalmed, and both are embalmed in Egypt, and the book ends before either practice is ever repeated.
- **v3 "forty days... seventy days"** — two numbers, two customs. Forty is the embalming; seventy is how long **Egypt** weeps. The country mourns the foreign shepherd's father for ten weeks, and the text gives the figure without remark.
- **v4 "speak, please, in Pharaoh's ears"** — Joseph does not go himself. The man who stood before Pharaoh at thirty and was given the signet ring now asks the household to carry a message for him. The text gives the fact and no reason for it.
- **v5 "in my grave that I dug for myself"** — *kariti*, and the verb is ordinarily to dig. But 49:30 says plainly that **Abraham** bought that cave from Ephron, and *karah* also carries the sense of acquiring by purchase elsewhere in the Bible. Either Jacob is claiming a digging the book never mentions, or he means he got it for himself. The word allows both and the chapter does not choose.
- **vv8, 9 what they take and what they leave** — chariots and horsemen go up; the little ones and the flocks stay in Goshen. Nothing explains it here. In Exodus 10:24 Pharaoh will make the same division and get it backwards — *only your flocks and your herds shall be left; your little ones may go too* — and Moses refuses. The two verses are four hundred years and one book apart.
- **v10 "the threshing floor of the Bramble"** — *goren ha-atad*. *Atad* is the thorn-bush, and a threshing floor is the flat open ground where grain is beaten out. It is an odd place name and an odd place to stop — a working floor, named for a thorn, on the far side of the Jordan, and the great mourning happens there rather than at the tomb.
- **v11 "Abel-mizraim"** — the Canaanites watch, and what they see is Egypt: *this is a heavy mourning **for Egypt***. The name they give the place, *Avel Mitsrayim*, turns on a sound — *evel* is mourning, and *avel* is a meadow — so the name reads as *mourning of Egypt* and as *meadow of Egypt* at once. Jacob is carried home by what looks from a distance like an Egyptian state funeral, and the locals name the ground after the wrong country.
- **v15 "What if Joseph hates us"** — *yistemenu*, from *satam*, and it is the verb of 49:23, where Jacob's blessing said *the masters of arrows **hated** him*. Their father has been dead one verse. Seventeen years in Egypt under Joseph's protection have not convinced them, and the thing they have been waiting for is the funeral.
- **vv16–17 the message their father never sent** — they claim Jacob left instructions before he died. Chapter 49 records his last words to all twelve sons in full, and this is not among them, and nothing anywhere in the book reports him saying it. They may be quoting something unrecorded. They may be inventing a dead man's wish to save their lives. The narrator supplies no verdict, and Joseph's only response to the message is to weep.
- **v17 "lift the offense"** — *sa na pesha*, and the verb is *nasa*, to lift or carry. Hebrew's word for forgiving is picking a thing up and carrying it away, which means somebody is still holding it. *Pesha* is not an accident or a lapse; it is rebellion, a deliberate breach.
- **v18 "Here we are, your slaves"** — *hinnennu lekha la-avadim*. This is the dream of 37:7 arriving in full: eleven brothers on the ground in front of him, saying the word out loud. They sold him into slavery for twenty pieces of silver and now offer themselves as the same thing. He refuses it in the next breath.
- **v19 "Am I in the place of God?"** — *ha-tachat Elohim ani*, four words, and they are his **father's** words. Jacob said exactly this to Rachel in 30:2, shouting, when she demanded children: *am I in the place of God, who has withheld from you the fruit of the womb?* The son says the same sentence to refuse a power that is actually being handed to him.
- **v20 "you planned evil... God planned it for good"** — *chashavtem* and *chashavah*, the same verb twice, from *chashav*, to reckon, devise, work something out. Not two events. One act, and two intentions running through it, and the text puts them in the same sentence in the same word without explaining how both can be true.
- **v21 "spoke to their heart"** — *va-yedabber al libbam*, the idiom for reassuring speech. Its only other appearance in Genesis is 34:3, where Shechem *spoke to the heart* of Dinah after raping her. The book uses the phrase twice, and this is the second.
- **vv24–25 "God will surely visit you"** — *paqod yifqod*, the verb doubled, and it becomes the password of the Exodus. Moses says it to the elders in Egypt; and Exodus 13:19 quotes this oath back word for word — *Moses took **the bones of Joseph** with him, for he had made the sons of Israel swear, saying, God will surely visit you, and you shall carry my bones up from here*. Joshua 24:32 finishes it: the bones are buried at **Shechem**, in the field Jacob bought from the sons of Hamor for a hundred qesitah — which is Genesis 33:19. It takes four hundred years and two more books, and Joseph ends up in the one piece of ground his father paid for.
- **v26 "and he was put in the coffin in Egypt"** — the last words of Genesis, and the last word is *aron*, a chest. It is the same word as the Ark of the Covenant, and it has appeared only once before in this book. Genesis opens with God making a world and closes with a box in Egypt, with a promise in it.
