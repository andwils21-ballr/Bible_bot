# Genesis 23

**1** And the lives of Sarah were a hundred years and twenty years and seven years — the years of the lives of Sarah.

**2** And Sarah died in Kiriath-arba — that is Hebron — in the land of Canaan. And Abraham came to mourn for Sarah and to weep for her.

**3** And Abraham rose from before the face of his dead, and he spoke to the sons of Heth, saying,

**4** "A sojourner and a settler I am with you. Give me a holding for a grave with you, and let me bury my dead from before my face."

**5** And the sons of Heth answered Abraham, saying to him,

**6** "Hear us, my lord. A prince of God you are among us. In the choicest of our graves, bury your dead. No man among us will withhold his grave from you, from burying your dead."

**7** And Abraham rose and bowed to the people of the land, to the sons of Heth.

**8** And he spoke with them, saying, "If it is with your soul to bury my dead from before my face, hear me, and press for me with Ephron son of Zohar,

**9** and let him give me the cave of Machpelah that is his, which is at the end of his field. For full silver let him give it to me, in your midst, as a holding for a grave."

**10** And Ephron was sitting among the sons of Heth. And Ephron the Hittite answered Abraham in the ears of the sons of Heth, before all who came in at the gate of his city, saying,

**11** "No, my lord — hear me. The field I have given to you, and the cave that is in it, to you I have given it. Before the eyes of the sons of my people I have given it to you. Bury your dead."

**12** And Abraham bowed before the people of the land.

**13** And he spoke to Ephron in the ears of the people of the land, saying, "Only — if you would only hear me. I have given the silver of the field. Take it from me, and let me bury my dead there."

**14** And Ephron answered Abraham, saying to him,

**15** "My lord, hear me. Land of four hundred shekels of silver — between me and you, what is that? So bury your dead."

**16** And Abraham heard Ephron, and Abraham weighed out to Ephron the silver that he had spoken of in the ears of the sons of Heth: four hundred shekels of silver, passing to the merchant.

**17** And the field of Ephron that is in Machpelah, which faces Mamre — the field and the cave that is in it, and every tree that is in the field, that is in all its border round about —

**18** was raised up to Abraham as a purchase, before the eyes of the sons of Heth, before all who came in at the gate of his city.

**19** And after that, Abraham buried Sarah his wife in the cave of the field of Machpelah, facing Mamre — that is Hebron — in the land of Canaan.

**20** And the field was raised up, and the cave that is in it, to Abraham, as a holding for a grave, from the sons of Heth.

## Notes

- **v1 "the lives of Sarah… the years of the lives of Sarah"** — *chayyei Sarah*, the plural of life, twice, opening and closing the verse. Hebrew habitually speaks of a lifespan in the plural, and the verse spends its whole length saying her age and then naming her again. She is the only woman in the Hebrew Bible whose age at death is recorded.
- **v2 "to mourn for Sarah and to weep for her"** — *lispod* is formal, public lament, the wailing of a funeral; *livkot* is simply to cry. The verse gives him both, in that order, and the next verse has him rising and getting to work.
- **v4 "A sojourner and a settler I am with you"** — *ger ve-toshav*. Two words that sit awkwardly together: a *ger* is a resident foreigner with no land rights, a *toshav* is a dweller. Abraham has been promised this entire country four times by now, and he opens negotiations by stating that he owns none of it.
- **v4, 9, 20 "a holding"** — *achuzzah*, from a root meaning to grasp or seize — a possession you have hold of. It is the same word used in 17:8, where God promised him *all the land of Canaan as an everlasting **achuzzah***. The only *achuzzah* he ever gets is a grave, and he pays cash for it.
- **v6 "A prince of God you are among us"** — *nesi Elohim attah be-tokhenu*. High courtesy, and possibly more: *nasi* is a chieftain, and the phrase could be read as *a mighty prince*, Hebrew sometimes using *Elohim* as an intensifier. They honor him and then sell him a field at a stiff price.
- **vv6, 8, 11, 13, 15, 16 "hear me"** — *shema'eni* and its variants run through the chapter six times, and the negotiation is conducted entirely in the language of courtesy. Every party bows, every party defers, and nobody says a direct thing until v15.
- **v11 "The field I have given to you"** — Abraham asked for the **cave** at the end of the field (v9). Ephron offers the **field** — with the cave thrown in. This is not generosity; it is how you turn a sale of a burial plot into a sale of real estate, and it is why the price lands where it does. Abraham's next words go straight past the gift and name the silver.
- **v15 "between me and you, what is that?"** — the whole transaction in one sentence. He names a price, calls it nothing, and does not lower it. Four hundred shekels is a great deal of silver; when Jeremiah buys a field it costs seventeen.
- **v16 "passing to the merchant"** — *over la-socher*, silver at the standard current among traders — weighed, not counted, because coinage does not yet exist. He pays in public, by weight, at merchant standard, in front of witnesses at the city gate. Nothing about this can later be disputed.
- **vv17, 20 "was raised up"** — *va-yaqom*, the verb for standing or rising, used here in the legal sense of a transfer being made to stand — confirmed, established. The same verb that describes Abraham *rising* from beside his dead in v3 is used of the deed.
- **vv17–18** — the boundary description is a legal one: the field, the cave, every tree, all its border round about, before the eyes of the sons of Heth, before all who came in at the gate. It reads like a contract because it is one, and the narrator slows down to record every clause.
- **The shape of the chapter** — Abraham spends twenty verses and four hundred shekels acquiring a grave. It is the only piece of the promised land he ever owns, the promise is not mentioned once, and the chapter ends with the word *Heth*. Everything he was told he would be given, he buys, from the people who already live there.
