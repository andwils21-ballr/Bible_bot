# Genesis 17

**1** And Abram was ninety-nine years old, and the LORD appeared to Abram and said to him, "I am God Almighty. Walk about before Me, and be whole.

**2** And I will set My covenant between Me and you, and I will multiply you beyond measure."

**3** And Abram fell on his face, and God spoke with him, saying,

**4** "As for Me — here is My covenant with you. And you shall become father of a roar of nations.

**5** And your name shall no longer be called Abram, but your name shall be Abraham, for father of a roar of nations I have made you.

**6** And I will make you fruitful beyond measure, and I will make you into nations, and kings shall come out of you.

**7** And I will raise up My covenant between Me and you, and between your seed after you through their generations, for an everlasting covenant — to be God to you, and to your seed after you.

**8** And I will give to you, and to your seed after you, the land where you have lived as a foreigner — all the land of Canaan — as an everlasting holding. And I will be God to them."

## The Sign

**9** And God said to Abraham, "And you — you shall keep My covenant, you and your seed after you through their generations.

**10** This is My covenant that you shall keep, between Me and you and your seed after you: every male among you shall be circumcised.

**11** And you shall circumcise the flesh of your foreskin, and it shall be a sign of covenant between Me and you.

**12** And at eight days old every male among you shall be circumcised, through your generations — one born of the house, and one bought with silver from any foreigner who is not of your seed.

**13** Circumcised, he must be circumcised — the one born of your house and the one bought with your silver. And My covenant shall be in your flesh, an everlasting covenant.

**14** And an uncircumcised male, who does not circumcise the flesh of his foreskin — that soul shall be cut off from his people. He has broken My covenant."

## Sarah

**15** And God said to Abraham, "Sarai your wife — you shall not call her name Sarai, for Sarah is her name.

**16** And I will bless her, and also give you a son from her. And I will bless her, and she shall become nations. Kings of peoples shall come from her."

**17** And Abraham fell on his face, and he laughed. And he said in his heart, "Shall a child be born to a man of a hundred years? And shall Sarah, a daughter of ninety years, bear?"

**18** And Abraham said to God, "If only Ishmael might live in Your favor!"

**19** And God said, "No — Sarah your wife is bearing you a son, and you shall call his name Isaac. And I will raise up My covenant with him, for an everlasting covenant, for his seed after him.

**20** And as for Ishmael, I have heard you. Look — I have blessed him, and I will make him fruitful, and I will multiply him beyond measure. Twelve chiefs he shall father, and I will make him into a great nation.

**21** But My covenant I will raise up with Isaac, whom Sarah shall bear to you at this appointed time next year."

**22** And He finished speaking with him, and God went up from upon Abraham.

**23** And Abraham took Ishmael his son, and all born of his house, and all bought with his silver — every male among the men of Abraham's house — and he circumcised the flesh of their foreskin on that very day, as God had spoken with him.

**24** And Abraham was ninety-nine years old when the flesh of his foreskin was circumcised.

**25** And Ishmael his son was thirteen years old when the flesh of his foreskin was circumcised.

**26** On that very day Abraham was circumcised, and Ishmael his son.

**27** And all the men of his house — born of the house and bought with silver from a foreigner — were circumcised with him.

## Notes

- **v1 "God Almighty"** — *El Shaddai*, and nobody knows what *Shaddai* means. It has been tied to a word for mountain, to a word for breast, to a root for destroying; none of these is established. "Almighty" comes down to English through the Greek and Latin translators, who had to pick something. The name appears here for the first time in the Bible, and it is used mostly in the Abraham stories and in Job.
- **v1 "Walk about before Me, and be whole"** — *hithhallekh lefanai ve-heyeh tamim*. *Hithhallekh* is the form used of God walking about in the garden (3:8), of Enoch (5:22, 24) and of Noah (6:9) — the two men who walked *with* God. Abraham is told to walk *before* Him, which is different. And *tamim*, whole, is the word used of Noah in 6:9: unblemished, entire, sound. It is the word for an animal fit to be offered.
- **v2, 6, 20 "exceedingly, exceedingly"** — *bi-me'od me'od*, the intensifier doubled, three times in this chapter. It last appeared doubled in 7:19, where the flood waters *overpowered exceedingly, exceedingly*. The construction is rare and the book saves it.
- **vv4–5 "father of a roar of nations"** — *av hamon goyim*. *Hamon* is not "multitude" in the sense of a count; it is the **noise** a crowd makes — a tumult, a roar, a rumble. Isaiah uses it of the sea. The promise is not that his descendants will be numerous but that they will be loud enough to hear.
- **v5 "Abram… Abraham"** — the name changes by one letter, a *he* inserted into the middle. *Av-ram* is "exalted father," which is what he was called for ninety-nine years while childless. The new name is explained in the verse as *av hamon*, father of a roar — which is a play on the sound rather than a derivation; *Abraham* is not literally built from *hamon*. The text is doing what Lamech did with Noah in 5:29: making the name mean what the moment needs it to mean.
- **v10 "every male among you shall be circumcised"** — the sign of this covenant is not a rainbow set in the sky where God will see it (9:13–16), and not animals cut in half while God passes alone through the pieces (15:17). It is cut into the people themselves, permanently, in the place the next generation comes from. The three covenant signs in this book move steadily closer in: sky, ground, body.
- **v13 "Circumcised, he must be circumcised"** — the infinitive absolute doubling the verb, the same construction as *mot tamut*, "dying you shall die," in 2:17. It is the grammar of a decree.
- **v14 "shall be cut off"** — *ve-nikhretah*, from *karat*, to cut. The same verb as *karat berit*, to **cut** a covenant (15:18). The one who will not be cut is cut off. The pun is in the Hebrew and is exact.
- **v15 "Sarai… Sarah"** — her name changes too, and hers is the only woman's name changed by God in the Hebrew Bible. Both forms appear to mean princess or noblewoman; unlike Abraham's, this change is given no explanation at all. One letter, and the text moves on.
- **v17 "and he laughed"** — *va-yitschaq*. And in v19 the son is named *Yitschaq*, Isaac — *he laughs*. The name is the father's reaction turned into a person. Abraham falls on his face in v3 in reverence and falls on his face again in v17 laughing, and God names the child after the second one.
- **v17 "Shall a child be born to a man of a hundred years?"** — he says this *in his heart*. The laugh is silent, and God answers it in the next verse anyway. When Sarah laughs the same laugh in 18:12, also to herself, she is asked about it directly.
- **v18 "If only Ishmael might live before You"** — Abraham's only request in the chapter, and it is to keep the son he already has rather than receive the one he is promised. *Lu*, "if only," is the particle of wishing for something you do not expect.
- **v21 "But My covenant I will raise up with Isaac"** — the two sons are given different things, one verse apart. To Ishmael in v20: fruitful, multiplied beyond measure, twelve chiefs, a great nation. Four promises, all about **size**. To Isaac in v21: the covenant — which is not a larger version of the same thing. It carries terms the other list does not: land held permanently (v8, with the borders named at 15:18); a relationship, *I will be God to them* (vv7–8); perpetuity, *berit olam*, four times in this chapter; an obligation with a sign that can be broken (vv10–14); and a blessing that runs past the family to *all the nations of the earth* (12:3, 22:18). A great nation is for itself. This one is not.

  And Ishmael receives the sign anyway. Verses 23–26 circumcise him that same day, aged thirteen, and the text makes a point of saying so twice. He carries the mark of a covenant this verse raises up with someone else, and Genesis does not reconcile the two.
- **v20 "Twelve chiefs"** — *nesi'im*, from *nasa*, to lift up: a *nasi* is one raised over a people, the head of a tribe rather than a royal son. Ishmael gets twelve of them, and so does Jacob. The number the chosen line is famous for is given to the son who was sent away, in the same breath.
- **v20 "I have heard you"** — *shema'tikha*, and the boy's name is *Yishma-El*, God hears. The pun is made deliberately: *as for Ishmael, I have heard you.* The same wordplay was made to Hagar in 16:11. Both times, the hearing is for the son who is not the chosen one.
- **v23, 26 "on that very day"** — *be-etsem ha-yom ha-zeh*, the idiom from 7:13, where Noah entered the box. Both times it marks a day on which a thing was done without delay, the moment the word came.
- **The arithmetic** — Abraham is ninety-nine, Ishmael thirteen. Ishmael was born in 16:16 when Abraham was eighty-six. Thirteen silent years pass between the two chapters, and the text says nothing about any of them.
