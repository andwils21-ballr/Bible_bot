# Genesis 27

**1** And it came to pass, when Isaac was old and his eyes were too dim to see, that he called Esau his elder son and said to him, "My son." And he said to him, "Here I am."

**2** And he said, "Look now — I am old. I do not know the day of my death.

**3** And now take up, please, your gear — your quiver and your bow — and go out to the field, and hunt me game.

**4** And make me savory food, such as I have loved, and bring it to me, and let me eat, so that my soul may bless you before I die."

**5** And Rebekah was listening as Isaac spoke to Esau his son. And Esau went to the field to hunt game, to bring it.

**6** And Rebekah said to Jacob her son, "Look — I have heard your father speaking to Esau your brother, saying,

**7** 'Bring me game and make me savory food, and let me eat, and let me bless you before the LORD before my death.'

**8** And now, my son, listen to my voice, to what I am commanding you.

**9** Go, please, to the flock, and take me from there two good kids of the goats, and I will make them into savory food for your father, such as he has loved.

**10** And you shall bring it to your father, and he will eat, so that he may bless you before his death."

**11** And Jacob said to Rebekah his mother, "Look — Esau my brother is a hairy man, and I am a smooth man.

**12** Perhaps my father will feel me, and I shall be in his eyes like a mocker, and I will bring on myself a curse and not a blessing."

**13** And his mother said to him, "Upon me be your curse, my son. Only listen to my voice, and go, take them for me."

**14** And he went and took and brought them to his mother, and his mother made savory food, such as his father had loved.

**15** And Rebekah took the garments of Esau her elder son — the precious ones that were with her in the house — and clothed Jacob her younger son.

**16** And the skins of the kids of the goats she put on his hands, and on the smooth of his neck.

**17** And she gave the savory food and the bread that she had made into the hand of Jacob her son.

## The Touching

**18** And he came to his father and said, "My father." And he said, "Here I am. Who are you, my son?"

**19** And Jacob said to his father, "I am Esau your firstborn. I have done as you spoke to me. Rise, please, sit, and eat from my game, so that your soul may bless me."

**20** And Isaac said to his son, "How is it you found it so quickly, my son?" And he said, "Because the LORD your God made it happen before me."

**21** And Isaac said to Jacob, "Come near, please, and let me feel you, my son — whether you are this, my son Esau, or not."

**22** And Jacob came near to Isaac his father, and he felt him, and he said, "The voice is the voice of Jacob, and the hands are the hands of Esau."

**23** And he did not recognize him, because his hands were hairy like the hands of Esau his brother. And he blessed him.

**24** And he said, "Are you this, my son Esau?" And he said, "I am."

**25** And he said, "Bring it near to me, and let me eat from my son's game, so that my soul may bless you." And he brought it near to him, and he ate; and he brought him wine, and he drank.

**26** And Isaac his father said to him, "Come near, please, and kiss me, my son."

**27** And he came near and kissed him. And he smelled the smell of his garments, and he blessed him, and said,  
"See — the smell of my son  
is like the smell of a field that the LORD has blessed.

**28** And may God give you from the dew of the heavens,  
and from the fat places of the earth,  
and abundance of grain and new wine.

**29** Peoples shall serve you,  
and nations shall bow down to you.  
Be master to your brothers,  
and let your mother's sons bow down to you.  
Those who curse you, cursed;  
and those who bless you, blessed."

## Too Late

**30** And it came to pass, as Isaac finished blessing Jacob — and it came to pass, Jacob had only just gone out from the face of Isaac his father — that Esau his brother came in from his hunting.

**31** And he also made savory food and brought it to his father, and said to his father, "Let my father rise and eat from his son's game, so that your soul may bless me."

**32** And Isaac his father said to him, "Who are you?" And he said, "I am your son, your firstborn, Esau."

**33** And Isaac trembled a very great trembling, and said, "Who then was it that hunted game and brought it to me, and I ate of it all before you came, and I blessed him? He shall indeed be blessed."

**34** When Esau heard the words of his father, he cried out a cry, great and bitter beyond measure, and said to his father, "Bless me — me also, my father!"

**35** And he said, "Your brother came with deceit and took your blessing."

**36** And he said, "Is it because his name is called Jacob that he has heeled me these two times? My birthright he took, and here, now, he has taken my blessing." And he said, "Have you not reserved a blessing for me?"

**37** And Isaac answered and said to Esau, "Look — I have set him as master over you, and all his brothers I have given to him as servants, and with grain and new wine I have sustained him. And for you, then — what can I do, my son?"

**38** And Esau said to his father, "Is there only one blessing to you, my father? Bless me — me also, my father!" And Esau lifted his voice and wept.

**39** And Isaac his father answered and said to him,  
"Look — away from the fat places of the earth shall be your dwelling,  
and away from the dew of the heavens above.

**40** And by your sword you shall live,  
and your brother you shall serve.  
And it shall be, when you break loose,  
that you will tear his yoke from off your neck."

**41** And Esau harbored hatred against Jacob over the blessing with which his father had blessed him. And Esau said in his heart, "Let the days of mourning for my father draw near, and I will kill Jacob my brother."

**42** And the words of Esau her elder son were told to Rebekah. And she sent and called Jacob her younger son and said to him, "Look — Esau your brother is consoling himself concerning you, to kill you.

**43** And now, my son, listen to my voice. Rise, flee for yourself to Laban my brother, to Harran.

**44** And stay with him a few days, until your brother's heat turns away —

**45** until your brother's anger turns from you, and he forgets what you have done to him. Then I will send and take you from there. Why should I be bereaved of both of you in one day?"

**46** And Rebekah said to Isaac, "I am disgusted with my life because of the daughters of Heth. If Jacob takes a wife from the daughters of Heth like these, from the daughters of the land — what is life to me?"

## Notes

- **v1 "his eyes were too dim to see"** — the chapter turns entirely on the senses. Isaac cannot see, so he tests by **hearing** (v22), by **touch** (v21–23), by **taste** (v25), and by **smell** (v27). Four senses, and three of them lie to him. Only hearing gets it right, and he overrules it.
- **v4, 19, 25, 31 "so that my soul may bless you"** — *nafshi*, the throat-word again. The blessing is located in the *nephesh*, and *nephesh* begins as the throat — which is why the whole scene is about food going down it. Isaac must eat before he can bless; the blessing comes up out of the same passage the meal went into.
- **v12 "like a mocker"** — *ki-mta'tea*, from a root meaning to mock or mislead. The same fear that shadows *metsacheq* in 21:9 and 26:8: a word for playing that can turn into a word for deceiving.
- **v13 "Upon me be your curse"** — Rebekah takes the curse onto herself in one clause and moves straight on to the instruction. She is the only person in the chapter who is never uncertain.
- **vv8, 13, 43 "listen to my voice"** — *shema be-qoli*, three times, all from Rebekah. It is the same phrase God used of Abraham in 26:5 (*Abraham listened to My voice*) and commanded Abraham in 21:12 (*listen to her voice*). The formula for obedience to God is what the mother uses to arrange a fraud.
- **v22 "The voice is the voice of Jacob, and the hands are the hands of Esau"** — the most quoted line in the chapter, and in Hebrew it is four words against four: *ha-qol qol Ya'aqov ve-ha-yadayim yedei Esav*. Isaac states the contradiction out loud, in perfect balance, and then blesses anyway. He has the evidence and does not act on it.
- **v24 "Are you this, my son Esau?" And he said, "I am."** — *ani*. One word. It is the plainest lie in the book and the shortest sentence in the chapter.
- **v27 "the smell of a field that the LORD has blessed"** — he is smelling Esau's clothes, which smell of the outdoors because Esau is *a man of the field* (25:27). The one sense that tells him the truth about the garments tells him a lie about the man.
- **vv28–29 against vv39–40** — read them as a pair. Jacob gets *from the dew of the heavens and from the fat places of the earth*; Esau gets **the same two phrases in the same order**, but the preposition *min* can mean *from* in the sense of *out of* or in the sense of *away from*. Esau's blessing is either the same gift or its exact negation, and the Hebrew will not say which. English Bibles split on this verse, and they are not being careless — the ambiguity is real.
- **v33 "trembled a very great trembling"** — *va-yecherad… charadah gedolah ad me'od*, the verb doubled by its own noun and then intensified twice. This is the strongest description of fear in Genesis, and it belongs to the man who has just been deceived by his own son, in the instant he realizes he cannot take it back.
- **v33 "He shall indeed be blessed"** — Isaac does not revoke it. Whatever a blessing is in this book, it is not a wish that can be withdrawn; it has left him and gone somewhere, and he speaks about it as a thing already standing.
- **v34 "cried out a cry, great and bitter"** — *va-yits'aq tse'aqah gedolah u-marah*. *Tse'aqah* is the outcry of someone wronged — the word for the cry of Sodom in 18:20 and for Abel's blood in 4:10. It is the word for a cry that reaches a judge, and here it is Esau's, and no judge answers it.
- **v36 "he has heeled me these two times"** — *va-ya'qeveni*, the verb made from *aqev*, heel, which is what his name is made from (25:26). English cannot do it: *Jacob* would have to become a verb. He was named for grabbing a heel, and his brother uses the name as an accusation twice over.
- **v38 "Is there only one blessing to you, my father?"** — and then he weeps. Hebrews narrative almost never reports weeping from men; this is the second time in four chapters, after Abraham for Sarah.
- **v40 "when you break loose"** — *ka'asher tarid*, and the verb is uncertain. It may mean to grow restless, to roam, to become unruly. Something about not staying still. The prophecy of Edom's eventual freedom rests on a word nobody can pin down.
- **v42 "consoling himself concerning you"** — *mitnachem lekha*, from *nacham*, the root of Noah's name and of God's relenting in 6:6. What consoles Esau is the plan to kill his brother. The comfort-word is put to that use without comment.
- **v45 "Why should I be bereaved of both of you in one day?"** — she foresees that a killing costs two sons: the one dead and the one who must flee or be executed for it. She is right. Jacob leaves in the next chapter and she never sees him again — the text never records them meeting, and her death is never reported.
- **v46 "I am disgusted with my life"** — *qatsti be-chayyai*. Having just arranged the fraud, she goes to her blind husband and gives an entirely different reason for sending Jacob away: Hittite wives. It is true (26:34–35) and it is not why.
