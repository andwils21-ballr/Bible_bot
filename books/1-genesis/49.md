# Genesis 49

**1** And Jacob called to his sons and said, "Gather, and I will tell you what will befall you in the end of days.

**2** Assemble and hear, sons of Jacob,  
and hear Israel your father.

**3** Reuben, you are my firstborn,  
my strength and the first of my vigor,  
surpassing in rank and surpassing in power.

**4** Reckless as water — you shall not surpass,  
for you went up on your father's bed.  
Then you defiled it — he went up on my couch.

**5** Simeon and Levi are brothers;  
instruments of violence are their trade.

**6** Into their council let my soul not come,  
in their assembly let my honor not be joined —  
for in their anger they killed a man,  
and in their pleasure they hamstrung an ox.

**7** Cursed be their anger, for it is strong,  
and their fury, for it is hard.  
I will divide them in Jacob  
and scatter them in Israel.

**8** Judah — you, your brothers shall praise.  
Your hand on the neck of your enemies.  
Your father's sons shall bow down to you.

**9** A lion's cub is Judah.  
From the prey, my son, you have gone up.  
He crouched, he lay down like a lion,  
and like a lioness — who will rouse him?

**10** The staff shall not turn aside from Judah,  
nor the ruler's rod from between his feet,  
until Shiloh comes,  
and to him the obedience of peoples.

**11** Binding his foal to the vine,  
and to the choice vine his donkey's colt,  
he has washed his clothing in wine,  
and his garment in the blood of grapes.

**12** Eyes darker than wine,  
and teeth whiter than milk.

**13** Zebulun shall dwell at the shore of the seas,  
and he shall be a shore for ships,  
and his flank upon Sidon.

**14** Issachar is a bony donkey,  
lying down between the saddlebags.

**15** And he saw that rest was good,  
and that the land was pleasant,  
and he bent his shoulder to bear,  
and became a gang of forced labor.

**16** Dan shall judge his people,  
as one of the tribes of Israel.

**17** Let Dan be a serpent on the road,  
a horned viper on the path,  
biting the horse's heels,  
so his rider falls backward.

**18** For your salvation I have waited, LORD.

**19** Gad — a raiding band shall raid him,  
and he shall raid at their heel.

**20** From Asher, his bread is rich,  
and he shall give a king's delicacies.

**21** Naphtali is a doe let loose,  
giving words of beauty.

**22** A fruitful son is Joseph,  
a fruitful son over a spring,  
branches striding over a wall.

**23** And the archers embittered him and shot,  
and the masters of arrows hated him.

**24** But his bow stayed firm,  
and the arms of his hands were made supple,  
from the hands of the Mighty One of Jacob,  
from there, the Shepherd, the Stone of Israel.

**25** From the God of your father — and may He help you —  
and with the Almighty — and may He bless you:  
blessings of the heavens above,  
blessings of the deep crouching beneath,  
blessings of breasts and womb.

**26** The blessings of your father have prevailed  
over the blessings of my forebears,  
to the boundary of the everlasting hills.  
Let them be on the head of Joseph,  
on the crown of the one set apart from his brothers.

**27** Benjamin is a wolf that tears.  
In the morning he eats the prey,  
and at evening he divides the spoil."

## The Cave

**28** All these are the tribes of Israel, twelve, and this is what their father spoke to them. And he blessed them — each man according to his blessing he blessed them.

**29** And he commanded them and said to them, "I am being gathered to my people. Bury me with my fathers, in the cave that is in the field of Ephron the Hittite,

**30** in the cave that is in the field of Machpelah, which faces Mamre in the land of Canaan, which Abraham bought, the field, from Ephron the Hittite, as a holding for burial.

**31** There they buried Abraham and Sarah his wife. There they buried Isaac and Rebekah his wife. And there I buried Leah.

**32** The purchase of the field and the cave that is in it was from the sons of Heth."

**33** And Jacob finished commanding his sons, and he gathered his feet into the bed, and expired, and was gathered to his people.

## Notes

- **v1 "in the end of days"** — *be-acharit ha-yamim*, and this is its first appearance in the Bible. It becomes the standing phrase of the prophets for the far horizon. Here it is a dying man in Egypt telling twelve sons what is coming, and what he tells them turns out to be mostly about land, war and character.
- **vv3–4 Reuben, and a sentence the father cannot finish to his face** — *kochi ve-reshit **oni***, my strength and the first of my *on*, the same ambiguous word Rachel used naming Ben-oni in 35:18. Then *pachaz ka-mayim*, **reckless as water**, a rare word for froth or instability. And watch the grammar break: *for **you** went up on your father's bed. Then **you** defiled it — **he** went up on my couch.* He is speaking to Reuben, and on the last three words he switches to the third person, as if turning away.
- **vv5–7 Simeon and Levi** — not a blessing. *Instruments of violence are their **mekheroteihem*** — a word that occurs nowhere else and is not recoverable; weapons, plans, and dwellings have all been proposed from the consonants. What is clear is the sentence passed: *I will divide them in Jacob and scatter them in Israel*. Levi receives no territory and is dispersed through the other tribes; Simeon's portion is absorbed inside Judah's. A curse spoken over two sons becomes the map.
- **v6 "they hamstrung an ox"** — singular, and chapter 34 mentions no ox. It says they killed every male in Shechem and drove off the flocks and herds. The poem remembers the day in two images, *they killed a man* and *they hamstrung an ox*, and neither matches the prose exactly.
- **v8 "your brothers shall praise you"** — *yodukha*, from *yadah*, to give thanks or praise — the root Leah named him from in 29:35. And the next line is *yadkha*, **your hand**, on your enemies' neck: *Yehudah*, *yodukha*, *yadkha*, three sounds off one line. Then *your father's sons shall bow down to you* — which is Joseph's dream from 37:7, reassigned.
- **v9 "from the prey, my son, you have gone up"** — *mi-**teref** beni alita*, and *teref* is the noun of *tarof toraf*, **torn, torn**, which is what Jacob cried over the bloodied coat in 37:33 and what Judah quoted back to him in 44:28. The word for the lie Judah helped stage is the word his blessing is built on.
- **v10 "until Shiloh comes"** — the most disputed line in Genesis, and the Hebrew will not settle it. The consonants on the page are שילה and the reading tradition marks שילו, one of the places where written and read were preserved side by side. The candidates all come out of those letters: a place name, *Shiloh*; *shai lo*, **tribute to him**; *she-lo*, **that which is his**. Nothing in the verse decides. What is not in doubt is the frame — a staff that does not depart from Judah, until something or someone arrives.
- **v15 "became a gang of forced labor"** — *va-yehi le-**mas** oved*. *Mas* is the corvée, conscripted state labour, and the word turns up again in Exodus 1:11: *they set over them **chiefs of forced labour**... and he built store-cities for Pharaoh, Pithom and Rameses*. Issachar's blessing is that he saw the land was pleasant and lay down under the load.
- **v17 "a horned viper on the path"** — *shefifon*, a snake-word that occurs once in the Bible and cannot be pinned down further than *some kind of viper*. The image is exact regardless: a small thing on the road that brings down a horse by the heel, so the rider goes over backwards. Dan is the tribe that judges, and his picture is an ambush.
- **v18 "For your salvation I have waited, LORD"** — the poem stops. For one line Jacob is not talking to his sons at all; he turns and addresses God, by name, and then goes straight back to Gad in the next verse. It is the only sentence in the chapter aimed at anyone but the twelve, and nothing prepares it or follows from it.
- **v19 Gad, in sound** — *Gad gedud yegudennu ve-hu yagud aqev*. Four words in the line are built from the same two consonants as his name. English can put *raid* next to *raid* and lose the rest; in Hebrew the whole line rattles.
- **vv24–25 three names for God, all new** — *avir Ya'aqov*, the **Mighty One** of Jacob; *ro'eh even Yisra'el*, the **Shepherd**, the **Stone** of Israel. The shepherd title appeared for the first time in the previous chapter (48:15), out of the same mouth. And the blessings are stacked in a vertical line — *the heavens above*, *the deep crouching beneath*, *breasts and womb*. The deep is *tehom*, the dark water of 1:2. Creation's top, bottom and middle, invoked over one son.
- **v28 "he blessed them"** — the narrator's summary calls all twelve speeches blessings, including *cursed be their anger* and *you shall not surpass*. And *ish asher ke-virkhato*, **each according to his blessing** — meaning the one that fits him. What each son gets is what he is.
- **v31 "And there I buried Leah"** — the last thing he says about his own life. He lists the cave's occupants in pairs, Abraham and Sarah, Isaac and Rebekah, and then puts himself in beside Leah, the wife he did not choose and whose eyes the book would not describe. Rachel is not there. She is on the Ephrath road where he buried her (35:19), and he mentioned it two chapters ago for no reason anyone can see (48:7). He asks to be laid next to the other one.
- **v33 "he gathered his feet into the bed"** — *va-ye'esof raglav el ha-mittah*. His first recorded act as a man was *va-yissa raglav*, he **lifted his feet** and went to the east (29:1). His last is to draw them back in. And the verb of the gathering, *asaf*, is the same one in *was gathered to his people* in the same verse.
