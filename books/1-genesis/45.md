# Genesis 45

**1** Joseph could not hold himself in before all those standing by him, and he called out, "Take every man out from me!" No man stood with him when Joseph made himself known to his brothers.

**2** He gave his voice in weeping, and Egypt heard, and the house of Pharaoh heard.

**3** Joseph said to his brothers, "I am Joseph. Is my father still alive?" His brothers could not answer him, for they were terrified before his face.

**4** Joseph said to his brothers, "Come near to me, please." They came near. He said, "I am Joseph your brother, whom you sold into Egypt.

**5** Now, do not be grieved, and do not let it burn in your eyes that you sold me here — for it was to preserve life that God sent me ahead of you.

**6** For these two years the famine has been within the land, and there are five more years in which there is no plowing and no harvest.

**7** God sent me ahead of you to set for you a remnant in the land, and to keep you alive for a great deliverance.

**8** Now — it was not you who sent me here, but God. He has made me a father to Pharaoh, and lord of all his house, and ruler in all the land of Egypt.

**9** Hurry and go up to my father and say to him, 'So says your son Joseph: God has made me lord of all Egypt. Come down to me, do not stand still.

**10** You shall dwell in the land of Goshen, and you shall be near me — you and your sons and your sons' sons, and your flocks and your herds and all that is yours.

**11** I will sustain you there, for there are five more years of famine — otherwise you will be dispossessed, you and your house and all that is yours.'

**12** Here, your eyes see, and the eyes of my brother Benjamin, that it is my mouth speaking to you.

**13** You shall tell my father of all my weight in Egypt, and all that you have seen. Hurry, and bring my father down here."

**14** He fell on the neck of Benjamin his brother and wept, and Benjamin wept on his neck.

**15** He kissed all his brothers and wept over them, and after that his brothers spoke with him.

## Pharaoh Hears

**16** The sound was heard in Pharaoh's house, saying, "Joseph's brothers have come." It was good in the eyes of Pharaoh and in the eyes of his servants.

**17** Pharaoh said to Joseph, "Say to your brothers, 'Do this: load your beasts and go, come to the land of Canaan.

**18** Take your father and your households and come to me, and I will give you the good of the land of Egypt, and you will eat the fat of the land.'

**19** You are commanded: 'Do this — take wagons for yourselves from the land of Egypt, for your little ones and for your wives, and carry your father, and come.

**20** Let your eye not pity your belongings, for the good of all the land of Egypt is yours.'"

**21** The sons of Israel did so. Joseph gave them wagons, by Pharaoh's mouth, and gave them provisions for the road.

**22** To all of them he gave, to each man, changes of clothing — and to Benjamin he gave three hundred pieces of silver and five changes of clothing.

**23** To his father he sent the like of this: ten donkeys carrying the good of Egypt, and ten she-donkeys carrying grain and bread and food for his father for the road.

**24** He sent his brothers off, and they went, and he said to them, "Do not be stirred up on the road."

**25** They went up from Egypt and came to the land of Canaan, to Jacob their father.

**26** They told him, saying, "Joseph is still alive, and he is ruler in all the land of Egypt." His heart went numb, for he did not believe them.

**27** They spoke to him all Joseph's words that he had spoken to them, and he saw the wagons Joseph had sent to carry him. The spirit of Jacob their father revived.

**28** Israel said, "Enough. Joseph my son is still alive. Let me go and see him before I die."

## Notes

- **v1 "could not hold himself in"** — *ve-lo yakhol Yosef le-hit'appeq*, and the verb is the rare one from 43:31, where he washed his face and **did** hold himself in. Same word, two chapters apart, this time with a *could not* in front of it. Judah's speech is what stands between them.
- **v1 "made himself known"** — *be-hitvadda Yosef el echav*, and the root is *yada*, **to know** — the verb of 4:1 and of the tree of knowing. Three chapters have run on *nakar*, recognizing and refusing to recognize; the recognition itself, when it finally comes, is not *nakar* at all. He makes himself **known**.
- **vv1–2 he clears the room and then everyone hears** — *take every man out from me*, and *no man stood with him*. Then *he gave his voice in weeping, and Egypt heard, and the house of Pharaoh heard*. He arranges privacy and immediately makes it useless. It is the third time he has wept in three chapters (42:24, 43:30), and the first time he does not hide it.
- **v3 "I am Joseph. Is my father still alive?"** — he asked this in 43:27 and was told yes. He asks it again in the same breath as his own name, which means the first answer was given to an Egyptian official and does not count. And *they could not answer him, for they were terrified before his face* — *nivhalu mi-panav*. They have spent three chapters trying to see this face.
- **v4 "whom you sold into Egypt"** — he says it plainly, to their faces, and it is the first time anyone in the book has stated it out loud. In 42:21 they said *we are guilty concerning our brother* without naming what they did. In 40:15 Joseph told the cupbearer he had been **stolen**, not sold. Here the sentence is complete for the first time, and the man saying it is the one it was done to.
- **vv5, 7, 8 "God sent me"** — *shelachani Elohim*, three times in four verses, and the third goes furthest: *it was not you who sent me here, but God*. It does not cancel v4. He has just said *you sold me*, and he does not withdraw it. Both sentences are allowed to stand, and the text never reconciles them.
- **v7 "a great deliverance"** — *lifleitah gedolah*, and *peleitah* is an escape, a remnant that gets out. Jacob used it in 32:9, splitting his camps before meeting Esau: *the camp that is left will be an **escape***. The word his father reached for in terror is the word Joseph uses for what the whole family has become.
- **v8 "a father to Pharaoh"** — *le-av le-Far'oh*, a court title for a senior counselor. The boy who was taken from his father, and who named his firstborn for forgetting *all my father's house* (41:51), is styled father to a king — three verses before he starts begging for his own father to be brought down.
- **v12 "that it is my mouth speaking to you"** — *ki fi ha-medabber aleikhem*. In 42:23 there was an interpreter between them, and they said things in front of him they would never have said to him. He is telling them that the translator is gone, and has been for some time.
- **v15 "and after that his brothers spoke with him"** — they have not said one word since verse 3. Joseph talks for twelve verses, weeps on Benjamin's neck, kisses all of them, and only then do they speak. What they said is not recorded.
- **v22 he does it again, on purpose** — everyone gets a change of clothing; Benjamin gets three hundred pieces of silver and **five**. In 43:34 Benjamin's portion at the table was five times the others'. And the thing that started all of this in 37:3 was a garment given to one son and not the rest. Joseph hands ten older brothers the exact arrangement that got him sold, and then in v24 tells them not to get worked up on the way home.
- **v24 "Do not be stirred up on the road"** — *al tirgezu ba-darekh*, from *ragaz*, to tremble, quake, be agitated — and in some uses, to quarrel. So it reads either as *do not be afraid* or as *do not fight among yourselves*, and the Hebrew does not choose. Given what is in Benjamin's saddlebags, both readings have something to work with.
- **v26 "his heart went numb"** — *va-yafag libbo*, from *pug*, to grow faint or go slack; it is a rare verb and this is its picture of shock. The narrator adds the reason without softening it: *ki lo he'emin lahem*, **for he did not believe them**. His sons tell him the truth and he does not believe them, which is the exact consequence of what they did in 37:32.
- **v27 the wagons are what convince him** — *and he saw the wagons Joseph had sent to carry him*, and only then *the spirit of Jacob their father revived*. Words from these ten men were not enough. Physical evidence sent from Egypt was. The last time they brought him evidence from a distance it was a coat.
- **v28 "Enough"** — *rav*, one word, and the narrator switches his name in the same sentence: *and **Israel** said*. He has been Jacob for two verses of numb disbelief. He becomes Israel when he decides to go.
