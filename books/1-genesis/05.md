# Genesis 5

**1** This is the book of the begettings of Adam. In the day God created humankind, in the likeness of God He made him.

**2** Male and female He created them, and He blessed them and called their name Humankind in the day they were created.

**3** And Adam lived a hundred and thirty years, and he fathered in his likeness, according to his image, and called his name Seth.

**4** And the days of Adam after he fathered Seth were eight hundred years, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**5** And all the days of Adam that he lived were nine hundred and thirty years. And he died.

**6** And Seth lived a hundred and five years, and he fathered Enosh.

**7** And Seth lived after he fathered Enosh eight hundred and seven years, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**8** And all the days of Seth were nine hundred and twelve years. And he died.

**9** And Enosh lived ninety years, and he fathered Kenan.

**10** And Enosh lived after he fathered Kenan eight hundred and fifteen years, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**11** And all the days of Enosh were nine hundred and five years. And he died.

**12** And Kenan lived seventy years, and he fathered Mahalalel.

**13** And Kenan lived after he fathered Mahalalel eight hundred and forty years, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**14** And all the days of Kenan were nine hundred and ten years. And he died.

**15** And Mahalalel lived sixty-five years, and he fathered Jared.

**16** And Mahalalel lived after he fathered Jared eight hundred and thirty years, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**17** And all the days of Mahalalel were eight hundred and ninety-five years. And he died.

**18** And Jared lived a hundred and sixty-two years, and he fathered Enoch.

**19** And Jared lived after he fathered Enoch eight hundred years, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**20** And all the days of Jared were nine hundred and sixty-two years. And he died.

## The One Who Was Not

**21** And Enoch lived sixty-five years, and he fathered Methuselah.

**22** And Enoch walked with God after he fathered Methuselah three hundred years, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**23** And all the days of Enoch were three hundred and sixty-five years.

**24** And Enoch walked with God. And he was not, for God took him.

**25** And Methuselah lived a hundred and eighty-seven years, and he fathered Lamech.

**26** And Methuselah lived after he fathered Lamech seven hundred and eighty-two years, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**27** And all the days of Methuselah were nine hundred and sixty-nine years. And he died.

**28** And Lamech lived a hundred and eighty-two years, and he fathered a son.

**29** And he called his name Noah, saying, "This one will bring us relief from our work and from the pain of our hands, out of the ground which the LORD has cursed."

**30** And Lamech lived after he fathered Noah five hundred and ninety-five years, and he fathered sons and daughters.

**31** And all the days of Lamech were seven hundred and seventy-seven years. And he died.

**32** And Noah was five hundred years old, and Noah fathered Shem, Ham, and Japheth.

## Notes

- **v1 "the book of the begettings"** — *sepher toledot*, the same structuring word as 2:4, now with *sepher*, a written document, in front of it. This is the only place in Genesis that calls a section a book, and it reads like a citation — as though a list already existed and is being copied in.
- **v2 "called their name Humankind"** — *adam*, the same word that has been the man's name since chapter 2, given here to **both** of them together, male and female, as a single name. English prints "Adam" in v3 and "man" or "mankind" in v2, which conceals that it is one word doing both jobs. The species and the individual are named identically, and v2 says the name belongs to the pair.
- **v3 "in his likeness, according to his image"** — *demut* and *tselem*, the two words from 1:26, now used of Adam fathering Seth. What God did in making humankind is what a human does in having a child. The echo is exact and deliberate, and it is the chapter's quietest claim.
- **The repetition** — the formula runs: lived X years, fathered N, lived Y more years, fathered sons and daughters, all his days were Z, **and he died**. Eight times. It is monotonous on purpose; the spec for this project says a genealogy is supposed to be monotonous, and smoothing it out would destroy the effect. Read aloud, the phrase *and he died* lands like a drum. Nothing else in the chapter is trying to happen.
- **v18 "Enoch"** — the second man of this name. The first was Cain's son, four verses into the other genealogy (4:17), and the Hebrew is identical: *Chanokh*. Genesis 5 also has a **Lamech**, and Cain's line had one too. See the note at 4:17–18 for the two lists set side by side — six of the seven names are the same or nearly the same, and the two that match exactly belong to men who are opposites.
- **vv21–24 Enoch** — and then the drum stops. Enoch's entry is the shortest life in the chapter, and its closing formula is missing. Instead of *and he died*, it says *and he was not, for God took him*. The chapter has spent twenty verses training the reader to expect three words, and then withholds them. That is the whole point of the repetition: it exists to be broken once.
- **v22, 24 "walked with God"** — *hithhallekh et-ha-Elohim*, the reflexive form of the verb, the same form used in 3:8 of God walking about in the garden at the breeze of the day. Whatever Adam lost the ability to be near, Enoch is described as doing alongside it.
- **v24 "and he was not"** — *ve-einennu*, a flat negation of existence. Not "he departed," not "he was taken up" — *and he was not*. The taking is stated in the next clause, but the first thing said is simply his absence.
- **v29 "Noah … relief"** — Lamech explains the name from *yenachamenu*, "he will comfort/relieve us," from the root *nacham*. But the name *Noach* comes from a different root, *nuach*, to rest. It is a loose pun, not an etymology, and Hebrew naming does this constantly — the sound is close enough, and the meaning is what the parent wants to say. Noting it because a reader who checks will find the mismatch and wonder whether the translation erred. It did not; the text is punning.
- **v29 "the pain of our hands"** — *'itsavon*, the same word as the sentences in 3:16 and 3:17. Lamech is quoting the curse. And he names his son for the end of it, three chapters before the flood.
- **The ages** — the Hebrew, Greek and Samaritan traditions do not agree on the numbers in this chapter, and the Greek disagreement has a shape. Against the Hebrew, the Septuagint adds **exactly one hundred years** to the age at which a man fathers his son — Adam 130 becomes 230, Seth 105 becomes 205, Enosh 90 becomes 190, Kenan 70 becomes 170, Mahalalel 65 becomes 165, Enoch 65 becomes 165. But **Jared stays at 162 and Methuselah at 187** in both. It is not drift; six entries are moved by the same round number and two are left alone. The figures here follow the Hebrew. That the traditions diverge is a plain fact worth knowing.
