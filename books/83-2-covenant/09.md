# 2nd Book of the Covenant 9

**1** "But My judgment will come on the bishops and the priests, for they have led My people astray for the sake of their own pleasure. Through the prophet Isaiah too I had set it out for them and said: *I have ordered weeping, lamentation, and girding with sackcloth; but they have made merry, slaughtering oxen and sacrificing sheep, and saying, 'Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die.' This has been made known in the ears of the Lord Almighty. Their sin will not be forgiven them until they die.* For the sacrifice of the Lord is a gentle spirit, a gentle and pure heart; this sacrifice the Lord does not despise. *Turn, then, to Me, all of you, and I too will turn to you, says the Lord Almighty.*

**2** Do not become like your fathers, of whom the prophets speak, who ate the manna in the wilderness and did not know the grace of the Lord. It was not known to them, for they longed for meat and were filled according to the evil desire of their hearts. As the Lord has said: *My people have eaten meat and drunk wine; they have grown fat and gross; they have kicked My beloved; the priest and the prophet themselves have raved because of wine.* But the sons of Jonadab son of Rechab never drank wine, and kept the commandments of their fathers.

## Notes

- **v1 "Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die… will not be forgiven them until they die"** — Isaiah 22:12–14: *the Lord GOD of hosts called for weeping and mourning… but instead there was joy and gladness, killing oxen and slaughtering sheep… "Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die." … "Surely this iniquity will not be atoned for you until you die."* The words on the sacrifice the Lord does not despise are Psalm 51:17, *The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit*, where the Ethiopic as Guerrier gives it has *gentle*.
- **v2 "the sons of Jonadab son of Rechab never drank wine"** — the Rechabites of Jeremiah: *We will drink no wine, for Jonadab son of Rechab our father commanded us* (Jeremiah 35:6), held up there, as here, against a people that did not keep its Lord's word.
- **Source text** — L. Guerrier and S. Grébaut, *Le Testament en Galilée de Notre-Seigneur Jésus-Christ*, Patrologia Orientalis 9.3 (1913), public domain. English translation by this project.
