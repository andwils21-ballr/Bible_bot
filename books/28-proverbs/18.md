# Proverbs 18

**1** Whoever isolates himself seeks his own desire;  
he breaks out against all sound wisdom.

**2** A fool takes no pleasure in understanding,  
but only in laying bare his own heart.

**3** When the wicked man comes, contempt comes also,  
and with dishonor comes reproach.

**4** The words of a man's mouth are deep waters;  
the fountain of wisdom is a gushing stream.

**5** It is not good to show favor to the wicked,  
to turn aside the righteous in judgment.

**6** A fool's lips walk into a quarrel,  
and his mouth invites a beating.

**7** A fool's mouth is his ruin,  
and his lips are a snare to his soul.

**8** The words of a whisperer are like delicious morsels;  
they go down into the inner parts of the body.

**9** Whoever is slack in his work  
is a brother to him who destroys.

**10** The name of the LORD is a strong tower;  
the righteous runs into it and is safe.

**11** The rich man's wealth is his strong city,  
and like a high wall in his imagination.

**12** Before destruction a man's heart is haughty,  
but humility comes before honor.

**13** If one gives an answer before he hears,  
it is his folly and shame.

**14** A man's spirit will endure sickness,  
but a crushed spirit, who can bear?

**15** An understanding heart acquires knowledge,  
and the ear of the wise seeks knowledge.

**16** A man's gift makes room for him  
and brings him before the great.

**17** The one who states his case first seems right,  
until his neighbor comes and examines him.

**18** The lot puts an end to quarrels  
and decides between the powerful.

**19** A brother offended is harder to win than a strong city,  
and quarrels are like the bars of a fortress.

**20** From the fruit of a man's mouth his stomach is satisfied;  
he is satisfied by the yield of his lips.

**21** Death and life are in the power of the tongue,  
and those who love it will eat its fruit.

**22** He who finds a wife finds a good thing  
and obtains favor from the LORD.

**23** The poor man pleads for mercy,  
but the rich man answers roughly.

**24** A man of many companions may come to ruin,  
but there is a friend who sticks closer than a brother.

## Notes

- **v1 "Whoever isolates himself"** — the one who cuts himself off from others to follow his own will; the line is hard, and the Greek reads it differently.
- **v8 "like delicious morsels"** — *mitlahamim* occurs only here and in 26:22, where the verse is repeated.
- **vv10–11** — set side by side: the righteous runs into the name of the LORD, the rich man trusts the strong city of his wealth (10:15a, and the English there is followed) — "in his imagination."
- **v12** — the second line is the same as 15:33b.
- **v17 "until his neighbor comes"** — the read form; the written form is a spelling variant.
- **v18 "The lot puts an end to quarrels"** — as in 16:33.
- **v19 "quarrels"** — the read form *midyanim*, as in 6:14; the verse is hard, and the Greek reads "A brother helped by a brother is like a strong city."
- **v22** — the second line is the same as 8:35b, "and obtains favor from the LORD."
- **v24 "A man of many companions may come to ruin"** — *lehitro'ea*, to be broken, a play on *re'im*, "companions."
