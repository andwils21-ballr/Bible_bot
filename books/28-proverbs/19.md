# Proverbs 19

**1** Better is a poor man who walks in his integrity  
than one who is crooked in speech and is a fool.

**2** Desire without knowledge is not good,  
and whoever hurries with his feet misses the way.

**3** A man's folly ruins his way,  
and his heart rages against the LORD.

**4** Wealth adds many friends,  
but the poor man is separated from his friend.

**5** A false witness will not go unpunished,  
and one who breathes out lies will not escape.

**6** Many seek the favor of a noble,  
and everyone is a friend to a man who gives gifts.

**7** All the brothers of a poor man hate him;  
how much more do his friends go far from him!  
He pursues them with words; words are all he has.

**8** Whoever gets sense loves his own soul;  
whoever keeps understanding will find good.

**9** A false witness will not go unpunished,  
and one who breathes out lies will perish.

**10** Luxury is not fitting for a fool,  
still less for a servant to rule over princes.

**11** A man's good sense makes him slow to anger,  
and it is his glory to overlook an offense.

**12** A king's wrath is like the roaring of a lion,  
but his favor is like dew on the grass.

**13** A foolish son is ruin to his father,  
and a wife's quarreling is a continual dripping.

**14** House and wealth are an inheritance from fathers,  
but a prudent wife is from the LORD.

**15** Laziness casts into a deep sleep,  
and a slack soul will go hungry.

**16** Whoever keeps the commandment keeps his life;  
whoever is careless of his ways will die.

**17** Whoever is gracious to the poor lends to the LORD,  
and He will repay him for his deed.

**18** Discipline your son while there is hope,  
and do not set your heart on putting him to death.

**19** A man of great wrath will pay the penalty;  
for if you rescue him, you will have to do it again.

**20** Listen to advice and accept discipline,  
so that you may be wise in your latter end.

**21** Many are the plans in a man's heart,  
but it is the purpose of the LORD that will stand.

**22** What is desired in a man is loyal love,  
and a poor man is better than a liar.

**23** The fear of the LORD leads to life,  
and whoever has it rests satisfied; he will not be visited by harm.

**24** The sluggard buries his hand in the dish  
and will not even bring it back to his mouth.

**25** Strike a scoffer, and the simple will learn shrewdness;  
reprove one who understands, and he will gain knowledge.

**26** He who robs his father and drives away his mother  
is a son who brings shame and disgrace.

**27** Cease, my son, to hear discipline,  
only to stray from the words of knowledge.

**28** A worthless witness mocks at justice,  
and the mouth of the wicked swallows iniquity.

**29** Judgments are ready for scoffers,  
and beatings for the backs of fools.

## Notes

- **vv5, 9** — the same first line, "A false witness will not go unpunished"; the second lines differ, "will not escape" and "will perish."
- **v7** — a three-line saying, rare in this collection. The last line is hard: the read form *lo*, "to him," gives "words are all he has"; the written form *lo*, "not," gives "they are not there."
- **v10** — the form of 17:7, "not fitting… still less."
- **v12** — the first line returns at 20:2a.
- **v16 "will die"** — the read form *yamut*; the written form *yumat*, "will be put to death."
- **v17 "Whoever is gracious to the poor lends to the LORD"** — God takes the debt on Himself (Matthew 25:40).
- **v18 "do not set your heart on putting him to death"** — do not let discipline become cruelty, or: do not leave him uncorrected to his ruin.
- **v19 "A man of great wrath"** — the read form *gedal*; the written form is unclear.
- **v21** — as in 16:9.
- **v24 "The sluggard buries his hand in the dish"** — repeated at 26:15.
