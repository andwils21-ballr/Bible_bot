# Proverbs 13

**1** A wise son hears his father's discipline,  
but a scoffer does not listen to rebuke.

**2** From the fruit of his mouth a man eats good,  
but the desire of the treacherous is violence.

**3** Whoever guards his mouth preserves his life;  
whoever opens wide his lips comes to ruin.

**4** The soul of the sluggard craves and gets nothing,  
but the soul of the diligent is richly supplied.

**5** The righteous hates a lying word,  
but the wicked brings shame and disgrace.

**6** Righteousness guards the one whose way is blameless,  
but wickedness overthrows the sinner.

**7** One pretends to be rich, yet has nothing;  
another pretends to be poor, yet has great wealth.

**8** The ransom of a man's life is his wealth,  
but the poor man hears no threat.

**9** The light of the righteous rejoices,  
but the lamp of the wicked will be put out.

**10** By insolence comes only strife,  
but with those who take advice is wisdom.

**11** Wealth gained from nothing will dwindle,  
but whoever gathers little by little will increase it.

**12** Hope deferred makes the heart sick,  
but a desire fulfilled is a tree of life.

**13** Whoever despises the word brings ruin on himself,  
but whoever fears the commandment will be rewarded.

**14** The teaching of the wise is a fountain of life,  
to turn away from the snares of death.

**15** Good sense wins favor,  
but the way of the treacherous is hard.

**16** Every shrewd man acts with knowledge,  
but a fool flaunts folly.

**17** A wicked messenger falls into trouble,  
but a faithful envoy brings healing.

**18** Poverty and disgrace come to him who ignores discipline,  
but whoever heeds reproof is honored.

**19** A desire fulfilled is sweet to the soul,  
but to turn away from evil is detestable to fools.

**20** Whoever walks with the wise becomes wise,  
but the companion of fools suffers harm.

**21** Disaster pursues sinners,  
but the righteous are repaid with good.

**22** A good man leaves an inheritance to his children's children,  
but the sinner's wealth is laid up for the righteous.

**23** The fallow ground of the poor yields much food,  
but it is swept away through injustice.

**24** Whoever spares his rod hates his son,  
but whoever loves him disciplines him early.

**25** The righteous eats to the satisfying of his soul,  
but the belly of the wicked suffers want.

## Notes

- **v1 "A wise son hears his father's discipline"** — the Hebrew has no verb in the first line, "A wise son — a father's discipline"; "hears" comes from the second line.
- **v8 "the poor man hears no threat"** — the rich man must pay to save his life; the poor man has nothing anyone would threaten him for.
- **v11 "Wealth gained from nothing"** — *mehevel*, "from a breath," wealth with nothing behind it; the Greek reads "gained hastily with lawlessness."
- **vv12, 19 "a desire fulfilled"** — the same phrase twice: in v12 it is a tree of life, in v19 sweet to the soul.
- **v14** — the second line is the same as 14:27b.
- **v15 "the way of the treacherous is hard"** — *eitan*, enduring or hard; the Greek reads "is in ruin."
- **v20 "Whoever walks with the wise becomes wise"** — the read forms; the written forms say "Walk with the wise and be wise."
- **v23 "The fallow ground of the poor yields much food"** — the poor man's land could feed him, if injustice did not take it.
- **v24 "Whoever spares his rod hates his son"** — the rod of correction (22:15; 23:13–14; Hebrews 12:7–11); "early" may mean "diligently."
