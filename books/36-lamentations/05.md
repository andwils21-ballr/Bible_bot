# Lamentations 5

**1** Remember, LORD, what has happened to us;  
look, and see our disgrace!

**2** Our inheritance has been turned over to strangers,  
our houses to foreigners.

**3** We have become orphans, fatherless;  
our mothers are like widows.

**4** We must pay money for the water we drink;  
our wood comes to us for a price.

**5** With a yoke on our necks we are driven;  
we are weary and given no rest.

**6** We have given our hand to Egypt,  
and to Assyria, to get enough bread.

**7** Our fathers sinned, and are no more,  
and we bear their iniquities.

**8** Slaves rule over us;  
there is no one to rescue us from their hand.

**9** We get our bread at the risk of our lives,  
because of the sword in the wilderness.

**10** Our skin is hot as an oven  
from the burning heat of famine.

**11** Women are raped in Zion,  
virgins in the cities of Judah.

**12** Princes are hanged by their hands;  
no respect is shown to the elders.

**13** Young men carry the millstones,  
and boys stagger under loads of wood.

**14** The elders have left the gate,  
the young men their music.

**15** The joy of our hearts has ceased;  
our dancing has turned to mourning.

**16** The crown has fallen from our head;  
woe to us, for we have sinned!

**17** For this our heart has grown faint;  
for these things our eyes have grown dim,

**18** for Mount Zion, which lies desolate,  
with foxes prowling over it.

**19** You, LORD, are enthroned forever;  
Your throne is to all generations.

**20** Why do You forget us forever,  
forsake us for so many days?

**21** Bring us back to You, LORD, that we may return;  
renew our days as of old,

**22** unless You have utterly rejected us,  
and are very angry with us.

## Notes

- **vv1–22 "Remember, LORD, what has happened to us"** — twenty-two verses, the number of the alphabet, but no longer in its order: the acrostic has broken off. The voice is *we* throughout, a prayer of the people.
- **v6 "We have given our hand to Egypt, and to Assyria"** — the policy Jeremiah mocked at the start, *what are you doing on the road to Egypt… on the road to Assyria?* (Jeremiah 2:18). *Given the hand* is a pledge of submission.
- **v7 "Our fathers sinned, and are no more, and we bear their iniquities"** — the proverb Jeremiah said would one day end, *The fathers have eaten sour grapes, and the children's teeth are set on edge* (Jeremiah 31:29); v16 adds *for we have sinned*.
- **vv16, 18 "The crown has fallen from our head… Mount Zion, which lies desolate, with foxes prowling over it"** — Jeremiah's word to the king and the queen mother, *your beautiful crown has come down from your heads* (Jeremiah 13:18), and Micah's, quoted at Jeremiah's trial, *Zion shall be plowed as a field* (Jeremiah 26:18).
- **v19 "You, LORD, are enthroned forever"** — the line of the psalm, *But You, LORD, are enthroned forever* (Psalm 102:12): the city's throne is gone; His is not.
- **v21 "Bring us back to You, LORD, that we may return"** — the words of Ephraim's repentance in Jeremiah, *Bring me back, that I may return* (Jeremiah 31:18).
- **v22 "unless You have utterly rejected us, and are very angry with us"** — *ki im*, which can mean *unless*, *but*, or *even though*, and the book ends on it without an answer. Read as *unless*, the last verse names the one fear that could undo the prayer of v21; read as *but*, it is a statement, *But You have utterly rejected us*; the Greek has *For You have utterly rejected us*.
