# Ezra 6

## The Scroll at Ecbatana

**1** Then Darius the king made a decree, and search was made in the house of the archives, where the treasures were stored in Babylon.

**2** At Ecbatana, the citadel in the province of Media, a scroll was found, and in it was written this record:

**3** "In the first year of Cyrus the king, Cyrus the king made a decree concerning the house of God at Jerusalem: Let the house be rebuilt, the place where sacrifices are offered, and let its foundations be laid. Its height shall be sixty cubits and its width sixty cubits,

**4** with three courses of great stones and one course of new timber. Let the cost be paid from the king's house.

**5** Also let the vessels of gold and silver of the house of God, which Nebuchadnezzar took out of the temple in Jerusalem and brought to Babylon, be returned and brought back to the temple in Jerusalem, each to its place, and you shall put them in the house of God."

## The Decree of Darius

**6** "Now, Tattenai, governor of the province Beyond the River, Shethar-bozenai and your associates, the officials who are in the province Beyond the River, keep away from there.

**7** Let the work on this house of God alone. Let the governor of the Jews and the elders of the Jews rebuild this house of God on its site.

**8** Moreover, I make a decree regarding what you shall do for these elders of the Jews for the rebuilding of this house of God: the cost is to be paid to these men in full and without delay from the royal revenue, the tribute of the province Beyond the River.

**9** Whatever is needed, young bulls, rams and lambs for burnt offerings to the God of heaven, wheat, salt, wine and oil, as the priests in Jerusalem require, let it be given to them day by day without fail,

**10** so that they may offer sacrifices of soothing aroma to the God of heaven and pray for the life of the king and his sons.

**11** I also make a decree that if anyone alters this word, a beam shall be pulled out of his house, and he shall be lifted up and impaled on it, and his house shall be made a dunghill for this.

**12** May the God who has caused His name to dwell there overthrow any king or people who puts out a hand to alter this, or to destroy this house of God that is in Jerusalem. I, Darius, make a decree; let it be done in full."

## The House Finished

**13** Then Tattenai the governor of the province Beyond the River, Shethar-bozenai and their associates did in full what Darius the king had sent.

**14** The elders of the Jews built and prospered through the prophesying of Haggai the prophet and Zechariah son of Iddo. They built and finished by the decree of the God of Israel and by the decree of Cyrus, Darius and Artaxerxes king of Persia.

**15** This house was finished on the third day of the month of Adar, in the sixth year of the reign of Darius the king.

**16** The sons of Israel, the priests and the Levites and the rest of the returned exiles, celebrated the dedication of this house of God with joy.

**17** At the dedication of this house of God they offered a hundred bulls, two hundred rams and four hundred lambs, and as a sin offering for all Israel twelve male goats, according to the number of the tribes of Israel.

**18** They appointed the priests in their divisions and the Levites in their sections for the service of God at Jerusalem, as it is written in the book of Moses.

## The Passover

**19** On the fourteenth day of the first month the returned exiles kept the Passover.

**20** For the priests and the Levites had purified themselves together; all of them were clean. They slaughtered the Passover lamb for all the returned exiles, for their brothers the priests and for themselves.

**21** The sons of Israel who had returned from exile ate it, together with everyone who had separated himself from the uncleanness of the nations of the land to join them in seeking the LORD, the God of Israel.

**22** They kept the Festival of Unleavened Bread seven days with joy, for the LORD had made them joyful and had turned the heart of the king of Assyria to them, to strengthen their hands in the work of the house of God, the God of Israel.

## Notes

- **v2 "At Ecbatana… a scroll was found"** — not in Babylon, where the search began (v1), but in the city in Media, *Achmeta*. The decree of Cyrus survives in the Persian archive, as the elders said it would (5:13).
- **vv3–4 "sixty cubits… three courses of great stones and one course of new timber"** — Solomon's house was *sixty cubits long, twenty cubits wide and thirty cubits high* (1 Kings 6:2), and his inner court was built *with three courses of dressed stone and one course of cedar beams* (6:36). The decree gives no length, and a height and width far beyond Solomon's; the Greek has the height, sixty cubits, as well.
- **v10 "sacrifices of soothing aroma"** — *nichochin*, the Aramaic form of the law's *re'ach nichoach*, **soothing aroma** (Genesis 8:21; Leviticus 1:9). The king of Persia decrees the offering in the words of the Torah.
- **v11 "impaled on it, and his house shall be made a dunghill"** — the same threat the king of Babylon makes to his wise men: *your houses shall be made a dunghill* (Daniel 2:5).
- **v12 "the God who has caused His name to dwell there"** — the words of Deuteronomy for the place of worship: *the place that the LORD your God will choose, to make His name dwell there* (Deuteronomy 12:11), here in the mouth of Darius.
- **v14 "Cyrus, Darius and Artaxerxes"** — Artaxerxes reigned after the house was finished; his decree in favor of the house comes in the next chapter (7:11–26). The verse names the three kings whose decrees served it.
- **vv16–17 "a hundred bulls… twelve male goats, according to the number of the tribes of Israel"** — at Solomon's dedication there were *twenty-two thousand* cattle and *a hundred and twenty thousand* sheep (1 Kings 8:63). The offering is small, but it is made *for all Israel*, twelve tribes, though only Judah, Benjamin and Levi have returned.
- **v19 "On the fourteenth day of the first month"** — the book returns from Aramaic to Hebrew here.
- **v21 "everyone who had separated himself from the uncleanness of the nations of the land"** — the law allows a guest to keep the Passover once his males are circumcised: *he shall be as a native of the land* (Exodus 12:48).
- **v22 "turned the heart of the king of Assyria to them"** — so the Hebrew and the Greek name the king of Persia, by the title of the empire that first carried Israel away. *The king's heart is in the hand of the LORD… He turns it wherever He wishes* (Proverbs 21:1).
