# 1 Chronicles 20

## Rabbah Taken

**1** And it came to pass at the turn of the year, at the time when kings go out, that Joab led out the army and laid waste the land of the sons of Ammon, and came and besieged Rabbah. But David stayed in Jerusalem. Joab struck Rabbah and tore it down.

**2** David took the crown of their king from his head and found that it weighed a talent of gold, and there was a precious stone in it, and it was placed on David's head. He brought out the spoil of the city, a very great amount.

**3** He brought out the people who were in it and sawed them with saws, with iron picks and with saws. So David did to all the cities of the sons of Ammon. Then David and all the people returned to Jerusalem.

## The Philistine Giants

**4** And it came to pass after this that war broke out at Gezer with the Philistines. Then Sibbecai the Hushathite struck down Sippai, who was one of the descendants of the Rephaim, and they were subdued.

**5** There was again war with the Philistines, and Elhanan son of Jair struck down Lahmi the brother of Goliath the Gittite, the shaft of whose spear was like a weaver's beam.

**6** There was again war at Gath, and there was a man of great size who had six fingers on each hand and six toes on each foot, twenty-four in number. He too was descended from the Rapha.

**7** He taunted Israel, and Jonathan son of Shimea, David's brother, struck him down.

**8** These were descended from the Rapha in Gath, and they fell by the hand of David and by the hand of his servants.

## Notes

- **vv1–3 and 2 Samuel 11–12** — v1 is 2 Samuel 11:1, and vv2–3 are 2 Samuel 12:30–31. Between them Samuel tells of Bathsheba and Uriah, Nathan's parable and the death of the child (2 Samuel 11:2–12:25). Chronicles tells none of it, and Joab takes Rabbah himself: Samuel's message from Joab to David, *camp against the city and capture it, or I will capture the city myself* (2 Samuel 12:28), is not here.
- **v1 "But David stayed in Jerusalem"** — the words that in Samuel begin the story of Bathsheba (2 Samuel 11:1); here nothing follows from them.
- **v2 "the crown of their king"** — *malkam*, which is also the name of the Ammonite god *Milcom* (1 Kings 11:5).
- **v3 "sawed them with saws, with iron picks and with saws"** — *va-yasar ba-megerah*, a verb found only here; the Greek has *sawed through them with saws*. Samuel has *va-yasem*, **put them to**, *set them to the saws* (2 Samuel 12:31), a sentence of labor, and the brick kiln. The two verbs differ in one letter; the text keeps the Hebrew of each. Chronicles has *saws* twice where Samuel has *axes*.
- **vv4–8 and 2 Samuel 21:15–22** — the four giants of Samuel, three of them told here; Samuel's first, *Ishbi-benob*, who nearly killed David (2 Samuel 21:16–17), is not. Samuel places the first fight at *Gob*; Chronicles at *Gezer*.
- **v5 "Elhanan son of Jair struck down Lahmi the brother of Goliath"** — written *Ya'ur*, read *Ya'ir*. Samuel has *Elhanan son of Jaare-oregim, the Bethlehemite, struck down Goliath the Gittite* (2 Samuel 21:19); 1 Samuel 17 gives Goliath to David. The text keeps the Hebrew of each, and the note on 2 Samuel 21:19 sets out the letters.
- **vv4, 6, 8 "the Rephaim… the Rapha"** — *ha-Refa'im* in v4, *ha-Rafa* in vv6 and 8: the giants of old, as *Og king of Bashan… was left of the remnant of the Rephaim* (Deuteronomy 3:11).
- **v6 "six fingers on each hand and six toes on each foot"** — the Hebrew says *his fingers six and six, twenty-four*; the hands and feet are supplied from Samuel (2 Samuel 21:20).
