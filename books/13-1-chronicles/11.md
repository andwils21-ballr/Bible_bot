# 1 Chronicles 11

## David Anointed at Hebron

**1** Then all Israel gathered to David at Hebron and said, "We are your bone and your flesh.

**2** In times past, even when Saul was king, it was you who led Israel out and brought them in. The LORD your God said to you, 'You shall shepherd My people Israel, and you shall be leader over My people Israel.'"

**3** So all the elders of Israel came to the king at Hebron, and David made a covenant with them at Hebron before the LORD, and they anointed David king over Israel, according to the word of the LORD by Samuel.

## Jerusalem Taken

**4** David and all Israel went to Jerusalem, that is, Jebus, where the Jebusites, the inhabitants of the land, were.

**5** The inhabitants of Jebus said to David, "You will not come in here." But David took the stronghold of Zion, that is, the City of David.

**6** David said, "Whoever strikes the Jebusites first shall be chief and commander." Joab son of Zeruiah went up first, and he became chief.

**7** David lived in the stronghold; therefore they called it the City of David.

**8** He built the city all around, from the Millo to the surrounding wall, and Joab restored the rest of the city.

**9** David grew greater and greater, and the LORD of hosts was with him.

## David's Mighty Men

**10** These are the chiefs of David's mighty men, who held strongly with him in his kingdom, with all Israel, to make him king, according to the word of the LORD concerning Israel.

**11** This is the list of David's mighty men: Jashobeam son of Hachmoni, chief of the officers. He wielded his spear against three hundred, whom he killed at one time.

**12** After him was Eleazar son of Dodo the Ahohite; he was one of the three mighty men.

**13** He was with David at Pas-dammim when the Philistines were gathered there for battle. There was a plot of ground full of barley, and the people fled from the Philistines.

**14** But they took their stand in the middle of the plot, defended it and struck down the Philistines, and the LORD saved them with a great victory.

**15** Three of the thirty chiefs went down to the rock, to David at the cave of Adullam, while the army of the Philistines was camped in the Valley of Rephaim.

**16** David was then in the stronghold, and the Philistine garrison was then at Bethlehem.

**17** David longed and said, "If only someone would give me water to drink from the well of Bethlehem that is by the gate!"

**18** So the three broke through the camp of the Philistines, drew water from the well of Bethlehem that was by the gate, and carried it and brought it to David. But David would not drink it; he poured it out to the LORD.

**19** He said, "Far be it from me before my God that I should do this. Shall I drink the blood of these men who went at the risk of their lives? For at the risk of their lives they brought it." So he would not drink it. These things the three mighty men did.

**20** Abishai, the brother of Joab, was chief of the Three. He wielded his spear against three hundred, whom he killed, and won a name among the Three.

**21** Of the Three he was doubly honored, and he became their commander, but he did not attain to the Three.

**22** Benaiah son of Jehoiada was the son of a man of valor from Kabzeel, great in deeds. He struck down the two Ariels of Moab. He also went down and struck down a lion inside a pit on a day of snow.

**23** He struck down an Egyptian, a man of great stature, five cubits tall. In the Egyptian's hand was a spear like a weaver's beam, but he went down to him with a staff, snatched the spear out of the Egyptian's hand and killed him with his own spear.

**24** These things Benaiah son of Jehoiada did, and he won a name among the three mighty men.

**25** He was honored among the Thirty, but he did not attain to the Three. David set him over his bodyguard.

## The Thirty

**27** Shammoth the Harorite, Helez the Pelonite,

**35** Ahiam son of Sachar the Hararite, Eliphal son of Ur,

## Notes

- **vv1–9 and 2 Samuel 5** — the account of 2 Samuel 5:1–10, without David's age and years (2 Samuel 5:4–5) and without the taunt of *the blind and the lame*. Chronicles adds *according to the word of the LORD by Samuel* (v3), the anointing in Bethlehem (1 Samuel 16:13).
- **v6 "Joab son of Zeruiah went up first, and he became chief"** — not in Samuel, where Joab is already commander of David's army (2 Samuel 2:13; 8:16).
- **v8 "Joab restored the rest of the city"** — *yechayyeh*, **gave life to**, the verb used for rebuilding: *Will they revive the stones out of the heaps of rubble?* (Nehemiah 4:2).
- **v11 "Jashobeam son of Hachmoni, chief of the officers… three hundred"** — written *ha-sheloshim*, **the thirty**, read *ha-shalishim*, **the officers**; the Greek has *the thirty*, and Samuel has *chief of the Three* (2 Samuel 23:8). Samuel names him *Josheb-basshebeth the Tahchemonite* and counts *eight hundred*.
- **vv13–14 "Pas-dammim… a plot of ground full of barley… they took their stand"** — in Samuel this stand is Shammah's, at Lehi, in a field of lentils (2 Samuel 23:11–12). Between *the Philistines were gathered there for battle* in Eleazar's story and the same words in Shammah's, Chronicles lacks the end of the one and the beginning of the other, and tells the stand under Eleazar's name. The Greek agrees with the Hebrew here. *Pas-dammim* is the *Ephes-dammim* where the Philistines camped against Saul before Goliath came out (1 Samuel 17:1).
- **v19 "Shall I drink the blood of these men"** — the law forbids eating blood, *for the life of the flesh is in the blood* (Leviticus 17:11). David counts the water as the men's lives, *be-nafshotam*, **at the risk of their lives**, and pours it out *to the LORD*.
- **v20 "and won a name among the Three"** — written *ve-lo*, **and not**, read *ve-lo*, **and to him**: *and he had a name*. The text follows the read form, as Samuel has it (2 Samuel 23:18).
- **v23 "five cubits tall… a spear like a weaver's beam"** — Samuel says only *a man of great stature*; the *weaver's beam* is the spear-shaft of Goliath (1 Samuel 17:7).
- **vv26–47 "The mighty men of the armies"** — the list of 2 Samuel 23:24–39, with many names spelled differently (*Shammoth* for *Shammah*, *Sibbecai* for *Mebunnai*, *Hurai* for *Hiddai*). Samuel ends with *Uriah the Hittite: thirty-seven in all*; Chronicles goes on with sixteen more names, Reubenites and men from beyond the Jordan among them, and *Ithmah the Moabite* (v46). Written *Ye'u'el*, read *Ye'i'el* (v44).
