# Baruch 4

**1** She is the book of the commandments of God,  
the law that endures forever.  
All who hold fast to her will live,  
but those who forsake her will die.

**2** Turn, Jacob, and take hold of her;  
walk toward her radiance, in the presence of her light.

**3** Do not give your glory to another,  
or what is good for you to a foreign nation.

**4** Happy are we, Israel,  
for what pleases God is made known to us.

## Take Courage

**5** Take courage, my people,  
you who keep alive the name of Israel!

**6** You were sold to the nations,  
but not for destruction;  
because you angered God,  
you were handed over to your enemies.

**7** For you provoked Him who made you  
by sacrificing to demons and not to God.

**8** You forgot the everlasting God, who brought you up,  
and you grieved Jerusalem, who reared you.

**9** For she saw the anger that came upon you from God, and she said:  
"Listen, you neighbors of Zion;  
God has brought great mourning upon me.

**10** For I have seen the captivity of my sons and daughters,  
which the Everlasting brought upon them.

**11** With joy I nurtured them,  
but with weeping and mourning I sent them away.

**12** Let no one rejoice over me,  
a widow, forsaken by many;  
I have been left desolate because of the sins of my children,  
because they turned away from the law of God,

**13** and did not know His ordinances,  
nor walk in the ways of God's commandments,  
nor tread the paths of discipline in His righteousness.

**14** Let the neighbors of Zion come;  
remember the captivity of my sons and daughters,  
which the Everlasting brought upon them.

**15** For He brought against them a nation from far away,  
a shameless nation of a strange language,  
who had no respect for the old  
and no pity for the child.

**16** They led away the widow's beloved sons  
and left her alone, bereft of her daughters.

**17** But I — how can I help you?

**18** For He who brought these calamities upon you  
will deliver you from the hand of your enemies.

**19** Go, my children, go;  
for I have been left desolate.

**20** I have taken off the robe of peace  
and put on the sackcloth of my supplication;  
I will cry to the Everlasting all my days.

**21** Take courage, my children; cry to God,  
and He will deliver you from tyranny, from the hand of your enemies.

**22** For I have put my hope in the Everlasting for your salvation,  
and joy has come to me from the Holy One,  
because of the mercy that will soon come to you  
from your everlasting Savior.

**23** For I sent you out with mourning and weeping,  
but God will give you back to me with joy and gladness forever.

**24** For as the neighbors of Zion have now seen your captivity,  
so they will soon see your salvation from your God,  
which will come to you with great glory  
and with the splendor of the Everlasting.

**25** My children, bear patiently the anger that has come upon you from God.  
Your enemy has pursued you,  
but you will soon see his destruction  
and will set your foot on their necks.

**26** My tender ones have traveled rough roads;  
they were carried off like a flock seized by enemies.

**27** Take courage, my children, and cry to God,  
for He who brought this upon you will remember you.

**28** For as your mind was set on going astray from God,  
turn now and seek Him with ten times the zeal.

**29** For He who brought these calamities upon you  
will bring you everlasting joy with your salvation."

## Take Courage, Jerusalem

**30** Take courage, Jerusalem,  
for He who named you will comfort you.

**31** Wretched are those who mistreated you  
and rejoiced at your fall;

**32** wretched are the cities that your children served as slaves;  
wretched is she who received your sons.

**33** For just as she rejoiced at your fall  
and was glad at your ruin,  
so she will grieve at her own desolation.

**34** I will take away her joy in her crowds,  
and her boasting will be turned to mourning.

**35** For fire will come upon her from the Everlasting for many days,  
and she will be inhabited by demons for a long time.

**36** Look toward the east, Jerusalem,  
and see the joy that is coming to you from God.

**37** Here come your sons, whom you sent away;  
they are coming, gathered from east to west  
at the word of the Holy One,  
rejoicing in the glory of God.

## Notes

- **v1 "She is the book of the commandments of God, the law that endures forever"** — wisdom is the law, as Sirach says at the end of Wisdom's own song, *All these things are the book of the covenant of the Most High God, the law that Moses commanded* (Sirach 24:23). *Will live… will die* is the choice of Deuteronomy 30:15.
- **v4 "Happy are we, Israel"** — Moses' last blessing, *Happy are you, Israel! Who is like you, a people saved by the LORD* (Deuteronomy 33:29), now said in the first person.
- **vv7–8 "by sacrificing to demons and not to God… You forgot the everlasting God, who brought you up"** — the Song of Moses: *They sacrificed to demons, not God*, and *you forgot the God who brought you forth in labor* (Deuteronomy 32:17–18).
- **vv9–29 "Listen, you neighbors of Zion… a widow, forsaken by many"** — Jerusalem speaks as a mother, the city of Lamentations who *has become like a widow* (Lamentations 1:1), now sending her children off and promising their return.
- **v15 "a nation from far away, a shameless nation of a strange language, who had no respect for the old and no pity for the child"** — the curse of the law, *a nation from far away… whose language you will not understand, a nation hard of face, who will not lift the face of the old or show favor to the young* (Deuteronomy 28:49–50).
- **vv10, 14, 20, 22, 24, 35 "the Everlasting"** — *ho aiōnios*, used here as a name for God, six times in the chapter, beside *the Holy One* (vv22, 37) and *your everlasting Savior* (v22).
- **vv20, 5:1 "I have taken off the robe of peace and put on the sackcloth… Take off the garment of your mourning"** — Jerusalem puts on mourning here, and in the next chapter is told to take it off.
- **vv30–35 "He who named you will comfort you… wretched is she who received your sons… inhabited by demons"** — the comfort of Isaiah, *Comfort, comfort My people* (Isaiah 40:1), and the fate of the unnamed city that took the exiles, as in Isaiah's oracle on Babylon, where the Greek has *demons* for the *goat-spirits* of the Hebrew (Isaiah 13:21).
- **vv36–37 "Look toward the east, Jerusalem… Here come your sons"** — *Lift up your eyes all around and see: they all gather together, they come to you; your sons will come from afar* (Isaiah 60:4).
