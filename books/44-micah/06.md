# Micah 6

## The LORD's Case

**1** Hear, please, what the LORD says:  
Rise, plead your case before the mountains,  
and let the hills hear your voice.

**2** Hear, you mountains, the LORD's case,  
you enduring foundations of the earth;  
for the LORD has a case against His people,  
and He will argue it out with Israel.

**3** My people, what have I done to you?  
How have I wearied you?  
Answer against Me!

**4** For I brought you up from the land of Egypt  
and redeemed you from the house of slaves;  
and I sent before you  
Moses, Aaron and Miriam.

**5** My people, remember what Balak king of Moab devised,  
and what Balaam son of Beor answered him,  
from Shittim to Gilgal,  
so that you may know the righteous acts of the LORD.

## What Does the LORD Require?

**6** With what shall I come before the LORD  
and bow myself before God on high?  
Shall I come before Him with burnt offerings,  
with calves a year old?

**7** Will the LORD be pleased with thousands of rams,  
with ten thousands of rivers of oil?  
Shall I give my firstborn for my transgression,  
the fruit of my body for the sin of my soul?

**8** He has told you, O man, what is good;  
and what does the LORD require of you  
but to do justice, and to love loyal love,  
and to walk humbly with your God?

## Wicked Scales

**9** The voice of the LORD calls to the city,  
and sound wisdom sees Your name:  
Hear the rod, and the One who appointed it!

**10** Are there still, in the house of the wicked,  
treasures of wickedness,  
and the scant ephah that is cursed?

**11** Shall I count pure the one with wicked scales,  
with a bag of false weights?

**12** Her rich men are full of violence;  
her inhabitants speak lies,  
and their tongue is deceit in their mouth.

**13** So I in turn have struck you with a sickening blow,  
making you desolate because of your sins.

**14** You will eat and not be satisfied,  
and your hunger will be inside you;  
you will put away but not save,  
and what you save I will give to the sword.

**15** You will sow but not reap;  
you will tread olives but not anoint yourself with oil,  
and tread grapes for new wine but not drink the wine.

**16** The statutes of Omri are kept,  
and all the works of the house of Ahab,  
and you have walked in their counsels;  
so I will make you a desolation,  
and her inhabitants a thing to be hissed at,  
and you will bear the scorn of My people.

## Notes

- **v8 "what does the LORD require of you but to do justice, and to love loyal love, and to walk humbly with your God?"** — Moses asked it first: *And now, Israel, what does the LORD your God ask of you but to fear the LORD your God, to walk in all His ways and to love Him* (Deuteronomy 10:12). *Ahavat chesed*, **to love loyal love**, is the *chesed* the LORD desires over sacrifice, *For I desire loyal love and not sacrifice* (Hosea 6:6), and the *chesed* He delights in (7:18). *Hatsnea lekhet*, **to walk humbly**, from *tsana*, modest, careful, as in *with the humble is wisdom* (Proverbs 11:2); the Greek has *to be ready to walk with the Lord your God*. *Adam*, **O man**: a human being, any one.
- **v9 "sound wisdom sees Your name: Hear the rod, and the One who appointed it!"** — obscure. *Ve-tushiyyah yir'eh shemekha*, **and sound wisdom sees Your name**; *yir'eh*, **sees**, has the letters of *yir'ah*, **fear**, and the Greek has *He will save those who fear His name*. *Shim'u matteh u-mi ye'adah*, **hear the rod, and who appointed it**: *matteh* is a rod and also a tribe, and the Greek has *hear, O tribe*. *Ye'adah*, **appointed it**, is feminine, which may point back to *the city*. As the lines stand, the rod is the punishment, and the city is told to heed it and the One who set it.
- **vv13–15 "a sickening blow… You will eat and not be satisfied… you will tread olives but not anoint yourself with oil"** — the curses of the law come due: *you will eat and not be satisfied* (Leviticus 26:26), and *you will not anoint yourself with oil, for your olives will drop off* (Deuteronomy 28:40), as Amos also said of the vineyards (Amos 5:11). *Ve-yeshchakha*, **your hunger**, is found only here, and its sense is a guess from the line; the Greek has *it will grow dark in you*.
