# Didascalia 1

## The Doctrine of the Didascalia of the Fathers, the Holy and Pure Lights of the World, the Apostles

In the name of God the Father Almighty, and of His only-begotten Son Jesus Christ, and of the Paraclete, the Holy Spirit.

**1** We, the twelve apostles, servants of His only-begotten Son Jesus Christ, gathered together in one place in Jerusalem, the city of the great King — and with us our good and holy brother Paul, the apostle of all the nations, and James, the brother of our Lord, who is the bishop of this city of Jerusalem — have ordained this right teaching and have appointed ranks of office for the Church, after the pattern of what is in heaven. We charge each one of you, giving thanks that our Lord Jesus has called us to His glory. The bishop is in the likeness of the shepherd of the sheep, the presbyter in the likeness of a teacher, the deacon as a servant, and the subdeacon the same; and the reader also, and the skilled singers… and the rest of the people as those who listen to the word of the Gospel and understand the word of admonition. This is how we have ordained the law for the Church: in this book of admonition, which we have written and sent into the world with Clement, our brother who serves with us, so that people may walk by the commandment of the churches under heaven and have perfect understanding, and that, when they have heard the commandments written in it, they may find eternal life, honor and glory from our Lord Jesus Christ, who has given us this mystery, which is from Himself. But those who do not keep His law will be thrown into the fire that is not quenched, and their dwelling will be in the fire of Gehenna forever, as it is written.

**2** Apostles and presbyters and all the people who have believed in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ and who work righteousness and peace will find compassion and mercy from God the Father, the Ruler of the whole world, and from His Son Jesus Christ, and will come to know Him in the holy Church of God. Those who serve Him will be a pure and chosen vineyard. They will find eternal life by this faith and victory by the Holy Spirit, and will be strong by the power of Jesus Christ and by faith in Him who has redeemed them by the sprinkling of His holy and precious blood. He has given them the privilege of calling on the Almighty, that He should be their Father and their God and their Friend, and that they should be fellow heirs and sharers with His Holy Son. Listen to the holy admonition, you who, at the command of our Savior, receive the words of the Gospel and its good news, which is full of glory.

**3** Children of God, keep His commandments and do His will. Anyone who seeks to follow sin and does everything that does not please God will be counted with those who break His law and His commandment. And now I say to you: keep far from injustice and oppression, and do not desire to take the greater portion for yourselves and leave the lesser to your neighbor. For it is written in the Law, "You shall not covet your neighbor's wife, or his field, or his male slave, or his female slave, or his ox, or his donkey, or anything" — nothing your brother owns, nor anything like it; that is adultery. Anyone who desires to do this in the lust of his heart will be far from God, our Lord Jesus Christ, to whom be glory with His Father and the Holy Spirit forever and ever. Amen. For He teaches us, gives us understanding and strengthens us by the Holy Spirit, so that He may fulfill the Law, in which it is written, "You shall not commit adultery; but I say to you, everyone who has looked at a woman and desired her has already committed adultery with her in his heart." Whoever desires to do this will be condemned with the adulterers. Anyone who plans in his heart and desires to take his neighbor's ox, or his donkey, or his field, unjustly, and keeps on doing it, will be counted with the adulterers. For the prophet says, "Woe to those who join house to house and add field to field and hedge to hedge, to take what is not theirs." Again it is said, "Know, you who live on the earth, that this word was heard in the ears of the Lord of hosts." In another place it is said, "Cursed is everyone who moves his neighbor's boundary stone; and the people shall say, So be it." That is why Moses says, "Do not covet your neighbor's boundary stone, and do not take away the law that your fathers ordained." From these things come fear, death and trouble, and judgment from God will find those who do them. But those who listen to the word of God and keep His glorious law in faith will find compassion and mercy. For it is said, "What you hate for yourself, do not do to your neighbor." As you do not want anyone else to look at your wife to lead her into sin, so do not look at another man's wife with evil thoughts. As you do not want another to take your garment, so do not take another's garment. As you do not want cursing, reproach and beating for yourself, so do not do them to your neighbor. If anyone has cursed you, bless him; for it is written in the Book of Numbers, "Blessed are those who bless you, and cursed are those who curse you." It is also written in the Gospel, "Bless those who curse you, and pray for those who mistreat you; do good to those who hate you, and do not repay evil to those who mistreat you, but bear with them patiently." For the Scripture says, "Do not take vengeance on your enemy for the evil he has done to you; for this the Lord will help you and bring judgment on your enemy." For it is said in the Gospel, "Love your enemies, and pray for those who persecute you, so that you may be sons of your Father who is in heaven; for He makes the sun rise on the evil and the good, and sends rain on the just and the unjust." Beloved, let us understand these commandments, so that we may be sons of light.

**4** Bear patiently with one another, sons of God. Let the husband also bear patiently with his wife, and not be haughty or false, but merciful and upright, and cherish her alone in love and humility. Let him not take pleasure in another woman or desire her, or she may lead him astray into evil. If you commit adultery and do this, you will surely die, the wrath of God will come on you, and you will be condemned with a bitter condemnation. If you think evil in your heart but do not commit adultery with her, it is still sin for you. If you have prepared and adorned yourself so that your neighbor's wife may love you, you have gone astray and have become a stumbling block to her, and have made her commit adultery. For this, condemnation will come on you, for God says, "You shall not covet," and "You shall not commit adultery." You have led her astray to love you by the beauty of your youth and the charm of your face, and so you have become her partner in the work of sin and in your curse. That is why you should pray to the Lord your God that all this sin may not find you out. You ought to please God in purity, so that you may find rest and eternal life before God. For it is said, "Do not let the hair of your head grow long, but cut it and purify yourself, so that you may serve God and not be separated from Him." Do not anoint yourself with perfumed oil, or dress in splendid clothes to lead women astray, or boast, or become an occasion of sin; but seek purity and doing good. You, man of God, do not let the hair of your head grow long; do not braid it or curl it, for all this is a sign of impurity and adultery. For it is written in Deuteronomy, "You shall not wear a garment woven of wool and linen together; you shall make yourself twisted tassels for your garment." But we ought not to shave our beards, or change the nature of man into another form. If you do this, you break the law and are far from God, who created you in His image and His likeness. If you want to please God, do no evil, and put far from you everything your God hates.

**5** Do not be a drunkard, and do not walk in the streets with adulterers, without wisdom and knowledge; but work and live by the work of your hands, seek to do everything that pleases God, remember the word of our Lord Jesus and follow Him at all times. For the Scripture says, "…and who reads His law day and night; whether on the road, or in the house, or while you sleep, love the Lord your God with all your heart, with all your mind and with all your strength," so that you may find compassion and mercy from Him.

## Notes

- **The whole book** — worked from the English of J. M. Harden, *The Ethiopic Didascalia* (1920), made from the Ethiopic. No Ge'ez or other source language was used; T. P. Platt's printed Ethiopic (1834), read by machine, was used only to check the shape of a passage. The chapters are Harden's, and each chapter's own title stands as its heading; each verse is one of Harden's paragraphs. Harden's references to the Greek *Apostolic Constitutions* (*[i., 1.]*) are left out of the text. Where Harden marks a gap (*the skilled singers…*, v1), nothing is supplied.
- **v1 "the city of the great King"** — Jerusalem, in the words of Psalm 48:2, *qiryat melekh rav*, and of Jesus in Matthew 5:35: *nor by Jerusalem, for it is the city of the great King*.
- **v1 "the fire that is not quenched"** — Mark 9:48, *where their worm does not die and the fire is not quenched* (*to pyr ou sbennytai*). Gehenna is the Valley of Hinnom below Jerusalem, the Gospels' name for the place of punishment.
- **v3 "You shall not covet your neighbor's wife, or his field"** — the command as Deuteronomy gives it, with the wife first and the field in the list (Deuteronomy 5:21, *sadehu*), not as Exodus 20:17, *You shall not covet your neighbor's house*. The Didascalia then calls coveting what is another's *adultery*, and in the next sentence joins it to Jesus' teaching that looking with desire is adultery in the heart (Matthew 5:28).
- **v3 "this word was heard in the ears of the Lord of hosts"** — Isaiah 5:9 reads *be-oznai YHWH tseva'ot*, **in my ears the LORD of hosts** has spoken: the prophet hears. In the Didascalia the word is heard *in the ears of the Lord*: the complaint against the land-grabbers of Isaiah 5:8 has reached God.
- **v3 "the law that your fathers ordained"** — the boundary stone of Proverbs 22:28, *do not move the ancient boundary your fathers set* (*asher asu avotekha*), and the curse of Deuteronomy 27:17. The fathers set the boundary; here the boundary has become a law.
- **v3 "What you hate for yourself, do not do to your neighbor"** — Tobit 4:15, *what you hate, do to no one* (*ho miseis mēdeni poiēsēs*). The book goes on to apply it three times, to a wife, a garment and a curse.
- **v3 "Blessed are those who bless you"** — this project's wording of Numbers 24:9, Balaam's blessing on Israel. In Numbers it is God's promise; the Didascalia makes it the ground of a command, to bless the one who curses, and joins it to Luke 6:28, *bless those who curse you*. The saying against revenge is Proverbs 20:22: *Do not say, "I will repay evil"; wait for the LORD, and He will save you*.
- **v4 hair, beard and cloth** — the rulings draw on the Law: the garment of *wool and linen together* and the *twisted tassels* of Deuteronomy 22:11–12, and the beard, which Leviticus 19:27 says *you shall not ruin*. *In His image and His likeness* is Genesis 1:26–27.
- **Source text** — J. M. Harden, *The Ethiopic Didascalia* (1920), public domain. English translation by this project.
