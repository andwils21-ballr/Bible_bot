# Letter of Jeremiah 1

**1** A copy of a letter that Jeremiah sent to those who were about to be taken to Babylon as captives by the king of the Babylonians, to tell them what God had commanded him.

**2** Because of the sins you have committed before God, you will be taken to Babylon as captives by Nebuchadnezzar king of the Babylonians.

**3** When you have arrived in Babylon, you will stay there many years, a long time, up to seven generations; after that I will bring you out from there in peace.

**4** Now in Babylon you will see gods of silver and gold and wood carried on people's shoulders, gods that make the nations afraid.

**5** So be careful not to become like the foreigners yourselves, and do not let fear of these gods take hold of you

**6** when you see the crowd before them and behind them worshipping them. But say in your hearts, "It is You we must worship, Master."

**7** For My angel is with you, and he cares for your souls.

**8** Their tongues are smoothed by a carpenter, and they themselves are gold-plated and silver-plated; but they are false and cannot speak.

**9** People take gold, as for a girl who loves ornaments,

**10** and make crowns for the heads of their gods. Sometimes the priests even take gold and silver from their gods and spend it on themselves,

**11** and give some of it to the prostitutes on the roof. They dress the gods in clothing like people, these gods of silver and gods of gold and of wood.

**12** Yet the gods are not kept safe from tarnish and decay, though they are clothed in purple garments.

**13** Their faces are wiped because of the dust of the house, which lies thick on them.

**14** One holds a scepter like a man who judges a country, yet he cannot put to death anyone who sins against him.

**15** Another has a dagger in his right hand, and an axe, yet he cannot save himself from war or from robbers. From this it is plain that they are not gods. So do not be afraid of them.

**16** For as a man's pot is useless once it is broken,

**17** so are their gods, once they are set up in their houses. Their eyes are full of dust from the feet of those who come in.

**18** As the courts are walled in around a man who has wronged a king, like a man led off to death, so the priests secure the gods' houses with doors and locks and bars, so that robbers will not plunder them.

**19** They light lamps for them, more than they light for themselves, and the gods cannot see one of them.

**20** They are like one of the beams of the house; their insides, people say, are eaten away. When creeping things from the ground devour them and their clothing, they do not perceive it.

**21** Their faces are blackened by the smoke of the house.

**22** Bats, swallows and other birds light on their bodies and heads, and so do cats.

**23** From this you will know that they are not gods. So do not be afraid of them.

**24** The gold they wear for beauty will not shine unless someone wipes off the tarnish; even when they were being cast, they did not perceive it.

**25** They were bought at any price, and there is no breath in them.

**26** Having no feet, they are carried on shoulders, showing everyone their own disgrace. Those who serve them are put to shame too,

**27** because if one of them falls to the ground, it is through them that it gets up again. If someone sets it upright, it will not move by itself; if it is tipped over, it cannot straighten itself. Gifts are set before them as before the dead.

**28** Their priests sell the sacrifices offered to them and use the money for themselves. Their wives likewise preserve some of the meat with salt and share none of it with the poor or the helpless.

**29** A woman in her impurity and a woman after childbirth touch their sacrifices. Since you know from these things that they are not gods, do not be afraid of them.

**30** How can they be called gods? Women set meals before gods of silver and gold and wood.

**31** In their houses the priests sit with their tunics torn, their heads and beards shaved, and their heads uncovered.

**32** They howl and shout before their gods, as people do at a meal for the dead.

**33** The priests take some of the gods' clothing and dress their wives and children in it.

**34** Whether someone does them evil or good, they cannot repay it. They cannot set up a king or remove one.

**35** In the same way they cannot give wealth or even a copper coin. If someone makes a vow to them and does not keep it, they will not call him to account.

**36** They cannot save anyone from death or rescue the weak from the strong.

**37** They cannot give a blind man back his sight, or rescue anyone in distress.

**38** They cannot take pity on a widow or do good to an orphan.

**39** These wooden, gold-plated and silver-plated things are like stones from the mountain, and those who serve them will be put to shame.

**40** How then can anyone think or say that they are gods?

**41** Even the Chaldeans themselves dishonor them. When they see someone who is mute and cannot speak, they bring Bel to him and ask that the man may speak, as though Bel could perceive.

**42** Yet they cannot see this for themselves and abandon their gods, for they have no perception.

**43** The women, with cords around them, sit along the roads, burning bran for incense.

**44** When one of them is drawn away by a passer-by and lies with him, she taunts the woman next to her, because the other was not thought as worthy as she was, and her cord was not broken.

**45** Everything done for these gods is false. How then can anyone think or say that they are gods?

**46** They are made by carpenters and goldsmiths; they can be nothing but what the craftsmen want them to be.

**47** Those who make them do not live long themselves;

**48** how then can the things they have made be gods? They have left lies and disgrace to those who come after.

**49** When war or disaster comes on them, the priests consult together where they can hide with them.

**50** How then can anyone fail to perceive that they are not gods, who cannot save themselves from war or from disaster?

**51** Since they are wooden, gold-plated and silver-plated, it will be known afterward that they are false. It will be plain to all the nations and their kings that they are not gods but the work of human hands, and there is no work of God in them.

**52** How then is it to be known that they are not gods?

**53** They cannot set up a king over a country or give people rain.

**54** They cannot decide their own case or right a wrong, for they are powerless.

**55** They are like crows between heaven and earth. When fire breaks out in a house of wooden, gold-plated or silver-plated gods, their priests will flee and escape, but the gods will be burned up where they stand, like beams.

**56** They cannot stand against a king or against enemies.

**57** How then can anyone accept or think that they are gods? Wooden, silver-plated and gold-plated gods cannot save themselves from thieves or robbers.

**58** The strong strip off their gold and silver and go away with the clothing they wear, and the gods cannot help themselves.

**59** So it is better to be a king who shows his courage, or a useful pot in a house that its owner uses, than these false gods; or a door of a house that keeps safe what is in it, than these false gods; or a wooden pillar in a palace, than these false gods.

**60** For sun and moon and stars, which shine and are sent to serve, are obedient.

**61** So too the lightning, when it flashes, is plain to see; and the wind blows in every land.

**62** When God commands the clouds to go over the whole world, they do what they are told.

**63** The fire sent from above to consume mountains and woods does what it is commanded. But these idols are not like them in form or in power.

**64** So no one should think or say that they are gods, since they cannot decide a case or do good to people.

**65** Since you know, then, that they are not gods, do not be afraid of them.

**66** They can neither curse kings nor bless them.

**67** They cannot show signs in the heavens among the nations, or shine like the sun, or give light like the moon.

**68** The wild animals are better than they are, for they can run to shelter and help themselves.

**69** So in no way is it plain to us that they are gods. Therefore do not be afraid of them.

**70** Like a scarecrow in a cucumber field, which guards nothing, so are their wooden, gold-plated and silver-plated gods.

**71** In the same way their wooden, gold-plated and silver-plated gods are like a thornbush in a garden, on which every bird perches, or like a corpse thrown out in the dark.

**72** From the purple and the shining cloth rotting on them it will be known that they are not gods. They themselves will be eaten in the end, and will be a disgrace in the land.

**73** Better, then, is a righteous man who has no idols, for he will be far from disgrace.

## Notes

- **vv1–2 "A copy of a letter that Jeremiah sent"** — English Bibles print the letter as the last chapter of Baruch (Baruch 6), and the verse numbers here follow that chapter. Swete's Greek gives it a book of its own after Lamentations, and joins the heading and the first sentence as its verse 1, so its numbers run one behind the English. It survives only in Greek.
- **v3 "up to seven generations"** — Jeremiah's own letter to the exiles gave a number in years: *When seventy years are completed for Babylon, I will attend to you* (Jeremiah 29:10).
- **vv6–7 "It is You we must worship, Master… he cares for your souls"** — *Despota*, the word of Judith's prayer, *Master of heaven and earth* (Judith 9:12). *Ho ekzētōn tas psychas*, "the one who seeks out your souls," is the phrase of the psalm, *no one cares for my soul* (Psalm 142:4), where the Greek has the same words: the angel is the one who does.
- **vv8, 25 "they are false and cannot speak… there is no breath in them"** — the letter works out Jeremiah 10 at length: *they cannot speak; they have to be carried* (10:5), and *there is no breath in them* (10:14), in the same Greek words, *ouk estin pneuma en autois*.
- **vv15, 23, 29, 65, 69 "So do not be afraid of them"** — the refrain closes each section, five times, the words of Jeremiah 10:5, *Do not be afraid of them*, where the Greek also has *mē phobēthēte auta*. A second refrain asks *How then can anyone think or say that they are gods?* (vv40, 45; varied at 52, 57, 64).
- **v29 "A woman in her impurity and a woman after childbirth touch their sacrifices"** — under the law a woman after childbirth *shall not touch anything holy* (Leviticus 12:4). In these temples she handles the offerings.
- **v31 "their tunics torn, their heads and beards shaved, and their heads uncovered"** — the mourning the law forbade Israel's priests: *They shall not make a bald patch on their heads, and they shall not shave off the edge of their beards* (Leviticus 21:5); the high priest *shall not let his hair hang loose and shall not tear his clothes* (21:10).
- **vv20, 24, 41–42, 50 "they do not perceive it… for they have no perception"** — *aisthanomai*, to perceive, and its noun *aisthēsis*. The gods do not perceive the worms or the furnace; Bel is asked to heal as though he could perceive; and in v42 the same noun is said of the worshippers, who cannot see what is in front of them. The letter ends with the question that applies to everyone, *How then can anyone fail to perceive* (v50).
- **v70 "Like a scarecrow in a cucumber field"** — the picture of the Hebrew of Jeremiah 10:5, *They are like a scarecrow in a cucumber field*, which the Greek of Jeremiah does not have. The letter has it in its own Greek, *prosbaskanion*, a charm against the evil eye, set up to guard the crop.
- **v72 "the purple and the shining cloth"** — *marmaros*, the word for marble and for anything that gleams. Set beside the purple and said to rot, it is cloth here, a bright fabric on the image.
