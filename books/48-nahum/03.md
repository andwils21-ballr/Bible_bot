# Nahum 3

## The City of Bloodshed

**1** Woe to the city of bloodshed,  
all of it full of lies and plunder;  
the prey never leaves it!

**2** The sound of the whip,  
the sound of the rumbling wheel,  
the galloping horse,  
the skipping chariot!

**3** The horseman charging,  
the flame of the sword,  
the lightning of the spear,  
a multitude of the slain,  
heaps of corpses,  
no end to the bodies;  
they stumble over their bodies —

**4** all because of the great prostitution of the prostitute,  
graceful and fair, mistress of sorceries,  
who sells nations by her prostitution  
and clans by her sorceries.

**5** I am against you, declares the LORD of hosts;  
I will lift up your skirts over your face,  
and show the nations your nakedness  
and the kingdoms your shame.

**6** I will throw loathsome things on you,  
and treat you with contempt,  
and make you a spectacle.

**7** And it shall come to pass that all who see you  
will flee from you and say,  
"Nineveh is laid waste;  
who will mourn for her?"  
Where shall I seek comforters for you?

## Are You Better Than No-amon?

**8** Are you better than No-amon,  
who sat by the streams of the Nile,  
with water all around her,  
whose rampart was the sea,  
the sea her wall?

**9** Cush was her strength, and Egypt,  
without end;  
Put and the Libyans were your helpers.

**10** Yet she too went into exile,  
into captivity;  
her little ones too were dashed to pieces  
at the head of every street;  
they cast lots for her honored men,  
and all her great men were bound in chains.

**11** You too will be drunk;  
you will be hidden;  
you too will seek a stronghold from the enemy.

**12** All your fortresses are fig trees  
with first-ripe figs:  
if they are shaken,  
they fall into the mouth of the eater.

**13** Your troops are women among you;  
the gates of your land are wide open to your enemies;  
fire has devoured your bars.

## Like the Locust

**14** Draw water for the siege;  
strengthen your fortresses;  
go into the clay and tread the mortar;  
take hold of the brick kiln!

**15** There the fire will devour you;  
the sword will cut you off;  
it will devour you as the young locust devours.  
Make yourself as heavy as the young locust;  
make yourself as heavy as the locust!

**16** You multiplied your merchants  
more than the stars of the heavens;  
the young locust strips and flies away.

**17** Your guards are like the locust,  
your marshals like a cloud of locusts  
that settle in the walls on a cold day;  
the sun rises and they flee,  
and no one knows where they are.

**18** Your shepherds slumber, king of Assyria;  
your nobles lie still;  
your people are scattered on the mountains,  
with no one to gather them.

**19** There is no easing your hurt;  
your wound is grievous.  
All who hear the news of you  
clap their hands over you;  
for on whom has your evil not passed continually?

## Notes

- **vv1–3 "Woe to the city of bloodshed… no end to the bodies"** — *Hoy ir damim*, nearly the words of Ezekiel's woe on Jerusalem, *Woe to the city of bloodshed* (Ezekiel 24:6), now said of Nineveh. *Teref*, **prey**, is the word of the lion's den (2:12–13). The battle comes in short cries without verbs, sound after sound. *Ve-ein qetseh la-geviyyah*, **no end to the bodies**, are the words of *no end to the treasure* (2:9), and *without end* comes again in v9. *Sus doher*, **the galloping horse**, as in *the galloping, galloping of his stallions* (Judges 5:22), and *meraqqedah*, **skipping**, as the chariots of Joel 2:5. Written *yikkashelu*, read *ve-khashelu*, **they stumble**: the same verb.
- **v4 "the great prostitution of the prostitute… mistress of sorceries"** — *zenunim* and *keshafim*, **prostitution** and **sorceries**, the pair that Jehu threw in Joram's face, *the whoring of your mother Jezebel and her many sorceries* (2 Kings 9:22). The city is a prostitute who sells: what she trades is *nations* and *clans*. *Zonah* and *zenunim* are the plain words, as in *a wife of prostitution* (Hosea 1:2).
- **vv5–6 "I will lift up your skirts over your face… loathsome things"** — the LORD's words to Jerusalem in Jeremiah, *I Myself will lift up your skirts over your face, and your shame will be seen* (Jeremiah 13:26), now turned on Nineveh. *Ve-nibbaltikh*, **treat you with contempt**, is the verb of *son treats father with contempt* (Micah 7:6). *Ke-ro'i*, **a spectacle**, from *ra'ah*, **see**: v7 goes on, *all who see you*.
- **v7 "who will mourn for her? Where shall I seek comforters for you?"** — *mi yanud lah*, **who will mourn for her**, as in *Who will mourn for you?* (Jeremiah 15:5). *Menachamim*, **comforters**, is from *nacham*, the root of the prophet's own name (1:1): Nahum, **comfort**, finds no one to comfort Nineveh. The question is Isaiah's to Zion, *by whom shall I comfort you?* (Isaiah 51:19), and the cry of Lamentations, *she has no one to comfort her* (Lamentations 1:2).
- **vv8–10 "Are you better than No-amon… Cush… Put and the Libyans… her little ones too were dashed to pieces"** — *No Amon* is the Egyptian city of *Amon of No* (Jeremiah 46:25), the *No* that Ezekiel saw broken open (Ezekiel 30:16). *Ye'orim*, **the streams of the Nile**, as in *the streams of Egypt* (Isaiah 19:6). Cush, Egypt and Put are sons of Ham in the table of nations (Genesis 10:6), and the Libyans marched with Shishak (2 Chronicles 12:3). Her fall reads as Samaria's: *their little ones will be dashed to pieces* (Hosea 13:16), *at the head of every street* (Lamentations 2:19), and the lots cast for captives as in Joel 3:3. *Nikhbaddeiha… ziqqim*, **her honored men… chains**, as in *to bind their kings with chains and their honored men with fetters of iron* (Psalm 149:8). The Greek reads v8 quite differently: *Prepare a portion, tune the string*.
- **vv11–13 "You too will be drunk… fig trees with first-ripe figs… Your troops are women among you"** — *tishkeri*, **you will be drunk**, the drinking of the cup (Obadiah 16). *Ma'oz*, **stronghold**, is what the LORD is to those who take refuge in Him (1:7); Nineveh will look for one from the enemy. The figs are Isaiah's picture, *a first-ripe fig before the summer: whoever sees it swallows it as soon as it is in his hand* (Isaiah 28:4). *Women… bars* are the words Jeremiah gives the fall of Babylon, *they have become like women; her dwellings are set on fire; her bars are broken* (Jeremiah 51:30).
- **v14 "Draw water for the siege… go into the clay and tread the mortar; take hold of the brick kiln!"** — a taunt: get ready for a siege. *Mei matsor*, **water for the siege**, the *matsor* of Micah 5:1. *Chomer*, **mortar**, and bricks were Israel's labor in Egypt, *in mortar and in bricks* (Exodus 1:14), and the mortar of Babel (Genesis 11:3); now Nineveh must tread it. *Malben*, **brick kiln**, as in 2 Samuel 12:31.
- **vv15–17 "Make yourself as heavy as the young locust… the locust… a cloud of locusts"** — three locust words: *yeleq*, **young locust**, *arbeh*, **locust**, and *gov govai*, word for word **locusts of locusts**, the *gov* of Amos's vision, *He was forming locusts* (Amos 7:1). *Hitkabbed*, **make yourself heavy**, is the weight of the locusts of Egypt, *very heavy* (Exodus 10:14). *You multiplied your merchants more than the stars* uses the blessing of Abraham, *I will multiply your seed as the stars of the heavens* (Genesis 22:17), for a trader's crowd that, like locusts, strips and flies off. *Tafsserayikh*, **your marshals**, is the word of *appoint a marshal against her* (Jeremiah 51:27). *Minnezarayikh*, **your guards**, is found only here, and its sense is a guess.
- **vv18–19 "Your shepherds slumber… scattered on the mountains… on whom has your evil not passed continually?"** — the rulers are *shepherds*, and the people are left as in Micaiah's vision, *all Israel scattered on the mountains, like sheep that have no shepherd* (1 Kings 22:17). *Nachlah makkatekha*, **your wound is grievous**, are the words of *Your hurt is incurable, your wound is grievous* (Jeremiah 30:12), said there of Zion and here of Nineveh. *Taq'u khaf*, **clap their hands**, is the clapping of *Clap your hands, all you peoples* (Psalm 47:1). The book ends, as Jonah does, on a question, and its last word but one is *ra'atkha*, **your evil**, the evil that *has come up before Me* (Jonah 1:2).
