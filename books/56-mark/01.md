# Mark 1

## John the Baptizer

**1** The beginning of the good news of Jesus the Messiah.

**2** As it is written in Isaiah the prophet: "I am sending My messenger before Your face, who will prepare Your way;

**3** a voice of one crying out in the wilderness: 'Prepare the way of the Lord; make His paths straight.'"

**4** John the baptizer appeared in the wilderness, preaching a baptism of repentance for the forgiveness of sins.

**5** The whole Judean countryside and all the people of Jerusalem were going out to him, and they were being baptized by him in the Jordan River, confessing their sins.

**6** John wore clothing of camel's hair and a leather belt around his waist, and he ate locusts and wild honey.

**7** He preached, saying, "After me comes one who is stronger than I; I am not worthy to stoop down and untie the strap of His sandals.

**8** I have baptized you with water, but He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit."

## The Baptism and the Testing of Jesus

**9** In those days Jesus came from Nazareth of Galilee and was baptized by John in the Jordan.

**10** At once, as He was coming up out of the water, He saw the heavens torn open and the Spirit coming down on Him like a dove.

**11** A voice came from the heavens: "You are My Son, the beloved; in You I am well pleased."

**12** At once the Spirit drove Him out into the wilderness.

**13** He was in the wilderness forty days, being tested by Satan. He was with the wild animals, and the angels served Him.

## The First Disciples

**14** After John was handed over, Jesus went into Galilee, preaching the good news of God

**15** and saying, "The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God has come near; repent, and believe in the good news."

**16** As He passed along beside the Sea of Galilee, He saw Simon and Andrew, Simon's brother, casting a net in the sea, for they were fishermen.

**17** Jesus said to them, "Come, follow Me, and I will make you become fishers of people."

**18** At once they left their nets and followed Him.

**19** Going on a little farther, He saw James the son of Zebedee and John his brother, who were in the boat mending the nets.

**20** At once He called them; and they left their father Zebedee in the boat with the hired men and went after Him.

## A Day in Capernaum

**21** They went into Capernaum, and at once, on the sabbath, He went into the synagogue and taught.

**22** They were astonished at His teaching, for He was teaching them as one who has authority, and not as the scribes.

**23** At once, there in their synagogue, was a man with an unclean spirit, and he cried out,

**24** "What have You to do with us, Jesus of Nazareth? Have You come to destroy us? I know who You are: the Holy One of God."

**25** Jesus rebuked him, saying, "Be silent, and come out of him!"

**26** The unclean spirit threw him into convulsions, cried out with a loud voice, and came out of him.

**27** They were all amazed, so that they asked one another, "What is this? A new teaching, with authority! He commands even the unclean spirits, and they obey Him."

**28** At once the report of Him went out everywhere, into all the surrounding region of Galilee.

**29** At once they left the synagogue and went into the house of Simon and Andrew, with James and John.

**30** Simon's mother-in-law was lying in bed with a fever, and at once they told Him about her.

**31** He came to her, took her by the hand and raised her up; the fever left her, and she began to serve them.

**32** When evening came, after the sun had set, they brought to Him all who were ill and those possessed by demons.

**33** The whole town was gathered at the door.

**34** He healed many who were ill with various diseases, and drove out many demons; and He would not let the demons speak, because they knew Him.

**35** Very early in the morning, while it was still dark, He got up and went out to a deserted place, and there He prayed.

**36** Simon and those with him hunted for Him,

**37** found Him, and said to Him, "Everyone is looking for You."

**38** He said to them, "Let us go elsewhere, into the nearby towns, so that I may preach there also; for that is why I came out."

**39** He went preaching in their synagogues throughout all Galilee, and driving out the demons.

## A Leper Made Clean

**40** A leper came to Him, pleading with Him on his knees and saying to Him, "If You are willing, You can make me clean."

**41** Moved with anger, He stretched out His hand and touched him, and said to him, "I am willing; be made clean."

**42** At once the leprosy left him, and he was made clean.

**43** He sternly warned him and at once sent him away,

**44** saying to him, "See that you say nothing to anyone; but go, show yourself to the priest, and offer for your cleansing what Moses commanded, as a testimony to them."

**45** But he went out and began to proclaim it freely and to spread the word, so that Jesus could no longer go openly into a town, but stayed out in deserted places; and people came to Him from everywhere.

## Notes

- **v1 "The beginning of the good news of Jesus the Messiah"** — *archē*, **beginning**, is the first word of Genesis in Greek, *en archē*. *Euangelion*, **good news**, is the word for a herald's message, as in Isaiah's *herald of good news* (Isaiah 40:9) and *How beautiful on the mountains are the feet of him who brings good news* (Isaiah 52:7). The edition marks *Messiah* as a place where manuscripts differ.
- **vv2–3 "As it is written in Isaiah the prophet: I am sending My messenger before Your face"** — the first lines are not Isaiah's but Exodus 23:20, *I am sending a messenger before you*, joined to Malachi 3:1, *I am sending My messenger, and he will clear the way before Me*, as in Matthew 11:10. Only v3 is Isaiah 40:3. Mark names the greater prophet for all three. The edition marks *in Isaiah the prophet* as a place where manuscripts differ.
- **vv4–6 "John the baptizer… camel's hair and a leather belt around his waist… locusts and wild honey"** — *ho baptizōn*, **the baptizer**, the one who baptizes. *Baptisma metanoias*, **a baptism of repentance**: *metanoia* is a change of mind, a turning back. John is dressed as Elijah was, *a hairy man, with a leather belt girded around his waist* (2 Kings 1:8), the prophet promised before the day of the LORD (Malachi 4:5). Locusts are clean food, *the locust of every kind* (Leviticus 11:22).
- **vv10–11 "the heavens torn open… You are My Son, the beloved; in You I am well pleased"** — *schizomenous*, **torn open**, as Isaiah prayed, *Oh, that You would tear open the heavens and come down* (Isaiah 64:1); Mark uses the same verb at the end for the curtain of the sanctuary (15:38). The voice joins *You are My son* (Psalm 2:7) and *My chosen one, in whom My soul delights* (Isaiah 42:1). *Ho agapētos*, **the beloved**, is the Greek word for Isaac, *your beloved one* (Genesis 22:2). In Matthew the voice says *This is My Son* (Matthew 3:17); in Mark it speaks to Jesus.
- **vv12–13 "the Spirit drove Him out… forty days… with the wild animals, and the angels served Him"** — *ekballei*, **drove out**, the verb Mark uses for driving out demons (1:34, 39). Forty days, as Moses *was there with the LORD forty days and forty nights* (Exodus 34:28). Only Mark has the wild animals.
- **vv14–15 "The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God has come near; repent, and believe in the good news"** — *kairos*, **the time**, the appointed time, not the passing of hours. *Ēngiken*, **has come near**, the word of Matthew 3:2 and 4:17; Mark says *the kingdom of God* where Matthew says *the kingdom of heaven*.
- **vv16–20 "fishers of people… they left their father Zebedee in the boat"** — Jeremiah's *fishers* were sent to gather the people for judgment, *I am about to send for many fishers… and they will fish for them* (Jeremiah 16:16). Elisha asked first to kiss his father and mother (1 Kings 19:20); these men leave at once.
- **vv21–28 "as one who has authority, and not as the scribes… What have You to do with us… the Holy One of God"** — *exousia*, **authority**, the word that runs through the chapter (vv22, 27) and into 2:10. *Ti hēmin kai soi*, **What have You to do with us**, word for word **What to us and to You?**, as the widow said to Elijah, *What have I to do with you, man of God?* (1 Kings 17:18). *Phimōthēti*, **Be silent**, word for word **Be muzzled**. In v31 *ēgeiren*, **raised her up**, is the verb later used of Jesus rising (16:6), and she *began to serve*, *diēkonei*, as the angels did (v13).
- **vv40–42 "A leper came to Him… Moved with anger, He stretched out His hand and touched him"** — *lepros*, **leper**: the law's *tsara'at* (Leviticus 13:45–46), the person who must live *outside the camp* and cry *Unclean!* Jesus touches him. *Orgistheis*, **moved with anger**, is the edition's reading, and it marks the word as a place where manuscripts differ; at what Jesus is angry, the verse does not say.
- **vv43–45 "show yourself to the priest, and offer for your cleansing what Moses commanded"** — the law of the blight's cleansing, *He shall be brought to the priest* (Leviticus 14:2). *Embrimēsamenos*, **sternly warned**, a word for snorting with anger. The man spreads the word anyway, and Jesus, who was free to enter towns, now must stay outside them, like the leper before Him.
