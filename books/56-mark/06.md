# Mark 6

## A Prophet Without Honor

**1** He went away from there and came to His home town, and His disciples followed Him.

**2** When the sabbath came, He began to teach in the synagogue, and many who heard Him were astonished, saying, "Where did this man get these things? What is this wisdom given to Him, and these mighty works done by His hands?

**3** Is this not the carpenter, the son of Mary, and the brother of James and Joses and Judas and Simon? Are not His sisters here with us?" They took offense at Him.

**4** Jesus said to them, "A prophet is not without honor except in his home town, among his relatives and in his own house."

**5** He could do no mighty work there, except that He laid His hands on a few sick people and healed them.

**6** He was amazed at their unbelief. Then He went around the villages, teaching.

## The Twelve Sent Out

**7** He called the twelve to Him and began to send them out two by two, and gave them authority over the unclean spirits.

**8** He commanded them to take nothing for the road except a staff: no bread, no bag, no copper in their belts;

**9** but to wear sandals, and not to put on two tunics.

**10** He said to them, "Wherever you enter a house, stay there until you leave that place.

**11** If any place will not welcome you or listen to you, as you leave, shake off the dust under your feet as a testimony to them."

**12** They went out and preached that people should repent.

**13** They drove out many demons, and anointed many sick people with oil and healed them.

## The Death of John

**14** King Herod heard of it, for His name had become known. People were saying, "John the baptizer has been raised from the dead, and that is why these powers are at work in him."

**15** Others said, "It is Elijah." Others said, "He is a prophet, like one of the prophets."

**16** When Herod heard it, he said, "John, whom I beheaded, has been raised."

**17** For Herod himself had sent and seized John and bound him in prison, because of Herodias, the wife of his brother Philip, whom he had married.

**18** For John had been saying to Herod, "It is not lawful for you to have your brother's wife."

**19** Herodias held it against him and wanted to kill him, but she could not;

**20** for Herod feared John, knowing that he was a righteous and holy man, and he kept him safe. When he heard him he was greatly perplexed, and yet he liked to listen to him.

**21** An opportunity came when Herod on his birthday gave a banquet for his officials, the commanders and the leading men of Galilee.

**22** When the daughter of Herodias herself came in and danced, she pleased Herod and those reclining at table with him. The king said to the girl, "Ask me for whatever you want, and I will give it to you."

**23** He swore to her, "Whatever you ask me, I will give you, up to half of my kingdom."

**24** She went out and said to her mother, "What shall I ask for?" She said, "The head of John the baptizer."

**25** At once she hurried back to the king and asked, saying, "I want you to give me at once on a platter the head of John the Baptist."

**26** The king was deeply grieved, but because of his oaths and those reclining at table, he did not want to refuse her.

**27** At once the king sent an executioner with orders to bring his head. He went and beheaded him in the prison,

**28** brought his head on a platter and gave it to the girl, and the girl gave it to her mother.

**29** When his disciples heard of it, they came and took his body and laid it in a tomb.

## Five Thousand Fed

**30** The apostles gathered around Jesus and told Him all that they had done and taught.

**31** He said to them, "Come away by yourselves to a deserted place and rest a while." For many were coming and going, and they had no time even to eat.

**32** They went away in the boat to a deserted place by themselves.

**33** Many saw them going and recognized them, and they ran there on foot from all the towns and got there ahead of them.

**34** When He went ashore, He saw a great crowd, and He was moved with compassion for them, because they were like sheep that have no shepherd; and He began to teach them many things.

**35** When it was already late, His disciples came to Him and said, "This is a deserted place, and the hour is already late.

**36** Send them away so that they can go into the surrounding countryside and villages and buy themselves something to eat."

**37** He answered them, "You give them something to eat." They said to Him, "Are we to go and buy two hundred denarii worth of bread and give it to them to eat?"

**38** He said to them, "How many loaves do you have? Go and see." When they had found out, they said, "Five, and two fish."

**39** He ordered them to have everyone sit down in groups on the green grass.

**40** They sat down in companies, by hundreds and by fifties.

**41** Taking the five loaves and the two fish, He looked up to heaven, said the blessing, broke the loaves and gave them to His disciples to set before the people; and He divided the two fish among them all.

**42** They all ate and were satisfied.

**43** They picked up twelve baskets full of broken pieces, and of the fish.

**44** Those who ate the loaves were five thousand men.

## Walking on the Sea

**45** At once He made His disciples get into the boat and go ahead of Him to the other side, to Bethsaida, while He sent the crowd away.

**46** After He had said goodbye to them, He went away to the mountain to pray.

**47** When evening came, the boat was in the middle of the sea, and He was alone on the land.

**48** He saw them straining at the oars, for the wind was against them. In the fourth watch of the night He came toward them, walking on the sea; and He meant to pass by them.

**49** When they saw Him walking on the sea, they thought it was a ghost and cried out;

**50** for they all saw Him and were terrified. At once He spoke with them and said to them, "Take heart; I am the One. Do not be afraid."

**51** He got into the boat with them, and the wind stopped. They were utterly astounded,

**52** for they had not understood about the loaves; their hearts were hardened.

**53** When they had crossed over, they came to land at Gennesaret and moored there.

**54** When they got out of the boat, at once the people recognized Him,

**55** ran about that whole region, and began to carry the sick on their mats to wherever they heard He was.

**56** Wherever He went, into villages or towns or countryside, they laid the sick in the marketplaces and begged Him that they might touch even the tassel of His cloak; and all who touched it were made well.

## Notes

- **vv1–6 "the carpenter, the son of Mary… they took offense at Him… He was amazed at their unbelief"** — *ho tektōn*, **the carpenter**; Matthew has *the carpenter's son* (Matthew 13:55). *The son of Mary*, with no father named. *Eskandalizonto*, **they took offense**, the verb of 4:17. *Ouk edynato*, **He could do no**: Mark says *could not*, where Matthew says *did not do many* (Matthew 13:58). The brothers' names are as Matthew has them, with *Joses* for *Joseph*.
- **vv7–13 "two by two… nothing for the road except a staff… shake off the dust… anointed many sick people with oil"** — in Matthew the twelve are told to take *no sandals, no staff* (Matthew 10:10); Mark allows both. *Eis martyrion autois*, **as a testimony to them**, the words of 1:44. Only Mark has the anointing with oil.
- **vv14–16 "King Herod… John… has been raised from the dead… Elijah… a prophet"** — Matthew calls him *Herod the tetrarch* (Matthew 14:1); Mark gives him the title *king*, used five times in the story. The three guesses come back at 8:28. *Ēgerthē*, **has been raised**, the word of resurrection.
- **vv17–20 "the wife of his brother Philip… It is not lawful for you to have your brother's wife… a righteous and holy man"** — the law, *You shall not uncover the nakedness of your brother's wife* (Leviticus 18:16). *Eneichen*, **held it against him**, a grudge kept. Only Mark tells that Herod protected John and *liked to listen to him*.
- **vv21–29 "on his birthday gave a banquet… up to half of my kingdom… on a platter"** — *heōs hēmisous tēs basileias mou*, **up to half of my kingdom**, as the Persian king promised Esther, *Even to half of the kingdom, it shall be given you* (Esther 5:3). *Spekoulatōra*, **an executioner**, a word borrowed from Latin. *Ptōma*, **body**, a corpse. The mother names him *the baptizer* (v24), the girl *the Baptist* (v25), Mark's two words.
- **vv30–44 "like sheep that have no shepherd… in groups on the green grass… by hundreds and by fifties… twelve baskets full"** — the words Moses used when he asked for a leader, *so that the congregation of the LORD may not be like sheep that have no shepherd* (Numbers 27:17). The people are set out as Israel was in the wilderness, *officers of hundreds, officers of fifties* (Exodus 18:21). *Symposia symposia… prasiai prasiai*, word for word **dinner party by dinner party… garden bed by garden bed**, the crowd laid out in rows of color on the green. Elisha fed a hundred men with twenty loaves, *they ate and had some left* (2 Kings 4:44). A denarius was a day's wage (Matthew 20:2).
- **vv45–52 "walking on the sea… He meant to pass by them… Take heart; I am the One… their hearts were hardened"** — Job says of God, *He alone stretches out the heavens and treads on the back of the sea* (Job 9:8), and *If He passes by me, I do not see Him* (Job 9:11). *Parelthein*, **to pass by**, is the word of the LORD's glory before Moses, *when My glory passes by* (Exodus 33:22). *Egō eimi*, **I am the One**, as Matthew 14:27, the words that can mean *It is I* and recall the name of God (Exodus 3:14). *Pepōrōmenē*, **hardened**, from the root of *the hardness of their hearts* in 3:5, now said of the disciples.
