# Mark 2

## A Paralyzed Man Forgiven and Healed

**1** When He came back to Capernaum after some days, it was heard that He was at home.

**2** Many gathered, so that there was no longer room for them, not even at the door; and He was speaking the word to them.

**3** Some people came, bringing to Him a paralyzed man, carried by four of them.

**4** When they could not bring him to Him because of the crowd, they removed the roof above where He was; and when they had dug through, they let down the mat on which the paralyzed man was lying.

**5** When Jesus saw their faith, He said to the paralyzed man, "Child, your sins are forgiven."

**6** Some of the scribes were sitting there, reasoning in their hearts,

**7** "Why does this man speak like this? He is blaspheming! Who can forgive sins but God alone?"

**8** At once Jesus, knowing in His spirit that they were reasoning this way within themselves, said to them, "Why are you reasoning about these things in your hearts?

**9** Which is easier: to say to the paralyzed man, 'Your sins are forgiven,' or to say, 'Get up, pick up your mat, and walk'?

**10** But so that you may know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins" — He said to the paralyzed man,

**11** "I say to you, get up, pick up your mat, and go home."

**12** He got up, at once picked up the mat, and went out in front of them all, so that they were all amazed and glorified God, saying, "We have never seen anything like this!"

## The Call of Levi

**13** He went out again beside the sea; all the crowd came to Him, and He taught them.

**14** As He passed by, He saw Levi the son of Alphaeus sitting at the tax booth, and He said to him, "Follow Me." He got up and followed Him.

**15** As He was reclining at table in his house, many tax collectors and sinners were reclining with Jesus and His disciples; for there were many of them, and they followed Him.

**16** When the scribes of the Pharisees saw that He was eating with the sinners and tax collectors, they said to His disciples, "Why does He eat with tax collectors and sinners?"

**17** When Jesus heard it, He said to them, "It is not the healthy who need a doctor, but those who are ill. I did not come to call the righteous, but sinners."

## The Bridegroom

**18** John's disciples and the Pharisees were fasting. People came and said to Him, "Why do John's disciples and the disciples of the Pharisees fast, but Your disciples do not fast?"

**19** Jesus said to them, "Can the wedding guests fast while the bridegroom is with them? As long as they have the bridegroom with them, they cannot fast.

**20** But the days will come when the bridegroom is taken away from them, and then they will fast, on that day.

**21** No one sews a patch of unshrunk cloth on an old garment; otherwise the patch pulls away from it, the new from the old, and the tear becomes worse.

**22** No one pours new wine into old wineskins; otherwise the wine will burst the skins, and the wine is lost, and the skins as well. New wine goes into fresh wineskins."

## Lord of the Sabbath

**23** One sabbath He was going through the grainfields, and as they made their way His disciples began to pick the heads of grain.

**24** The Pharisees said to Him, "Why are they doing what is not lawful on the sabbath?"

**25** He said to them, "Have you never read what David did when he was in need and hungry, he and those with him —

**26** how he entered the house of God in the time of Abiathar the high priest, and ate the bread of the Presence, which it is not lawful for anyone but the priests to eat, and gave some also to those who were with him?"

**27** He said to them, "The sabbath was made for man, and not man for the sabbath.

**28** So the Son of Man is Lord even of the sabbath."

## Notes

- **vv3–4 "carried by four of them… they removed the roof… let down the mat"** — *apestegasan tēn stegēn*, **they unroofed the roof**, and *exoryxantes*, **dug through**. *Krabattos*, **mat**, a poor man's bedding; Matthew has *klinē*, **bed** (Matthew 9:2).
- **vv5–7 "Child, your sins are forgiven… Who can forgive sins but God alone?"** — *aphientai*, **are forgiven**, said without naming who forgives. The scribes hear the LORD's own word, *I, I am He who blots out your transgressions* (Isaiah 43:25), and *heis ho theos*, **God alone**, word for word **one, God**, is the creed, *the LORD is one* (Deuteronomy 6:4).
- **vv8–12 "knowing in His spirit… the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins"** — *tō pneumati autou*, **in His spirit**, Jesus' own spirit, lowercase. *Ho huios tou anthrōpou*, **the Son of Man**, Jesus' name for Himself, from Daniel's vision, *there came one like a son of man* (Daniel 7:13), to whom *authority* was given. The visible healing is the proof of the forgiveness no one can see. The sentence breaks off as He turns to the man.
- **vv14–15 "Levi the son of Alphaeus… reclining at table in his house"** — Matthew names him *Matthew* (Matthew 9:9). *Telōnion*, **the tax booth**, where tolls were taken. *In his house*: Levi's, or Jesus' own (2:1, *at home*); the Greek does not make it plain. *Katakeisthai*, **reclining**, the posture at a feast.
- **vv18–20 "Can the wedding guests fast while the bridegroom is with them?… taken away from them"** — *hoi huioi tou nymphōnos*, word for word **the sons of the bridal chamber**, the groom's friends. The prophets call the LORD Israel's bridegroom, *as the bridegroom rejoices over the bride, so your God will rejoice over you* (Isaiah 62:5). *Aparthē*, **is taken away**, is found only here and in the same saying in Matthew and Luke: the first hint of His death.
- **vv21–22 "a patch of unshrunk cloth… new wine into old wineskins"** — *agnaphou*, **unshrunk**, not yet washed by the fuller; it shrinks and tears the old cloth. *Kainos*, **new**, and *palaios*, **old**, run through both sayings.
- **vv23–26 "pick the heads of grain… in the time of Abiathar the high priest… the bread of the Presence"** — the law allowed picking by hand in a neighbor's field, *you may pick the heads with your hand* (Deuteronomy 23:25); the Pharisees object to doing it on the sabbath. In 1 Samuel the priest who gave David the bread is *Ahimelech the priest* (1 Samuel 21:1), Abiathar's father; *epi Abiathar* may mean **in the time of Abiathar**, or **at the passage about Abiathar**, as Mark names a passage of Exodus *epi tou batou*, **at the passage about the bush** (12:26). *Hoi artoi tēs protheseōs*, **the bread of the Presence**, *set on the table… before Me always* (Exodus 25:30), was given to David as *holy bread* (1 Samuel 21:6).
- **vv27–28 "The sabbath was made for man… the Son of Man is Lord even of the sabbath"** — only Mark has v27. The sabbath was given as rest for all, *you, or your son, or your daughter, your male slave… or your animal* (Exodus 20:10). *Anthrōpos*, **man**, and *the Son of Man* are one word: the sabbath was made for man, and the Son of Man is its Lord.
