# Mark 4

## The Sower

**1** Again He began to teach beside the sea. A very large crowd gathered around Him, so that He got into a boat on the sea and sat in it, and the whole crowd was on the land beside the sea.

**2** He taught them many things in parables, and in His teaching He said to them:

**3** "Listen! A sower went out to sow.

**4** As he sowed, some seed fell along the path, and the birds came and ate it up.

**5** Other seed fell on rocky ground, where it did not have much soil, and it sprang up at once because the soil was not deep.

**6** When the sun rose it was scorched, and because it had no root it withered.

**7** Other seed fell among the thorns, and the thorns came up and choked it, and it bore no fruit.

**8** Other seeds fell into the good soil and bore fruit, coming up and growing, and they yielded thirtyfold, sixtyfold and a hundredfold."

**9** He said, "Whoever has ears to hear, let him hear."

## Why Parables

**10** When He was alone, those around Him with the twelve asked Him about the parables.

**11** He said to them, "To you the mystery of the kingdom of God has been given; but for those outside everything comes in parables,

**12** so that 'they may look and look, and not see; and listen and listen, and not understand; otherwise they might turn and be forgiven.'"

**13** He said to them, "Do you not understand this parable? How then will you understand all the parables?

**14** The sower sows the word.

**15** These are the ones along the path where the word is sown: when they hear, Satan comes at once and takes away the word that was sown in them.

**16** In the same way, these are the ones sown on rocky ground: when they hear the word, they at once receive it with joy;

**17** yet they have no root in themselves but last only a while; then, when trouble or persecution comes because of the word, at once they fall away.

**18** Others are the ones sown among the thorns: these are the ones who have heard the word,

**19** but the worries of this age, the deceitfulness of wealth and the desires for other things come in and choke the word, and it becomes unfruitful.

**20** Those sown on the good soil are the ones who hear the word and welcome it, and bear fruit, thirtyfold, sixtyfold and a hundredfold."

## A Lamp and a Measure

**21** He said to them, "Is a lamp brought in to be put under a basket, or under a bed? Is it not to be put on the lampstand?

**22** For nothing is hidden except to be made known, nor is anything secret except to be brought into the light.

**23** If anyone has ears to hear, let him hear."

**24** He said to them, "Pay attention to what you hear. With the measure you use, it will be measured to you, and more will be added to you.

**25** For whoever has, more will be given to him; and whoever does not have, even what he has will be taken from him."

## Seeds Growing

**26** He said, "The kingdom of God is like this: a man scatters seed on the ground,

**27** and sleeps and rises night and day, and the seed sprouts and grows, he does not know how.

**28** The earth bears fruit by itself: first the blade, then the head, then the full grain in the head.

**29** When the grain is ready, at once he puts in the sickle, because the harvest has come."

**30** He said, "To what shall we compare the kingdom of God, or what parable shall we use for it?

**31** It is like a mustard seed, which, when it is sown on the ground, is the smallest of all the seeds on the ground;

**32** yet when it is sown it grows up and becomes larger than all the garden plants, and puts out large branches, so that the birds of the sky can nest in its shade."

**33** With many such parables He spoke the word to them, as they were able to hear it.

**34** He did not speak to them without a parable; but in private He explained everything to His own disciples.

## The Storm

**35** That day, when evening came, He said to them, "Let us go across to the other side."

**36** Leaving the crowd, they took Him with them in the boat, just as He was; and other boats were with Him.

**37** A great windstorm arose, and the waves were breaking into the boat, so that the boat was already filling.

**38** He was in the stern, asleep on the cushion. They woke Him and said to Him, "Teacher, do You not care that we are perishing?"

**39** He woke up, rebuked the wind, and said to the sea, "Be quiet! Be still!" The wind stopped, and there was a great calm.

**40** He said to them, "Why are you afraid? Have you still no faith?"

**41** They were filled with great fear and said to one another, "Who then is this, that even the wind and the sea obey Him?"

## Notes

- **vv3–9 "Listen! A sower went out to sow… thirtyfold, sixtyfold and a hundredfold"** — *Akouete*, **Listen!**, opens the parable, and *let him hear* closes it (v9): it is a parable about hearing. *Idou*, **look**, after it is cut. Mark counts up, *thirty, sixty, a hundred*; Matthew counts down (Matthew 13:8).
- **vv10–12 "the mystery of the kingdom of God… they may look and look, and not see… otherwise they might turn and be forgiven"** — *mystērion*, **mystery**, a secret now told to some; Matthew has *mysteries* (Matthew 13:11). The words are Isaiah's commission, *otherwise they might see with their eyes, and hear with their ears, and understand with their heart, and turn and be healed* (Isaiah 6:10), in a short form that ends *be forgiven*. *Hina*, **so that**, gives the purpose, as in Isaiah, where the prophet is sent to make the heart *fat*.
- **vv13–20 "The sower sows the word… Satan comes at once and takes away the word"** — *ho logos*, **the word**, eight times in the explanation: the seed is the message (2:2). *Satan* where Matthew has *the evil one* (Matthew 13:19). *Skandalizontai*, **they fall away**, word for word **they are tripped up**.
- **vv21–25 "Is a lamp brought in… With the measure you use… whoever has, more will be given"** — *erchetai ho lychnos*, word for word **does the lamp come**, as if the lamp were a person arriving. The sayings stand apart in Matthew (Matthew 5:15; 7:2; 13:12); Mark sets them together after the sower. *Blepete ti akouete*, **Pay attention to what you hear**: the measure is how one listens.
- **vv26–29 "he does not know how… the earth bears fruit by itself… at once he puts in the sickle, because the harvest has come"** — only Mark has this parable. *Automatē*, **by itself**, the Greek word behind *automatic*. The last words are Joel's, *Put in the sickle, for the harvest is ripe* (Joel 3:13).
- **vv30–32 "a mustard seed… the birds of the sky can nest in its shade"** — the great tree of the prophets, *in the shade of its branches they will live* (Ezekiel 17:23), and Nebuchadnezzar's tree, *the birds of the heavens lived in its branches* (Daniel 4:12): the smallest seed becomes the shelter of the nations. Matthew has *becomes a tree* (Matthew 13:32); Mark says *larger than all the garden plants*.
- **vv35–41 "asleep on the cushion… Be quiet! Be still!… Who then is this, that even the wind and the sea obey Him?"** — Jonah too was asleep in the storm (Jonah 1:5). *Pephimōso*, **Be still**, is the verb of 1:25, *Be silent*: He speaks to the sea as to an unclean spirit. The psalms give this power to God, *You rule the raging of the sea; when its waves rise, You still them* (Psalm 89:9), and *He made the storm a whisper* (Psalm 107:29). *Ephobēthēsan phobon megan*, **filled with great fear**, word for word **feared a great fear**: the calm frightens them more than the storm.
