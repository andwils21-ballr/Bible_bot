# Mark 7

## The Tradition of the Elders

**1** The Pharisees and some of the scribes who had come from Jerusalem gathered around Him,

**2** and they saw that some of His disciples were eating their bread with defiled hands, that is, unwashed.

**3** (The Pharisees, and all the Jews, do not eat unless they wash their hands with the fist, holding to the tradition of the elders;

**4** and when they come from the marketplace, they do not eat unless they wash themselves; and there are many other things they have received and hold to, the washing of cups, pitchers and copper pots and dining couches.)

**5** So the Pharisees and the scribes asked Him, "Why do Your disciples not walk according to the tradition of the elders, but eat their bread with defiled hands?"

**6** He said to them, "Isaiah prophesied rightly about you hypocrites, as it is written: 'This people honors Me with their lips, but their heart is far from Me;

**7** in vain do they worship Me, teaching as teachings the commandments of men.'

**8** You leave the commandment of God and hold to the tradition of men."

**9** He said to them, "How well you set aside the commandment of God in order to keep your tradition!

**10** For Moses said, 'Honor your father and your mother,' and, 'Whoever speaks evil of father or mother shall surely die.'

**11** But you say, 'If a man says to his father or mother, "Whatever support you might have had from me is Corban"' (that is, a gift to God),

**12** then you no longer let him do anything for his father or mother,

**13** making the word of God void by your tradition that you have handed down. You do many things like that."

## What Comes Out of a Person

**14** He called the crowd to Him again and said to them, "Listen to Me, all of you, and understand:

**15** there is nothing outside a person that by going into him can make him unclean; but the things that come out of a person are what make a person unclean."

**17** When He had gone into the house, away from the crowd, His disciples asked Him about the parable.

**18** He said to them, "Are you also without understanding? Do you not see that whatever goes into a person from outside cannot make him unclean,

**19** since it does not go into his heart but into his stomach, and is expelled into the latrine?" So He declared all foods clean.

**20** He said, "What comes out of a person is what makes a person unclean.

**21** For from within, out of the human heart, come evil thoughts, sexual immorality, thefts, murders,

**22** adulteries, greed, wickedness, deceit, sensuality, envy, slander, arrogance, foolishness.

**23** All these evil things come from within, and they make a person unclean."

## A Syrophoenician Woman

**24** From there He got up and went away to the region of Tyre. He entered a house and wanted no one to know it, but He could not be hidden.

**25** At once a woman whose little daughter had an unclean spirit heard about Him, and she came and fell at His feet.

**26** The woman was a Greek, a Syrophoenician by birth; and she asked Him to drive the demon out of her daughter.

**27** He said to her, "Let the children be fed first, for it is not right to take the children's bread and throw it to the little dogs."

**28** She answered Him, "Lord, even the little dogs under the table eat the children's crumbs."

**29** He said to her, "Because of this word, go; the demon has gone out of your daughter."

**30** She went home and found the child lying on the bed, and the demon gone.

## Ephphatha

**31** Then He went out again from the region of Tyre and came through Sidon to the Sea of Galilee, in the region of the Decapolis.

**32** They brought to Him a man who was deaf and could hardly speak, and begged Him to lay His hand on him.

**33** Taking him aside from the crowd by himself, He put His fingers into his ears, and after spitting He touched his tongue.

**34** Looking up to heaven, He sighed and said to him, "Ephphatha," which means, "Be opened."

**35** His ears were opened, the bond of his tongue was loosed, and he spoke plainly.

**36** He ordered them to tell no one; but the more He ordered them, the more widely they proclaimed it.

**37** They were astonished beyond measure, saying, "He has done all things well; He even makes the deaf hear and the mute speak."

## Notes

- **vv1–5 "defiled hands, that is, unwashed… with the fist… the tradition of the elders"** — *koinais chersin*, **defiled hands**, word for word **common hands**: *koinos*, **common**, is the opposite of holy, and Mark explains it. *Pygmē*, **with the fist**, is obscure: perhaps a cupped hand of water, or washing up to the wrist. *Baptisōntai*, **wash themselves**, and *baptismous*, **washing**, are the words of baptism. *The tradition of the elders*: rules handed down beside the written law.
- **vv6–8 "This people honors Me with their lips, but their heart is far from Me"** — Isaiah 29:13 in its Greek form, as Matthew 15:8–9. The Hebrew has *their fear of Me is a commandment of men learned by rote*; the Greek, *teaching as teachings the commandments of men*. *Hypokritēs*, **hypocrite**, is the word for an actor.
- **vv14–19 "nothing outside a person… can make him unclean… Thus He declared all foods clean"** — v16 is not in the edition, so the numbering here skips from 15 to 17. *Katharizōn panta ta brōmata*, **declaring all foods clean**, word for word **cleansing all foods**, is Mark's comment on what the saying means. Matthew does not have it (Matthew 15:17).
- **vv31–37 "deaf and could hardly speak… Ephphatha… He even makes the deaf hear and the mute speak"** — *mogilalon*, **who could hardly speak**, is the word of Isaiah's promise in Greek, *glōssa mogilalōn*, **the tongue of the mute** (Isaiah 35:6), and the crowd's words echo it: *the ears of the deaf unstopped… the tongue of the mute sing for joy* (Isaiah 35:5–6). *Ephphatha* is Aramaic, and Mark gives its meaning.
