# 2 Chronicles 30

## The Letters to All Israel

**1** Hezekiah sent to all Israel and Judah, and also wrote letters to Ephraim and Manasseh, that they should come to the house of the LORD at Jerusalem to keep the Passover to the LORD, the God of Israel.

**2** For the king and his officials and all the assembly in Jerusalem had taken counsel to keep the Passover in the second month,

**3** because they could not keep it at its time, since not enough priests had consecrated themselves, and the people had not gathered at Jerusalem.

**4** The thing seemed right to the king and to all the assembly.

**5** So they decreed to make a proclamation throughout all Israel, from Beersheba to Dan, that they should come to keep the Passover to the LORD, the God of Israel, at Jerusalem, for they had not kept it in great numbers as it is written.

**6** So the couriers went with the letters from the king and his officials throughout all Israel and Judah, as the king had commanded, saying, "Sons of Israel, return to the LORD, the God of Abraham, Isaac and Israel, so that He may return to the remnant of you who have escaped from the hand of the kings of Assyria.

**7** Do not be like your fathers and your brothers, who broke faith with the LORD, the God of their fathers, so that He made them a desolation, as you see.

**8** Now do not stiffen your necks as your fathers did. Give your hand to the LORD and come to His sanctuary, which He has consecrated forever, and serve the LORD your God, so that His burning anger may turn away from you.

**9** For if you return to the LORD, your brothers and your sons will find compassion before their captors and return to this land. For the LORD your God is gracious and merciful, and will not turn His face away from you if you return to Him."

**10** So the couriers passed from city to city through the land of Ephraim and Manasseh, as far as Zebulun, but they laughed them to scorn and mocked them.

**11** Yet some men of Asher, Manasseh and Zebulun humbled themselves and came to Jerusalem.

**12** In Judah also the hand of God was on them to give them one heart to do the command of the king and the officials by the word of the LORD.

## The Feast in the Second Month

**13** Many people gathered at Jerusalem to keep the Festival of Unleavened Bread in the second month, a very great assembly.

**14** They arose and removed the altars that were in Jerusalem, and they removed all the incense altars and threw them into the Kidron Valley.

**15** They slaughtered the Passover lamb on the fourteenth day of the second month. The priests and the Levites were ashamed, and they consecrated themselves and brought burnt offerings into the house of the LORD.

**16** They stood at their posts as prescribed, according to the instruction of Moses the man of God. The priests dashed the blood that they received from the hand of the Levites.

**17** For there were many in the assembly who had not consecrated themselves, so the Levites had charge of slaughtering the Passover lambs for everyone who was not clean, to consecrate them to the LORD.

**18** For a majority of the people, many of them from Ephraim, Manasseh, Issachar and Zebulun, had not cleansed themselves, yet they ate the Passover otherwise than as it is written. But Hezekiah prayed for them, saying, "May the good LORD pardon everyone

**19** who sets his heart to seek God, the LORD, the God of his fathers, even though not according to the purity of the sanctuary."

**20** The LORD heard Hezekiah and healed the people.

**21** The sons of Israel who were present at Jerusalem kept the Festival of Unleavened Bread seven days with great joy, and the Levites and the priests praised the LORD day by day with loud instruments to the LORD.

**22** Hezekiah spoke encouragingly to all the Levites who showed good understanding in the service of the LORD. They ate the food of the festival for the seven days, sacrificing peace offerings and giving thanks to the LORD, the God of their fathers.

**23** Then the whole assembly took counsel to keep another seven days, and they kept another seven days with joy.

**24** For Hezekiah king of Judah gave the assembly a thousand bulls and seven thousand sheep for offerings, and the officials gave the assembly a thousand bulls and ten thousand sheep. The priests also consecrated themselves in great numbers.

**25** All the assembly of Judah rejoiced, with the priests and the Levites, and all the assembly that came from Israel, and the guests who came from the land of Israel and who lived in Judah.

**26** So there was great joy in Jerusalem, for since the days of Solomon son of David king of Israel there had been nothing like it in Jerusalem.

**27** Then the Levitical priests arose and blessed the people. Their voice was heard, and their prayer came to His holy dwelling in heaven.

## Notes

- **vv1–27** — not in Kings, which tells of Josiah's Passover as the first since the judges (2 Kings 23:22). Chronicles gives Hezekiah one as well, and opens it to the north.
- **vv2–3, 15 "to keep the Passover in the second month"** — the law allows it for one who *is unclean because of a corpse, or is away on a long journey*: *In the second month, on the fourteenth day… they shall keep it* (Numbers 9:10–11). Here the whole people use the provision, for both reasons: the priests were not consecrated, and the people were not gathered.
- **v5 "from Beersheba to Dan"** — the whole land, as before the kingdom divided. Jehoshaphat's reform reached *from Beersheba to the hill country of Ephraim* (19:4).
- **vv6–9 "return to the LORD… so that He may return… if you return to Him"** — *shuvu… ve-yashov… u-ve-shuvkhem*, the verb *shuv* five times in four verses: Israel's turning, the LORD's turning, and the captives' return to the land.
- **v8 "do not stiffen your necks… Give your hand to the LORD"** — the charge of Exodus, *it is a stiff-necked people* (Exodus 32:9). *Tenu yad*, **give a hand**, is a pledge, the word for Solomon's officials pledging themselves to him (1 Chronicles 29:24).
- **v9 "the LORD your God is gracious and merciful"** — *chanun ve-rachum*, the name proclaimed on Sinai, *God merciful and gracious* (Exodus 34:6), with the two words in the other order.
- **vv18–20 "May the good LORD pardon everyone who sets his heart to seek God… The LORD heard Hezekiah and healed the people"** — the uncleanness of those who ate is not denied; the king asks that a heart that seeks be counted over it, and the LORD answers. *Healed*, *va-yirpa*, the word of the promise at the dedication, *I will hear from heaven… and heal their land* (7:14).
- **v26 "since the days of Solomon"** — since the dedication, when the people also kept the feast and then seven days more (7:8–9).
- **v27 "their prayer came to His holy dwelling in heaven"** — Solomon's prayer, *Hear from the place of Your dwelling, from the heavens* (6:21), answered.
