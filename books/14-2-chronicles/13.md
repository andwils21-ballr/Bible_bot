# 2 Chronicles 13

## Abijah King of Judah

**1** In the eighteenth year of King Jeroboam, Abijah began to reign over Judah.

**2** He reigned three years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Micaiah the daughter of Uriel of Gibeah. There was war between Abijah and Jeroboam.

**3** Abijah went out to battle with an army of mighty men of war, four hundred thousand chosen men, and Jeroboam drew up his battle line against him with eight hundred thousand chosen men, mighty men of valor.

## Abijah's Speech on Mount Zemaraim

**4** Abijah stood up on Mount Zemaraim, which is in the hill country of Ephraim, and said, "Hear me, Jeroboam and all Israel!

**5** Ought you not to know that the LORD, the God of Israel, gave the kingship over Israel to David forever, to him and to his sons, by a covenant of salt?

**6** Yet Jeroboam son of Nebat, a servant of Solomon son of David, rose up and rebelled against his lord.

**7** Worthless men gathered around him, scoundrels, and they proved too strong for Rehoboam son of Solomon, when Rehoboam was young and tender of heart and could not hold his own against them.

**8** And now you think you can hold your own against the kingdom of the LORD in the hand of the sons of David, because you are a great multitude and have with you the golden calves that Jeroboam made you for gods.

**9** Have you not driven out the priests of the LORD, the sons of Aaron, and the Levites, and made priests for yourselves like the peoples of other lands? Whoever comes to consecrate himself with a young bull and seven rams becomes a priest of what are no gods.

**10** But as for us, the LORD is our God, and we have not forsaken Him. We have priests ministering to the LORD who are sons of Aaron, and Levites at their work.

**11** They burn burnt offerings to the LORD every morning and every evening, and fragrant incense, and set out the bread on the pure table, and the golden lampstand with its lamps to burn every evening. For we keep the charge of the LORD our God, but you have forsaken Him.

**12** God is with us at our head, and His priests with the battle trumpets to sound the alarm against you. Sons of Israel, do not fight against the LORD, the God of your fathers, for you will not succeed."

## The Battle

**13** But Jeroboam had sent an ambush around to come at them from behind, so that his army was in front of Judah and the ambush behind them.

**14** When Judah turned, the battle was before them and behind. They cried out to the LORD, and the priests blew the trumpets.

**15** Then the men of Judah raised the battle shout. And it came to pass, as the men of Judah shouted, that God struck down Jeroboam and all Israel before Abijah and Judah.

**16** The sons of Israel fled before Judah, and God gave them into their hand.

**17** Abijah and his people struck them with a great slaughter, and five hundred thousand chosen men of Israel fell slain.

**18** So the sons of Israel were humbled at that time, and the sons of Judah prevailed, because they relied on the LORD, the God of their fathers.

**19** Abijah pursued Jeroboam and took cities from him: Bethel and its villages, Jeshanah and its villages, and Ephrain and its villages.

**20** Jeroboam did not recover his strength again in the days of Abijah. The LORD struck him, and he died.

**21** But Abijah grew strong. He took fourteen wives and fathered twenty-two sons and sixteen daughters.

**22** The rest of the acts of Abijah, his ways and his sayings, are written in the account of the prophet Iddo.

## Notes

- **vv1–2 and 1 Kings 15:1–8** — Kings calls him *Abijam* and judges him in one line: *He walked in all the sins that his father had done before him* (1 Kings 15:3). Chronicles gives no verdict and tells instead of his speech and his victory. His mother is *Micaiah the daughter of Uriel of Gibeah*; 11:20 and Kings name her *Maacah*.
- **v5 "by a covenant of salt"** — *berit melach*, the phrase of the priests' portion: *It is a covenant of salt forever before the LORD* (Numbers 18:19). Salt does not spoil; the covenant with David's house is meant to last the same way.
- **v7 "young and tender of heart"** — *na'ar ve-rakh levav*, the words David used of Solomon, *young and tender* (1 Chronicles 22:5). Rehoboam was forty-one when he began to reign (12:13); the word is about his nerve, not his age.
- **v8 "the kingdom of the LORD in the hand of the sons of David"** — the kingdom is the LORD's, as in the promise, *over My house and over My kingdom* (1 Chronicles 17:14).
- **v9 "to consecrate himself… a priest of what are no gods"** — *le-mallot yado*, **to fill his hand**, the words for ordaining a priest (1 Chronicles 29:5). *Lo elohim*, **no gods**: the Greek has *a priest to what is not God*.
- **v12 "His priests with the battle trumpets to sound the alarm"** — the law of the trumpets: *When you go to war… you shall sound a blast on the trumpets, and you will be remembered before the LORD your God* (Numbers 10:9). The trumpets sound in v14, and God strikes in v15.
- **v18 "because they relied on the LORD"** — *ki nish'anu*, the word on which Asa's reign will turn: *we rely on You* (14:11), and then *because you relied on the king of Aram and did not rely on the LORD* (16:7).
- **v20 "The LORD struck him, and he died"** — Kings says only that Jeroboam *lay down with his fathers* (1 Kings 14:20).
- **v22 "the account of the prophet Iddo"** — *midrash*, from *darash*, **to seek, to inquire**, the word Chronicles uses for seeking the LORD. *Midrash* is found only here and in 24:27.
