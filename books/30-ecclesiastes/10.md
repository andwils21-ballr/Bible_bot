# Ecclesiastes 10

**1** Dead flies make the perfumer's oil stink and ferment; a little folly outweighs wisdom and honor.

**2** A wise man's heart is at his right hand, but a fool's heart at his left.

**3** Even when the fool walks on the road, he lacks sense, and he says to everyone that he is a fool.

**4** If the ruler's temper rises against you, do not leave your place, for calmness lays great offenses to rest.

**5** There is an evil that I have seen under the sun, like a mistake that comes from the ruler:

**6** folly is set on great heights, and the rich sit in a low place.

**7** I have seen servants on horses, and princes walking on the ground like servants.

**8** He who digs a pit will fall into it, and a serpent will bite him who breaks through a wall.

**9** He who quarries stones will be hurt by them, and he who splits logs is endangered by them.

**10** If the iron is blunt and one does not sharpen its edge, he must use more strength; but wisdom brings the gain of success.

**11** If the serpent bites before it is charmed, there is no gain for the charmer.

**12** The words of a wise man's mouth are gracious, but the lips of a fool swallow him up.

**13** The beginning of the words of his mouth is folly, and the end of his talk is evil madness.

**14** A fool multiplies words, though no man knows what will be; who can tell him what will be after him?

**15** The toil of fools wearies him who does not know the way to the city.

**16** Woe to you, land, when your king is a youth and your princes eat in the morning!

**17** Blessed are you, land, when your king is the son of nobles and your princes eat at the proper time, for strength and not for drinking!

**18** Through sloth the roof sags, and through idle hands the house leaks.

**19** Bread is made for laughter, and wine gladdens life, and money answers everything.

**20** Even in your thoughts do not curse the king, and in your bedroom do not curse the rich, for a bird of the air will carry the sound, and a winged creature will tell the matter.

## Notes

- **v1 "Dead flies"** — *zevuvei mavet*, "flies of death": flies that have died in the oil, or flies that bring death. "Outweighs" — *yaqar*, "heavy" and so "precious": a little folly weighs more than wisdom and honor together.
- **v2 "at his right hand… at his left"** — the right is the side of strength and favor (Psalm 16:8; 110:1); the fool's heart takes him the wrong way.
- **v3 "he says to everyone that he is a fool"** — or "he says of everyone, 'He is a fool.'" The Hebrew allows both; either way the fool shows himself on the open road.
- **v4 "do not leave your place… lays great offenses to rest"** — *al tannach* and *yanniach*, the same verb: do not let go of your place; calm lets great offenses go. Compare Proverbs 15:1.
- **v8 "He who digs a pit will fall into it"** — the thought of Psalm 7:15 and Proverbs 26:27 (Reproof 2:27), here in other words (*gummats*, found only here, for "pit").
- **v11 "the charmer"** — *ba'al hallashon*, "the master of the tongue"; *lachash* is the charmer's whispered spell (Psalm 58:5; Jeremiah 8:17). A charm spoken too late is no gain; skill must be used in time.
- **vv16–17 "a youth… the son of nobles"** — *na'ar*, a boy, or a servant (the same word), set against one born free. Princes who "eat in the morning" begin the day with feasting (Isaiah 5:11).
- **v18 "Through sloth"** — *atsaltayim*, found only here, a dual form, matching the pair of idle hands in the second line.
