# Ecclesiastes 3

**1** For everything there is a season, and a time for every matter under heaven:

**2** a time to be born, and a time to die;  
a time to plant, and a time to uproot what is planted;

**3** a time to kill, and a time to heal;  
a time to break down, and a time to build up;

**4** a time to weep, and a time to laugh;  
a time to mourn, and a time to dance;

**5** a time to cast away stones, and a time to gather stones together;  
a time to embrace, and a time to refrain from embracing;

**6** a time to seek, and a time to lose;  
a time to keep, and a time to cast away;

**7** a time to tear, and a time to sew;  
a time to keep silence, and a time to speak;

**8** a time to love, and a time to hate;  
a time for war, and a time for peace.

**9** What gain has the worker from that at which he toils?

**10** I have seen the business that God has given to the children of man to be busy with.

**11** He has made everything beautiful in its time. He has also put eternity in their hearts, yet so that man cannot find out the work that God has done from beginning to end.

**12** I know that there is nothing better for them than to rejoice and to do good as long as they live;

**13** and also that every man should eat and drink and see good in all his toil — it is the gift of God.

**14** I know that whatever God does endures forever; nothing can be added to it, nor anything taken from it. God has done it, so that people fear before Him.

**15** What is, already has been; what is to be, already has been; and God seeks what has been driven away.

**16** Moreover, I saw under the sun: in the place of justice, there was wickedness, and in the place of righteousness, there was wickedness.

**17** I said in my heart, "God will judge the righteous and the wicked, for there is a time for every matter and for every work there."

**18** I said in my heart concerning the children of man: God is testing them, so that they may see that they themselves are beasts.

**19** For what befalls the children of man and what befalls the beasts is one and the same; as one dies, so dies the other. They all have one breath, and man has no advantage over the beasts, for all is vanity.

**20** All go to one place. All are from the dust, and all return to the dust.

**21** Who knows whether the spirit of man goes upward and the spirit of the beast goes down into the earth?

**22** So I saw that there is nothing better than that a man should rejoice in his works, for that is his portion. Who can bring him to see what will be after him?

## Notes

- **vv2–8** — fourteen pairs of opposites, every human act given its time; God holds the times (v11; Psalm 31:15).
- **v5 "a time to cast away stones, and a time to gather stones together"** — perhaps clearing a field and building a wall, or, as later readers took it, a picture of the marriage bed beside "a time to embrace."
- **v11 "He has also put eternity in their hearts"** — *ha'olam*, "eternity" or "the world": a sense of what lies beyond time, yet no grasp of the whole.
- **v15 "God seeks what has been driven away"** — *nirdaf*, "the pursued": God brings back what has passed, or God takes the side of the pursued.
- **vv19–21 "one breath… the spirit of man… the spirit of the beast"** — *ruach* each time; the breath of life that people and animals share (Genesis 7:22). Lowercase, since it is not God's Spirit.
- **v20 "All are from the dust, and all return to the dust"** — Genesis 3:19.
- **v21 "Who knows"** — the Preacher does not deny a difference; he says no one under the sun can see it. 12:7 gives his answer: *the spirit returns to God who gave it*.
