# Ecclesiastes 9

**1** All this I laid to heart, examining it all: that the righteous and the wise and their deeds are in the hand of God. Whether it is love or hate, man does not know; all is before them.

**2** It is the same for all: one fate comes to the righteous and to the wicked, to the good and to the clean and to the unclean, to him who sacrifices and to him who does not sacrifice. As is the good man, so is the sinner; he who swears is as he who fears an oath.

**3** This is an evil in all that is done under the sun, that one fate comes to all. Also, the hearts of the children of man are full of evil, and madness is in their hearts while they live, and afterward — to the dead.

**4** Whoever is joined to all the living has hope, for a living dog is better than a dead lion.

**5** For the living know that they will die, but the dead know nothing, and they have no more reward, for the memory of them is forgotten.

**6** Their love and their hate and their envy have already perished, and they have no more portion forever in anything that is done under the sun.

**7** Go, eat your bread with joy, and drink your wine with a merry heart, for God has already approved what you do.

**8** Let your garments always be white, and let oil not be lacking on your head.

**9** Enjoy life with the wife whom you love, all the days of your life of vanity that He has given you under the sun, all your days of vanity, for this is your portion in life and in your toil at which you toil under the sun.

**10** Whatever your hand finds to do, do it with your might, for there is no work or reckoning or knowledge or wisdom in Sheol, where you are going.

**11** Again I saw under the sun that the race is not to the swift, nor the battle to the strong, nor bread to the wise, nor riches to those of understanding, nor favor to those who have knowledge, but time and chance happen to them all.

**12** For man does not know his time. Like fish that are taken in an evil net, and like birds that are caught in a snare, so the children of man are snared at an evil time, when it falls suddenly upon them.

**13** This also I have seen as wisdom under the sun, and it seemed great to me:

**14** There was a little city with few men in it, and a great king came against it and besieged it and built great siegeworks against it.

**15** There was found in it a poor wise man, and he by his wisdom delivered the city. Yet no one remembered that poor man.

**16** So I said, "Wisdom is better than might," though the poor man's wisdom is despised and his words are not heard.

**17** The words of the wise heard in quiet are better than the shouting of a ruler among fools.

**18** Wisdom is better than weapons of war, but one sinner destroys much good.

## Notes

- **v1 "Whether it is love or hate, man does not know"** — whether love or hate awaits him, from God or from others; nothing in a man's lot shows it. "All is before them" — all lies ahead of them, unknown.
- **v2 "to the good and to the clean and to the unclean"** — the list runs in pairs, and "the good" stands without its partner. The Greek has the pair: "to the good and to the evil."
- **v4 "Whoever is joined"** — the read form *yechubbar*; the written form *yivchar*, "chooses," would give "Who is there who chooses?" The Greek agrees with the read form ("who shares with all the living?"). "A living dog… a dead lion" — the dog a despised scavenger (1 Samuel 17:43; 2 Samuel 9:8), the lion the king of beasts.
- **v5 "reward… memory"** — *sakhar* and *zikhram*, a play of sound: the dead lose both their wages and their remembrance.
- **v9 "Enjoy life"** — literally "see life," as 2:1 and 3:13 speak of "seeing good." "Your life of vanity… your days of vanity" — *hevel* twice in one verse.
- **v10 "Sheol"** — the place of the dead, where no work is done and no one praises (Psalm 6:5; 88:10–12; Isaiah 38:18).
- **v11 "time and chance"** — *et vafega*, "a time and an occurrence": *pega* is what one runs into without seeking it (1 Kings 5:4, "misfortune").
- **v15 "he by his wisdom delivered the city"** — or "he could have delivered the city by his wisdom, but no one remembered that poor man," that is, no one thought to ask him. The Hebrew verb allows both; v16, "his words are not heard," suits the second, and the Greek puts both verbs in the mood of what might be.
