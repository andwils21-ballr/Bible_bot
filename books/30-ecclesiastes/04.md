# Ecclesiastes 4

**1** Again I saw all the oppressions that are done under the sun. There were the tears of the oppressed, and they had no one to comfort them. On the side of their oppressors there was power, and they had no one to comfort them.

**2** So I praised the dead who are already dead more than the living who are still alive.

**3** But better than both is he who has not yet been, who has not seen the evil work that is done under the sun.

**4** Then I saw that all toil and all skill in work come from a man's envy of his neighbor. This also is vanity and a chasing after wind.

**5** The fool folds his hands and eats his own flesh.

**6** Better is one hand full of quietness than two hands full of toil and a chasing after wind.

**7** Again I saw vanity under the sun:

**8** one man who has no other, neither son nor brother, yet there is no end to all his toil, and his eyes are never satisfied with riches. "For whom am I toiling and depriving my soul of good?" This also is vanity and an unhappy business.

**9** Two are better than one, because they have a good reward for their toil.

**10** For if they fall, one will lift up his companion. But woe to him who is alone when he falls and has no one to lift him up!

**11** Again, if two lie together, they keep warm, but how can one keep warm alone?

**12** Though a man may overpower one who is alone, two will stand against him — and a threefold cord is not quickly broken.

**13** Better is a poor and wise youth than an old and foolish king who no longer knows how to take warning.

**14** For he came out of prison to reign, though in his kingdom he had been born poor.

**15** I saw all the living who walk under the sun, with that second youth who would stand in his place.

**16** There was no end of all the people, of all those whom he led. Yet those who come later will not rejoice in him. This also is vanity and a chasing after wind.

## Notes

- **v1 "they had no one to comfort them"** — said twice, as the Hebrew does.
- **v5 "eats his own flesh"** — the idle man destroys himself; v6 gives the balance between laziness and endless toil.
- **v8 "his eyes"** — the read form, singular *eino*; the written form plural. The Hebrew moves into the man's own question without "he says."
- **vv9–12** — the worth of a companion, at work, in a fall, in the cold, against an attacker; "a threefold cord," where two become three.
- **vv13–16** — a story of a youth who rose from prison to the throne and was forgotten in turn; the Hebrew does not say who.
- **v16 and Hebrew 4:17** — the Hebrew counts one more verse in this chapter; the English numbering, which begins chapter 5 with it, is followed, so it is rendered as 5:1.
