# Ecclesiastes 12

**1** Remember your Creator in the days of your youth, before the evil days come and the years draw near of which you will say, "I have no pleasure in them";

**2** before the sun and the light and the moon and the stars are darkened, and the clouds return after the rain;

**3** in the day when the keepers of the house tremble, and the strong men are bent, and the grinders cease because they are few, and those who look through the windows are dimmed;

**4** when the doors on the street are shut, and the sound of the grinding is low, and one rises at the sound of a bird, and all the daughters of song are brought low;

**5** when they are afraid also of what is high, and terrors are on the way; when the almond tree blossoms, and the grasshopper drags itself along, and the caper-berry fails; for man is going to his eternal home, and the mourners go about the streets;

**6** before the silver cord is snapped, or the golden bowl is broken, or the pitcher is shattered at the spring, or the wheel broken at the cistern;

**7** and the dust returns to the earth as it was, and the spirit returns to God who gave it.

**8** "Vanity of vanities," says the Preacher, "all is vanity."

**9** Besides being wise, the Preacher also taught the people knowledge, weighing and studying and arranging many proverbs.

**10** The Preacher sought to find pleasing words, and what was written uprightly, words of truth.

**11** The words of the wise are like goads, and like nails firmly fixed are the masters of collections; they are given by one Shepherd.

**12** Beyond these, my son, be warned: of making many books there is no end, and much study is a weariness of the flesh.

**13** The end of the matter; all has been heard. Fear God and keep His commandments, for this is the whole of man.

**14** For God will bring every deed into judgment, with every hidden thing, whether good or evil.

## Notes

- **v1 "your Creator"** — *bor'eikha*, from *bara*, the verb of Genesis 1:1; the form is plural, as *Elohim* is.
- **vv2–5** — the coming of old age and death, told as a great house going dark and still. The pictures are most often read this way: the keepers of the house, the arms; the strong men, the legs; the grinders (women grinding grain), the teeth, now few; those who look through the windows, the eyes; the almond tree's white blossom, white hair.
- **v5 "the caper-berry fails"** — *aviyyonah*, the caper-berry, eaten to stir the appetite; when it no longer works, desire is gone. The Greek also has the caper (*kapparis*). "His eternal home" — *beit olamo*, the grave.
- **v6 "is snapped"** — the read form *yerateq*; the written form *yirchaq*, "is removed." The silver cord and the golden bowl (a lamp hung on its cord), the pitcher and the wheel at the well: the things that hold and carry life, broken.
- **v7 "the dust returns… the spirit returns to God who gave it"** — Genesis 3:19 and 2:7: each goes back where it came from. This is the Preacher's answer to his question in 3:21. *ruach* lowercase, a man's own spirit.
- **v8** — the book closes with the words it opened with (1:2). Here the Hebrew has *haqqohelet*, "the Preacher," with the article.
- **v11 "goads… nails… one Shepherd"** — goads drive, nails hold fast. *Ba'alei asuppot*, "masters of collections," may be the collected sayings themselves or those who gather them. "One Shepherd" is most likely God, who is called the Shepherd in Genesis 49:24 and Psalm 80:1, the one source of the sayings.
- **v13 "this is the whole of man"** — *zeh kol ha'adam*, with no word for "duty": to fear God and keep His commandments is what a man is, all of him.
- **v14 "every hidden thing"** — compare 11:9, and Romans 2:16.
