# Ecclesiastes 5

**1** Guard your steps when you go to the house of God. To draw near to listen is better than to offer the sacrifice of fools, for they do not know that they are doing evil.

**2** Do not be rash with your mouth, nor let your heart be hasty to utter a word before God, for God is in heaven and you are on earth; therefore let your words be few.

**3** For a dream comes with much business, and a fool's voice with many words.

**4** When you vow a vow to God, do not delay paying it, for He has no pleasure in fools. Pay what you vow.

**5** It is better that you should not vow than that you should vow and not pay.

**6** Do not let your mouth lead your flesh into sin, and do not say before the messenger that it was a mistake. Why should God be angry at your voice and destroy the work of your hands?

**7** For when dreams increase, so do vanities and many words; but fear God.

**8** If you see in a province the oppression of the poor and the violation of justice and righteousness, do not be amazed at the matter, for one high official watches over another, and there are higher ones over them.

**9** But the gain of a land is in everything: a king for a cultivated field.

**10** He who loves money will not be satisfied with money, nor he who loves wealth with income. This also is vanity.

**11** When goods increase, those who eat them increase, and what advantage has their owner but to see them with his eyes?

**12** Sweet is the sleep of a laborer, whether he eats little or much, but the full stomach of the rich will not let him sleep.

**13** There is a grievous evil that I have seen under the sun: riches kept by their owner to his hurt,

**14** and those riches were lost in an unhappy business, and he fathered a son, but there is nothing in his hand.

**15** As he came from his mother's womb, naked he shall go again as he came, and he shall take nothing for his toil that he may carry away in his hand.

**16** This also is a grievous evil: just as he came, so shall he go, and what gain has he who toils for the wind?

**17** Moreover, all his days he eats in darkness, in much vexation and sickness and anger.

**18** What I have seen to be good and fitting is this: to eat and drink and see good in all the toil with which one toils under the sun the few days of his life that God has given him, for this is his portion.

**19** Also, every man to whom God has given riches and possessions and power to eat of them, to accept his portion and to rejoice in his toil — this is the gift of God.

**20** For he will not much remember the days of his life, because God keeps him occupied with joy in his heart.

## Notes

- **v1 and Hebrew 4:17** — the Hebrew places this verse at the end of chapter 4 and counts the rest of the chapter one lower (English 5:2 = Hebrew 5:1). The English numbering is followed. "To draw near to listen" — to listen is to obey (1 Samuel 15:22).
- **v2 "let your words be few"** — compare Matthew 6:7.
- **vv4–5 "When you vow a vow to God"** — nearly the words of Deuteronomy 23:21–23.
- **v6 "before the messenger"** — *mal'akh*; likely the priest or temple officer who received the vow (Malachi 2:7 calls the priest the messenger of the LORD of hosts). The Greek reads "before God." "A mistake" — *shegagah*, the unintentional sin of Leviticus 4 and Numbers 15:22–29.
- **v7 "vanities"** — *havalim*, the plural of *hevel*.
- **v8 "one high official watches over another"** — each official answers to one above him; the abuse goes up the chain, or the chain hides it.
- **v9** — the Hebrew is obscure. It may mean that a king is a gain to a land under cultivation, or that even a king depends on the field.
- **v15 "naked he shall go again as he came"** — Job 1:21; 1 Timothy 6:7.
- **vv18–20** — eating, drinking and joy in one's toil as God's gift, as in 2:24 and 3:13. "Keeps him occupied" — *ma'aneh*, God "answers" him or keeps him busy with joy.
