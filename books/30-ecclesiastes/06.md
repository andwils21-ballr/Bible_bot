# Ecclesiastes 6

**1** There is an evil that I have seen under the sun, and it lies heavy on mankind:

**2** a man to whom God gives riches and possessions and honor, so that he lacks nothing for his soul of all that he desires, yet God does not give him power to eat of it, but a stranger eats it. This is vanity and a grievous sickness.

**3** If a man fathers a hundred children and lives many years, so that the days of his years are many, but his soul is not satisfied with good, and he also has no burial, I say that a stillborn child is better off than he.

**4** For it comes in vanity and goes in darkness, and in darkness its name is covered.

**5** Moreover, it has not seen the sun or known anything; this one has more rest than that one.

**6** Even if he lives a thousand years twice over, yet sees no good — do not all go to one place?

**7** All the toil of man is for his mouth, and yet the soul is not filled.

**8** For what advantage has the wise man over the fool? What has the poor man who knows how to walk before the living?

**9** Better is the sight of the eyes than the wandering of the soul. This also is vanity and a chasing after wind.

**10** Whatever has been has already been named, and it is known what man is, and he is not able to dispute with one stronger than he.

**11** For there are many words that increase vanity. What advantage is that to man?

**12** For who knows what is good for man in life, the few days of his life of vanity, which he spends like a shadow? For who can tell man what will be after him under the sun?

---

## Notes

- **v2 "a stranger eats it"** — *nokhri*, a foreigner or an outsider; one man gathers and another enjoys (compare 2:18–21).
- **v3 "a hundred children… no burial"** — many children and long life were counted the greatest blessings; to lie unburied was the worst disgrace (Jeremiah 22:19). "A stillborn child" — *nefel*, as in Job 3:16 and Psalm 58:8.
- **vv4–5 "it"** — the stillborn child, which never sees the sun and has more rest than the man of v3.
- **vv7, 9 "the soul"** — *nefesh*, which also means the throat and the appetite: the toil goes into the mouth and the hunger stays; "the wandering of the soul" is desire that roams after what it does not have.
- **v10 "what man is"** — *adam*, the name given to the man taken from the ground (*adamah*, Genesis 2:7). "One stronger than he" — likely God (Job 9:32).
- **v12 "like a shadow"** — Psalm 144:4; Job 14:2; 1 Chronicles 29:15.
