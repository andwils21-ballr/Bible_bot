# Ecclesiastes 2

**1** I said in my heart, "Come now, I will test you with pleasure; enjoy what is good." But this also was vanity.

**2** I said of laughter, "It is madness," and of pleasure, "What does it do?"

**3** I searched in my heart how to cheer my body with wine — my heart still guiding me with wisdom — and how to lay hold of folly, until I might see what was good for the children of man to do under heaven during the few days of their life.

**4** I made great works: I built houses for myself; I planted vineyards for myself.

**5** I made myself gardens and parks and planted in them all kinds of fruit trees.

**6** I made myself pools of water from which to water a forest of growing trees.

**7** I bought male and female servants, and had servants born in my house. I also had great possessions of herds and flocks, more than any who had been before me in Jerusalem.

**8** I also gathered for myself silver and gold and the treasure of kings and provinces. I got singers, men and women, and the delights of the children of man — many concubines.

**9** So I became great and surpassed all who were before me in Jerusalem. My wisdom also remained with me.

**10** Whatever my eyes asked for I did not keep from them. I kept my heart from no pleasure, for my heart found pleasure in all my toil, and this was my portion from all my toil.

**11** Then I turned to all the works that my hands had done and to the toil I had spent in doing them, and indeed, all was vanity and a chasing after wind, and there was no gain under the sun.

**12** So I turned to consider wisdom and madness and folly. For what can the man do who comes after the king? Only what has already been done.

**13** Then I saw that there is more gain in wisdom than in folly, as there is more gain in light than in darkness.

**14** The wise man has his eyes in his head, but the fool walks in darkness. Yet I perceived that one fate befalls them all.

**15** So I said in my heart, "What befalls the fool will befall me also. Why then have I been so very wise?" I said in my heart that this also is vanity.

**16** For of the wise as of the fool there is no remembrance forever, since in the days to come all will long be forgotten. How the wise dies just like the fool!

**17** So I hated life, because what is done under the sun was grievous to me, for all is vanity and a chasing after wind.

**18** I hated all my toil at which I toil under the sun, since I must leave it to the man who will come after me,

**19** and who knows whether he will be wise or a fool? Yet he will be master of all for which I toiled and used my wisdom under the sun. This also is vanity.

**20** So I turned and gave my heart up to despair over all the toil at which I had toiled under the sun,

**21** because there is a man whose toil is done with wisdom and knowledge and skill, and he must leave it as a portion to a man who did not toil for it. This also is vanity and a great evil.

**22** What does a man get from all his toil and the striving of his heart at which he toils under the sun?

**23** For all his days are pain, and his business is vexation; even at night his heart does not rest. This also is vanity.

**24** There is nothing better for a man than to eat and drink and let his soul see good in his toil. This also, I saw, is from the hand of God,

**25** for who can eat or who can have enjoyment apart from me?

**26** For to the man who is good in His sight He has given wisdom and knowledge and joy, but to the sinner He has given the business of gathering and collecting, only to give to the one who is good in God's sight. This also is vanity and a chasing after wind.

## Notes

- **vv1–11** — the king's experiment: pleasure, wine, building, gardens, servants, treasure, music. He had everything, and found "no gain under the sun."
- **v8 "many concubines"** — *shiddah veshiddot*, words found only here; the meaning is uncertain, and "chests" or "cupbearers" have been proposed.
- **v12 "what can the man do who comes after the king?"** — no successor can go further; the line is hard in the Hebrew.
- **v14 "one fate"** — *miqreh*, what happens, chance, here death, which comes to wise and fool alike (3:19; 9:2–3).
- **v20 "gave my heart up to despair"** — *leya'esh*, to give up hope.
- **v24 "There is nothing better for a man than to eat and drink"** — the first of the book's refrains of simple joy (3:12–13, 22; 5:18; 8:15; 9:7), received as a gift "from the hand of God."
- **v25 "apart from me"** — the Hebrew is followed: who can enjoy more than the Preacher could? The Greek and some Hebrew manuscripts read "apart from Him."
- **v26 "the one who is good in His sight… the sinner"** — "sinner" (*chote*) may mean one who misses the mark, the one God does not favor.
