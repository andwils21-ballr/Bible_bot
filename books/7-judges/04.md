# Judges 4

## Deborah

**1** The sons of Israel again did evil in the eyes of the LORD after Ehud died.

**2** The LORD sold them into the hand of Jabin king of Canaan, who reigned in Hazor. The commander of his army was Sisera, who lived in Harosheth-hagoyim.

**3** The sons of Israel cried out to the LORD, for he had nine hundred chariots of iron, and he had oppressed the sons of Israel harshly for twenty years.

**4** Deborah, a prophetess, the wife of Lappidoth, was judging Israel at that time.

**5** She used to sit under the palm tree of Deborah between Ramah and Bethel in the hill country of Ephraim, and the sons of Israel went up to her for judgment.

**6** She sent and called Barak son of Abinoam from Kedesh in Naphtali, and said to him, "Has not the LORD, the God of Israel, commanded, 'Go, march to Mount Tabor, and take with you ten thousand men from the sons of Naphtali and the sons of Zebulun?

**7** I will draw out Sisera, the commander of Jabin's army, to meet you at the Kishon River, with his chariots and his troops, and I will give him into your hand.'"

**8** Barak said to her, "If you will go with me, I will go, but if you will not go with me, I will not go."

**9** She said, "I will certainly go with you. But the road you are taking will not lead to your glory, for the LORD will sell Sisera into the hand of a woman." Deborah rose and went with Barak to Kedesh.

**10** Barak called out Zebulun and Naphtali to Kedesh, and ten thousand men went up behind him, and Deborah went up with him.

**11** Heber the Kenite had separated from the Kenites, the sons of Hobab, Moses' father-in-law, and had pitched his tent as far away as the oak in Zaanannim, which is near Kedesh.

## The Battle at the Kishon

**12** They told Sisera that Barak son of Abinoam had gone up to Mount Tabor.

**13** Sisera called out all his chariots, nine hundred chariots of iron, and all the people who were with him, from Harosheth-hagoyim to the Kishon River.

**14** Deborah said to Barak, "Up! For this is the day the LORD has given Sisera into your hand. Has not the LORD gone out before you?" So Barak went down from Mount Tabor with ten thousand men behind him.

**15** The LORD threw Sisera and all his chariots and all his army into panic before Barak with the sword, and Sisera got down from his chariot and fled on foot.

**16** Barak pursued the chariots and the army as far as Harosheth-hagoyim, and the whole army of Sisera fell by the sword; not one was left.

## Jael

**17** Sisera fled on foot to the tent of Jael, the wife of Heber the Kenite, for there was peace between Jabin king of Hazor and the house of Heber the Kenite.

**18** Jael went out to meet Sisera and said to him, "Turn aside, my lord, turn aside to me. Do not be afraid." So he turned aside to her into the tent, and she covered him with a rug.

**19** He said to her, "Please give me a little water to drink, for I am thirsty." She opened a skin of milk, gave him a drink and covered him.

**20** He said to her, "Stand at the entrance of the tent. And it shall come to pass, if anyone comes and asks you, 'Is there a man here?', that you shall say, 'No.'"

**21** But Jael the wife of Heber took a tent peg, took the hammer in her hand, went to him quietly and drove the peg into his temple, and it went down into the ground. He was fast asleep and exhausted, and he died.

**22** Just then Barak came, pursuing Sisera, and Jael went out to meet him and said to him, "Come, and I will show you the man you are looking for." He went in with her, and there lay Sisera, fallen dead, with the peg in his temple.

**23** So on that day God subdued Jabin king of Canaan before the sons of Israel.

**24** The hand of the sons of Israel pressed harder and harder on Jabin king of Canaan, until they had destroyed Jabin king of Canaan.

## Notes

- **v2 "Jabin king of Canaan, who reigned in Hazor"** — Joshua defeated a Jabin king of Hazor and burned the city (Joshua 11:1, 10–11). A second Jabin reigns there again; the name may have been carried by the kings of Hazor.
- **v4 "Deborah, a prophetess, the wife of Lappidoth"** — *ishah nevi'ah eshet Lappidot*. *Deborah* means **bee**. *Eshet Lappidot* is usually read as *the wife of Lappidoth*, but *lappid* is a **torch** (the *torch of fire* of Genesis 15:17), and the phrase can also mean **a woman of torches**, a fiery woman. No husband is mentioned again. The Hebrew allows both; the text follows the usual reading.
- **vv6–7 "march… I will draw out Sisera"** — *mashakhta… u-mashakhti*, the same verb, **draw**, used of both sides: Barak draws his men to Tabor, and the LORD draws Sisera to the river.
- **v8 "If you will go with me, I will go"** — the Greek adds Barak's reason: *for I do not know the day on which the LORD gives success to His messenger with me*. Hebrews 11:32 names Barak among the people of faith.
- **v9 "into the hand of a woman"** — Barak, and any reader, will think of Deborah. The woman turns out to be Jael (v21).
- **v11 "Heber the Kenite"** — a Kenite clan that moved north, away from the Kenites in Judah (1:16). *Heber* is from the root **to join**, and v17 gives what he had joined: *peace* with Jabin. The note in v11 is there to explain v17.
- **v15 "The LORD threw Sisera… into panic"** — *va-yahom*, the verb of Exodus 14:24, where the LORD *threw the camp of Egypt into panic* at the sea. Here too the chariots are ruined by water: the Song of Deborah says *the torrent of Kishon swept them away* (5:21).
- **v20 "Is there a man here?… No"** — *ha-yesh poh ish… ayin*. He means a man hiding; the answer is truer than he knows. The one who will kill him is not a man.
- **v21 "He was fast asleep and exhausted"** — *nirdam va-ya'af*. *Nirdam* is from the root of *tardemah*, the *deep sleep* that fell on the human in Genesis 2:21. *Va-ya'af* is either **was weary** (from *ayef*) or **fainted, flew away**; the Greek reads *he was stunned and darkened*.
- **v24 "pressed harder and harder"** — *halokh ve-qashah*, **going and being hard**, the way Hebrew says a thing grows steadily. Chapter 5 tells the same victory as a song.
