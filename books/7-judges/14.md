# Judges 14

## The Woman at Timnah

**1** Samson went down to Timnah and saw a woman in Timnah, one of the daughters of the Philistines.

**2** He came back up and told his father and mother, "I have seen a woman in Timnah, one of the daughters of the Philistines. So now get her for me as a wife."

**3** His father and mother said to him, "Is there no woman among the daughters of your brothers, or among all our people, that you must go and take a wife from the uncircumcised Philistines?" But Samson said to his father, "Get her for me, for she is right in my eyes."

**4** His father and mother did not know that it was from the LORD, for he was seeking an occasion against the Philistines. At that time the Philistines were ruling over Israel.

## The Lion and the Honey

**5** Samson went down to Timnah with his father and mother, and they came to the vineyards of Timnah. A young lion came roaring at him.

**6** The Spirit of the LORD rushed on him, and he tore the lion in two as one tears a young goat, with nothing in his hand. But he did not tell his father or his mother what he had done.

**7** He went down and spoke to the woman, and she was right in Samson's eyes.

**8** After a time he returned to take her, and he turned aside to see the carcass of the lion. In the body of the lion was a swarm of bees and honey.

**9** He scraped the honey into his hands and went on, eating as he went. When he came to his father and mother, he gave some to them, and they ate. But he did not tell them that he had scraped the honey from the body of the lion.

## The Riddle

**10** His father went down to the woman, and Samson held a feast there, for that is what young men used to do.

**11** And it came to pass, when the people saw him, that they brought thirty companions to be with him.

**12** Samson said to them, "Let me tell you a riddle. If you can explain it to me within the seven days of the feast and find it out, I will give you thirty linen garments and thirty changes of clothes.

**13** But if you cannot explain it to me, you shall give me thirty linen garments and thirty changes of clothes." They said to him, "Tell us your riddle, and let us hear it."

**14** He said to them,

"Out of the eater came something to eat,  
and out of the strong came something sweet."

For three days they could not explain the riddle.

**15** And it came to pass on the seventh day that they said to Samson's wife, "Coax your husband to explain the riddle to us, or we will burn you and your father's house with fire. Did you invite us here to dispossess us?"

**16** Samson's wife wept over him and said, "You only hate me; you do not love me. You have set a riddle for the sons of my people, and you have not told me the answer." He said to her, "I have not even told my father or my mother. Shall I tell you?"

**17** She wept over him the seven days that the feast lasted. And it came to pass on the seventh day that he told her, because she pressed him hard. Then she explained the riddle to the sons of her people.

**18** The men of the city said to him on the seventh day, before the sun went down,

"What is sweeter than honey?  
What is stronger than a lion?"

He said to them,

"If you had not plowed with my heifer,  
you would not have found out my riddle."

## Ashkelon

**19** The Spirit of the LORD rushed on him, and he went down to Ashkelon and struck down thirty men of the town. He took their clothing and gave the changes of clothes to those who had explained the riddle. In hot anger he went up to his father's house.

**20** Samson's wife was given to his companion, who had been his friend.

## Notes

- **v1 "Timnah"** — a town on the Danite list (Joshua 19:43), down the valley from Zorah, where Samson's family lives.
- **v3 "she is right in my eyes"** — *hi yashrah be-einai*. It is the phrase the book ends on: *everyone did what was right in his own eyes* (21:25; also 17:6). The boy set apart from the womb chooses his wife by his own eyes.
- **v4 "it was from the LORD… an occasion"** — *to'anah*, found only here. It is a pretext or an occasion, and it is the LORD who is seeking it. The Greek has *vengeance*. The parents are not told, and the text names the LORD as the cause without saying how.
- **v6 "rushed on him"** — *va-titslach alav*, the verb of the LORD's Spirit coming on Saul in 1 Samuel 10:6, *will rush upon you*. It is used of Samson again in v19 and at 15:14. The Greek has *leapt on him*. *With nothing in his hand* is the point: the strength is not his own.
- **vv8–10 the carcass, the honey, the feast** — Numbers 6:6 says a Nazirite *shall not go near a dead body*, and 13:4 forbade the mother wine; here Samson turns aside to a carcass, eats from it, and holds a *mishteh*, a **drinking feast**, from *shatah*, **to drink**. The chapter never says that any of this breaks his vow, and it never says that it does not. Twice he keeps it from his parents (vv6, 9).
- **v15 "On the seventh day"** — the Hebrew has the men come on the seventh day, but v14 says they failed for three days, and v17 says the wife wept for all seven days of the feast. The Greek has *on the fourth day*, which fits both. The text follows the Hebrew.
- **v18 "before the sun went down"** — *be-terem yavo ha-charsah*. *Charsah* is found only here; *cheres* is a rare word for the sun (Job 9:7), and the traditional reading is sunset. The Greek has *before the sun rose*. The sentence gives the answer just before the time is up.
- **v18 "If you had not plowed with my heifer"** — a saying found only here: the men have plowed with his wife.
- **v19 "Ashkelon"** — a Philistine city here, as Gaza is in 16:1, though 1:18 says Judah *took* both. The Greek of 1:18 says Judah did *not* inherit them.
- **v20 "his companion, who had been his friend"** — *le-mereehu asher ra'ah lo*, **his companion who had befriended him**. The Greek has *one of his friends whom he had befriended*.
