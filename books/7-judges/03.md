# Judges 3

## The Nations Left in the Land

**1** These are the nations the LORD left in order to test Israel by them, all those who had not known any of the wars of Canaan —

**2** only so that the generations of the sons of Israel might know war, to teach it to those who had not known it before:

**3** the five lords of the Philistines, all the Canaanites, the Sidonians, and the Hivites who lived in the hill country of Lebanon, from Mount Baal-hermon as far as Lebo-hamath.

**4** They were there to test Israel by them, to know whether they would obey the commandments of the LORD, which He commanded their fathers through Moses.

**5** So the sons of Israel lived among the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Amorites, the Perizzites, the Hivites and the Jebusites.

**6** They took their daughters for themselves as wives, gave their own daughters to their sons, and served their gods.

## Othniel

**7** The sons of Israel did evil in the eyes of the LORD. They forgot the LORD their God and served the Baals and the Asherahs.

**8** The anger of the LORD burned against Israel, and He sold them into the hand of Cushan-rishathaim king of Aram-naharaim, and the sons of Israel served Cushan-rishathaim eight years.

**9** The sons of Israel cried out to the LORD, and the LORD raised up a deliverer for the sons of Israel, who saved them: Othniel son of Kenaz, Caleb's younger brother.

**10** The spirit of the LORD came upon him, and he judged Israel. He went out to war, and the LORD gave Cushan-rishathaim king of Aram into his hand, and his hand prevailed over Cushan-rishathaim.

**11** The land had rest forty years, and Othniel son of Kenaz died.

## Ehud

**12** The sons of Israel again did evil in the eyes of the LORD, and the LORD strengthened Eglon king of Moab against Israel, because they had done evil in the eyes of the LORD.

**13** He gathered to himself the sons of Ammon and Amalek, went and struck Israel, and they took possession of the city of palm trees.

**14** The sons of Israel served Eglon king of Moab eighteen years.

**15** The sons of Israel cried out to the LORD, and the LORD raised up a deliverer for them, Ehud son of Gera, the Benjaminite, a left-handed man. The sons of Israel sent tribute by him to Eglon king of Moab.

**16** Ehud made himself a sword with two edges, a cubit long, and he strapped it under his clothes on his right thigh.

**17** He presented the tribute to Eglon king of Moab. Eglon was a very fat man.

**18** And it came to pass, when he had finished presenting the tribute, that he sent away the people who had carried it.

**19** But he himself turned back at the carved images near Gilgal and said, "I have a secret word for you, O king." The king said, "Silence!" All who stood by him went out from him.

**20** Ehud came to him as he was sitting alone in his cool upper room. Ehud said, "I have a word from God for you," and the king rose from his seat.

**21** Ehud reached out his left hand, took the sword from his right thigh and thrust it into his belly.

**22** The hilt went in after the blade, and the fat closed over the blade, for he did not draw the sword out of his belly; and the dung came out.

**23** Ehud went out through the porch, shut the doors of the upper room behind him and locked them.

**24** When he had gone out, the king's servants came and saw that the doors of the upper room were locked, and they said, "He must be relieving himself in the cool room."

**25** They waited until they were ashamed, but he still did not open the doors of the upper room. So they took the key and opened them, and there lay their lord, fallen to the ground, dead.

**26** Ehud escaped while they delayed. He passed the carved images and escaped to Seirah.

**27** And it came to pass, when he arrived, that he blew the horn in the hill country of Ephraim, and the sons of Israel went down with him from the hill country, with him at their head.

**28** He said to them, "Follow me, for the LORD has given your enemies, the Moabites, into your hand." They went down after him and seized the fords of the Jordan against Moab, and let no one cross.

**29** At that time they struck about ten thousand men of Moab, all of them stout and all of them strong men, and not one escaped.

**30** So Moab was subdued that day under the hand of Israel, and the land had rest eighty years.

## Shamgar

**31** After him came Shamgar son of Anath, who struck six hundred Philistines with an oxgoad. He too saved Israel.

## Notes

- **v7 "the Baals and the Asherahs"** — *ha-asherot*, a feminine plural, set beside the Baals as *the Ashtaroth* are in 2:13. The more common *asherim* are the *sacred poles* of Deuteronomy 16:21; here the word names the goddesses themselves.
- **v8 "Cushan-rishathaim"** — the second half of the name is **double wickedness**, *rish'atayim*. Whether it is his name or what Israel called him, the text does not say. *Aram-naharaim*, **Aram of the two rivers**, is where Abraham's servant found Rebekah (Genesis 24:10).
- **v9 "a deliverer… who saved them"** — *moshia*, from *yasha*, **to save**, the root of the name *Joshua*. Othniel is the one judge whose story has nothing in it but the pattern of chapter 2: sin, sale, cry, deliverer, rest.
- **v15 "a left-handed man"** — *ish itter yad-yemino*, **a man bound in his right hand**. He is a *Ben-Yemini*, a Benjaminite, a **son of the right hand**, and his right hand is the one he does not use. Judges 20:16 counts seven hundred left-handed slingers in Benjamin.
- **vv15, 17, 18 "tribute"** — *minchah*, the word for the grain offering in Leviticus 2. Israel's gift to the king of Moab is the word for a gift to God.
- **v16 "a cubit long"** — *gomed*, a measure found only here. Its length is unknown; *cubit* is the usual guess, a short blade that can hide on the thigh.
- **vv19–20 "a secret word… a word from God"** — *davar* is both **word** and **thing**. Ehud has a *davar* for the king, and what he gives him is the sword. *Silence!* is *has*, a single syllable, and the room empties.
- **vv22–24 "the dung came out… relieving himself"** — *ha-parshedonah* in v22 and *ha-misderonah* in v23 are both found only here, and their meanings are argued; *the dung* and *the porch* are the usual readings. In v24 the servants say *he is covering his feet*, the Hebrew way of saying a man is relieving himself (so also 1 Samuel 24:3). The Greek does not have the last clause of v22.
- **v29 "all of them stout"** — *kol shamen*, **every fat one**, the Moabite soldiers described with the word for their fat king's body (v17 has *bari*, the *fat* cows of Genesis 41:2).
- **v31 "Shamgar son of Anath… an oxgoad"** — *Anath* is the name of a Canaanite goddess, and Shamgar is named again in the Song of Deborah (5:6). He fights with a farmer's tool, like Samson's jawbone (15:15).
