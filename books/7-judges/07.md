# Judges 7

## Too Many

**1** Jerubbaal, that is, Gideon, rose early with all the people who were with him and camped beside the spring of Harod. The camp of Midian was north of him, by the hill of Moreh, in the valley.

**2** The LORD said to Gideon, "The people with you are too many for Me to give Midian into their hand, or Israel will boast against Me, saying, 'My own hand has saved me.'

**3** Now proclaim in the hearing of the people, 'Whoever is afraid and trembling, let him turn back and leave Mount Gilead.'" Twenty-two thousand of the people turned back, and ten thousand remained.

**4** The LORD said to Gideon, "The people are still too many. Take them down to the water, and I will refine them for you there. And it shall come to pass that the one of whom I say to you, 'This one shall go with you,' shall go with you; and anyone of whom I say to you, 'This one shall not go with you,' shall not go."

**5** So he took the people down to the water, and the LORD said to Gideon, "Everyone who laps the water with his tongue, as a dog laps, set him by himself; and everyone who kneels down to drink, set apart as well."

**6** The number of those who lapped, putting their hand to their mouth, was three hundred men, and all the rest of the people knelt down to drink water.

**7** The LORD said to Gideon, "With the three hundred men who lapped I will save you and give Midian into your hand. Let all the rest of the people go, each to his place."

**8** The three hundred took the people's provisions in their hands, and their horns. He sent all the rest of the men of Israel away, each to his tent, and kept the three hundred men. The camp of Midian was below him in the valley.

## The Dream of the Barley Loaf

**9** And it came to pass that same night that the LORD said to him, "Rise, go down against the camp, for I have given it into your hand.

**10** But if you are afraid to go down, go down to the camp with Purah your servant,

**11** and you will hear what they are saying. Afterward your hands will be strengthened, and you will go down against the camp." So he went down with Purah his servant to the outposts of the men in ranks in the camp.

**12** Midian and Amalek and all the sons of the east lay along the valley like locusts for number, and their camels could not be counted, as many as the sand on the seashore.

**13** When Gideon came, a man was telling a dream to his friend. He said, "I had a dream: a round loaf of barley bread was tumbling into the camp of Midian. It came to the tent and struck it so that it fell, and turned it upside down, and the tent lay flat."

**14** His friend answered, "This is nothing else but the sword of Gideon son of Joash, a man of Israel. God has given Midian and the whole camp into his hand."

**15** And it came to pass, when Gideon heard the telling of the dream and its interpretation, that he bowed down in worship. He returned to the camp of Israel and said, "Rise, for the LORD has given the camp of Midian into your hand."

## Horns, Jars and Torches

**16** He divided the three hundred men into three companies and put horns into the hands of all of them, and empty jars, with torches inside the jars.

**17** He said to them, "Watch me and do the same. I am going to the edge of the camp, and it shall come to pass that as I do, so you shall do.

**18** When I blow the horn, I and all who are with me, you also blow the horns all around the camp, and say, 'For the LORD and for Gideon!'"

**19** Gideon and the hundred men who were with him came to the edge of the camp at the beginning of the middle watch, when they had just posted the guards. They blew the horns and smashed the jars that were in their hands.

**20** The three companies blew the horns and broke the jars, held the torches in their left hands and the horns in their right hands to blow, and cried out, "A sword for the LORD and for Gideon!"

**21** Each man stood in his place all around the camp, and the whole camp ran; they cried out and fled.

**22** When they blew the three hundred horns, the LORD set every man's sword against his fellow throughout the whole camp. The army fled as far as Beth-shittah toward Zererah, as far as the edge of Abel-meholah, by Tabbath.

## Oreb and Zeeb

**23** The men of Israel were called out from Naphtali, from Asher and from all Manasseh, and they pursued Midian.

**24** Gideon sent messengers throughout all the hill country of Ephraim, saying, "Come down against Midian and seize the waters ahead of them as far as Beth-barah, and the Jordan." So all the men of Ephraim were called out, and they seized the waters as far as Beth-barah, and the Jordan.

**25** They captured the two princes of Midian, Oreb and Zeeb. They killed Oreb at the rock of Oreb, and Zeeb they killed at the winepress of Zeeb, and they pursued Midian. They brought the heads of Oreb and Zeeb to Gideon beyond the Jordan.

## Notes

- **vv1, 3 "the spring of Harod… afraid and trembling"** — *Charod* and *chared*, **trembling**, from the same root. The army camps at Trembling Spring, and the first test sends the trembling home, as Deuteronomy 20:8 requires: *Who is the man who is afraid and faint of heart? Let him go and return to his house.*
- **v3 "leave Mount Gilead"** — Gilead is east of the Jordan, and the camp is in the valley of Jezreel on the west. The verb, *yitspor*, is found only here, and its sense is guessed from the context. The place and the verb are both left as they stand; the Greek reads the same.
- **v4 "I will refine them"** — *etsrefennu*, the verb for smelting metal to separate the pure from the dross. God tests the army the way a smith tests silver.
- **vv5–6 the lappers** — v5 describes lapping *with his tongue, as a dog laps*; v6 counts those who lapped *putting their hand to their mouth*. The Hebrew and the Greek agree, and the two descriptions are not harmonized. What matters to the story is the number: three hundred.
- **v13 "a round loaf of barley bread"** — a loaf of bread is no weapon. The dream shows a small, ordinary thing flattening the tent of a vast army, and the Midianite who hears it names it at once: *the sword of Gideon*.
- **v15 "its interpretation"** — *shivro*, **its breaking**. *Shever* is the plain word for a break, and only here is it used for the meaning of a dream: the dream is cracked open. Four verses later, the jars are broken.
- **v16 "torches inside the jars"** — *lappidim*, the same word as in *Lappidoth* (4:4). The clay hides the light until the jars are smashed.
- **v22 "the LORD set every man's sword against his fellow"** — Israel's three hundred hold horns and torches, not swords. The swords in the story are Midian's, turned on each other.
- **v25 "Oreb and Zeeb"** — **Raven** and **Wolf**. Zeeb dies at a winepress, where Gideon was first found hiding (6:11). Isaiah 10:26 remembers *the striking of Midian at the rock of Oreb*.
