# Judges 6

## Midian

**1** The sons of Israel did evil in the eyes of the LORD, and the LORD gave them into the hand of Midian seven years.

**2** The hand of Midian prevailed over Israel. Because of Midian the sons of Israel made for themselves the dens in the mountains, the caves and the strongholds.

**3** And it would come to pass, whenever Israel had sown, that Midian and Amalek and the sons of the east would come up and attack them.

**4** They would camp against them and destroy the produce of the land as far as Gaza, and leave no food in Israel, and no sheep or ox or donkey.

**5** For they would come up with their livestock and their tents, and come like locusts for number; they and their camels could not be counted. They came into the land to ruin it.

**6** Israel was brought very low because of Midian, and the sons of Israel cried out to the LORD.

## A Prophet

**7** And it came to pass, when the sons of Israel cried out to the LORD because of Midian,

**8** that the LORD sent a prophet to the sons of Israel, and he said to them, "Thus says the LORD, the God of Israel: 'I brought you up from Egypt and brought you out of the house of slaves.

**9** I delivered you from the hand of Egypt and from the hand of all who oppressed you, and drove them out before you and gave you their land.

**10** I said to you, "I am the LORD your God. Do not fear the gods of the Amorites in whose land you live." But you have not listened to My voice.'"

## The Messenger at Ophrah

**11** The messenger of the LORD came and sat under the terebinth at Ophrah, which belonged to Joash the Abiezrite, while Gideon his son was beating out wheat in the winepress, to hide it from Midian.

**12** The messenger of the LORD appeared to him and said to him, "The LORD is with you, mighty man of valor."

**13** Gideon said to him, "Please, my lord, if the LORD is with us, why has all this found us? Where are all His wonders that our fathers told us of, saying, 'Did not the LORD bring us up from Egypt?' But now the LORD has abandoned us and given us into the hand of Midian."

**14** The LORD turned to him and said, "Go in this strength of yours and save Israel from the hand of Midian. Have I not sent you?"

**15** He said to Him, "Please, my Lord, how can I save Israel? My clan is the weakest in Manasseh, and I am the least in my father's house."

**16** The LORD said to him, "Because I will be with you, you shall strike Midian as one man."

**17** He said to Him, "If I have found favor in Your eyes, give me a sign that it is You who are speaking with me.

**18** Please do not go away from here until I come back to You and bring out my offering and set it before You." He said, "I will stay until you come back."

**19** Gideon went in and prepared a young goat and unleavened bread from an ephah of flour. He put the meat in a basket and the broth in a pot, and brought them out to him under the terebinth and presented them.

**20** The messenger of God said to him, "Take the meat and the unleavened bread, lay them on this rock, and pour out the broth." He did so.

**21** The messenger of the LORD reached out the tip of the staff in his hand and touched the meat and the unleavened bread, and fire went up from the rock and consumed the meat and the unleavened bread. Then the messenger of the LORD went from his sight.

**22** Gideon saw that he was the messenger of the LORD, and Gideon said, "Alas, Lord GOD! For I have seen the messenger of the LORD face to face."

**23** The LORD said to him, "Peace to you. Do not be afraid; you shall not die."

**24** Gideon built an altar there to the LORD and called it The LORD Is Peace. To this day it is still in Ophrah of the Abiezrites.

## The Altar of Baal

**25** And it came to pass that night that the LORD said to him, "Take your father's bull, the second bull, seven years old. Tear down the altar of Baal that belongs to your father, and cut down the sacred pole beside it.

**26** Build an altar to the LORD your God on top of this stronghold, with the stones laid in order. Then take the second bull and offer it as a burnt offering with the wood of the sacred pole that you cut down."

**27** So Gideon took ten men from his servants and did as the LORD had told him. Because he was too afraid of his father's house and the men of the city to do it by day, he did it by night.

**28** When the men of the city rose early in the morning, the altar of Baal had been torn down, the sacred pole beside it had been cut down, and the second bull had been offered on the altar that had been built.

**29** They said to one another, "Who has done this?" They inquired and searched, and they said, "Gideon son of Joash has done this."

**30** The men of the city said to Joash, "Bring out your son, and he shall die, for he has torn down the altar of Baal and cut down the sacred pole beside it."

**31** But Joash said to all who stood against him, "Will you contend for Baal? Will you save him? Whoever contends for him shall be put to death by morning. If he is a god, let him contend for himself, because someone has torn down his altar."

**32** So on that day Gideon was called Jerubbaal, meaning, "Let Baal contend against him, because he tore down his altar."

## The Fleece

**33** All Midian and Amalek and the sons of the east gathered together, crossed over and camped in the Valley of Jezreel.

**34** The Spirit of the LORD clothed Gideon, and he blew the horn, and the Abiezrites were called out to follow him.

**35** He sent messengers throughout Manasseh, and they too were called out to follow him. He sent messengers to Asher, Zebulun and Naphtali, and they came up to meet them.

**36** Gideon said to God, "If You will save Israel by my hand, as You have said,

**37** I am laying a fleece of wool on the threshing floor. If there is dew on the fleece only, and all the ground is dry, then I will know that You will save Israel by my hand, as You have said."

**38** It was so. He rose early the next morning and squeezed the fleece and wrung the dew out of it, a bowl full of water.

**39** Gideon said to God, "Do not let Your anger burn against me; let me speak just once more. Please let me make one more test with the fleece: let it be dry on the fleece only, and on all the ground let there be dew."

**40** God did so that night. It was dry on the fleece only, and on all the ground there was dew.

## Notes

- **v2 "the dens in the mountains"** — *ha-minharot*, found only here. Its root is uncertain, and *dens*, clefts in the rock, is the usual reading; the Greek has *holes*. Israel is driven into the rock to hide.
- **v11 "beating out wheat in the winepress"** — wheat was threshed on an open floor where the wind could take the chaff. Gideon beats it in the pit of a winepress, where no one can see. The one the messenger calls *mighty man of valor* (v12) is hiding.
- **vv12–14 the messenger… The LORD turned to him** — the *messenger of the LORD* speaks in v12, and in v14 *the LORD* turns to Gideon and speaks. As at the bush (Exodus 3:2–4), the text moves from the messenger to the LORD without explaining.
- **vv13, 15 "my lord… my Lord"** — *bi adoni*, then *bi Adonai*. The consonants are the same; the vowels change from the word for a man of rank to the word used only of God, as in Moses' *Please, my Lord* (Exodus 4:10). Between v13 and v15, Gideon's guest has become the LORD.
- **v16 "Because I will be with you"** — *ki ehyeh immakh*, word for word what God says to Moses at the bush (Exodus 3:12). Gideon, like Moses, has just said he is too small for the task.
- **v24 "The LORD Is Peace"** — *YHWH Shalom*, answering the LORD's *Peace to you* in v23. It follows *The LORD Will See* (Genesis 22:14) and *The LORD Is My Banner* (Exodus 17:15), altars named for what the LORD did there.
- **v25 "the sacred pole"** — *ha-asherah*, the wooden object for the goddess Asherah that Deuteronomy 16:21 forbids beside the LORD's altar. Here it stands beside Baal's altar, on the land of Gideon's father.
- **v32 "Jerubbaal"** — *Yerubba'al*, **let Baal contend**, from *riv*, the verb of Joash's challenge in v31: *If he is a god, let him contend for himself.* The name is a dare, and Baal never answers it.
- **v34 "The Spirit of the LORD clothed Gideon"** — *lavshah*, **put on, wore**, the verb for putting on a garment. Gideon is not given the Spirit; the Spirit puts him on.
- **vv36–40 the fleece** — Gideon asks for a sign twice, and the second test is the harder one, since a fleece naturally holds dew. *A bowl full of water* in v38 is *sefel*, the word used in the Song of Deborah for the bowl Jael brought to Sisera (5:25).
