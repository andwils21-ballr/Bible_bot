# Judges 5

## The Song of Deborah and Barak

**1** Then Deborah and Barak son of Abinoam sang on that day:

**2** When locks hang loose in Israel,  
when the people offer themselves willingly —  
bless the LORD!

**3** Hear, you kings; give ear, you rulers:  
I, to the LORD I will sing;  
I will make music to the LORD, the God of Israel.

**4** LORD, when You went out from Seir,  
when You marched from the field of Edom,  
the earth shook, the heavens poured,  
the clouds poured down water.

**5** The mountains flowed down before the LORD,  
the One of Sinai,  
before the LORD, the God of Israel.

**6** In the days of Shamgar son of Anath,  
in the days of Jael, the roads were empty,  
and those who went on their way  
went by winding paths.

**7** The villagers were gone in Israel, they were gone,  
until I arose, Deborah,  
until I arose, a mother in Israel.

**8** They chose new gods;  
then war was at the gates.  
Was a shield to be seen, or a spear,  
among forty thousand in Israel?

**9** My heart is with the commanders of Israel,  
who offered themselves willingly among the people.  
Bless the LORD!

**10** You who ride on white donkeys,  
you who sit on saddle-rugs,  
and you who walk on the road — tell of it!

**11** Over the voice of the singers at the watering places,  
there they recount the righteous acts of the LORD,  
the righteous acts for His villagers in Israel.  
Then the people of the LORD went down to the gates.

## Awake, Deborah

**12** Awake, awake, Deborah!  
Awake, awake, sing a song!  
Arise, Barak, and lead away your captives,  
son of Abinoam!

**13** Then a remnant went down to the nobles, the people;  
the LORD went down for me against the mighty.

**14** From Ephraim came those whose root is in Amalek;  
after you, Benjamin, with your kinsmen;  
from Machir came down commanders,  
and from Zebulun those who carry the scribe's staff.

**15** The princes of Issachar were with Deborah;  
as Issachar was, so was Barak,  
sent into the valley at his heels.  
Among the clans of Reuben  
great were the resolves of heart.

**16** Why did you sit among the sheepfolds,  
to hear the piping for the flocks?  
Among the clans of Reuben  
great were the searchings of heart.

**17** Gilead stayed beyond the Jordan;  
and Dan, why does he live as a guest by the ships?  
Asher sat at the shore of the sea  
and stayed by his landings.

**18** Zebulun is a people that scorned death with their lives,  
and Naphtali on the heights of the field.

## The Stars Fought

**19** Kings came, they fought;  
then the kings of Canaan fought  
at Taanach, by the waters of Megiddo.  
They took no silver for spoil.

**20** From heaven the stars fought;  
from their courses they fought against Sisera.

**21** The torrent of Kishon swept them away,  
the ancient torrent, the torrent of Kishon.  
March on, my soul, with strength!

**22** Then the horses' hooves hammered  
with the galloping, galloping of his stallions.

**23** 'Curse Meroz,' said the messenger of the LORD,  
'curse its inhabitants utterly,  
because they did not come to the help of the LORD,  
to the help of the LORD against the mighty.'

## Jael

**24** Most blessed of women be Jael,  
the wife of Heber the Kenite;  
of women in the tent most blessed.

**25** He asked for water; she gave him milk.  
In a bowl fit for nobles she brought him curds.

**26** Her hand she put to the tent peg,  
her right hand to the workmen's hammer.  
She hammered Sisera, she crushed his head;  
she shattered and pierced his temple.

**27** Between her feet he sank, he fell, he lay;  
between her feet he sank, he fell;  
where he sank, there he fell, destroyed.

## Sisera's Mother

**28** Through the window she looked down and wailed,  
Sisera's mother, through the lattice:  
'Why is his chariot so slow in coming?  
Why do the hoofbeats of his chariots delay?'

**29** The wisest of her ladies answer her;  
she, too, gives herself the answer:

**30** 'Are they not finding and dividing the spoil?  
A womb, two wombs for every man;  
spoil of dyed cloth for Sisera,  
spoil of dyed cloth, embroidered,  
dyed cloth, two embroidered pieces  
for the necks of the spoil.'

**31** So may all Your enemies perish, LORD!  
But may those who love Him be like the sun  
when it goes out in its might.  
The land had rest forty years.

## Notes

- **v2 "When locks hang loose in Israel"** — *bi-fro'a pera'ot*, a verb with its own noun. *Pera* is long, uncut hair: the Nazirite *lets the locks of the hair of his head grow long* (Numbers 6:5), and Deuteronomy 32:42 has *the long-haired heads of the enemy*. Read this way, the song opens with fighters under a vow, their hair uncut. Older English reads *when leaders lead*; the Greek has *a revelation was revealed*, taking the verb as **uncover**.
- **v5 "The mountains flowed down… the One of Sinai"** — *harim nazelu*, from *nazal*, **to flow**, the word for *the flowing waters* of Exodus 15:8. The Greek has *were shaken*, reading *nazollu*, **quaked**, the form in Isaiah 64:1 (Hebrew 63:19), *the mountains quaked at Your presence*. *Zeh Sinai* is **this Sinai**; the same two words stand after *God* in Psalm 68:8, and there, as here, they read as a title: the God who met Israel at Sinai.
- **vv7, 11 "the villagers… His villagers"** — *perazon*, found only in this song, from *perazot*, **unwalled villages** (Ezekiel 38:11). The open country emptied until Deborah arose. The Greek reads *the mighty*. *Until I arose* is *shaqqamti*, which may be *I arose* or an old form of *you arose*; the Greek has *until Deborah arose*.
- **vv15–16 "resolves of heart… searchings of heart"** — *chiqqei lev*, then *chiqrei lev*: one letter changes. Reuben made great resolutions, then great deliberations, and stayed with the sheep.
- **v17 "why does he live as a guest by the ships?"** — *yagur*, the verb of the *ger*, the guest. Dan's first lot ran to the coast *opposite Joppa* (Joshua 19:46); the song asks why a tribe of Israel stayed by the ships, living like a guest.
- **vv20–21 "the stars fought… the torrent of Kishon swept them away"** — the prose of 4:15 says only that *the LORD threw Sisera… into panic*. The song says how: heaven fought, and the river rose over the chariots. *The ancient torrent*, *nachal qedumim*, is also read **the onrushing torrent**; the Greek has *torrent of the ancients*.
- **v24 "Most blessed of women be Jael"** — the Greek is *eulogētheiē en gynaixin*, **blessed among women**. Elizabeth greets Mary with the same words in Luke 1:42, *Eulogēmenē sy en gynaixin*.
- **vv26–27 "She hammered Sisera… between her feet he sank"** — the prose of 4:21 has him asleep when the peg goes in; the song has him fall at her feet. The two tellings are set side by side and neither is adjusted to the other. The Greek has *her left hand* to the peg.
- **v28 "wailed"** — *va-teyabbev*, found only here. His mother waits at the window, as the reader knows he is dead.
- **v30 "A womb, two wombs for every man"** — *racham rachamatayim*. *Racham* is the plain word for **womb**, and she uses it of the captured girls. The song lets her say it and does not comment. *For the necks of the spoil* is hard: most read it *for my neck, as spoil*.
