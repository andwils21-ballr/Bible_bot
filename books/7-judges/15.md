# Judges 15

## The Foxes

**1** After a time, in the days of the wheat harvest, Samson visited his wife with a young goat. He said, "I will go in to my wife in her room." But her father would not let him go in.

**2** Her father said, "I was sure that you hated her, so I gave her to your companion. Is not her younger sister better than she? Please, let her be yours instead."

**3** Samson said to them, "This time I am blameless toward the Philistines when I do them harm."

**4** Samson went and caught three hundred foxes. He took torches, turned the foxes tail to tail, and put one torch between each pair of tails.

**5** He set fire to the torches and let the foxes go into the standing grain of the Philistines. He burned up the stacked grain and the standing grain, and the vineyards and olive groves.

**6** The Philistines said, "Who did this?" They said, "Samson, the son-in-law of the Timnite, because he took his wife and gave her to his companion." So the Philistines went up and burned her and her father with fire.

**7** Samson said to them, "If you act like this, I will take revenge on you, and after that I will stop."

**8** He struck them down with a great slaughter. Then he went down and stayed in the cleft of the rock of Etam.

## Lehi

**9** The Philistines went up and camped in Judah and spread out at Lehi.

**10** The men of Judah said, "Why have you come up against us?" They said, "We have come up to bind Samson, to do to him as he did to us."

**11** Three thousand men of Judah went down to the cleft of the rock of Etam and said to Samson, "Do you not know that the Philistines rule over us? What is this you have done to us?" He said to them, "As they did to me, so I have done to them."

**12** They said to him, "We have come down to bind you and give you into the hand of the Philistines." Samson said to them, "Swear to me that you will not attack me yourselves."

**13** They said to him, "No. We will only bind you and give you into their hand, but we will not kill you." They bound him with two new ropes and brought him up from the rock.

**14** When he came to Lehi, the Philistines shouted as they met him. Then the Spirit of the LORD rushed on him, and the ropes on his arms became like flax that has caught fire, and the bonds melted off his hands.

**15** He found a fresh jawbone of a donkey, put out his hand, took it, and struck a thousand men with it.

**16** Samson said,

"With the jawbone of a donkey, heap upon heap!  
With the jawbone of a donkey I have struck down a thousand men."

**17** And it came to pass, when he had finished speaking, that he threw the jawbone from his hand, and he called that place Ramath-lehi.

## The Spring at Lehi

**18** He was very thirsty and called on the LORD and said, "You have given this great deliverance into the hand of Your servant, and now shall I die of thirst and fall into the hand of the uncircumcised?"

**19** God split open the hollow place that is at Lehi, and water came out of it. He drank, and his spirit returned, and he revived. So he called its name En-hakkore, which is at Lehi to this day.

**20** He judged Israel in the days of the Philistines twenty years.

## Notes

- **v4 "three hundred foxes"** — *shu'alim*, the word for both **fox** and **jackal**; translators divide over which is meant. The Greek has *foxes*. The burning falls in *the days of the wheat harvest* (v1), when the grain stands dry.
- **v6 "burned her and her father with fire"** — the threat of the thirty companions in 14:15: *we will burn you and your father's house with fire*. Samson's wife dies of the very thing she gave up his riddle to escape.
- **v8 "a great slaughter"** — the Hebrew is *shoq al yarekh*, **leg on thigh**, an expression found only here. It is thought to describe a thorough, merciless blow, but nothing else in the Bible fixes its sense. The Greek is literal, *leg upon thigh*.
- **v14 "the Spirit of the LORD rushed on him"** — the third time (14:6, 14:19). Each of the three is followed at once by an act of violence.
- **v15 "a fresh jawbone"** — *lechi chamor teriyyah*. *Teriyyah* is **moist, fresh**, the word for an open wound in Isaiah 1:6. The word marks a bone not yet dried out.
- **v16 "heap upon heap"** — *chamor chamorattayim*. *Chamor* is a **donkey**, and *chomer* is a **heap**; the line puns on the weapon. The same *heap* word describes the frogs in Exodus 8:14, *heap after heap*. The Greek reads *wiping out, I wiped them out*, taking the word from another root.
- **v17 "Ramath-lehi"** — *ramat lechi*, **the height of the jawbone**, or, from *ramah*, **to throw**, **the throwing of the jawbone**. The text gives the name at the moment he throws it.
- **v18 "this great deliverance"** — *ha-teshu'ah ha-gedolah*, from *yasha*, **to save**, the verb the messenger used of him in 13:5, *he shall begin to save Israel*. The first deliverance is the one he now calls great, and he asks for water.
- **v19 "the hollow place"** — *ha-maktesh*, found again in Zephaniah 1:11 as the name of a district in Jerusalem, and in Proverbs 27:22 as a **mortar**. It can be a hollow in the ground or in the jawbone; the Greek has *the pit*. *En-hakkore*, **spring of the caller**, is named for what he did in v18: *va-yiqra*, he called.
