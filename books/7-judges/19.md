# Judges 19

## Bethlehem

**1** And it came to pass in those days, when there was no king in Israel, that a Levite was living as a guest in the far parts of the hill country of Ephraim. He took for himself a secondary wife from Bethlehem in Judah.

**2** His secondary wife whored against him, and she left him and went to her father's house at Bethlehem in Judah, and she was there some four months.

**3** Her husband rose and went after her, to speak to her heart and bring her back. His servant was with him, and a pair of donkeys. She brought him into her father's house, and when the girl's father saw him, he was glad to meet him.

**4** His father-in-law, the girl's father, kept him there, and he stayed with him three days. They ate and drank and spent the nights there.

**5** And it came to pass on the fourth day that they rose early in the morning, and he got up to go. But the girl's father said to his son-in-law, "Refresh yourself with a bit of bread, and after that you may go."

**6** So the two of them sat down and ate and drank together, and the girl's father said to the man, "Please agree to stay the night, and let your heart be merry."

**7** The man got up to go, but his father-in-law pressed him, and he stayed there again for the night.

**8** He rose early in the morning on the fifth day to go, and the girl's father said, "Please refresh yourself, and wait until the day wears on." So the two of them ate.

**9** When the man got up to go, he and his secondary wife and his servant, his father-in-law, the girl's father, said to him, "The day has worn on toward evening. Please stay the night. The day is coming to an end; stay here and let your heart be merry. Tomorrow you can rise early for your journey and go home to your tent."

## Gibeah

**10** But the man would not stay the night. He rose and went, and came as far as opposite Jebus, that is, Jerusalem. He had with him a pair of saddled donkeys, and his secondary wife was with him.

**11** When they were near Jebus, the day was far spent, and the servant said to his master, "Come, let us turn aside to this city of the Jebusites and spend the night in it."

**12** His master said to him, "We will not turn aside into a city of foreigners who are not of the sons of Israel. We will go on to Gibeah."

**13** He said to his servant, "Come, let us draw near to one of these places and spend the night at Gibeah or at Ramah."

**14** So they passed on and went their way, and the sun went down on them near Gibeah, which belongs to Benjamin.

**15** They turned aside there to go in and spend the night at Gibeah. He went in and sat down in the open square of the city, but no one took them into his house for the night.

**16** Then an old man came in from his work in the field at evening. The man was from the hill country of Ephraim, and he was living as a guest in Gibeah; the men of the place were Benjaminites.

**17** He lifted up his eyes and saw the traveler in the open square of the city, and the old man said, "Where are you going, and where do you come from?"

**18** He said to him, "We are passing from Bethlehem in Judah to the far parts of the hill country of Ephraim. I am from there. I went to Bethlehem in Judah, and I am going to the house of the LORD, but no one takes me into his house.

**19** We have straw and fodder for our donkeys, and bread and wine for me and your maidservant and for the young man with your servants. We lack nothing."

**20** The old man said, "Peace to you. Let all your needs be on me; only do not spend the night in the square."

**21** He brought him into his house and gave the donkeys fodder. They washed their feet and ate and drank.

## The Night

**22** While they were making their hearts merry, the men of the city, worthless men, surrounded the house and beat on the door. They said to the old man, the master of the house, "Bring out the man who came into your house, so that we may know him."

**23** The man, the master of the house, went out to them and said to them, "No, my brothers, please do not do evil. Since this man has come into my house, do not do this outrage.

**24** Here are my virgin daughter and his secondary wife. Let me bring them out now. Violate them and do with them what is good in your eyes, but to this man do not do this outrageous thing."

**25** But the men would not listen to him. So the Levite seized his secondary wife and put her out to them, and they knew her and abused her all night until morning, and let her go as the dawn came up.

**26** The woman came at the turning of the morning and fell down at the entrance of the man's house where her master was, until it was light.

**27** Her master rose in the morning, opened the doors of the house and went out to go on his way. The woman, his secondary wife, was lying at the entrance of the house, with her hands on the threshold.

**28** He said to her, "Get up, let us go." But there was no answer. Then he put her on the donkey, and the man rose and went to his place.

**29** When he came to his house, he took the knife, seized his secondary wife and cut her limb by limb into twelve pieces, and sent her throughout all the territory of Israel.

**30** And it would come to pass that everyone who saw it would say, "Nothing like this has happened or been seen from the day the sons of Israel came up from the land of Egypt until this day. Consider it, take counsel and speak."

## Notes

- **v2 "His secondary wife whored against him"** — *va-tizneh alav*. Leaving him for her father's house is an odd act for an unfaithful wife, and the Greek does not have the verb at all: *his secondary wife went away from him*. The text follows the Hebrew.
- **v3 "to speak to her heart"** — *ledabber al libbah*, the words used of Shechem to Dinah (Genesis 34:3) and of Joseph to his brothers (50:21): to speak tenderly, to win back. Nothing in the chapter shows him doing it.
- **vv4–9 the father's hospitality** — five days of *eat and drink… let your heart be merry*. The delay puts the Levite on the road late on the fifth day, and the chapter turns on where he will spend the night. *Refresh yourself with a bit of bread* is *se'ad libbekha pat lechem*, Abraham's offer to his guests in Genesis 18:5.
- **v12 "a city of foreigners who are not of the sons of Israel"** — Jebus is passed by for safety, and Gibeah, a town of Benjamin, is chosen. What follows happens in Israel.
- **v18 "I am going to the house of the LORD"** — the Greek has *I am going to my house*. The Levite's house is in the hill country of Ephraim (v1), and so is Shiloh, where the house of God stood (18:31); either reading fits the road he is on. The text follows the Hebrew.
- **vv22–24 "so that we may know him"** — the scene repeats Sodom almost word for word: the house surrounded (Genesis 19:4), *Bring out… that we may know* (19:5), *my brothers, do not do evil* (19:7), and a host who offers the women instead, *do with them what is good in your eyes* (19:8). At Sodom the messengers pulled Lot inside and struck the men blind. Here no one comes.
- **vv23–24 "this outrage"** — *nevalah*, the word for what Shechem did to Dinah, *an outrage in Israel* (Genesis 34:7).
- **v28 "But there was no answer"** — the Hebrew does not say whether she is dead. The Greek adds *for she was dead*.
- **v29 "the knife… cut her limb by limb"** — *ha-ma'akhelet*, the knife Abraham took to slaughter his son (Genesis 22:10), and *va-yenatcheha*, the verb for cutting a burnt offering *into pieces* (Leviticus 1:6, 12). *Twelve pieces*, one for each tribe. Saul will do the same with a pair of oxen, *va-yenatchehu… va-yeshallach be-khol gevul Yisra'el*, the same words (1 Samuel 11:7).
- **v30 "Consider it, take counsel and speak"** — the chapter ends with a call to the tribes, and chapter 20 is their answer.
