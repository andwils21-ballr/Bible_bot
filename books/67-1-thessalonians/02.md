# 1 Thessalonians 2

## Like a Nurse, Like a Father

**1** For you yourselves know, brothers, that our coming to you was not in vain.

**2** Though we had already suffered and been shamefully treated at Philippi, as you know, we had the courage in our God to tell you the gospel of God in a hard struggle.

**3** For our appeal does not come from error or impurity or deceit,

**4** but just as we have been approved by God to be entrusted with the gospel, so we speak, not to please men, but to please God, who tests our hearts.

**5** For we never came with words of flattery, as you know, nor with a pretext for greed, God is witness,

**6** nor did we seek glory from men, whether from you or from others,

**7** though we could have made demands as apostles of Christ. But we were gentle among you, like a nursing mother caring for her own children.

**8** So, longing for you, we were glad to share with you not only the gospel of God but our own lives, because you had become dear to us.

**9** For you remember, brothers, our labor and toil: working night and day so as not to be a burden to any of you, we proclaimed to you the gospel of God.

**10** You are witnesses, and God also, how holy and righteous and blameless we were toward you believers,

**11** just as you know how, like a father with his own children, we treated each one of you,

**12** urging you and encouraging you and charging you to walk in a way worthy of God, who calls you into His own kingdom and glory.

## Received as the Word of God

**13** For this reason we also thank God constantly, that when you received the word of God that you heard from us, you accepted it not as the word of men but as what it truly is, the word of God, which is at work in you believers.

**14** For you, brothers, became imitators of the churches of God in Christ Jesus that are in Judea. You suffered the same things from your own countrymen as they did from the Jews,

**15** who killed both the Lord Jesus and the prophets, and drove us out. They displease God and oppose all men,

**16** hindering us from speaking to the Gentiles so that they may be saved, so as always to fill up the measure of their sins. But wrath has come upon them at last.

## Longing to See Them

**17** But since we were torn away from you, brothers, for a short time, in person not in heart, we were all the more eager, with great desire, to see you face to face,

**18** because we wanted to come to you, I, Paul, again and again, but Satan hindered us.

**19** For what is our hope or joy or crown of boasting before our Lord Jesus at His coming? Is it not you?

**20** For you are our glory and joy.

---

## Notes

- **vv14–15 "from the Jews, who killed both the Lord Jesus and the prophets"** — *hypo tōn Ioudaiōn*. The sentence sets two persecutions side by side: the Thessalonians suffered *from your own countrymen*, as the churches in Judea did from the people of Judea. *Ioudaioi* is both **Jews** and **Judeans**. Paul, a Jew himself (Philippians 3:5), speaks of those who drove the gospel out, as Jesus said, *Jerusalem, Jerusalem, who kills the prophets* (Matthew 23:37).
