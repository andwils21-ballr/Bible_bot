# 1 Meqabyan 3

## The Voice from Heaven

**1** A voice came from heaven saying, "Abya, Sila and Fentos, servants of God Most High: as you have seen these who died and rose before you, so you will rise after your death, and your faces will shine in the kingdom of heaven."

**2** They went with those men and became witnesses there.

**3** Then they prayed and gave thanks and bowed down to God, and death and the king's punishment did not frighten them.

## Before the King

**4** They went to the king's servants, and did not frighten them, but were like a lamb without evil. When they reached them, the servants seized them, beat them, bound them and flogged them, and brought them to the king and stood them before him.

**5** The king said to them, "You rebels, how is it that you will not sacrifice and bow down to my gods?"

**6** Those holy, blessed, chosen and joyful brothers, Abya, Sila and Fentos, precious as a gem of great price, answered him as with one mouth.

**7** They said to that plague of a king, "We will not sacrifice or bow down to unclean gods that have no heart or understanding,

**8** or to gold and silver, wood and stone, the work of the hands of the sons of men, the work of human hands, that have no soul, mind or understanding, that do no good to the one who does them good, and no harm to the one who does them harm."

**9** The king answered them, "Why do you act like this and revile my honored gods? They too know who wrongs them and reviles them."

**10** They answered him, "We revile them and do not honor them, for they are as nothing to us."

**11** The king answered them, "According to the evil of your deeds I will punish you, and I will destroy the beauty of your appearance with great affliction, with flogging and with fire.

**12** Now tell me whether you will sacrifice to my gods or not; if not, I will raise against you the scourge, the lash, the rope and the sword."

**13** They answered him, "We will not bow down or sacrifice to unclean gods." The king ordered them beaten with thick rods, then with whips, and after that torn until their inner parts could be seen.

**14** Then they bound them and kept them in the prison, in the guardhouse, until he could decide how to kill and punish them.

**15** They took them to the prison and bound them with heavy chains without mercy, and they stayed in the prison three days and three nights.

## The Second Trial

**16** After three days the king ordered a herald to go round, so that the princes, counselors, governors and elders of the city would gather.

**17** When King Tsirutsaydan had sat down in the court, he ordered those blessed men, Abya, Sila and Fentos, to be brought, and they stood before him, bound and wounded.

**18** The king said to them, "Have you chosen better while you sat these three days, or are you still in your former wickedness?"

**19** The blessed soldiers of God answered him, "We are steadfast, and we will not say yes to the blasphemy you speak, full of evil and uncleanness."

**20** That wicked man raged and ordered them hung up and their wounds opened again, and their blood flowed on the ground.

**21** Again he ordered them burned with torches of thorn, so that their flesh was scorched, and they did so. The blessed men said to him, "Speak on, vile one. The more you increase our punishment, the more our reward increases."

## The Beasts

**22** Again he ordered fierce beasts brought, leopard, bear and lion, kept from their food, to be let loose on them, to finish them off at once, bones and all.

**23** He ordered the keepers of the beasts to let them loose on them, and they did as he commanded. They bound the holy martyrs with their hands behind them, and bound them again to stakes they had driven into the ground.

**24** The beasts rushed at them roaring, but when they reached the martyrs they bowed down to them and greeted them.

**25** They turned back roaring on their keepers, frightened the keepers and drove them on until they had brought them before the king's face.

**26** There they killed seventy-five of the wicked company.

**27** Many were crushed as they pressed on one another in fear, until the king himself left his throne and fled. With difficulty they caught the beasts and took them back to their dens.

## Five Brothers

**28** Then there came *[words lost]*

**29** The martyrs answered their brothers, "It is not right for us to flee after we have stood up to bear witness. If you are afraid, go, flee yourselves."

**30** Their younger brothers said, "We too will stand with you before the king, and if you die, we will die with you."

**31** After this the king saw from the upper room of his royal palace that those blessed men had been set free, and that the five brothers were standing together. It was found out that the men who commanded the soldiers were their brothers, and they told the king. The king raged and roared like a wild boar in his fury.

**32** The king ordered the five brothers seized and put in prison until he could decide how to punish them. They took them to the prison and bound them with heavy chains in hollowed stocks, without mercy.

**33** King Tsirutsaydan said, "These misguided young men have worn me out. How hard the hearts of these men are! The evil of their deeds is as great as the strength of their might; when I say they will turn for the better, they make their hearts worse.

**34** I will repay them according to the evil of their deeds. I will burn their bodies with fire until they are ashes, and there I will scatter the ashes of their bodies like dust on a great mountain, for the wind to carry away."

## The Furnace

**35** After he had said this, he waited three days, and then ordered the holy men brought. When the holy men came near, he ordered a fire kindled in a furnace in a great pit, and things thrown into it to make the cauldrons boil and the fire blaze: pitch and papyrus, fat, *maʿāra gerā*, *ʾaflāṭis* and sulfur.

**36** When the furnace was blazing, his servants went to the king and said, "We have done what you commanded. Send the men who are to be thrown in."

**37** He ordered them to take the men and carry them into the furnace of fire, and they did as the king commanded. When the blessed men went into it, they gave up their souls to God.

**38** Angels received them while those who had thrown them in looked on, and carried their souls to the bosom of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, into the garden of joy.

## Notes

- **v1 "Abya, Sila and Fentos"** — *ʾAbyā, Silā, Fantos*, the sons of Maccabeus (2:2), named here for the first time. *These who died and rose before you* are the two men of 2:27.
- **v2 "They went with those men and became witnesses there"** — *ḥoru… wa-konu samāʿta*, past verbs: the verse tells it as done, and vv3–4 then tell how. *Samāʿt* is **witnesses**, the word the Church uses for its martyrs.
- **v8 "that do no good… and no harm"** — Jeremiah on the idols: *Do not be afraid of them, for they cannot do evil, neither is it in them to do good* (Jeremiah 10:5).
- **vv22–24 the beasts bowed down to them** — Daniel in the den: *My God sent His messenger and shut the lions' mouths* (Daniel 6:22).
- **v26 "seventy-five"** — the Ge'ez has *wa-5*, **and five**; the tens are lost. The number in the verse is the one the Ethiopian Church reads.
- **v28 "Then there came *[words lost]*"** — the Ge'ez has only *wa-maṣʾu*, **and they came**. Vv29–31 show what is missing: two younger brothers come to the three.
- **v31 "that the five brothers were standing together… in his fury"** — the EOTC Ge'ez breaks off after *wa-yəqawwəmu*, **and they were standing**; the rest of the verse is from Horovitz's Ge'ez excerpts (1905).
- **v35 "a furnace… pitch and papyrus"** — the furnace of Daniel 3, heated *seven times more than it was usually heated* (Daniel 3:19); in the Greek of Daniel the servants fed it *with naphtha, pitch, tow and brushwood* (3:46). *Maʿāra gerā* and *ʾaflāṭis* are two more burning materials whose names are not otherwise known.
- **v38 "the bosom of Abraham"** — *ḥəḍna ʾAbrəhām*, where the angels carried Lazarus (Luke 16:22).
- **Source text** — Ge'ez: Beta Masaheft, Universität Hamburg (text of the EOTC printed Bible), CC BY-SA 4.0; v31 completed from J. Horovitz's Ge'ez excerpts (1905). English translation by this project.
