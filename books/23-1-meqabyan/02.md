# 1 Meqabyan 2

## Maccabeus and His Sons

**1** There was a man of the tribe of Benjamin whose name was Maccabeus.

**2** He had *[words lost]*

**3** They found them and said to them, "Will you not bow down and sacrifice to the gods of Tsirutsaydan the king, as he commanded?

**4** If you refuse, we will seize you and take you to the king, and we will destroy all your goods, as the king commanded."

**5** These fine young men answered them, "As for us, there is One we bow down to: the God of our fathers, Creator of the heavens and the earth and the sea and all that is in it, Creator of the sun and moon and stars and clouds. He is the true God, the One we serve and believe in."

**6** These servants of the king were four commanders, each with fifty soldiers who carried their weapons of war.

**7** When they tried to seize these holy men, they escaped from their hands, and no one harmed them. They went away carrying their weapons, for they were very strong and mighty young men.

**8** One of them could kill a bear by strangling it, and he would strangle it at once, as one strangles a hen.

**9** One of them could kill a lion with a single blow of a stone or a single stroke of a club.

**10** One of them could kill a hundred in battle at once with one sword. So their name and their hunting were spoken of in all the land of Moab and of the Chaldeans.

**11** They were strong in might, and they had beauty, grace and good speech.

**12** The beauty of their appearance was wonderful, but above all the beauty of their hearts surpassed it, because they worshiped God and did not fear death.

## The Brothers on the Mountain

**13** When they frightened the soldiers, no one could seize them, and being strong, they escaped to a high mountain.

**14** The soldiers went back into the city, shut the city gates, and terrified the people, saying, "If you do not bring us these men, the Maccabees, we will burn your city with fire, and send to the king and destroy your city."

**15** At that time the people of the city came out, the poor and the rich, men, widows and orphans, and all cried out together. They lifted their eyes to the mountain and cried out to them, "Do not destroy us and our city."

**16** Then the brothers wept together, and they feared God.

**17** They turned their faces toward the east, spread out their hands, and prayed to God together, saying, "Shall we refuse these men, O Lord, these who transgress Your law and Your commandment?

**18** We do not want to hear the word of that wicked man, who does not believe in Your law but in gold and silver, wood and stone, the work of men's hands.

**19** He holds that these things created him, though You are his Creator, You who kill and make alive. He sheds human blood and eats human flesh.

**20** We do not want to see the face of that wicked man or to hear his word.

**21** But if You command us, we will go to him and hand ourselves over to death for Your sake, and we will not hear the word of that wicked man when he says, 'Sacrifice to my gods.'

**22** We, O Lord, have trusted in You with all our heart, the God of our fathers, who did Your will and walked in Your law, the God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, who tests the heart and the kidneys,

**23** who examines the righteous and the sinner and searches what is in the heart of man. Nothing is hidden from You; what is secret is uncovered before You.

**24** We have no other god but You.

**25** But be our strength and might and refuge in this work by which we serve You, so that we may hand ourselves over to death for Your holy name.

**26** When Israel went into the land of Egypt, You heard the prayer of Jacob; and now we ask You, holy God."

## The Two Men

**27** When two men appeared to them and stood before them, beautiful to look at, swords of fire like lightning came down on them and killed them at once and cut off their heads; and at once they rose alive, as before.

**28** The beauty of their appearance became greater still. They shone more than the sun and were more beautiful than before.

## Notes

- **v1 "Maccabeus"** — *Maqābyos*, the man the book is named from (1:1 note). He is of Benjamin, and is not Judas Maccabeus of the Greek 1 Maccabees.
- **v2 "He had *[words lost]*"** — the Ge'ez breaks off after *wa-bottu*, **and he had**. Horovitz's Ge'ez excerpts (1905) go on with three sons, the young men of v5, whose names are given at 3:1.
- **v6 "fifty soldiers"** — the numeral is lost in the Ge'ez, which has *wa-baba%*, **each with %**. The number in the verse is the one the Ethiopian Church reads.
- **vv8–9 the bear and the lion** — David told Saul, *your servant has struck down both lions and bears* (1 Samuel 17:36), and Samson *tore the lion as one tears a young goat* (Judges 14:6).
- **v12 "the beauty of their hearts surpassed it"** — the book sets their bodily beauty (v11) against the beauty of the heart; the words for both are *sən*. The Lord said to Samuel, *man looks on the outward appearance, but the LORD looks on the heart* (1 Samuel 16:7).
- **v19 "You who kill and make alive"** — *za-təqatəl wa-taḥayyu*, the words of the song of Moses, *I kill and I make alive* (Deuteronomy 32:39), and of Hannah, *The LORD kills and makes alive* (1 Samuel 2:6).
- **v22 "who tests the heart and the kidneys"** — *ləbba wa-kwəlyāta*, the words of Jeremiah, *O LORD of hosts, who judges righteously, who tests the kidneys and the heart* (Jeremiah 11:20; Psalm 7:9).
- **v26 "You heard the prayer of Jacob"** — Genesis tells of God speaking to Jacob as he went down to Egypt, *Do not be afraid to go down to Egypt* (Genesis 46:3). The prayer it gives him is earlier, before he met Esau (32:9–12).
- **v27 "When two men appeared to them… and at once they rose alive"** — the EOTC Ge'ez breaks off after *wa-soba ʾastarʾayəwwomu*, **when they appeared to them**; the rest of the verse is from Horovitz's Ge'ez excerpts (1905). Who was struck is said at 3:1: *these who died and rose before you*, the two men.
- **Source text** — Ge'ez: Beta Masaheft, Universität Hamburg (text of the EOTC printed Bible), CC BY-SA 4.0; v27 completed from J. Horovitz's Ge'ez excerpts (1905). English translation by this project.
