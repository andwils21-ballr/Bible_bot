# Jonah 4

## Jonah's Anger

**1** But this was a great evil to Jonah, and he was angry.

**2** He prayed to the LORD and said, "Please, LORD, was this not what I said while I was still in my own land? That is why I hurried to flee to Tarshish; for I knew that You are a God gracious and merciful, slow to anger and abundant in loyal love, and You relent of the evil.

**3** So now, LORD, please take my life from me, for my death is better than my life."

**4** The LORD said, "Is it right for you to be angry?"

## The Plant

**5** Jonah went out of the city and sat east of the city. He made himself a booth there and sat under it in the shade, until he could see what would become of the city.

**6** The LORD God appointed a plant, and it grew up over Jonah to be a shade over his head, to deliver him from his evil; and Jonah was very glad about the plant.

**7** But God appointed a worm when dawn came up the next day, and it struck the plant, and it withered.

**8** When the sun rose, God appointed a scorching east wind, and the sun struck Jonah's head, and he grew faint. He asked that he might die, and said, "My death is better than my life."

**9** God said to Jonah, "Is it right for you to be angry about the plant?" He said, "It is right for me to be angry, even to death."

**10** The LORD said, "You had pity on the plant, for which you did not labor and which you did not make grow, which came into being in a night and perished in a night.

**11** Should I not have pity on Nineveh, the great city, in which there are more than a hundred and twenty thousand people who do not know their right hand from their left, and many animals?"

## Notes

- **v1 "this was a great evil to Jonah"** — *va-yera el Yonah ra'ah gedolah*, word for word **it was evil to Jonah, a great evil**. The evil Nineveh turned from and the evil God relented of (3:10) now lands on the prophet, and it is *great*, the book's word for the city (1:2; 3:3). *Va-yichar lo*, **he was angry**, word for word **it burned to him**.
- **vv2–3 "a God gracious and merciful, slow to anger and abundant in loyal love, and You relent of the evil… take my life"** — the LORD's own name from Sinai, *God merciful and gracious, slow to anger, and abundant in loyal love and truth* (Exodus 34:6), in the same words and order as Joel 2:13, with *gracious* first and *relent of the evil* added; the English is Joel's. Jonah gives it as his reason for running: he knew what God is. *Take my life* is Elijah's word under the broom tree, *now, O LORD, take my life, for I am no better than my fathers* (1 Kings 19:4). *Ve-attah*, **So now**, draws the consequence.
- **vv4, 9 "Is it right for you to be angry?"** — *ha-heitev charah lakh*. *Heitev* is **do well** or **do right**, and also **greatly**, as the Greek takes it: *Are you very grieved?* Jonah's answer in v9 uses the same words back, *heitev charah li ad mavet*, **it is right for me to be angry, even to death**, or **I am greatly angry, even to death**. The question is close to the LORD's to Cain, *Why has it burned in you… if you do well* (Genesis 4:6–7), and Cain too went out and settled *east of Eden* (Genesis 4:16).
- **v5 "sat east of the city… a booth… until he could see what would become of the city"** — *sukkah*, **booth**, the shelter of the feast, *You shall live in booths for seven days* (Leviticus 23:42). The forty days are still running (3:4), and Jonah waits to see whether the city will be overturned.
- **vv6–8 "The LORD God appointed a plant… God appointed a worm… God appointed a scorching east wind"** — *va-yeman*, **appointed**, as with the fish (1:17), now three times in three verses. *Qiqayon*, **a plant**, is found only in this chapter, and what plant it was is not known; the Greek has *a gourd*. *Le-hatsil lo me-ra'ato*, **to deliver him from his evil**: the shade is for his misery, and the word is again *ra'ah* (v1). *Va-yismach… simchah gedolah*, word for word **he rejoiced a great joy**: Jonah's only gladness in the book is for his own shade. *Charishit*, **scorching**, is found only here; the east wind is the LORD's wind of Hosea 13:15.
- **v8 "He asked that he might die"** — *va-yish'al et nafsho lamut*, word for word the words of Elijah, *He asked that he might die* (1 Kings 19:4). *Va-yit'allaf*, **he grew faint**, as the young men who *will faint for thirst* (Amos 8:13).
- **vv10–11 "You had pity on the plant… Should I not have pity on Nineveh"** — *chasta*, **you had pity**, and *lo achus*, **should I not have pity**, the verb of Joel's *Spare Your people, LORD* (Joel 2:17). *She-bin laylah hayah u-vin laylah avad*, word for word **which was a son of a night and perished a son of a night**. *Shteim esreh ribbo*, **twelve ten-thousands**, a hundred and twenty thousand. *Who do not know their right hand from their left* may be the little children, like those of Moses' day *who today do not know good and evil* (Deuteronomy 1:39), or the whole people of the city. The book ends on the LORD's question, with no answer from Jonah, and the last words are *and many animals*, the beasts that fasted in sackcloth (3:7–8).
