# Jonah 2

## Jonah's Prayer

**1** Then Jonah prayed to the LORD his God from the belly of the fish,

**2** and said:  
"I called out of my distress to the LORD,  
and He answered me;  
out of the belly of Sheol I cried for help,  
and You heard my voice.

**3** You cast me into the depths,  
into the heart of the seas,  
and the current swirled around me;  
all Your breakers and Your waves  
have gone over me.

**4** I said, 'I am driven away  
from before Your eyes;  
yet I will look again  
toward Your holy temple.'

**5** The waters surrounded me up to my neck;  
the deep swirled around me;  
reeds were wrapped around my head.

**6** I went down to the roots of the mountains;  
the earth, its bars were behind me forever.  
Yet You brought up my life from the pit,  
LORD my God.

**7** When my soul grew faint within me,  
I remembered the LORD,  
and my prayer came to You,  
to Your holy temple.

**8** Those who hold to empty idols  
forsake their loyal love.

**9** But I, with the sound of thanksgiving,  
will sacrifice to You;  
what I have vowed I will pay.  
Salvation belongs to the LORD!"

**10** The LORD spoke to the fish, and it vomited Jonah out onto the dry ground.

## Notes

- **v1 "from the belly of the fish"** — in the Hebrew text vv1–10 are 2:2–11, and the Greek follows the Hebrew; the English numbering is followed. In this verse alone the fish is *dagah*, a feminine form; it is *dag* in 1:17 and 2:10. *Me'ei*, **belly**, is the inward parts, as in *from your inward parts* (Genesis 25:23).
- **v2 "I called out of my distress to the LORD, and He answered me… You heard my voice"** — the prayer is made of the psalms. The first line is *In my distress I called to the LORD, and He answered me* (Psalm 120:1), and the second has the words of *to my God I cried for help. From His temple He heard my voice* (Psalm 18:6). *Mi-beten She'ol*, **out of the belly of Sheol**, uses *beten*, the word for **belly** and **womb**, as in *Two nations are in your belly* (Genesis 25:23).
- **v3 "You cast me into the depths… all Your breakers and Your waves have gone over me"** — the sailors hurled him (1:15), but Jonah says *You* cast me. *Tashlikheni metsulah*, **You cast me into the depths**, is the verb and noun of Micah's hope, *You will cast all their sins into the depths of the sea* (Micah 7:19), and of the Egyptians who *went down into the depths like a stone* (Exodus 15:5). The last line is word for word the psalm's, *all Your breakers and Your waves have gone over me* (Psalm 42:7). *Bi-levav yammim*, **into the heart of the seas**, is where the ship of Tyre sank (Ezekiel 27:26).
- **v4 "I am driven away from before Your eyes; yet I will look again toward Your holy temple"** — the psalm says *I am cut off from before Your eyes* (Psalm 31:22); *nigrashti*, **driven away**, has nearly the letters of its *nigrazti*, **cut off**, and is the verb of Cain, *You have driven me out this day* (Genesis 4:14). *Akh*, **yet**, makes the second line a hope; the Greek reads it as a question, *shall I look again toward Your holy temple?*
- **vv5–6 "The waters surrounded me up to my neck… reeds were wrapped around my head… I went down to the roots of the mountains"** — *ad nefesh*, **up to my neck**, as in *the waters have come up to my neck* (Psalm 69:1), and *afafuni*, **surrounded me**, as in *The cords of death surrounded me* (Psalm 18:4). *Tehom*, **the deep**, is the deep of *darkness lay over the face of the deep* (Genesis 1:2). *Suf*, **reeds**, are the reeds of *the Sea of Reeds* (Exodus 15:4). *Yaradti*, **I went down**, is the last step of the descent of chapter 1 (1:3, 5), down to *the roots of the mountains*.
- **vv6–7 "Yet You brought up my life from the pit… When my soul grew faint within me, I remembered the LORD"** — *va-ta'al*, **You brought up**, the turn of the prayer, as in *You brought up my soul from Sheol; You kept me alive, so that I did not go down to the pit* (Psalm 30:3), and *shachat*, **the pit**, as in *who redeems your life from the pit* (Psalm 103:4). *Be-hit'attef alai nafshi*, **when my soul grew faint within me**, is the psalm's *When my spirit grows faint within me* with *soul* for *spirit* (Psalm 142:3), and *my prayer came to You* answers *Let my prayer come before You* (Psalm 88:2).
- **v8 "Those who hold to empty idols forsake their loyal love"** — *meshammerim havlei shav* are nearly the words of *I hate those who hold to empty idols* (Psalm 31:6). *Chasdam*, **their loyal love**, may be the *chesed* they themselves owe, or the LORD, the one who gives it, whom they leave. The Greek has *their mercy*. Spoken from the fish, the line stands beside chapter 1, where the men who once cried each *to his god* (1:5) left their gods and *greatly feared the LORD* (1:16).
- **v9 "with the sound of thanksgiving… what I have vowed I will pay. Salvation belongs to the LORD!"** — *be-qol todah*, **with the sound of thanksgiving**, as in *to make the sound of thanksgiving heard* (Psalm 26:7); *what I have vowed I will pay*, as *I will pay my vows to the LORD* (Psalm 116:14). The sailors made vows on deck (1:16); Jonah makes his in the fish. The last words are the psalm's, *Salvation belongs to the LORD* (Psalm 3:8).
- **v10 "it vomited Jonah out onto the dry ground"** — *va-yaqe*, **vomited out**, is the land's verb in the law, *it will vomit you out* (Leviticus 18:28). *Ha-yabbashah*, **the dry ground**, is what the LORD made (1:9) and what the sailors could not reach (1:13).
