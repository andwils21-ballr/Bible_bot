# Ruth 3

## Naomi's Plan

**1** Naomi her mother-in-law said to her, "My daughter, should I not seek rest for you, so that it may be well with you?

**2** And now, is not Boaz our relative, whose young women you have been with? Tonight he is winnowing barley at the threshing floor.

**3** Wash and anoint yourself, put on your clothes and go down to the threshing floor. But do not make yourself known to the man until he has finished eating and drinking.

**4** When he lies down, note the place where he lies. Then go in, uncover his feet and lie down, and he will tell you what to do."

**5** She said to her, "All that you say I will do."

## At Midnight

**6** She went down to the threshing floor and did all that her mother-in-law had commanded her.

**7** When Boaz had eaten and drunk, and his heart was merry, he went to lie down at the end of the heap of grain. Then she came quietly, uncovered his feet and lay down.

**8** And it came to pass at midnight that the man was startled and turned over, and there was a woman lying at his feet.

**9** He said, "Who are you?" She said, "I am Ruth your servant. Spread your wing over your servant, for you are a redeemer."

**10** He said, "May you be blessed by the LORD, my daughter. You have made this last loyal love greater than the first, in not going after young men, whether poor or rich.

**11** And now, my daughter, do not be afraid. All that you say I will do for you, for all the people at the gate of my town know that you are a woman of worth.

**12** And now, it is true that I am a redeemer, but there is a redeemer nearer than I.

**13** Stay here tonight. And it shall come to pass in the morning, if he will redeem you, good, let him redeem. But if he is not willing to redeem you, then as the LORD lives, I will redeem you myself. Lie down until the morning."

**14** So she lay at his feet until the morning, and rose before one could recognize another. He said, "Let it not be known that the woman came to the threshing floor."

**15** He said, "Bring the cloak you are wearing and hold it out." She held it, and he measured out six measures of barley and put it on her. Then he went into the town.

## Naomi Waits

**16** She came to her mother-in-law, who said, "How did it go, my daughter?" She told her all that the man had done for her,

**17** and said, "He gave me these six measures of barley, for he said, 'Do not go back empty to your mother-in-law.'"

**18** She said, "Wait, my daughter, until you know how the matter turns out, for the man will not rest until he has settled it today."

## Notes

- **v1 "rest for you"** — *manoach*, the word Naomi prayed in 1:9: *May the LORD grant that you find rest, each of you in the house of her husband.* What she asked the LORD for, she now sets out to arrange.
- **vv4, 7, 8, 14 "his feet"** — *margelotav*, **the place of his feet**, found in this chapter and once in Daniel. *Feet* can be a polite word for what is not named (Judges 3:24, *covering his feet*), and the chapter never says more than *feet*. What happens at night is left as the text leaves it.
- **v9 "Spread your wing over your servant"** — *kenafekha*, the **wing** or corner of a garment. Boaz had prayed that she would find refuge under the LORD's *wings* (2:12); she asks him to be the answer. To spread the corner of one's garment over a woman is a sign of taking her in marriage: God says it of Jerusalem, *I spread My wing over you… and entered into a covenant with you* (Ezekiel 16:8), and Deuteronomy 22:30 forbids uncovering a father's *wing*, his wife.
- **v9 "for you are a redeemer"** — the *go'el* buys back a family's land (Leviticus 25:25). Ruth joins the duty of the redeemer to the duty of marrying a dead man's widow (Deuteronomy 25:5), which the law gives to a brother, not a redeemer. Chapter 4 holds the two together.
- **v10 "this last loyal love greater than the first"** — the first was to Naomi (1:8, 2:11); the last is to the family's dead, in seeking a husband who can raise up a name for Mahlon (4:10).
- **v11 "a woman of worth"** — *eshet chayil*, as Boaz is *a man of worth* (2:1). The same two words open the poem of Proverbs 31:10, *A woman of worth, who can find?* *All the people at the gate* is **all the gate of my people**, the town's gathering place and law court, where chapter 4 is set.
- **v12 "a redeemer nearer than I"** — the story's one obstacle, named at the moment it seems solved. The written text has an extra *im* here that is not read.
- **v14 "before one could recognize another"** — *yakkir*, the verb of 2:10, *that you should take notice of me*.
- **v15 "six measures of barley"** — the measure is not named. Six of anything would be a heavy load for one woman, and the Hebrew gives no unit.
- **v17 "Do not go back empty"** — *reqam*, the word of Naomi's lament: *the LORD has brought me back empty* (1:21). Boaz's words are not reported in vv14–15; Ruth reports them here, in words that answer Naomi.
