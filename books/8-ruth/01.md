# Ruth 1

## Moab

**1** And it came to pass in the days when the judges ruled that there was a famine in the land, and a man of Bethlehem in Judah went to live as a guest in the country of Moab, he and his wife and his two sons.

**2** The man's name was Elimelech, his wife's name was Naomi, and the names of his two sons were Mahlon and Chilion. They were Ephrathites from Bethlehem in Judah. They came into the country of Moab and stayed there.

**3** Elimelech, Naomi's husband, died, and she was left with her two sons.

**4** They took Moabite wives for themselves; the name of the one was Orpah, and the name of the other Ruth. They lived there about ten years.

**5** Then both Mahlon and Chilion also died, and the woman was left without her two children and without her husband.

## The Road Back

**6** She rose with her daughters-in-law to return from the country of Moab, for she had heard in the country of Moab that the LORD had visited His people and given them bread.

**7** She went out from the place where she had been, and her two daughters-in-law with her, and they set out on the way to return to the land of Judah.

**8** Naomi said to her two daughters-in-law, "Go, return each of you to her mother's house. May the LORD show you loyal love, as you have shown it to the dead and to me.

**9** May the LORD grant that you find rest, each of you in the house of her husband." Then she kissed them, and they lifted up their voices and wept.

**10** They said to her, "No, we will return with you to your people."

**11** But Naomi said, "Turn back, my daughters. Why will you go with me? Have I still sons in my womb who could become your husbands?

**12** Turn back, my daughters, go; for I am too old to have a husband. If I said, 'I have hope,' even if I had a husband tonight and should also bear sons,

**13** would you wait for them until they were grown? Would you hold yourselves back from having husbands for them? No, my daughters; it is far more bitter for me than for you, for the hand of the LORD has gone out against me."

**14** They lifted up their voices and wept again. Orpah kissed her mother-in-law, but Ruth clung to her.

**15** She said, "See, your sister-in-law has gone back to her people and to her gods. Return after your sister-in-law."

**16** But Ruth said, "Do not press me to leave you, to turn back from following you. For where you go, I will go, and where you lodge, I will lodge. Your people shall be my people, and your God my God.

**17** Where you die, I will die, and there I will be buried. May the LORD do so to me and more also, if anything but death parts me from you."

**18** When she saw that she was determined to go with her, she stopped speaking to her.

## Bethlehem

**19** So the two of them went on until they came to Bethlehem. And it came to pass, when they came to Bethlehem, that the whole town was stirred because of them, and the women said, "Is this Naomi?"

**20** She said to them, "Do not call me Naomi; call me Mara, for the Almighty has dealt very bitterly with me.

**21** I went out full, and the LORD has brought me back empty. Why call me Naomi, when the LORD has testified against me, and the Almighty has brought disaster on me?"

**22** So Naomi returned, and Ruth the Moabite, her daughter-in-law, with her, who returned from the country of Moab. They came to Bethlehem at the beginning of the barley harvest.

## Notes

- **v1 "in the days when the judges ruled"** — *bi-ymei shefot ha-shofetim*, **in the days of the judging of the judges**. The book is set in the time of Judges and follows it in the canon; it ends with the name of David (4:17, 22). *Bethlehem* is **house of bread**, and the story begins with a famine there.
- **v1 "to live as a guest"** — *lagur*, the verb of the *ger*. An Israelite family becomes the guests in Moab; the Moabite woman will become the guest in Israel.
- **v2 "Elimelech… Naomi… Mahlon and Chilion"** — *Elimelekh* is **my God is king**, a name in a book that follows *there was no king in Israel* (Judges 21:25). *Naomi* is **pleasant**, the name she refuses in v20. *Ephrathites*: Ephrath is an old name of Bethlehem (Genesis 35:19).
- **v4 "Moabite wives"** — Deuteronomy 23:3 says *No Ammonite or Moabite shall enter the assembly of the LORD*. The book tells, without arguing, how a Moabite woman came into the line of David.
- **v6 "the LORD had visited His people"** — *paqad*, the verb of Joseph's promise, *God will surely visit you* (Genesis 50:24). The LORD acts in the book twice by the narrator's word: here, and when He gives Ruth conception (4:13).
- **v8 "loyal love"** — *chesed*, the book's key word. Naomi asks the LORD to show it to the Moabite women who have shown it to her family; it returns at 2:20 and 3:10.
- **v14 "Ruth clung to her"** — *davqah*, the verb of Genesis 2:24, *a man leaves his father and his mother and clings to his woman*. Ruth leaves her mother's house (v8) and clings to her mother-in-law.
- **vv16–17 "Your people shall be my people, and your God my God"** — Ruth swears by *the LORD*, the God of Israel, not the gods of Moab to which Orpah returned (v15). *May the LORD do so to me and more also* is the form of a solemn oath.
- **v20 "call me Mara"** — *Mara*, **bitter**, against *Naomi*, **pleasant**. *The Almighty*, *Shaddai*, as in Numbers 24:4. She uses the old name for God twice, with *the LORD* twice between.
- **v21 "the LORD has testified against me"** — *anah vi*, **answered against me**, the phrase for a witness in court. The Greek has *the Lord has humbled me*, reading the same letters from another root.
