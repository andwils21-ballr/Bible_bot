# 4 Baruch 5

**1** Abimelech carried the figs in the heat of the day, and coming upon a tree, he sat down in its shade to rest a little.

**2** He leaned his head on the basket of figs and fell asleep, and he slept sixty-six years; he did not wake from his sleep.

**3** Afterward, when he got up from his sleep, he said, "I slept pleasantly for a little while, but my head is heavy, because I did not get enough sleep."

**4** Then he uncovered the basket of figs and found them dripping milk.

**5** He said, "I would like to sleep a little longer, because my head is heavy. But I am afraid that if I sleep I will be late waking, and Jeremiah my father will think poorly of me; for if he were not in a hurry, he would not have sent me out at dawn today.

**6** So I will get up and go on in the heat. Is there not heat and toil every day?"

**7** So he got up, lifted the basket of figs onto his shoulders and went into Jerusalem. He did not recognize it, not the house and not his own place, and he found none of his family and none of the people he knew.

**8** He said, "Blessed be the Lord, for a great trance has fallen on me today.

**9** This is not the city of Jerusalem. I have lost my way, because I came by the mountain road when I got up from my sleep; and because my head was heavy from not getting enough sleep, I have lost my way.

**10** It will be a wonder to tell Jeremiah that I lost my way."

**11** He went out of the city, and looking carefully he saw the landmarks of the city, and he said, "This is the city, yet I have lost my way."

**12** He went back into the city and searched, and found none of his own people, and he said, "Blessed be the Lord, for a great trance has fallen on me."

**13** Again he went outside the city, and stayed there grieving, not knowing where to go.

**14** He put down the basket, saying, "I will sit here until the Lord takes this trance away from me."

**15** As he sat, he saw an old man coming in from the field, and Abimelech said to him, "I say to you, old man: what city is this?"

**16** He said to him, "It is Jerusalem."

**17** Abimelech said to him, "Where is Jeremiah the priest, and Baruch the reader, and all the people of this city? I could not find them."

**18** The old man said to him, "Are you not from this city, that you remember Jeremiah today and ask about him after so long a time?

**19** Jeremiah is in Babylon with the people. They were taken captive by King Nebuchadnezzar, and Jeremiah is with them to bring them good news and to teach them the word."

**20** As soon as Abimelech heard this from the old man, he said, "If you were not an old man, and if a man were allowed to insult one older than himself, I would laugh at you and say you are mad, for saying, 'The people have been taken captive to Babylon.'

**21** Even if the lattices of the heavens had come down on them, there has not been time yet for them to go to Babylon.

**22** How long is it since my father Jeremiah sent me to the estate of Agrippa to bring a few figs to give to the sick among the people?

**23** I went and got them, and coming to a tree in the heat, I sat down to rest a little, leaned my head on the basket and fell asleep.

**24** When I woke, I uncovered the basket of figs, thinking I was late, and I found the figs dripping milk, just as they were when I gathered them.

**25** Yet you say the people have been taken captive to Babylon.

**26** So that you may know, take, look at the figs."

**27** He uncovered the basket of figs for the old man, and the old man saw them dripping milk.

**28** When he saw them, the old man said, "My son, you are a righteous man, and God did not want you to see the desolation of the city, so He brought this trance on you.

**29** Today it is sixty-six years since the people were taken captive to Babylon.

**30** So that you may learn, child, that what I tell you is true, look up at the field and see that the crops have not yet begun to grow.

**31** Look at the figs too: it is not their season. Then you will know."

**32** Then Abimelech cried out with a loud voice, "I will bless You, God of heaven and earth, the rest of the souls of the righteous in every place!"

**33** Then he said to the old man, "What month is this?"

**34** He said, "Nisan, that is, Aviv."

**35** Abimelech took some of the figs and gave them to the old man, and said to him, "God will light your way to the city above, Jerusalem."

## Notes

- **vv2, 29 "he slept sixty-six years"** — the number is the same in the Greek and the Ge'ez, here, at v29, and when Baruch and Jeremiah count the years (6:8; 7:28). God had promised to shelter him *until I bring the people back to the city* (3:14); he sleeps through the whole exile and wakes on the eve of the return.
- **vv8, 12, 14, 28 "a great trance has fallen on me… He brought this trance on you"** — *ekstasis*, a state of being taken out of oneself. Abimelech uses it of his confusion; the old man sees what it was. The Greek of Genesis 2:21 has the same word for Adam's sleep: *The LORD God made a deep sleep fall upon the human*.
- **vv4, 24, 30–31 "dripping milk, just as they were when I gathered them… it is not their season"** — the figs are as fresh as the hour they were gathered. The old man reads them as a sign: in Nisan no fig is in season and no crop is up, yet these were picked sixty-six years before and have not spoiled.
- **v17 "Baruch the reader"** — *anagnōstēs*, the one who reads aloud, as Baruch did: *Then Baruch read from the book the words of Jeremiah in the house of the LORD* (Jeremiah 36:10).
- **v21 "Even if the lattices of the heavens had come down on them"** — *hoi katarraktai tou ouranou*, the words of the Greek of Genesis for the flood: *the lattices of the heavens were opened* (Genesis 7:11). Abimelech means that not even the flood could have moved a whole people to Babylon in one morning.
- **v34 "Nisan, that is, Aviv"** — the month of the exodus, *Today you are going out, in the month of Aviv* (Exodus 13:4). The Ge'ez also names the day, the twelfth of the month.
- **v35 "the city above, Jerusalem"** — the old man is blessed with the city that is not destroyed, the Jerusalem above, as Paul names it (Galatians 4:26).
- **Source text** — Greek: Online Critical Pseudepigrapha, CC BY 4.0. Ge'ez: A. Dillmann, *Chrestomathia Aethiopica* (1866), public domain. English translation by this project.
