# 4 Baruch 6

**1** After this Abimelech went outside the city and prayed to the Lord.

**2** An angel of the Lord came, took him by the right hand and brought him back to the place where Baruch was sitting, and he found him in a tomb.

**3** When they saw each other, they both wept and kissed each other.

**4** Baruch looked up and saw the figs covered over in Abimelech's basket.

**5** He lifted his eyes to heaven and prayed, saying:

**6** "You are the God who gives a reward to those who love You. Prepare yourself, my heart; rejoice and be glad in your tent, and say to your house of flesh, 'Your mourning has been turned into joy; for the Almighty is coming, and He will lift you up in your tent, for no sin has been found in you.'

**7** Take refreshment in your tent, in your virgin faith, and believe that you will live.

**8** Look at this basket of figs: they are sixty-six years old, and they have not withered or gone bad, but are dripping with milk.

**9** So my flesh will become for you, if you do what you were commanded by the angel of righteousness.

**10** He who kept the basket of figs will keep you again by His power."

**11** When Baruch had said this, he said to Abimelech, "Get up, and let us pray that the Lord will make known to us how we can send word to Jeremiah in Babylon about the shelter you were given on the way."

**12** Baruch prayed, saying, "Our strength, Lord God, the chosen light that came out of Your mouth,

**13** we beg and entreat Your goodness, great name that no one can know: hear the voice of Your servants, and let knowledge come into our hearts.

**14** What shall we do, and how shall we send this word to Jeremiah in Babylon?"

**15** While Baruch was still praying, an angel of the Lord came and said all these words to Baruch: "Counselor of the light, do not worry about how to send word to Jeremiah; for tomorrow at the hour of light an eagle will come to you, and you will reach Jeremiah.

**16** So write in the letter: Say to the sons of Israel: Let the stranger who has come among you be set apart, and let them keep fifteen days; after that I will bring you into your city, says the Lord.

**17** Whoever is not set apart from Babylon will not enter the city; and I will rebuke them, so that the Babylonians will not take them back again, says the Lord."

**18** When the angel had said this, he left Baruch.

**19** Baruch sent to the market of the Gentiles and got papyrus and ink, and wrote a letter that read: "Baruch the servant of God writes to Jeremiah in the captivity of Babylon:

**20** Rejoice and be glad, for God has not let us leave this body grieving for the city that was laid waste and outraged.

**21** For this reason the Lord has had compassion on our tears, and has remembered the covenant He made with our fathers Abraham, Isaac and Jacob.

**22** He sent His angel to me, and he told me these words, which I have sent to you.

**23** These, then, are the words the Lord said, the God of Israel, who brought us out of the land of Egypt, out of the great furnace: Because you did not keep My ordinances, but your heart was lifted up and you stiffened your necks before Me, in anger and wrath I handed you over to the furnace in Babylon.

**24** So if you listen to My voice, says the Lord, from the mouth of Jeremiah My servant, whoever listens, I will bring him up out of Babylon; but whoever does not listen will become a stranger to Jerusalem and to Babylon.

**25** You will test them by the water of the Jordan; whoever does not listen will be made known. This is the sign of the great seal."

## Notes

- **v6 "for the Almighty is coming"** — *ho hikanos*, "the Sufficient One," the word the Greek of Ruth uses for *Shaddai*: *the Almighty has dealt very bitterly with me* (Ruth 1:20).
- **v6 "Your mourning has been turned into joy"** — Jeremiah's promise for the return, *I will turn their mourning into joy* (Jeremiah 31:13), here said by Baruch to his own heart.
- **vv6–10 "your tent… your house of flesh… So my flesh will become for you"** — *skēnōma*, a tent, is the body, the dwelling of the heart. Baruch reads the figs as a sign: what kept them sixty-six years without decay will keep his flesh in the same way, and *you will live* (v7).
- **v16 "let them keep fifteen days"** — the Ge'ez has fifteen too. In 9:1–2 the feast in Jerusalem runs nine days, and Jeremiah offers alone on the tenth.
- **vv16–17, 24 "Let the stranger who has come among you be set apart… whoever does not listen will become a stranger to Jerusalem and to Babylon"** — the test of the return is separation from Babylon. Those who keep Babylon with them belong to neither city, and chapter 8 tells what became of them.
- **vv19–25 the letter** — in the Ge'ez the main text stops where the letter begins; its editor printed the letter in a note, as a text with corrupt and missing words. The Greek gives it whole.
- **v23 "out of the land of Egypt, out of the great furnace… I handed you over to the furnace in Babylon"** — Jeremiah's word for Egypt: *out of the land of Egypt, out of the iron furnace* (Jeremiah 11:4). Babylon is a second furnace, and the God who brought them out of the first will bring them out of this one.
- **v25 "You will test them by the water of the Jordan"** — as Gideon's men were sorted at the water: *Take them down to the water, and I will refine them for you there* (Judges 7:4).
- **Source text** — Greek: Online Critical Pseudepigrapha, CC BY 4.0. Ge'ez: A. Dillmann, *Chrestomathia Aethiopica* (1866), public domain. English translation by this project.
