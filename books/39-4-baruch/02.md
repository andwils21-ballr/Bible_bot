# 4 Baruch 2

**1** Jeremiah ran and told these things to Baruch. When they went into the temple of God, Jeremiah tore his clothes, put dust on his head and went into the sanctuary of God.

**2** When Baruch saw him with dust sprinkled on his head and his clothes torn, he cried out with a loud voice, "Father Jeremiah, what is the matter with you? What sin has the people committed?"

**3** For whenever the people sinned, Jeremiah would sprinkle dust on his head and pray for the people until the sin was forgiven.

**4** Baruch asked him, "Father, what is this?"

**5** Jeremiah said to him, "Keep from tearing your clothes; rather, let us tear our hearts. Let us not draw water for the troughs, but let us weep and fill them with tears, for the Lord will not have mercy on this people."

**6** Baruch said, "Father Jeremiah, what has happened?"

**7** Jeremiah said, "God is handing the city over into the hands of the king of the Chaldeans, to take the people captive to Babylon."

**8** When Baruch heard this, he too tore his clothes, and said, "Father Jeremiah, who made this known to you?"

**9** Jeremiah said to him, "Wait with me a little while, until the sixth hour of the night, so that you may know that this word is true."

**10** So they both stayed at the altar weeping, with their clothes torn.

## Notes

- **vv1–2 "tore his clothes, put dust on his head"** — the signs of mourning, and in this book a signal Baruch knows: v3 explains that Jeremiah did it whenever the people sinned, and prayed until the sin was forgiven. Baruch reads the dust and asks only what the sin is. The Ge'ez begins the chapter in the temple, without the run to Baruch.
- **v2 "Father Jeremiah"** — Baruch's name for him in every speech of the chapter. In the book of Jeremiah, Baruch is the scribe who writes at his dictation (Jeremiah 36:18); here he speaks to him as a son.
- **v5 "let us tear our hearts"** — the call of Joel 2:13, to tear the heart and not the garment. Jeremiah turns it the other way: Joel called for it so that the LORD would relent, and Jeremiah says it in the same breath as *the Lord will not have mercy on this people*.
- **v5 "fill them with tears"** — Jeremiah's own wish, *Oh, that my head were waters, and my eyes a fountain of tears* (Jeremiah 9:1), made into a picture: the troughs that hold water for the animals are to be filled by weeping.
- **v10 "they both stayed at the altar weeping"** — *thysiastērion*, the altar. The two wait in the temple for the hour God named, and the next chapter opens at it.
