# 4 Baruch 4

**1** When morning came, the army of the Chaldeans surrounded the city.

**2** The great angel blew the trumpet, saying, "Enter the city, army of the Chaldeans, for the gate has been opened for you.

**3** Let the king enter with his multitude, and let him take all the people captive."

**4** Jeremiah took the keys of the temple, went outside the city and threw them down before the sun, saying, "I say to you, sun: take the keys of the temple of God, and keep them until the day when the Lord asks you about them.

**5** For we have not been found worthy to keep them; we have become stewards of falsehood."

**6** While Jeremiah was still weeping for the people, they dragged him off with the people to Babylon.

**7** Baruch put dust on his head, sat down and wept this lament, saying, "Why has Jerusalem been laid waste? Because of the sins of the beloved people she was handed over into the hands of enemies, because of our sins and the people's.

**8** But do not let the lawless boast and say, 'We were strong enough to take the city of God by our own might.' It was because of our sins that she was handed over to you.

**9** Our God will have compassion on us and bring us back to our city; but you will have no life.

**10** Blessed are our fathers, Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, for they went out of this world and did not see the destruction of this city."

**11** When he had said this, Baruch went outside the city weeping and saying, "Grieving for you, Jerusalem, I have gone out from you."

**12** He stayed in a tomb, sitting there, and angels came to him and told him everything the Lord revealed to him through them.

## Notes

- **vv1–3 "the gate has been opened for you… let the king enter"** — God's word of 1:9, *unless I first open its gates*, kept. The Chaldeans are invited in by an angel; they do not break in.
- **vv4–5 "threw them down before the sun… we have become stewards of falsehood"** — the keys are given to the sun to keep until the Lord asks for them, and Jeremiah says why: *we have not been found worthy to keep them*. He was *of the priests who were in Anathoth* (Jeremiah 1:1), and he speaks for all who held the temple. *Epitropoi*, "stewards," are those put in charge of what belongs to another.
- **v7 "Baruch put dust on his head, sat down and wept this lament"** — the words of the line that opens the Greek of Lamentations: *Jeremiah sat weeping, and lamented this lament over Jerusalem*. *Thrēnos*, "lament," is that book's Greek name. Here it is Baruch who sits and laments, since Jeremiah has been taken away.
- **v7 "the beloved people"** — the phrase that names *the beloved* of 3:11.
- **v8 "We were strong enough to take the city of God by our own might"** — the boast Jeremiah feared in 1:6, *I have prevailed over the holy city of God*. Baruch answers it with the book's own account: the city fell to the sins of its people, not to the strength of Babylon.
- **v10 "Blessed are our fathers… they did not see the destruction of this city"** — the dead are counted happy, as the Preacher counted them: *So I praised the dead who are already dead more than the living who are still alive* (Ecclesiastes 4:2). The fathers were spared the sight, as Abimelech is to be (3:13).
- **v12 "He stayed in a tomb"** — *mnēmeion*, a burial place; the Ge'ez has a tomb too. Baruch, left behind in the ruined city, lives among the dead, and there the angels bring him word.
