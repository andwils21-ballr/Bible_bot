# 4 Baruch 7

**1** Baruch got up and went out of the tomb, and found the eagle sitting outside the tomb.

**2** The eagle spoke to him in a human voice and said, "Greetings, Baruch, steward of the faith."

**3** Baruch said to him, "You who speak are chosen from all the birds of the sky; that is plain from the brightness of your eyes. So show me: what are you doing here?"

**4** The eagle said to him, "I was sent here so that you may send through me any word you want."

**5** Baruch said to him, "Can you carry this word to Jeremiah in Babylon?"

**6** The eagle said to him, "That is what I was sent for."

**7** Baruch took the letter and fifteen figs from Abimelech's basket and tied them to the eagle's neck, and said to him, "I say to you, king of the birds: go in peace and in health, and bring me back word.

**8** Do not be like the raven that Noah sent out, which did not come back to him in the ark; be like the dove, which the third time brought word to the righteous man.

**9** So you too, carry this good word to Jeremiah and to those in chains with him, so that it may go well with you. Carry this papyrus to the people and to the chosen one of God.

**10** If all the birds of the sky surround you and want to fight you, fight back. The Lord give you strength.

**11** Do not turn aside to the right or to the left, but go straight like an arrow. Go in the power of God, and the glory of the Lord will be with you all the way you go."

**12** Then the eagle flew off with the letter on its neck and went to Babylon. When it arrived, it rested on a tree outside the city in a deserted place.

**13** It kept silent until Jeremiah came by, for he and some others of the people were going out to bury a dead man outside the city.

**14** Jeremiah had asked King Nebuchadnezzar, "Give me a place where I may bury the dead of my people," and the king had given it to him.

**15** As they went along weeping with the dead man, they came opposite the eagle.

**16** The eagle cried out with a loud voice, "I say to you, Jeremiah, chosen one of God: go and gather the people and come here, so that they may hear the letter I have brought you from Baruch and Abimelech."

**17** When Jeremiah heard this, he glorified God; he went and gathered the people, with the women and children, and came to where the eagle was.

**18** The eagle came down on the dead man, and he came back to life.

**19** This happened so that they would believe.

**20** All the people were amazed at what had happened, and said, "Is this not the God who appeared to our fathers in the wilderness through Moses, and now has appeared to us through this eagle?"

**21** The eagle said, "I say to you, Jeremiah: come, untie this letter and read it to the people." So he untied the letter and read it to the people.

**22** When the people heard it, they wept and put dust on their heads, and said to Jeremiah, "Save us, and tell us what to do so that we may enter our city again."

**23** Jeremiah answered them, "Keep everything you heard from the letter, and the Lord will bring us into our city."

**24** Jeremiah also wrote a letter to Baruch, saying: "My beloved son, do not be careless in your prayers, asking God for us, that He may prosper our way until we come out from under the decrees of this lawless king.

**25** For you were found righteous before God, and He did not let you come here, so that you would not see the affliction the Babylonians have brought on the people.

**26** It is like a father with an only son, when the son is handed over for punishment: those who see the father and comfort him cover his face, so that he will not see how his son is punished and be broken still more by grief.

**27** That is how God had mercy on you and did not let you come to Babylon, so that you would not see the affliction of the people.

**28** From the time we came here, grief has not left us: sixty-six years today.

**29** Often when I went out I found some of the people hanged by King Nebuchadnezzar, weeping and saying, 'Have mercy on us, god Zar!'

**30** When I heard this I grieved and wept a double weeping, not only because they were hanged, but because they were calling on a foreign god, saying, 'Have mercy on us.'

**31** I remembered the feast days we kept in Jerusalem before we were taken captive, and as I remembered I groaned, and went back to my house in pain and weeping.

**32** So now pray, in the place where you are, you and Abimelech, for this people, that they will listen to my voice and to the judgments of my mouth, and that we may go out from here.

**33** For I tell you, all the time we have spent here they have kept at us, saying, 'Sing us a song from the songs of Zion, the song of your God.'

**34** We say to them, 'How could we sing for you in a foreign land?'"

**35** After this Jeremiah tied the letter to the eagle's neck, saying, "Go in peace, and may the Lord watch over us both."

**36** The eagle flew off, came to Jerusalem and gave the letter to Baruch. He untied it and read it, kissed it, and wept when he heard of the griefs and afflictions of the people.

**37** Jeremiah took the figs and gave them out to the sick among the people, and he stayed on, teaching them to keep away from the pollutions of the nations of Babylon.

## Notes

- **v8 "the raven that Noah sent out, which did not come back to him in the ark"** — so the Greek of Genesis has it: the raven went out and *did not return*. The Hebrew says it went *going out and returning, until the waters dried up* (Genesis 8:7). The dove's third flight counts the raven as the first: the dove was sent twice after it, and came back the second time with *an olive leaf, freshly plucked* (8:11).
- **v11 "Do not turn aside to the right or to the left"** — the words of the law, *Do not turn aside to the right or to the left* (Deuteronomy 5:32), given to a bird.
- **vv13–14 "going out to bury a dead man outside the city… Give me a place where I may bury the dead of my people"** — the work Tobit did in Nineveh: *if I saw any of my people dead and thrown out behind the wall of Nineveh, I buried him* (Tobit 1:17).
- **vv18–20 "The eagle came down on the dead man, and he came back to life… Is this not the God who appeared to our fathers in the wilderness through Moses"** — at Sinai God spoke of the exodus with the same bird: *how I carried you on eagles' wings and brought you to Myself* (Exodus 19:4).
- **vv29–30 "Have mercy on us, god Zar!… they were calling on a foreign god"** — no god of this name is known from Scripture. *Zar* is the Hebrew word for *strange, foreign*, as in the psalm, *There shall be no strange god among you* (Psalm 81:9), where the Hebrew is *el zar*; and v30 calls him *a foreign god*.
- **vv33–34 "Sing us a song from the songs of Zion… How could we sing for you in a foreign land?"** — the psalm of the exile: *"Sing us one of the songs of Zion!" How could we sing the LORD's song in a foreign land?* (Psalm 137:3–4). The Greek has the psalm's words, *pōs aisōmen… epi gēs allotrias*.
- **v37 "Jeremiah took the figs and gave them out to the sick among the people"** — the errand of 3:21, *give them to the sick among the people*, done at last, sixty-six years later and in Babylon.
- **Source text** — Greek: Online Critical Pseudepigrapha, CC BY 4.0. Ge'ez: A. Dillmann, *Chrestomathia Aethiopica* (1866), public domain. English translation by this project.
