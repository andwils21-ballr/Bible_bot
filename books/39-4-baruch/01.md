# 4 Baruch 1

**1** And it came to pass, when the sons of Israel were taken captive by the king of the Chaldeans, that God spoke to Jeremiah, saying, "Jeremiah, My chosen one, get up and go out of this city, you and Baruch, for I am going to destroy it because of the multitude of the sins of those who live in it.

**2** For your prayers are like a firm pillar in the middle of it, and like an unbreakable wall around it.

**3** So now get up and go out, before the army of the Chaldeans surrounds it."

**4** Jeremiah answered, "I beg You, Lord, permit me, Your servant, to speak before You."

**5** The Lord said to him, "Speak, My chosen one Jeremiah."

**6** Jeremiah spoke, saying, "Lord Almighty, are You handing the chosen city over into the hands of the Chaldeans, so that the king may boast with the multitude of his people and say, 'I have prevailed over the holy city of God'?

**7** No, my Lord! But if it is Your will, let it be destroyed by Your own hands."

**8** The Lord said to Jeremiah, "Since you are My chosen one, get up and go out of this city, you and Baruch, for I am going to destroy it because of the multitude of the sins of those who live in it.

**9** For neither the king nor his army will be able to enter it unless I first open its gates.

**10** So get up and go to Baruch, and tell him these words.

**11** Get up at the sixth hour of the night, both of you, and come to the walls of the city, and I will show you that unless I first destroy the city, they cannot enter it."

**12** When the Lord had said this, He left Jeremiah.

## Notes

- **v1 "when the sons of Israel were taken captive by the king of the Chaldeans"** — the book is rendered from the Greek, and its verse numbers follow the Greek. The other witness is the Ge'ez, headed *The Rest of the Words of Baruch*; it has been read against the Greek for names, numbers and the shape of each passage, and the notes say where the two differ. The story is set on the night before Jerusalem falls.
- **vv1, 5, 8 "Jeremiah, My chosen one"** — God's name for the prophet throughout the book. Twice in this chapter He gives the command to leave the city in the same words (vv1, 8), before and after Jeremiah's plea.
- **v2 "like a firm pillar in the middle of it, and like an unbreakable wall around it"** — at Jeremiah's call God said, *I Myself have made you today a fortified city, an iron pillar and bronze walls against the whole land* (Jeremiah 1:18). There the prophet stood against the city; here his prayers are its pillar and wall, and the city cannot fall while he is in it. *Adamantinos*, "unbreakable," is literally "of adamant."
- **vv6–7 "so that the king may boast… let it be destroyed by Your own hands"** — Jeremiah does not ask that the city be spared. He asks that the Chaldeans not have the glory of taking it. Baruch's lament answers the same boast (4:8).
- **vv9, 11 "unless I first open its gates… unless I first destroy the city"** — what Lamentations said no one believed, *that foe or enemy could enter the gates of Jerusalem* (Lamentations 4:12). In this book they do not: the city is opened from heaven before Babylon walks in (4:1–2).
- **v11 "the sixth hour of the night"** — midnight, with the hours of the night counted from dusk. The Ge'ez has the same number here, at 2:9, and at 3:1, where the Greek says only *the hour*.
