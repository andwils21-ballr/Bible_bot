# Joshua 10

## Five Kings Against Gibeon

**1** And it came to pass, when Adoni-zedek king of Jerusalem heard that Joshua had taken Ai and devoted it to destruction, doing to Ai and its king as he had done to Jericho and its king, and that the inhabitants of Gibeon had made peace with Israel and were among them,

**2** that he was very afraid, because Gibeon was a great city, like one of the royal cities, and because it was greater than Ai, and all its men were mighty.

**3** So Adoni-zedek king of Jerusalem sent to Hoham king of Hebron, to Piram king of Jarmuth, to Japhia king of Lachish and to Debir king of Eglon, saying,

**4** "Come up to me and help me, and let us strike Gibeon, for it has made peace with Joshua and with the sons of Israel."

**5** The five kings of the Amorites — the king of Jerusalem, the king of Hebron, the king of Jarmuth, the king of Lachish and the king of Eglon — gathered and went up, they and all their armies, and camped against Gibeon and fought against it.

**6** The men of Gibeon sent to Joshua at the camp at Gilgal, saying, "Do not let your hands drop from your servants. Come up to us quickly and save us and help us, for all the kings of the Amorites who live in the hill country have gathered against us."

**7** Joshua went up from Gilgal, he and all the fighting men with him, and all the mighty men of valor.

**8** The LORD said to Joshua, "Do not be afraid of them, for I have given them into your hand. Not one of them will stand before you."

**9** Joshua came upon them suddenly, having marched up all night from Gilgal.

**10** The LORD threw them into panic before Israel, and He struck them with a great blow at Gibeon, and chased them by the way of the ascent of Beth-horon, and struck them as far as Azekah and Makkedah.

**11** And it came to pass, as they fled before Israel, while they were on the descent of Beth-horon, that the LORD threw great stones on them from the heavens as far as Azekah, and they died. More died from the hailstones than the sons of Israel killed with the sword.

## Sun, Stand Still

**12** Then Joshua spoke to the LORD on the day the LORD gave the Amorites over to the sons of Israel, and he said in the sight of Israel:

"Sun, stand still at Gibeon,  
and moon, in the Valley of Aijalon."

**13** So the sun stood still, and the moon stopped,  
until the nation took vengeance on its enemies.

Is this not written in the Book of Jashar? The sun stopped in the middle of the heavens and did not hurry to go down for about a whole day.

**14** There has been no day like it before or since, when the LORD listened to the voice of a man, for the LORD fought for Israel.

**15** Then Joshua returned, and all Israel with him, to the camp at Gilgal.

## The Cave at Makkedah

**16** These five kings fled and hid themselves in the cave at Makkedah.

**17** It was told to Joshua, "The five kings have been found hiding in the cave at Makkedah."

**18** Joshua said, "Roll great stones against the mouth of the cave, and set men by it to guard them.

**19** But you, do not stay there. Pursue your enemies and cut off their rear guard. Do not let them enter their cities, for the LORD your God has given them into your hand."

**20** And it came to pass, when Joshua and the sons of Israel had finished striking them with a very great blow until they were finished, and the survivors among them had escaped into the fortified cities,

**21** that all the people returned safely to the camp, to Joshua at Makkedah. No one sharpened his tongue against any of the sons of Israel.

**22** Then Joshua said, "Open the mouth of the cave and bring these five kings out to me from the cave."

**23** They did so, and brought these five kings out to him from the cave: the king of Jerusalem, the king of Hebron, the king of Jarmuth, the king of Lachish and the king of Eglon.

**24** And it came to pass, when they brought these kings out to Joshua, that Joshua called all the men of Israel and said to the commanders of the fighting men who had gone with him, "Come near and put your feet on the necks of these kings." They came near and put their feet on their necks.

**25** Joshua said to them, "Do not be afraid and do not lose heart. Be strong and courageous, for so the LORD will do to all your enemies against whom you fight."

**26** Afterward Joshua struck them and put them to death and hanged them on five trees, and they hung on the trees until evening.

**27** And it came to pass at the time of sunset that Joshua commanded, and they took them down from the trees and threw them into the cave where they had hidden, and put great stones at the mouth of the cave, which are there to this very day.

## The Southern Cities

**28** Joshua took Makkedah on that day and struck it and its king with the sword. He devoted them and every person in it to destruction; he left no survivor. He did to the king of Makkedah as he had done to the king of Jericho.

**29** Joshua and all Israel with him passed on from Makkedah to Libnah and fought against Libnah.

**30** The LORD gave it also, and its king, into the hand of Israel, and he struck it and every person in it with the sword; he left no survivor in it. He did to its king as he had done to the king of Jericho.

**31** Joshua and all Israel with him passed on from Libnah to Lachish, and camped against it and fought against it.

**32** The LORD gave Lachish into the hand of Israel, and he took it on the second day and struck it and every person in it with the sword, according to all that he had done to Libnah.

**33** Then Horam king of Gezer came up to help Lachish, and Joshua struck him and his people until he had left him no survivor.

**34** Joshua and all Israel with him passed on from Lachish to Eglon, and they camped against it and fought against it.

**35** They took it on that day and struck it with the sword, and every person in it he devoted to destruction on that day, according to all that he had done to Lachish.

**36** Joshua and all Israel with him went up from Eglon to Hebron and fought against it.

**37** They took it and struck it with the sword, and its king and all its towns and every person in it; he left no survivor, according to all that he had done to Eglon. He devoted it and every person in it to destruction.

**38** Then Joshua and all Israel with him turned back to Debir and fought against it.

**39** He took it, and its king and all its towns, and they struck them with the sword and devoted every person in it to destruction; he left no survivor. As he had done to Hebron, so he did to Debir and its king, and as he had done to Libnah and its king.

**40** So Joshua struck all the land — the hill country and the Negev and the lowland and the slopes — and all their kings. He left no survivor, but devoted everything that breathed to destruction, as the LORD, the God of Israel, had commanded.

**41** Joshua struck them from Kadesh-barnea to Gaza, and all the land of Goshen as far as Gibeon.

**42** All these kings and their land Joshua took at one time, because the LORD, the God of Israel, fought for Israel.

**43** Then Joshua returned, and all Israel with him, to the camp at Gilgal.

## Notes

- **v1 "Adoni-zedek king of Jerusalem"** — **my lord is righteous**, a name built like *Melchizedek*, **my king is righteous**, the king of Salem who blessed Abram in Genesis 14:18. This is the first time the Bible names the city Jerusalem.
- **v6 "Do not let your hands drop from your servants"** — the Gibeonites now call on the covenant that they obtained by deceit, and Joshua honors it.
- **v10 "threw them into panic"** — *va-yehummem*, the verb of the Sea of Reeds, Exodus 14:24, when the LORD *threw the camp of Egypt into panic*.
- **v11 "great stones… hailstones"** — the hail that struck Egypt (Exodus 9) falls now on the Amorites. More die by God's hand than by Israel's.
- **vv12–13 "Sun, stand still"** — *dom*, **be silent, be still**. Two lines of verse quoted from *the Book of Jashar*, *sefer ha-yashar*, **the Book of the Upright**, a lost collection of songs; 2 Samuel 1:18 quotes it again for David's lament over Saul and Jonathan. The verse gives no account of how; it says what was asked and what happened.
- **v13 "a whole day"** — *ke-yom tamim*, the word *tamim*, **whole**.
- **v14 "when the LORD listened to the voice of a man"** — the wonder the text stresses is not the sun but the hearing: God did what a man asked. *The LORD fought for Israel*, as Moses promised at the sea: *The LORD will fight for you* (Exodus 14:14).
- **v15 and v43** — the return to Gilgal is told twice, once in v15 after the poem and again in v43 after the southern campaign. The Greek does not have v15 or v43.
- **v21 "No one sharpened his tongue"** — the idiom of Exodus 11:7, *not a dog will sharpen its tongue*: no one dared say a word.
- **v24 "put your feet on the necks of these kings"** — a sign of complete victory, done by the commanders, not by Joshua, so that they would see the promise with their own eyes (v25).
- **vv26–27 hanged until evening** — and taken down at sunset, as Deuteronomy 21:23 commands and as with the king of Ai (8:29).
- **vv28–39 six cities** — Makkedah, Libnah, Lachish, Eglon, Hebron, Debir, each told with the same words. The refrain *he left no survivor* runs through the list.
