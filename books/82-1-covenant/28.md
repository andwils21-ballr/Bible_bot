# 1st Book of the Covenant 28

## Him We Confess

**1** "After that let the bishop teach the mysteries to the people. If he is not present, let a presbyter speak, so that the faithful may know whom they are drawing near to, and who is their God and Father. Then let the teaching of the mysteries be said like this. *The Initiation into the Mysteries, which is said to the faithful before the Offering.* 'We confess Him who was before all, and was present, and is, and is coming; who suffered and was buried, and rose, and was glorified by the Father; who loosed our cords from death, who rose from the dead; who is not only Man but God as well; who by the Holy Spirit restored the flesh of Adam, with his soul, to immortality, because He kept Adam by the Spirit; who clothed Himself with dead Adam and made him live; who went up into heaven; under whom, after the cross, Death fell and was conquered, when its bonds were dissolved, by which the Devil once grew strong and prevailed against us; through whose passion Death was shown to be useless and weak, when Jesus cut its cords and its power, when its snares were cut and He struck it in the face, and Death, filled with darkness, was shaken and afraid as it saw the Only-begotten Son; who in His human soul went down, in the Godhead, into Sheol; who came down from the pure heights above the heavens. Him we confess, the undivided Thought who is from the Father and of one will with Him; Him, the Maker of heaven with His Father, who is the Crown of the angels, the Strength of the archangels, the Raiment of the hosts and the Spirit of the Dominions; Him, the Ruler of the everlasting kingdom and Prince of the saints, the unfathomable Understanding of the Father; Him who is the Wisdom, the Power, the Lord, the Thought, the Understanding, the Hand, the Arm of the Father.

**2** As we believe, we confess Him who is our Light, Salvation, Savior, Protector, Helper, Teacher, Deliverer, Rewarder, Supporter, Strength, Wall; our Shepherd, Entrance, Door, Way,

**3** Life, Medicine, Provision, Drink and Judge. We confess Him able to suffer yet not able to suffer, Son yet not created, dead yet alive, the Son of the Father, beyond grasp yet grasped; who, sinless Himself, bore our sins when He left the Father's heaven; whose body, broken, becomes our salvation, and His blood and Spirit our life and holiness, and the water our cleansing; who gives light to the hearts of those who fear Him, being with them in all things; who has made us strangers to the whole way of the Devil; the Renewer of souls, in whom we have all put our trust.

## The Word Made Flesh

**4** He, being God, eternal God before the ages with the Father, when He saw the world perishing in the bonds of sin, trodden down by the power of a cunning wild beast, and made subject to death through ignorance and error, resolved to heal the human race. He came to a virgin womb, hidden from all the camps of the heavenly ones, and cast the opposing hosts into ignorance. When He, the Incorruptible, clothed Himself with corruptible flesh, making flesh that was under death incorruptible, He showed in the flesh of dead Adam, with which He clothed Himself, an example of incorruption, and by that example the things of corruption were done away.

**5** He gave holy commandments through the Gospel, which proclaims the kingdom beforehand. By this Gospel, as a figure of the kingdom, we learned to live; through this Gospel the bonds of the Devil have been cut, so that we may reach immortality instead of death, and instead of ignorance may receive the grace of watchfulness.

**6** So He, the Son of God, the Lord, became Man. He took on the dead race of Adam in all its kinds by emptying Himself; He who is the First came to birth as Man, though He is God; He who was foreknown by the prophets, preached by the apostles, praised by angels and glorified by the Father of all was crucified for us. His cross is our life, our strength, our salvation, for it is the hidden mystery, the joy beyond words, and through it the whole nature of mankind, always bearing it, is made one with God and not to be parted from Him. For it is the kindly power, never parted from God, that these lips cannot speak as is fitting, that was hidden from the beginning; but now the mystery that is revealed, which is for the faithful, will be, not as it seems to be, but as it is.

**7** This is the cross in which we boast, so that we may be glorified; and those who carry it, the faithful and perfect, separate their souls from everything that can be felt and everything that is seen, as from something not true. By it ask for yourselves, you who act like men: make your visible ears deaf, make your bodily eyes blind, so that you may know the will of Christ and the whole mystery of your salvation. Holy men and women, whose part it is to boast in the Lord, listen to the inner man.

## Death Cries Out

**8** Our Lord, when He taught us and appointed a covenant for us and made us members of His household, came after His passion into Sheol and took all the earth captive. He made the nature of death captive to life. When Death saw Him going down in His soul into Sheol, it was deceived, and hoped He was food for it, as was its custom. But when it saw in Him the beauty of the Godhead, it cried out aloud, saying: "Who is this who has clothed Himself with Man, who was under me, and has conquered me? Who is this who snatches from destruction flesh that was bound by me? Who is this who has clothed Himself with earth, but is Himself heaven? Who is this who was born into corruption, but suffers no corruption? Who is this, a stranger to my laws? Who is this who takes captive those who are mine? Who is this who strives with the power of burning Death, and conquers darkness? What is this new glory in this sight, which keeps me from doing what I would? Who is this new dead One without sin? Who is this who by the abundance of light puts out darkness, and does not let me rule over those who are mine, but draws to heaven the souls that were given to me? What is this glory that keeps the body from decay? Who is this whom I cannot touch? What is this glory, past searching out by all around it? Woe is me! I am put to flight by Him and by those who are His, for I cannot harm them."

**9** He is the Christ who was crucified, by whom what was on the left was placed on the right, what was beneath became as what was above, and what was behind as what was before. When He rose from the dead and trod down Sheol and by death killed Death, after He rose on the third day, He gave thanks to the Father, saying: "I give thanks to You, O My Father, not with these lips that are fixed together, nor with a bodily tongue through which truth and lying go out, nor with this created and material word; but I give thanks to You, the King, with that Voice which through You understands all things, which does not come by a bodily organ, which does not fall on fleshly ears, which —

## When It Is Said

**10** After the people are taught the Initiation into the mysteries, let the Eucharist be offered. But let the Initiation into the mysteries not be said each time, only at Pascha, on the Sabbath, on the first day of the week, and on the days of Epiphany and of Pentecost.

## Notes

- **v1 "was before all, and was present, and is, and is coming"** — the title the Lord God gives Himself in Revelation, *who is and who was and who is to come* (Revelation 1:8), here confessed of the Son.
- **v8 "hoped He was food for it"** — Death takes Him for food, as it takes all; the prophet turns it the other way, *He will swallow up death forever* (Isaiah 25:8), and Paul quotes it, *Death has been swallowed up in victory* (1 Corinthians 15:54).
- **v9 "which —"** — the Son's prayer breaks off in the text held here, and v10 begins the rule for when the Initiation is said. The break is kept.
- **Source text** — J. Cooper and A. J. Maclean, *The Testament of Our Lord, translated into English from the Syriac* (1902), public domain. English translation by this project.
