# 1st Book of the Covenant 46

**1** *Of Male and Female Virgins*

**2** "A male or female virgin is not appointed or ordained by man, but is set apart of his own will and is called a virgin.

**4** A hand is not laid on him for virginity, for this setting apart is of their own free will. But it is right for virgins to be fixed and bound in the suffering of a sound body, to be constant in fasting and in prayer, in weeping and in mourning daily, always to expect their departure from the flesh, and to strive as people about to depart. Let them not serve rage or debauchery or drunkenness or empty talk, or be taken up with worldly work or distraction; they are as one who is on the cross. Let their hearts be lifted up, with all meekness and seemliness of thought, with meditation on the Holy Scriptures, with faithful thoughts and kind comforts, so that when they pray they may be answered in what they ask for the faithful who wish to provide for them. Let them not despise these things, so that through them also a share of life may be given to those who provide for them. Let them be strengthened in love and kindness and in true and perfect grace. Let them be constant in comforting, comforting their neighbors, instructing and teaching those who have lately become faithful, in understanding and in knowledge and in kindness, stirring up the very young, examples of holiness among them in all good things. Let the women do the same. Let them speak and work in order and in grace and in knowledge, so that they may truly be the salt of the earth, as it is said. Let the women who are virgins cover their heads in the church, hiding only their hair; and let them be counted worthy of honor by everyone, so that the rest of the women who wish to may follow their example.

**5** If anyone among the people appears to have a gift of healing or of knowledge or of tongues, a hand is not laid on him, for the work is plain to see. But let them have honor.

**6** The First Book of Clement is ended.

## Notes

- **v3** — the paragraph numbered 3 in the text held here is the edition's footnote to v2, not part of the text, so no verse stands there.
- **v6 "The First Book of Clement is ended"** — the close of Cooper and Maclean's first book, as the Syriac gives it; their second book follows as chapters 47–73.
- **Source text** — J. Cooper and A. J. Maclean, *The Testament of Our Lord, translated into English from the Syriac* (1902), public domain. English translation by this project.
