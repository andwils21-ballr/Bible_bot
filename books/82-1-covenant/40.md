# 1st Book of the Covenant 40

**1** "Let a widow be appointed when she is chosen, if she has long remained without a husband, and if, though often pressed by men to marry, she has not married, because of the faith. If not, it is not yet right that she be chosen; let her be tested for a time: whether she is devout; whether, if she has children, she has brought them up in holiness, has not taught them worldly wisdom, and has made them examples of the holy law and of the Church; whether she has loved and honored strangers, has been constant in prayer, has lived meekly, and has gladly helped the afflicted; whether it has been revealed to the saints about her; whether she has not neglected the saints, and has served with all her strength; whether she is fit to bear and endure the burden, praying without ceasing, complete in all things, fervent in spirit, with the eyes of her heart opened in everything, always kind, loving innocence, owning nothing in this world, but always taking up and carrying the cross, crucifying all evil, staying by the altar night and day, working gladly and in secret. If she has one or two or three of like mind in My name, I am among them. Let her be complete in the Lord, as one who is visited by the Spirit. Let her do what is made known to her with fear and earnestness. Let her instruct the women who do not obey, teach those who have not learned, turn back those who are foolish, and instruct them to be serious. Let her test the deaconesses; let her make those who come in know what sort of people they are and who they are, and instruct them to stay. To those who listen let her patiently give the counsel that is proper; to those who are disobedient after three instructions let her not speak. Let her love those who wish to live in virginity or in purity, and correct those who oppose themselves modestly and quietly. Let her be at peace with everyone. Let her privately shut the mouths of those who talk much and idly; if they do not listen, let her take an older woman with her, or bring it to the bishop's hearing. In the church let her be silent. In prayer let her be persistent. Let her visit the women who are sick, and each first day of the week take one or two deacons with her and help them. If she has any possessions, let her give them to the poor and the faithful; if she has nothing, let the Church help her. Let her do no worldly work, as though for a trial, but have these works of the Spirit: let her continue in prayer and fasting, ask for nothing great, receive what the Lord gives, and not be anxious for her children, but hand them over to the Church, so that living in the house of God they may be fit for the service of the priesthood. Her requests to God will be acceptable; they are the sacrifice and altar of God. For those who have served well will be praised by the archangels. But as for those who are dissolute and raging and drunken, babblers and busybodies and evil, those who love pleasures much, the figures of their souls, which stand before the Father of lights, perish and are carried away to live in darkness; or their visible deeds, going up before the Most High, drag them easily to the pit, so that, after this world is changed and passes away, the figures of their souls may stand against them as witnesses and not let them look up. For the figure and type of every soul stands before God from the foundation of the world. So let her be chosen who can go to meet the holy bowls. To them belong the twelve presbyters who praise My Father who is in heaven, who receive the prayers of every holy soul and offer them to the Most High as a soothing aroma.

## Notes

- **v1 "the holy bowls… who receive the prayers of every holy soul"** — John's vision of heaven: *the twenty-four elders fell down before the Lamb, each holding a harp and golden bowls full of incense, which are the prayers of the saints* (Revelation 5:8). Here the elders are twelve, and the widow chosen is one fit to go and meet them.
- **Source text** — J. Cooper and A. J. Maclean, *The Testament of Our Lord, translated into English from the Syriac* (1902), public domain. English translation by this project.
