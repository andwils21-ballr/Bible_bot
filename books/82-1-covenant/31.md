# 1st Book of the Covenant 31

**1** "After he is ordained, let him be constant at the altar, laboring in prayer without ceasing. Sometimes let him rest alone in some house from the things of the Lord's house, but without ceasing from prayer or cutting it short by one hour.

**2** Let him fast three days each week all the year, so that he may be perfected in understanding, and beyond that as his strength allows; not wandering about, going here and there with every spirit, but doing everything with energy.

**3** If it is revealed to a presbyter or bishop to speak, let him speak; if not, let him not neglect or despise his work.

**4** If it is revealed to a presbyter to visit his parishes and speak the word, let him go; if not, let him pray to God; and if it is revealed to him to speak to them, let him speak to them, always taking up the burden and load of Him who was crucified for him, and praying for all the people.

**5** Let a presbyter or bishop not be anxious about food or clothing. God takes thought and cares for His own in the things He knows. If, when he receives food or clothing from someone, he is told to receive from another as well, let it be enough for him to receive from the first alone, and only as far as is fitting and as he needs, not to excess.

**6** In firmness of faith let a presbyter always be unchanging, for it is men like these that God desires. Let him test the heart of each one, so that no evil, kept buried within, makes him a stranger to the grace of God.

**7** Let him not allow weeds to grow in the good wheat, but take them out of it, and cut off those who bring them in. Let darkness not cover his light. Let him teach all the faithful at all times to finish their course as if in the day, because the children of light do not walk in darkness. Let the presbyter's teaching be fitting, quiet and moderate, joined with fear and trembling, and the bishop's in the same way. In teaching, let them not speak empty things, but such things as the hearers can keep when they hear them. Let the presbyter remember all that he teaches. For in the day of the Lord the Word, he will be required to testify to the people what he spoke, so that those who did not listen may be reproved; for he must stand before the glory of God, speaking what he has taught. Let him teach like this, then, so that he does not perish. Let him pray for those who hear, that the Lord may give them understanding of the Spirit, of knowledge, of truth. Let him not throw pearls before pigs to no purpose, but search out those who are worthy, who have heard and have done it; otherwise, if the Word has not borne fruit in them but has perished, he himself may prove to be the cause of its perishing. Let him not give the holy things to the dogs. Let him discern the signs of those who hear the word and bear good fruit. In all things, without anxiety, let him keep the matter for the bishop.

**8** Let him not neglect or despise those who do good works through teaching, but watch for signs in them, and judge spiritually from the signs that appear in them: sighs, weeping, earnest conversations, silence, sadness, patience, the head humbly bowed. What trains best, and brings suffering, is weeping and groaning.

**9** The work these do is watching, self-control, fasting, quietness, unceasing prayer, meditation, faith, meekness, love for others, labor, weariness, love, submission, goodness, seriousness, and every work of light. But the signs of those who do not bear the fruits of life are these: sloth, love of pleasure, eyes wandering in all directions, disobedience, complaining, restlessness, a laziness that will not move, wandering about.

**10** The work these do is gluttony, debauchery, anger, unbelief, idle and untimely laughter, confusion, neglect, error, disturbance, wantonness, love of gain, love of money, envy, strife, drunkenness, high-mindedness, empty talk, love of praise, and every work of darkness.

**11** Let him recognize fruits like these, and speak to those who are worthy; but let him not waste time on those who do not receive his teaching. For those who sow on earth without fruits will reap miseries.

**12** Let the presbyter, as is right and fitting, go with the deacon to the houses of those who are sick and visit them; let him consider and say to them what is fitting and proper, above all to the faithful. Let him urge that the sick who are poor be helped by the Church, so that those who do kindness may also enter into the joy of their Lord. Let him strengthen those who have newly become catechumens with the words of the prophets and the Gospels, with the word of teaching. Let him not neglect his prayers, for he is the figure of the archangels; but let him know that God did not spare the angels who sinned.

**13** Let him fast, and if it is proper, let him receive of the cup. Let wine be enough for him as far as, in his judgment, it profits and helps him, so that he does not take to his loss the drink that was for his healing. In sickness let him eat herbs and fish, so that he may also take care for his work. In everything let the priest be an example to the faithful of the work of holiness.

**14** Let the presbyter praise and give thanks in the same way as the bishop.
