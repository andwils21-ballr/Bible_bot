# 1st Book of the Covenant 22

**1** "Let him be constant at the altar. Let him persist in prayer day and night, above all at the fixed hours of the night: at the first hour, at midnight, and at early twilight, when the morning star rises; then also in the morning, and at the third, sixth and ninth hours, and at the twelfth hour, at the lighting of the lamps. If he also offers prayers without ceasing at every hour, for the people and for himself, he does well. Let him stay in the house of the Church alone. If he has one or two of like mind with him, it is good for him to be with them, to pray together as one. For you know that I have told you: where two or three are gathered in My name, I am among them. If he cannot stay all night, let him at least keep the hours I have named, for then the angels visit the Church.

**2** Let him fast three days each week all the year. But for three weeks after his appointment let him keep the fast by the number of the eighteen Exalted Entrances through which the Only-begotten passed when He came to His passion. On the first day of the week only, let him eat bread with oil and honey and salt, and all the fruits of trees; but let him not taste wine at all, except the cup of the Offering alone, whether he is sick or well, for it is good that this be for the priests only. After these weeks, all the year, let him fast three days each week, and for the rest of the time fast as his strength allows. Let him not eat meat at all: not that he is to blame if he tastes or eats it, but because, when he has chosen weakness, such strong food does not fit, and so that he may keep watch.

**3** Let the Offering be only on the Sabbath, on the first day of the week, and on a fast day. On the eve let him instruct and teach these things as a mystery to those he has tested as having ears to hear. If he is sick in body, let him quickly see to his healing, eating fish and taking a little of the wine of the holy things regularly, so that the Church does not also come to an end because he lies sick, but those who learn may have joy. When he teaches in the Church, let him speak carefully, as a man who knows that he speaks for a testimony the teaching of the whole ministry of the Father of all, the teaching that is accurately written. Let him say all these things, all that he knows accurately and has long remembered. For if he knows what he is saying, he may hope that his hearers will have known these things too. With all his labor let him plead with the Lord that his word may bear the fruits of the Holy Spirit in those who hear.

**4** Let him do everything in order and with knowledge. Let him dismiss the catechumens after he has admonished them with the meditations and admonitions of the Prophets and Apostles and with instructive words, so that they may know Him whom they confess. But let him teach the faithful as a mystery, having first dismissed the catechumens; and after the teaching of the mysteries let him offer, so that, knowing in what mystery they share, they may offer with fear.
