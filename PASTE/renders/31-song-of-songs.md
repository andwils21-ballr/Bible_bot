## Song of Songs 1–4 — rendered 2026-10-07, 5:58 PM CDT (report 6:01 PM CDT)

**Source:** Hebrew (Westminster Leningrad Codex), poetry throughout, set in lines. A new writer: the style is taken from the Hebrew itself, short lines of praise and longing, with refrains that return word for word (2:7 = 3:5; 2:17a = 4:6a; 1:15 = 4:1a; "him whom my soul loves," 1:7 and four times in 3:1–4). Written and read forms at 1:17, 2:11, 2:13 and 4:9, spelling only. Greek checked in Swete for every note that cites it (1:2, 1:5, 1:7, 2:1, 2:4, 2:7, 2:12, 2:17, 3:9, 3:10, 4:9, 4:12, 4:13).

**What these chapters are:** "Let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth," "I am black and lovely," "Tell me, you whom my soul loves" (1); "I am a flower of Sharon," "his banner over me was love," "the winter is past," "the little foxes," "My beloved is mine, and I am his" (2); the night search through the city, Solomon's litter and wedding crown (3); the praise of the bride, "a garden locked… a fountain sealed," "Let my beloved come to his garden" (4).

**No speaker labels.** The Hebrew names no speakers; the change of voice shows in the grammar (Hebrew marks "you" and "my" as masculine or feminine). The text adds no headings like "She" or "He"; notes point out a change where it matters (1:2, 1:15–16, 4:16).

**Word table for the book:** *dodi* "my beloved" (her word for him); *ra'yati* "my darling" (his word for her, nine times); *dodim* "love" (1:2, 4; 4:10); *kallah* "bride"; *achoti* "my sister"; *hayyafah bannashim* "most beautiful among women"; *mum* "blemish" (4:7), as the word table has it. *hinneh* is cut each time (1:15–16; 2:8, 9, 11; 4:1); 3:7 has "It is Solomon's own litter," answering the question of 3:6.

**Choices for you:**
- ***dodim*, "love," or "caresses"** — the plural noun means love shown, lovemaking; "love" is how most English Bibles have it, and the text keeps "love." Verses: 1:2 *For your love is better than wine*; 1:4 *we will praise your love more than wine*; 4:10 *How beautiful is your love… How much better is your love than wine*. The same word comes again in 5:1 and 7:12.
- **1:5 "black and lovely" or "black but lovely"** — the Hebrew and the Greek have "and"; older English Bibles have "but." Now: *I am black and lovely.*
- **2:1 "a flower of Sharon"** — "rose" (older English) has no support in the word; "crocus" is a modern guess; the Greek has "a flower of the plain." Now: *I am a flower of Sharon, a lily of the valleys.*
- **No speaker labels** (above). Many modern Bibles add "She," "He," "Friends." Recommendation: none in the text, as the Hebrew has none; a note where the voice changes.
