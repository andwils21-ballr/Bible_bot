**What these chapters are:**
- **5:** two more night visions. A flying scroll, twenty cubits by ten, is the curse that goes out over the land against the thief and the one who swears falsely, and it eats up their houses. Then a woman named Wickedness is shut inside a basket the size of an ephah under a lead cover, and two women with stork's wings carry it off to Shinar, Babylon, to set it up in a house there.
- **6:** the last vision: four chariots with horses of four colors come out from between two bronze mountains, the four winds of heaven, and patrol the earth; those sent north have set the LORD's Spirit at rest there. Then Zechariah is told to take silver and gold from returned exiles, make crowns and set them on Joshua the high priest, and to announce the man whose name is the Branch, who will build the LORD's temple and rule on his throne, with a priest and peace between the two.
- **7:** two years later, the town of Bethel asks the priests whether to go on fasting in the fifth month, for the temple's fall. The LORD asks whether those seventy years of fasting were ever for Him, and repeats what the former prophets said: true justice, loyal love, no oppression of the widow, the fatherless, the guest or the poor. The fathers would not listen, so the land was made desolate.
- **8:** ten promises, each opening *This is what the LORD of hosts says*. The LORD has returned to Zion; old people and children will fill Jerusalem's streets again; He will bring His people home from east and west. The curse of Haggai's day is undone: the vine, the land and the sky will give again, and Judah will be a blessing. The fasts for Jerusalem's fall will become feasts, and the nations will come to seek the LORD, ten men taking hold of one Jew's garment because God is with them.

**Choices for you:**
- **5:3, *niqqah***
  - (a) **every thief will be purged from here** (now): the curse will clear them out of the land, and v4 says how: it *will consume* the house.
  - (b) **every thief has gone unpunished**: the same verb's common sense, **be held innocent, go unpunished**; the scroll is sent because thieves and perjurers have got away with it until now.
  - Recommend (a): v4 at once describes the punishment, and the Greek has *will be punished*.
- **5:6, *zot einam***
  - (a) **This is their appearance throughout the land** (now): the Hebrew, word for word *their eye*; the basket is what the LORD's eyes see in the land (4:10).
  - (b) **This is their iniquity throughout the land**: the Greek's reading, *avonam*, one letter different in Hebrew.
  - Recommend (a): the Hebrew makes sense and answers *the eyes of the LORD* in 4:10.
- **6:8, *heniḥu et ruchi***
  - (a) **have set My Spirit at rest** (now): *ruach* as the Spirit, the fixed term.
  - (b) **have appeased My anger**: *ruach* as temper, as in Judges 8:3, and the verb Ezekiel uses with wrath (Ezekiel 16:42); the Greek has *My anger*.
  - Recommend (a), with the note giving (b): the fixed term holds unless you rule this a use of *ruach* as temper.
- **6:13, *ve-hayah kohen al kis'o***
  - (a) **There will be a priest on his throne** (now): a second person, the priest beside the ruling Branch, as *between the two of them* says; the Greek has *at his right hand*.
  - (b) **He will be a priest on his throne**: the Branch himself is king and priest in one.
  - Recommend (a): *the two of them* needs two persons.
- **7:2, *Beit El Sar-etser ve-Regem melekh va-anashav***
  - (a) **Bethel had sent Sharezer and Regem-melech and his men** (now): the town sends two men; the question in v3 is the town's, *I*.
  - (b) **Bethel-sharezer had sent Regem-melech and his men**: one man whose name includes *Bethel*; *Sharezer* alone is a man's name, as one of Sennacherib's sons (2 Kings 19:37).
  - Recommend (a): Bethel is a town elsewhere in the Bible, and the town speaking as *I* in v3 fits it; but *his men*, singular, leaves (b) a fair reading.
