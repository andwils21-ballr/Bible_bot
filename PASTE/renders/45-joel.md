**What these chapters are:**
- **1:** a plague of locusts like none the elders have seen, four waves of them, has stripped the land. Joel calls everyone to mourn: the drunkards for the wine, the farmers for the harvest, the priests because there is no grain or wine left to offer. He calls a fast and a solemn assembly, for the day of the LORD is near. Even the cattle groan and the wild animals cry out to God.
- **2:** the locusts become an army on the march, the LORD's own, with the sun and moon dark before it. Yet even now the LORD calls: return to Me with all your heart; tear your hearts, not your garments, for He is gracious and merciful. The whole people, from infants to newlyweds, is to gather and plead. The LORD answers: He drives the northern army into the sea and gives back the grain, the wine, the oil and the rain, repaying the years the locusts ate. And afterward He will pour out His Spirit on everyone, young and old, slaves too, and everyone who calls on His name will escape.

**Choices for you:**
- **The four locust names, Joel 1:4 and 2:25 (affects several books).** Joel names four insects side by side: *gazam*, *arbeh*, *yeleq*, *chasil*, so they need four different English words. This also settles the *chasil* question from the 2 Chronicles spot check. Right now the project has *locust* for *arbeh* everywhere; *young locusts* for *yeleq* (Psalm 105:34); *grasshopper* for *chasil* (1 Kings 8:37; 2 Chronicles 6:28; Psalm 78:46) but *caterpillar* in Isaiah 33:4; *grasshopper* for *chagav* too (Leviticus 11:22; 2 Chronicles 7:13); and *locust* for *gazam* in Amos 4:9, which clashes with *arbeh*.
  - (a) **cutting locust, locust, young locust, grasshopper** (now): *chasil* keeps *grasshopper*, as in 1 Kings 8:37, so *grasshopper* still stands for two Hebrew words (*chasil* and *chagav*). Isaiah 33:4 *caterpillar* becomes *grasshopper*.
  - (b) **cutting locust, locust, young locust, consuming locust**: *chasil* gets its own name, from its verb in *for the locust will consume it* (Deuteronomy 28:38), and *grasshopper* is left to *chagav* alone. 1 Kings 8:37, 2 Chronicles 6:28, Psalm 78:46 and Isaiah 33:4 change to *consuming locust*.
  - Either way, Amos 4:9 changes from *locust* to *cutting locust*, and Jeremiah 51:14 and 51:27 (*locusts*, *bristling locusts*) become *young locusts* and *bristling young locusts*, so each Hebrew word has one English word.
  - Recommend (b): one English word for each Hebrew word, as the spot check asked, and the name comes from a verb the reader can see in Deuteronomy.
- **2:17, *li-mshol bam goyim***
  - (a) **for the nations to rule over them** (now): *mashal* with *bam*, **over them**, and the nations as the ones doing it; the Greek has *to rule over them*.
  - (b) **for the nations to make a proverb of them**: *mashal* as the *proverb* of *a horror, a proverb and a byword* (Deuteronomy 28:37), next to *scorn* in the same line.
  - Recommend (a): the wording, *bam*, **over them**, and the Greek; the note gives the other reading.
- **2:23, *ha-moreh li-tsdaqah***
  - (a) **the early rain for righteousness** (now): the next line uses *moreh* for the early rain, and the verse is about rain.
  - (b) **the Teacher for righteousness**: *moreh* as **teacher**, as in *your eyes will see your Teacher* (Isaiah 30:20).
  - Recommend (a): the same word is the rain in the next line; the note gives *teacher*.
