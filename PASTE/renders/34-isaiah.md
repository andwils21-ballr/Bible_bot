### Choices

**Isaiah 4:2, *tsemach YHWH*: "the branch of the LORD" or "the Branch of the LORD"?**

Reads now: "In that day the branch of the LORD shall be beautiful and glorious, / and the fruit of the land shall be the pride and splendor / of the survivors of Israel."

1. **Lowercase** (as now): what the LORD makes grow, in parallel with *the fruit of the land*; the land restored after the judgment.
2. **Capital "Branch"**: the title of the coming king, as in Jeremiah 23:5, *a righteous Branch*, and Zechariah 3:8 and 6:12, *My servant the Branch*. The line becomes a promise of a person.

**Recommendation:** lowercase here, with the note pointing to Jeremiah and Zechariah. The parallel with *the fruit of the land* governs this verse. When Jeremiah 23:5 and Zechariah come, the title can be capitalized there.

### Choices

**Isaiah 1:18: promise or question?**

Reads now: "Come now, let us settle this," says the LORD: / "Though your sins are like scarlet, / they shall be white as snow; / though they are red like crimson, / they shall become like wool."

The Hebrew is *im yihyu chata'eikhem kashshanim kashsheleg yalbinu*: "if your sins are like scarlet, as snow they will be white."
1. **A promise** (as now): God offers to wash the sins white. This is the usual reading.
2. **A judge's challenge**: "If your sins are scarlet, shall they pass for white as snow?" The *if* is then a rebuke, and vv19–20 give the real terms: obey or be eaten by the sword. The chapter's frame is a lawsuit (*let us settle this*), which supports reading the line as part of the argument.

The grammar allows both. **Recommendation:** keep the promise, with the note giving the other reading. The line is too well known as a promise to change without strong reason, and the context does not force the question.
