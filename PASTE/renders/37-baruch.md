## Baruch 1–5 — rendered 2026-10-20, 12:52 PM CDT

**Source:** Greek (Swete) throughout. The book survives only in Greek, so there is no Hebrew to weigh it against and no departures. Verse counts follow Swete in all five chapters. **This is the whole book**: five chapters. Spec Part 2 has no Baruch section, so the Greek-book rules apply: *Kyrios* is *the Lord*, *Kyrios Pantokrator* *Lord Almighty* (3:1, 4), *Hades* for *hadēs* (2:17; 3:11, 19), and capital pronouns for God.

**Form kept:**
- 1:1–14 is prose narrative; the confession (1:15–3:8) is prose prayer; the wisdom poem (3:9–4:4) and the comfort of Jerusalem (4:5–5:9) are set as verse lines. Headings mark the turns: *The Confession* (1:15), *Wisdom* (3:9), *Take Courage* (4:5), *Take Courage, Jerusalem* (4:30).
- *And now* is kept for the turn inside the prayer at 2:11, as in other Greek books. *Kai idou* at 2:25 is cut, as *hinneh* is. No sentence starts with *And*.
- *Ho aiōnios* is *the Everlasting*, a name for God (4:10, 14, 20, 22, 24, 35; 5:2). *Ho hagios* is *the Holy One* (4:22, 37; 5:5).
- 4:5 *my people* is lowercase: the speaker is the poet, not God.
- Matched to this project's English: Jeremiah 7:34 and 36:30 (2:23–25); Lamentations 5:19 *enthroned forever* (3:3); Deuteronomy 28:53 (2:3), 4:27 (2:13), 32:17 *demons* (4:7).

**What these chapters are:**
- **1:** Baruch's book read to the exiles in Babylon; money sent to Jerusalem with a request for prayer; the confession begins.
- **2:** the confession goes on: the curses of the law came true, and the prayer asks God to turn, quoting His promise of return.
- **3:** the prayer of *the dead of Israel*; then a poem: no one found wisdom but God, who gave it to Israel.
- **4:** wisdom is the law; Jerusalem, as a mother, sends her children away and promises their return; the city that took them will fall.
- **5:** Jerusalem told to take off mourning and watch her children come home.

**Left standing on purpose:**
- 1:2, *in the fifth year*: the Greek gives no month and no starting point; the English does not supply one.
- 2:23, *from the streets of Jerusalem*: the Greek *exōthen*, "outside," is the word for Jerusalem's streets in this saying; the English matches Jeremiah 7:34.

**Choices for you:**
- **3:38, *ōphthē… synanestraphē***
  - (a) **Afterward she appeared on earth and lived among people** (now): wisdom, the subject of the whole poem, who in the next verse is *the book of the commandments of God* (4:1).
  - (b) **Afterward He appeared on earth and lived among people**: God, the subject of vv32–37. Many early Christian readers took it this way.
  - Recommend (a): the poem is about wisdom from 3:9 to 4:4, and 4:1 goes straight on with *She*. The note gives both.
- **2:25, *en apostolē***
  - (a) **by pestilence** (now): the third of the three, famine, sword and pestilence, as Greek Jeremiah uses the word.
  - (b) **by exile**: *a sending away*, the plain sense of the word.
  - Recommend (a): the line names ways the people *died*, and Greek Jeremiah 39:36 has the same three with the same word.
