**What these chapters are:**
- **5:** the earthly tent will be taken down, and we long for the house from God; we walk by faith, not by sight, and all must appear before Christ's judgment seat. The love of Christ compels us: one died for all. *If anyone is in Christ, there is a new creation*; God was reconciling the world to Himself, and *He made Him who knew no sin to be sin for us*.
- **6:** *now is the day of salvation*. Paul's ministry in beatings and sleepless nights, *as dying, and yet we live… as having nothing, yet possessing everything*. Open your hearts; do not be unequally yoked with unbelievers, for *we are the temple of the living God*.
- **7:** Titus came and brought comfort: the Corinthians grieved over Paul's letter, and their sorrow led to repentance. *Sorrow as God wills produces a repentance that leads to salvation and brings no regret.*
- **8:** the churches of Macedonia, in deep poverty, gave beyond their means for the saints in Jerusalem. *Though He was rich, for your sake He became poor.* Give out of what you have, for fairness, as with the manna; Titus and two brothers will come to carry the gift.

**Choices for you:**
- **5:17, *ei tis en Christō, kainē ktisis***
  - (a) **if anyone is in Christ, there is a new creation** (now): the world made new, which the believer enters.
  - (b) **if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation**: the believer himself made new.
  - Recommend (a): Paul's only other use, *neither circumcision… nor uncircumcision, but a new creation* (Galatians 6:15), is the new world, and *the old things have passed away; new things have come* is Isaiah's new thing for the whole people (Isaiah 43:18–19).
- **5:21, *hamartian epoiēsen***
  - (a) **He made Him who knew no sin to be sin for us** (now): Christ took the place of sin itself.
  - (b) **He made Him who knew no sin to be a sin offering for us**: the Hebrew *chatat* means both *sin* and *sin offering* (Leviticus 4:3).
  - Recommend (a): Paul writes plain *hamartia*, not *peri hamartias* (*an offering for sin*) as in Romans 8:3, and the line sets *sin* against *the righteousness of God*; the note gives (b).

**What these chapters are:**
- **1:** Paul blesses *the God of all comfort*, who comforted him when he was near death in Asia so that he can comfort others. He changed his travel plans, but his word is not *Yes and No*: all God's promises are *Yes* in Christ, and God has anointed and sealed us and given the Spirit as a pledge.
- **2:** he wrote a letter in tears instead of making a painful visit; now the man who caused sorrow should be forgiven and comforted. God leads the apostles in Christ's triumph, and through them spreads *the aroma of Christ*, life to some and death to others.
- **3:** the Corinthians are Paul's letter of recommendation, written by the Spirit on hearts. If the ministry of the stone tablets came with glory, Moses' face too bright to look at, the ministry of the Spirit has more. A veil still lies over the reading of *the old covenant*, but *whenever anyone turns to the Lord, the veil is taken away*.
- **4:** the God who said *Light will shine out of darkness* has shone in our hearts. *We have this treasure in clay pots*: troubled but not crushed, carrying the dying of Jesus so that His life may show. The outer self wastes away; the inner is renewed day by day, toward *an eternal weight of glory*.
