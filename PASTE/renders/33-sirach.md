## Sirach 1–4 — rendered 2026-10-07, 6:41 PM CDT

**Source:** Greek (Swete), with English verse numbers. A new writer: the style is taken from the Greek, two-line sayings in the manner of Proverbs, set in lines. Echoes in the notes checked against the Greek in `sources/` (Proverbs 8:22; Exodus 34:6; Luke 6:35) and against this project's English where quoted (2 Samuel 24:14; 1 Kings 18:21; Proverbs 9:5, 16:6, 17:3; Reproof 4:13, 5:27; Psalm 22:5, 131:1; Deuteronomy 29:29; Exodus 20:12, 22:23).

**Verses with no Greek:** Swete gives 1:5, 7, 21 and 3:19, 25 their numbers and no words (checked in the raw `sources/swete-src/` data: the word slots are empty). Each keeps its number with an italic line, as Nehemiah 7:68 does, and a note. Logged in NOTES_FOR_ANDREW.md with the full list for the book.

**The translator's foreword** is in `sources/swete-src/` but not printed by the source helper, and is not rendered (choice below).

**Doubled lines in the Greek held here:** 3:5 repeats the second line of 3:4; 3:26 gives the "stubborn heart" line twice. Rendered as they stand, with notes.

**What these chapters are:** "All wisdom is from the Lord," the fear of the Lord as wisdom's beginning, fullness, crown and root (1); "My child, if you come forward to serve the Lord God, prepare your soul for testing," "Let us fall into the hands of the Lord" (2); honoring father and mother, "charity atones for sins," "Do not seek what is too hard for you" (3); the poor and the orphan, wisdom testing her pupil, "Fight to the death for the truth" (4).

**Word table for the book:** *Kyrios* "the Lord"; *sophia* "wisdom"; *paideia* "discipline"; *phobos Kyriou* "the fear of the Lord"; *teknon* "my child" (the teacher's address); *nomos* "the law" (2:16); *eleēmosynē* "charity" (3:30, as Tobit's "acts of charity"), "kindness" (3:14); *exilaskesthai* "atone"; *bdelygma* "detestable" (1:25); *Hypsistos* "the Most High" (4:10).

**The "And" check:** none. 1:19, 3:26 and 4:27 begin without the Greek's *kai*.
