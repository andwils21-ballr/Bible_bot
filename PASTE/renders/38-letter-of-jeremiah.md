## Letter of Jeremiah 1 — rendered 2026-10-20, 5:54 PM CDT

**Source:** Greek (Swete) throughout. The letter survives only in Greek, so there are no departures. **This is the whole book**: one chapter, rendered in the same run as 4 Baruch 1–4 (report in `PASTE/renders/39-4-baruch.md`). It has no Part 2 section in the spec, so the rules for Greek books apply.

**Verse numbers:** English Bibles print the letter as Baruch 6, with 73 verses: the heading is verse 1 and *Because of the sins* is verse 2. Swete prints it as a book of its own after Lamentations and joins the heading and the first sentence as its verse 1, so its numbers run one behind (Swete 72 = English 73). The text follows the English numbers, and the first note says so.

**Form kept:**
- Set as prose, a letter. The two refrains are kept word for word each time: *So do not be afraid of them* (vv15, 23; *do not be afraid of them* closing vv29, 65; *Therefore do not be afraid of them*, v69), and *How then can anyone think or say that they are gods?* (vv40, 45), with the Greek's own variations at vv52, 57 and 64.
- *Xylina kai perichrysa kai periargyra* is *wooden, gold-plated and silver-plated* every time, in the Greek's order (silver before gold at v57, as the Greek has it).
- *Aisthanomai*, "perceive," and its noun keep one English (vv20, 24, 41, 42, 50), since the letter turns the word from the idols to their worshippers.
- Doubled verbs made plain: v5 *aphomoiōthentes… aphomoiōthēte* (*become like*); v35 *euchēn euxamenos* (*makes a vow*).
- No *And* starts a sentence. *Despota* is *Master*, as in Judith 9:12.

**Echoes in the notes, checked against this project's English where quoted:**
- Jeremiah 10:5, 10:14, 29:10; Psalm 142:4; Judith 9:12; Leviticus 12:4, 21:5, 21:10.

**Checked against the sources:**
- Swete's Greek: Letter of Jeremiah 1–72; Greek Jeremiah 10:5 (*mē phobēthēte auta*; no scarecrow), 10:14 (*ouk estin pneuma en autois*); Greek Psalm 141:5 (*ho ekzētōn tēn psychēn mou*).

**The checker** found no errors and nothing to look at.

**What this chapter is:** a single sermon against the idols of Babylon, sent to the exiles before they go: the gods are made, dressed, cleaned, guarded and robbed by men; they cannot speak, see, save or perceive; so do not be afraid of them.

**Left standing on purpose:**
- v11, *the prostitutes on the roof*: *stegos* is a roof or a covered house; the letter says no more about who they are.
- v55, *like crows between heaven and earth*: the picture is not explained in the letter, and the English does not explain it.

**Choices for you:**
- **v48, *pōs te dē mellei ta hyp' autōn kataskeuasthenta***
  - (a) **how then can the things they have made be gods?** (now): the letter's own question, asked in every refrain.
  - (b) **how then can the things they have made last?**: v47 has just said the makers *do not live long*, and the sentence asks whether their work will.
  - Recommend (a): the next words, *They have left lies and disgrace to those who come after*, say their work does outlast them, as a disgrace; what it cannot be is a god.
