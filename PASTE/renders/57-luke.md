## Luke 1–2 — rendered 2026-11-01, 1:44 PM CST

**Source:** Greek (SBLGNT). The edition's readings are followed. A new writer: Luke's Greek is fuller and more formal than Mark's, and in these two chapters it is close to the Greek of the Old Testament; the style is taken from his Greek, not carried over from Mark. The songs (1:46–55, 68–79; 2:14, 29–32) are set as poetry, as Hannah's song in 1 Samuel 2.

**Form kept:**
- The preface (1:1–4) is one long sentence in Luke's Greek; it is kept as one sentence over four verses.
- *Egeneto*, **it came to pass**, at the head of scenes (1:5, 8, 23, 41, 59; 2:1, 6, 15, 46) is plain; no sentence starts with *And*.
- *Idou* cut (1:20, 31, 36, 44, 48; 2:10, 25, 34, 48), except *Here is the servant of the Lord* (1:38), Mary's answer.
- Fixed terms: *angel* for *angelos* (1:11–38; 2:9–21), the Greek ruling; *the Holy Spirit* (1:15, 35, 41, 67; 2:25–26); *the Spirit* (2:27); *his spirit*, John's own (1:80), and *my spirit*, Mary's (1:47), lowercase.
- Capital pronouns for God and for Jesus, from the angel's words to Mary on (1:31–33).
- *The Lord* for *kyrios*, God, throughout; *Lord* of Jesus in Elizabeth's *my Lord* (1:43) and the angel's *the Messiah, the Lord* (2:11).
- Kept the same as other books: *strong drink* (1:15), as Numbers 6:3 and Judges 13:4; *advanced in years* (1:7, 18), after Genesis 18:11; *disgrace* (1:25), as Genesis 30:23; *The Lord is with you* (1:28), as Judges 6:12; *virgin* (1:27), as Isaiah 7:14; *Blessed are you among women* (1:42), after Judges 5:24; *the shadow of death* (1:79), as Isaiah 9:2; *two turtledoves or two young pigeons* (2:24), as Leviticus 12:8; *the opener of… womb* (2:23), after Exodus 13:2; *a light for… the nations* (2:32), as Isaiah 42:6 and 49:6; *growing in stature and in favor… with God and with people* (2:52), after 1 Samuel 2:26.

**What these chapters are:**
- **1:** Luke tells Theophilus why he writes. In the temple an angel promises the old priest Zechariah a son, John, who will go before the Lord *in the spirit and power of Elijah*; Zechariah doubts and is struck silent. Six months later Gabriel tells Mary in Nazareth that she will bear a son, Jesus, the Son of the Most High, by the Holy Spirit. Mary visits Elizabeth, whose baby leaps at her greeting, and sings *My soul magnifies the Lord*. John is born; when Zechariah writes *His name is John* his speech returns, and he sings of the *Dawn from on high*.
- **2:** a Roman census brings Joseph and Mary to Bethlehem, where Jesus is born and laid in a manger. Angels announce Him to shepherds, *Glory to God in the highest*. At His presentation in the temple, old Simeon takes Him in his arms, *Now, Master, You are letting Your servant go in peace*, and warns Mary of a sword; the prophetess Anna gives thanks. At twelve, Jesus stays behind in the temple among the teachers: *Did you not know that I must be in My Father's house?*

**Choices for you:**
- **1:35, *to gennōmenon hagion klēthēsetai, huios theou***
  - (a) **the child to be born will be called holy, the Son of God** (now): *holy* is what He will be called, and *the Son of God* names Him.
  - (b) **the holy child to be born will be called the Son of God**: *holy* describes the child, and the name He is given is *Son of God*.
  - Recommend (a): it keeps the Greek order, *holy* first and then *the Son of God*; the Greek allows both, and the note gives (b).
- **2:49, *en tois tou patros mou dei einai me***
  - (a) **I must be in My Father's house** (now): the temple, where they found Him.
  - (b) **I must be about My Father's business**: *the things of My Father*, His affairs.
  - Recommend (a): they had been searching for the place He was, and He answers with a place.
