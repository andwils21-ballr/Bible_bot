**What these chapters are:**
- **3:** Jesus heals a withered hand on the sabbath, and the Pharisees begin to plot His death. Crowds come from every region; He appoints the twelve. His family thinks He is out of His mind, and scribes from Jerusalem say He drives out demons by Beelzebul; He answers that a divided house cannot stand, and that blasphemy against the Holy Spirit is never forgiven. His true family is whoever does the will of God.
- **4:** teaching from a boat: the sower and the four soils, why He speaks in parables, the lamp and the measure, the seed that grows while the farmer sleeps, and the mustard seed. That evening, crossing the sea, a storm nearly swamps the boat while He sleeps; He rebukes the wind and the sea, and the disciples ask, *Who then is this?*
- **5:** across the sea in Gentile country, He frees a man possessed by a *Legion* of unclean spirits, which go into two thousand pigs. Back on the other side, on His way to the dying daughter of Jairus, a woman sick for twelve years touches His cloak and is healed; then He takes the dead girl by the hand and says *Talitha koum*, and she gets up.
- **6:** His home town takes offense at *the carpenter, the son of Mary*. He sends the twelve out two by two. Herod, hearing of Him, thinks John has been raised; Mark tells how John was beheaded at Herod's birthday banquet. Jesus feeds five thousand with five loaves and two fish, walks on the sea at night, and the sick of Gennesaret are healed by touching His cloak.

**Choices for you:**
- **3:21, *elegon gar hoti exestē***
  - (a) **for they were saying, "He is out of His mind"** (now): *they* is His own people, the family that comes to seize Him; the verse follows from *His own people*.
  - (b) **for people were saying**: *elegon* as the crowd's talk, which brings the family out to take Him home.
  - Recommend (a): the subject of *went out* is *His own people*, and the same verb follows with no new subject.
- **4:12, *hina blepontes blepōsi… mēpote epistrepsōsin***
  - (a) **so that they may look and look, and not see… otherwise they might turn and be forgiven** (now): the parables are given to fulfil Isaiah's commission, as the prophet was sent to make the heart fat (Isaiah 6:10).
  - (b) **so that, as it is written, they look and look… unless they turn and are forgiven**: *hina* as *that it may be fulfilled*, and *mēpote* as *unless*, leaving the door open.
  - Recommend (a): *hina* and *mēpote* are purpose words, and the line is Isaiah's own.

## Mark 1–2 — rendered 2026-10-30, 11:44 PM CDT

**Source:** Greek (SBLGNT). The edition's readings are followed; where it marks a place where manuscripts differ and the meaning turns on it, the note says so (1:1, 2, 41). A new writer: the style is taken from Mark's Greek, not carried over from Malachi. Matthew's English is reused where the Greek is the same (1:3, 6, 8, 16–20, 40–44; 2:5, 9–10, 14–22, 25–28) and departs where Mark's Greek differs (1:4 *the baptizer*; 1:11 *You are My Son*; 1:17 *make you become*; 2:4 *mat*).

**Form kept:**
- *Euthys*, **at once**, kept every time it occurs (1:10, 12, 18, 20, 21, 23, 28, 29, 30, 42, 43; 2:8, 12): it is Mark's own mark of pace. See the Mark-wide choice below.
- Mark's present tense in narrative (*he comes*, *he says*) is set in the past, as English tells a story.
- *Kai* at the head of nearly every sentence is dropped; no sentence starts with *And*. *Kai egeneto*, **and it came to pass**, is plain (1:9; 2:23).
- *Ide* (2:24) cut.
- Capital pronouns for Jesus throughout, including in the mouths of the unclean spirit (1:24) and the scribes (2:7).
- Fixed terms: *leper* and *leprosy* for *lepros*, *lepra* (1:40, 42), with a note on *tsara'at*; *angels* for *angeloi* (1:13), the Greek ruling; *messenger* (1:2) where the line is Malachi's and Exodus's, as Matthew 11:10 has it.
- *Spirit* for *to pneuma* (1:10, 12), *the Holy Spirit* (1:8); *unclean spirit* (1:23, 26, 27) lowercase; *His spirit* (2:8), Jesus' own, lowercase.
- Kept the same as Matthew: *the good news* (1:1, 14–15); *Jesus the Messiah* (1:1), as Matthew 1:1; *A voice of one crying out in the wilderness: Prepare the way of the Lord; make His paths straight* (1:3), as Matthew 3:3; *clothing of camel's hair and a leather belt around his waist* (1:6); *fishers of people* (1:17); *tested* (1:13), as Matthew 4:1; *handed over* (1:14), as Matthew 4:12; *has come near* (1:15); *as one who has authority, and not as the scribes* (1:22), as Matthew 7:29; *unclean spirit* (1:23), as Matthew 12:43; *possessed by demons* (1:32), as Matthew 8:16; *If You are willing, You can make me clean… I am willing; be made clean* (1:40–41); *as a testimony to them* (1:44); *the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins* (2:10); *tax booth*, *tax collectors and sinners*, *reclining* (2:14–16); *It is not the healthy who need a doctor, but those who are ill* (2:17); *wedding guests… the bridegroom is taken away from them* (2:19–20); *unshrunk cloth… fresh wineskins* (2:21–22); *the house of God… the bread of the Presence* (2:26), as Matthew 12:4 and Exodus 25:30; *Lord… of the sabbath* (2:28), as Matthew 12:8.

**What these chapters are:**
- **1:** Mark begins with no birth story: John the baptizer appears in the wilderness as the promised messenger, and Jesus is baptized; the heavens tear open and a voice calls Him *My Son, the beloved*. After forty days in the wilderness, He comes into Galilee announcing that the kingdom of God has come near, and calls four fishermen. One sabbath in Capernaum He teaches with authority, drives an unclean spirit out of a man in the synagogue, heals Simon's mother-in-law, and by evening the whole town is at the door. He touches a leper and makes him clean, and the man's telling of it drives Jesus out of the towns.
- **2:** the first disputes. Four men lower a paralyzed man through a roof; Jesus forgives his sins, and when the scribes say only God can forgive, He heals him to show that the Son of Man has authority. He calls Levi the tax collector and eats with sinners; He answers the question of fasting with the bridegroom, the patch and the wineskins; and when His disciples pick grain on the sabbath, He points to David and the bread of the Presence: the sabbath was made for man, and the Son of Man is Lord even of the sabbath.

**Choices for you:**
- **Mark throughout, *euthys*** (the whole book, about forty times)
  - (a) **at once, every time** (now): Mark's word kept as a mark of his book, as Genesis keeps *And it came to pass*; it gives the story its pace.
  - (b) **by context**: *at once* where the meaning is immediacy, *then* or nothing where it only joins one scene to the next, as in 1:21, 23 and 29.
  - Recommend (a): the word is Mark's own and the repetition is his style; under the spec a book's mark needs your ruling.
- **2:15, *en tē oikia autou***
  - (a) **in his house** (now): Levi's house, the man just called (v14), as the feast for his friends, the tax collectors.
  - (b) **in His house**: Jesus' own house in Capernaum, where *it was heard that He was at home* (2:1).
  - Recommend (a): the nearest name is Levi, and the guests are his kind.
- **2:26, *epi Abiathar archiereōs***
  - (a) **in the time of Abiathar the high priest** (now): Abiathar, later David's high priest, was alive then; 1 Samuel names his father Ahimelech as the priest who gave the bread (1 Samuel 21:1).
  - (b) **at the passage about Abiathar the high priest**: Jesus names the section of the scroll, as Mark does with *epi tou batou*, **at the passage about the bush** (12:26).
  - Recommend (a): the plain sense of *epi* with a ruler's name is *in the time of*; the note gives (b).
