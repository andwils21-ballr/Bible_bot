**Widespread decision** (it reaches Colossians 2:8, 20, and the same word is in Hebrews 5:12 and 2 Peter 3:10, 12; listed first so it is settled before it spreads):

- **4:3, 9, *ta stoicheia tou kosmou***
  - (a) **the elements of the world** (now): keeps the Greek's whole range; the note gives the three readings.
  - (b) **the first principles of the world**: the ABCs a child learns under guardians (vv1–2), as *stoicheia* in Hebrews 5:12.
  - (c) **the elemental spirits of the world**: powers served as gods, beside *those that by nature are not gods* (v8).
  - Recommend (a): the Greek word itself means all three, and v3 (a child under guardians) and vv8–9 (gods that are not gods) pull opposite ways.

The two widespread choices raised in Romans 1–4 meet Galatians here and are rendered as in Romans, still open: *pistis Iēsou Christou*, **faith in Jesus Christ** (2:16, 20; 3:22), and *dikaioō*, **justify** (2:16–17; 3:8, 11, 24).

**What these chapters are:**
- **1:** Paul, an apostle *not from men nor through a man*, is astonished that the Galatians are turning to *a different gospel*, and twice calls it accursed. His gospel came by revelation: God set him apart from his mother's womb; he went to Arabia, then to Jerusalem for fifteen days with Cephas.
- **2:** fourteen years later the Jerusalem leaders gave him *the right hand of fellowship*, and Titus was not made to be circumcised. At Antioch Paul opposed Cephas to his face for drawing back from eating with Gentiles. *A person is not justified by works of the Law but through faith in Jesus Christ… I have been crucified with Christ. It is no longer I who live, but Christ who lives in me.*
- **3:** *Foolish Galatians!* Did you receive the Spirit by works of the Law or by hearing with faith? Abraham believed God; those of faith are his sons. Christ became a curse for us. The Law, 430 years after the promise, cannot cancel it; it was a guardian until Christ came. *There is neither Jew nor Greek… slave nor free… no male and female, for you are all one in Christ Jesus.*
- **4:** the heir as a child is no better than a slave; God sent His Son so that we might be sons, and the Spirit cries *Abba! Father!* Why turn back to the weak and poor elements? Paul remembers how they welcomed him in his weakness, and is in labor with them again. Hagar and Sarah, Sinai and the Jerusalem above: *we are not children of the slave woman, but of the free woman.*

**Choices for you:**
- **3:4, *tosauta epathete eikē***
  - (a) **Did you suffer so much for nothing?** (now): they had been persecuted for the faith.
  - (b) **Did you experience so much for nothing?**: the Spirit and the mighty works of vv2–5.
  - Recommend (a): every other time Paul uses *paschō* it is *suffer* (1 Corinthians 12:26; 2 Corinthians 1:6; Philippians 1:29; 1 Thessalonians 2:14), and this letter speaks of persecution in Galatia, *so it is now also* (4:29; 6:12).
- **3:15, *anthrōpou kekyrōmenēn diathēkēn***
  - (a) **even a man's covenant, once it has been ratified** (now): one English word in v15 and v17, so the reader sees Paul's argument move from the human example to God's covenant.
  - (b) **even a man's will, once it has been ratified**: the everyday sense of *diathēkē*, a last testament; v17 stays *covenant*.
  - Recommend (a): the argument rests on the one word, and the note gives *will*. Hebrews 9:15–17 will meet the same word in the same double sense.
- **3:24, *ho nomos paidagōgos hēmōn gegonen eis Christon***
  - (a) **the Law was our guardian until Christ came** (now): the Law's term ended when Christ came.
  - (b) **the Law was our guardian to lead us to Christ**: the Law pointed and led to Christ.
  - Recommend (a): the verses on each side measure time, *before faith came… until the faith that was to come… now that faith has come* (vv23, 25); the note gives (b).
