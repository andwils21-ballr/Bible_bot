**What these chapters are:**
- **7:** four visions begin. Locusts, then a fire that eats the deep; twice Amos pleads, *How can Jacob stand? He is so small*, and twice the LORD relents. Then the plumb line: no more passing by. Amaziah, priest of Bethel, reports Amos to King Jeroboam and orders him back to Judah. Amos answers: *I was no prophet… the LORD took me from following the flock*, and pronounces judgment on Amaziah's own house.
- **8:** the basket of summer fruit, *qayits*: the end, *qets*, has come. The merchants who cannot wait for the sabbath to end, so that they can cheat with false scales and buy the poor for sandals. The LORD swears never to forget; the sun will set at noon; and a famine will come, not of bread, but of hearing the word of the LORD.
- **9:** the last vision: the Lord beside the altar, and no escape, not in Sheol, nor heaven, nor the sea floor. Israel is no different from the Cushites to Him, yet He will not utterly destroy the house of Jacob. On that day He will raise up the fallen booth of David; the harvest will overtake the plowing; they will rebuild and plant and never be uprooted again.

**Choices for you:**
- **7:7–8, *anakh***
  - (a) **a wall made by a plumb line, with a plumb line in His hand** (now): a wall built true and the tool that tests it, set *among My people*: Israel measured, and the LORD will no longer pass them by.
  - (b) **a wall of hard metal, with hard metal in His hand**: the Greek, *adamant*, a hard metal or a hard stone.
  - Recommend (a): the word is found only in these verses, and the picture of a wall and a tool held up to it fits the plumb line; the note gives the Greek.
- **7:14, *lo navi anokhi***
  - (a) **I was no prophet, nor a prophet's son** (now): until the LORD took him from the flock (v15), as the Greek has it, *I was not a prophet*.
  - (b) **I am no prophet, nor a prophet's son**: he refuses the name of one who *earns his bread* by prophecy (v12), though he does prophesy.
  - Recommend (a): the Greek reads the past, and v15 tells what happened next, *But the LORD took me*.
- **8:7, *bi-g'on Ya'aqov***
  - (a) **The LORD has sworn by the pride of Jacob** (now): the same words as 6:8, *I abhor the pride of Jacob*: an oath by the pride He will not forget.
  - (b) **The LORD has sworn by the Pride of Jacob**: a name for God Himself, as Samuel calls Him *the Enduring One of Israel* (1 Samuel 15:29), and as He swears by His holiness (4:2) and by Himself (6:8).
  - Recommend (a): the words are 6:8's, and a capital would hide that they are the same; the note gives the name.
- **9:12, *lema'an yirshu et she'erit Edom***
  - (a) **so that they may possess the remnant of Edom and all the nations over whom My name is called** (now): the Hebrew; Edom is the brother of 1:11–12.
  - (b) **so that the rest of mankind may seek the LORD, and all the nations over whom My name is called**: the Greek, reading *adam*, **mankind**, for *Edom*, and *yidreshu*, **seek**, for *yirshu*, **possess**; James quotes it in Jerusalem (Acts 15:16–17).
  - Recommend (a): it is the Hebrew, and Edom is in the book's first oracles; the note gives the Greek and James.

**What these chapters are:**

- **6:** woe to the comfortable in Zion and Samaria, lounging on ivory beds, drinking from basins, untroubled by Joseph's ruin; they will be first into exile. The LORD abhors Jacob's pride; houses will be emptied of their dead; a nation will press Israel from one end of its new borders to the other.

**Choices for you:**
- **3:12, *u-vi-dmeshek arash***
  - (a) **and a piece of a couch** (now): the word is not known; the line beside it, *the corner of a bed*, asks for a part of a couch, and the Hebrew points it unlike Damascus (1:3; 5:27).
  - (b) **and in Damascus, on a couch**: the letters of Damascus, as the Greek reads them; Jeroboam had won Damascus back (2 Kings 14:28).
  - Recommend (a): the pointing marks it as another word, and the parallel line wants a part of the furniture; the note gives Damascus.
- **4:13, *u-maggid le-adam mah secho***
  - (a) **who declares to man what His thought is** (now): God makes His own mind known, as He reveals *His counsel to His servants the prophets* (3:7); the Greek, *announcing to men His anointed*, also reads it of God.
  - (b) **who declares to man what his thought is**: God shows a man his own thoughts, as in *I the LORD search the heart and test the mind* (Jeremiah 17:10).
  - Recommend (a): 3:7 has just said the LORD reveals His counsel, and the Greek reads it of God.
- **5:26, *u-nesatem*: future or past**
  - (a) **You will take up Sikkuth your king… and I will send you into exile** (now): the verb runs on into v27, a future: they will carry their gods into exile.
  - (b) **You took up Sikkuth your king…**: the past, in the wilderness, answering the question of v25; the Greek has the past, and Stephen quotes it so (Acts 7:43).
  - Recommend (a): *u-nesatem* and *ve-higleiti* have the same form, and the second is plainly future.
- **5:26, *Sikkut malkekhem***
  - (a) **Sikkuth your king** (now): a god's name, as the Hebrew points it, beside *Kiyyun your images, the star of your god*.
  - (b) **the tent of your king**: *sukkat*, the same letters, as the Greek has *the tent of Moloch*.
  - Recommend (a): the line names gods and images, and *the star of your god* follows.
- **6:13, *lo davar*, *qarnayim***
  - (a) **You who rejoice in nothing, who say, "Have we not taken horns for ourselves by our own strength?"** (now): words, as the Hebrew points *lo davar* and as the Greek reads both.
  - (b) **You who rejoice over Lo-debar, who say, "Have we not taken Karnaim for ourselves by our own strength?"**: two towns (2 Samuel 9:4; Genesis 14:5), with the pun left in the names.
  - Recommend (a): the Hebrew points *lo davar* as words, not as the town, and the note gives both towns.

## Amos 1–2 — rendered 2026-10-26, 1:10 PM CDT

**Source:** Hebrew (WLC). Swete's Greek checked throughout. No departures from the Hebrew; the Greek is quoted in the notes where it differs (1:1, 3, 6, 15; 2:7, 13). Hebrew, Greek and English numbering agree in these chapters. A new writer: the style is taken from the Hebrew, not carried over from Hosea.

**Form kept:**
- The eight oracles keep one refrain, word for word (1:3, 6, 9, 11, 13; 2:1, 4, 6): *This is what the LORD says: For three transgressions of…, and for four, I will not turn it back*. *This is what the LORD says* is the ruling of 2026-10-18; *turn it back* is the verb as Numbers 23:20 has it; *transgressions* for *pesha*, as Isaiah 53:5.
- The fire refrain is Hosea 8:14's: *I will send fire on…, and it will devour… strongholds* (1:4, 7, 10, 12; 2:2, 5). 1:14 has another verb, *hitsatti*, **I will kindle**, and the English keeps the difference.
- *Says the LORD* for *amar YHWH* (1:5, 15; 2:3); *says the Lord GOD* for *amar Adonai YHWH* (1:8); *declares the LORD* for *ne'um YHWH* (2:11, 16).
- *The Ammonites* for *benei Ammon* (1:13), as Jeremiah 49:2.
- God's *hinneh* + participle is *I am about to* (2:13). No *va-yehi* formula; no written and read forms.
- Kept the same as other books: *a sheep breeder* (1:1), as 2 Kings 3:4; *I will turn My hand against* (1:8), as Isaiah 1:25; *rip open the pregnant women* (1:13), as 2 Kings 8:12 and Hosea 13:16; *profane My holy name* (2:7), as Leviticus 22:32; *taken in pledge* (2:8), as Exodus 22:26; *fined* (2:8), as Exodus 21:22; *trample* for *sha'af* (2:7), as Psalm 56:1; *Flight will fail* (2:14), as Jeremiah 25:35; *forty years in the wilderness* (2:10), as Deuteronomy 29:5.
- 1:1 is prose; the oracles from 1:2 are set as poetry. No sentence starts with *And*.

**What these chapters are:**
- **1:** the words of Amos, a sheep breeder from Tekoa in Judah, spoken to the north two years before an earthquake. The LORD roars from Zion and the pastures wither. Then oracle after oracle against Israel's neighbors, each for *three transgressions, and for four*: Damascus threshed Gilead with iron; Gaza and Tyre sold whole peoples to Edom; Edom hunted his brother; Ammon ripped open the pregnant women of Gilead. Fire on each.
- **2:** Moab burned a king's bones to lime. Then Judah, for rejecting the law, and at last Israel, the eighth, whose charge runs on: the innocent sold for silver, the poor trampled, a father and son with one girl, pledged cloaks and fines' wine at the altar. The LORD who destroyed the Amorite and brought them out of Egypt gave them prophets and Nazirites, and they silenced both; on that day not even the bravest will escape.
