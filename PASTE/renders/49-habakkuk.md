**What these chapters are:**
- **1:** Habakkuk complains to God: how long must he cry *Violence!* and see wrongdoing while the law goes numb and the wicked surround the righteous? God answers that He is raising up the Chaldeans, a fierce nation, swift as leopards, who make their own law and laugh at kings and fortresses. Habakkuk answers back: You are too pure to look on evil, so why do You stay silent while the wicked swallow those more righteous than they, catching people like fish in a net and then worshipping the net?
- **2:** Habakkuk stands at his post to wait. The LORD tells him to write the vision plainly; it will come at its time and will not lie, so wait for it. The proud man's soul is not upright, *but the righteous will live by his faithfulness*. Then five woes on the conqueror: on the one who piles up what is not his, who builds his house by evil gain, who builds a city with blood, who makes his neighbor drunk to shame him, and who prays to a mute idol. The earth will be filled with the knowledge of the LORD's glory; the LORD is in His holy temple, and all the earth must be silent.
- **3:** a prayer-psalm. Habakkuk has heard what the LORD did and asks Him to do it again, and in wrath to remember compassion. He sees God coming from the south as at Sinai, with plague before Him; the mountains shake, the sun and moon stand still, and He rides out on the sea to save His people and His anointed. Habakkuk trembles, but he will wait. Even if the fig tree does not blossom and the fields and flocks fail, he will rejoice in the God of his salvation, who makes his feet like a deer's on the heights.

**Choices for you:**
- **2:4, *ve-tsaddiq be-emunato yichyeh* (affects how the New Testament quotations will read)**
  - (a) **but the righteous will live by his faithfulness** (now): *emunah* as steady, holding faithfulness, as in *His hands stayed steady* (Exodus 17:12) and *great is Your faithfulness* (Lamentations 3:23).
  - (b) **but the righteous will live by his faith**: *emunah* as trust; the Greek has *pistis*, which is both faith and faithfulness, and the New Testament gives the line with that word (Romans 1:17; Galatians 3:11; Hebrews 10:38).
  - Recommend (a): it is the word's sense everywhere else this project has rendered it; the note gives the Greek and the New Testament forms, and when Romans is rendered its English can carry *faith* from the Greek.
- **2:16, *ve-he'arel***
  - (a) **Drink, you too, and show your foreskin!** (now): from *orlah*, **foreskin**: the drunkard is exposed, as he exposed his neighbor (v15).
  - (b) **Drink, you too, and stagger!**: the same letters in another order, *ra'al*, **reel**, as the Greek has it (*be shaken in heart and quake*).
  - Recommend (a): it is the Hebrew, and it answers *their nakedness* in v15; the note gives the Greek.
