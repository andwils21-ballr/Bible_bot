**What this chapter is:**
- **3:** woe to Nineveh, the city of bloodshed: the battle in a rush of sounds, horses, chariots, flashing swords, heaps of the dead. Nineveh is a prostitute and sorceress who sold nations, and the LORD will strip her before them, with no one to mourn or comfort her. She is no better than No-amon in Egypt, which was guarded by water and allies, yet went into exile. Her fortresses will fall like ripe figs; she can draw water and make bricks for the siege, but fire will eat her like locusts, and her merchants and officials will fly off like locusts in the sun. Her rulers sleep, her people are scattered, her wound cannot be healed, and everyone who hears of it claps, for her evil had passed over them all.

**What these chapters are:**
- **1:** an oracle against Nineveh, the city spared in Jonah: the LORD is a jealous God who avenges; He is slow to anger but does not leave guilt unpunished. Seas dry up and mountains quake before Him, yet He is a stronghold to those who trust Him. Nineveh's plotting against the LORD will end in a full end. To Judah He promises: I afflicted you, but no more; I will break the Assyrian yoke. And the messenger comes over the mountains with the good news: keep your feasts, Judah, the worthless one is gone.
- **2:** the attack on Nineveh, told as if watched: red shields, chariots flashing through the streets, the river gates opened, the palace melting, the slave women moaning like doves, the people fleeing while officers shout *Stand!*, the city plundered of its endless treasure. Then the taunt: where now is the lions' den, where Assyria's lion tore prey for its cubs with no one to frighten it? The LORD of hosts is against her, and her messengers will be heard no more.
