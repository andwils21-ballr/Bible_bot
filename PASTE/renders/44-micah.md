## Micah 1 — rendered 2026-10-26, 10:58 PM CDT

**Source:** Hebrew (WLC). Swete's Greek checked throughout. No departures from the Hebrew; the Greek is quoted in the notes where it differs (1:1, 5, 8, 10–12). Hebrew, Greek and English numbering agree. A new writer: the style is taken from the Hebrew, not carried over from Amos.

**Form kept:**
- *The word of the LORD that came to…* (1:1), as Hosea 1:1. God's *hinneh* + participle is *about to* (1:3).
- Doubled verb to plain English: *bakho al tivku*, **do not weep at all** (1:10).
- Read forms followed: 1:3 *bamotei*; 1:8 *sholal* (note); 1:10 *hitpallashi*, **roll yourself**, where the written form is *I have rolled* (note).
- Town names kept as names, with their wordplay in the notes (1:10–15).
- Kept the same as other books: *about to come out from His place* (1:3), as Isaiah 26:21; *tread on the heights of the earth* (1:3), as Amos 4:13; *all that fills it* (1:2), as Psalm 24:1; *a prostitute's wages* (1:7), as Hosea 9:1; *stripped* (1:8), as Job 12:17; *jackals… ostriches* (1:8), as Job 30:29; *incurable* (1:9), as Jeremiah 15:18; *Tell it not in Gath* (1:10), as 2 Samuel 1:20; *the daughter of Zion* (1:13), as Isaiah 1:8; *a deceitful brook* (1:14), as Jeremiah 15:18.
- 1:1 is prose; the rest is set as poetry. No sentence starts with *And*.

**Echoes in the notes, checked against this project's English where quoted:**
- Deuteronomy 14:1, 22:23, 23:18; 1 Samuel 22:1; 2 Samuel 1:20; 1 Kings 9:16, 22:8, 22:28; Job 12:17, 30:29; Psalm 24:1, 97:5; Isaiah 20:2, 26:21; Jeremiah 15:18, 26:17–18; Hosea 1:1, 8:1, 9:1; Amos 4:13, 8:10.

**Checked against the sources:**
- Hebrew: *shim'u ammim kullam* in 1 Kings 22:28 and Micah 1:2; *Mikhayah* written, *Mikhah* read, in Jeremiah 26:18; *bamotei arets* (1:3) and *bamot Yehudah* (1:5); *etnan* three times in 1:7; the written and read forms above.
- Swete's Greek: Micah 1:1–16; *Meichaias* in 1 Kings 22:8, 28 and Micah 1:1.

**The checker** found 1 error, now fixed: v5 began with *And* (*u-mi*); it now reads *What are the high places of Judah?*

**What this chapter is:**
- **1:** Micah of Moresheth, prophesying under three kings of Judah, calls all peoples to hear: the LORD is coming down from His temple, and the mountains melt, for the sins of Samaria and Jerusalem. Samaria will become a heap of stones in a vineyard plot, her idols broken. The prophet mourns, stripped like a captive, because the wound has reached Jerusalem. Then a lament over the towns of Judah's lowland, each town's name turned into its sentence, ending with their children gone into exile.
