**What these chapters are:**
- **2:** woe to the powerful who plan at night and seize fields and houses in the morning; the LORD plans disaster for them in return, and they will lose their own share of the land. They tell the prophets not to preach, and would welcome a preacher of wine. Then, suddenly, a promise: the LORD will gather the remnant like a flock, and the one who breaks through will lead them out, with their king and the LORD at their head.
- **3:** Micah turns on the rulers who devour the people like meat in a pot, and on prophets who preach peace for a meal and war on those who will not feed them. Micah alone is full of the Spirit of the LORD. Because rulers, priests and prophets all work for pay and still say *Is not the LORD among us?*, Zion will be plowed like a field.
- **4:** in the last days the mountain of the LORD's house will rise above all; the nations will come to be taught, beat their swords into plowshares, and sit each under his vine and fig tree. The LORD will gather the lame and the scattered and reign on Zion. For now Zion writhes like a woman in labor and will go to Babylon, and there be redeemed; the nations gathered against her are sheaves for her threshing.
- **5:** with Jerusalem under siege and its judge struck on the cheek, the LORD promises a ruler from little Bethlehem, whose origins are from of old. He will shepherd in the LORD's strength, and he will be peace when Assyria comes. The remnant of Jacob will be like dew and like a lion among the nations. On that day the LORD will cut off everything Israel trusted instead of Him: horses, fortresses, sorcery, idols.

**Choices for you:**
- **4:9, *ha-melekh ein bakh***
  - (a) **Is there no king in you?** (now): Jerusalem's own king, beside *your counselor*; the next lines strike *the judge of Israel* (5:1), a man.
  - (b) **Is there no King in you?**: the LORD, named as reigning on Mount Zion just before (4:7), as the exiles ask in Jeremiah, *Is the LORD not in Zion? Is her King not in her?* (Jeremiah 8:19).
  - Recommend (a): the king stands with a counselor, and the siege that follows strikes a human judge; the note gives the other reading.
- **5:1, *titgodedi bat gedud***
  - (a) **Now gather in your troops, daughter of troops** (now): *gadad* as in *trooped to the house of a prostitute* (Jeremiah 5:7), the city mustering under siege.
  - (b) **Now gash yourself, daughter of troops**: *gadad* as in *You shall not gash yourselves* (Deuteronomy 14:1), the city in mourning, as in the shaved heads of 1:16.
  - Recommend (a): *daughter of troops* and *a siege is laid against us* make it war; the note gives both.
- **5:5, *ve-hayah zeh shalom***
  - (a) **This one will be peace** (now): the ruler of vv2–4 is himself the peace.
  - (b) **This will be the peace:** what follows, the shepherds raised against Assyria.
  - Recommend (a): the verse follows the ruler of v4 without a break, and *zeh*, **this one**, points back to him.
