# New Chapters: 4 Baruch

Reports on newly rendered chapters, newest at the top. Format: CLAUDE.md, section 5.

## 4 Baruch 5–9 — rendered 2026-10-20, 10:52 PM CDT

**Source:** the Greek (Online Critical Pseudepigrapha) is the base text, as in 1–4; the Ge'ez (Dillmann 1866, machine-read) was checked for names, numbers and the shape of each passage, and is never quoted. No departures from the Greek. **This finishes the book**: five chapters, the rest of its nine. The style matches 1–4.

**Where the witnesses differ (checked in the Ge'ez):**
- Agreeing: *sixty-six years* (5:2, 29; 6:8; 7:28); *fifteen days* (6:16); *Samaria* (8:11); the tenth day (9:2); *twelve* apostles (9:20); Jesus named, as Messiah and Son of God (9:14).
- Differing: 5:34, the Ge'ez also gives the day, *the twelfth* of Nisan; 9:1, the feast lasts *seven* days, not nine; 9:15, the span is *three hundred and three weeks of days*, not 477 years. Each is in a note.
- 6:19–25: the Ge'ez main text stops where Baruch's letter begins; its editor printed the letter in a note as corrupt. The Greek letter is followed.
- The checker's length flags (5:7; 6:2, 4; 7:1, 2, 11; 9:9, 26) are the Ge'ez being shorter; none changes what happens.

**Form kept:**
- *Ekstasis* is *trance* all four times in chapter 5, since the old man's word in v28 answers Abimelech's in vv8, 12, 14.
- *Ho hikanos* (6:6) is *the Almighty*, as the Greek of Ruth 1:20 uses it for *Shaddai*.
- *Iēsous Christos* (9:14) is *Jesus the Messiah*, and *ho christos autou* (9:19) *His Messiah*, as in Matthew. Capital pronouns for God and for Jesus throughout 9:14–21.
- *Aviv* for *Abib* (5:34), the fixed term.
- Every *idou* cut (5:29; 6:2, 8, 15; 9:12, 30). Doubled verbs made plain: 9:3 *ēuxato euchēn* (*He prayed*); 9:10 *eklausan klauthmon pikron* (*wept bitterly*); 9:22 *lithois lithobolēsōmen* (*stone him*).
- No sentence starts with *And*. Repeated phrases kept identical with 1–4: *the estate of Agrippa*, *the sick among the people*, *Father Jeremiah*, *chosen one of God*.
- Matched to this project's English: Genesis 7:11 *the lattices of the heavens* (5:21, the same Greek); Psalm 137:4 *How could we sing… in a foreign land?* (7:34); Deuteronomy 5:32 (7:11); Matthew 5:12 *Rejoice and be glad* (6:20).

**Checker CHECKs, standing:** 9:20 and 9:21, *coming into the world*. The world is not a place one enters or arrives in; the phrase is the idiom the spec keeps (*came into being*).

**Echoes in the notes, checked against this project's English where quoted:**
- Genesis 2:21 (and its Greek), 7:11, 8:7, 8:11; Exodus 13:4, 19:4; Deuteronomy 5:32; Judges 7:4; Ruth 1:20; 1 Samuel 7:12 (and its Greek); 2 Kings 17:24; Ezra 10:2–3; Tobit 1:17.
- Psalms 81:9 (Hebrew *el zar*), 107:9 (and its Greek), 137:3–4 (and its Greek); Isaiah 1:18, 6:1–3; Jeremiah 11:4, 31:13, 36:10.
- Named but not quoted, not yet rendered: Galatians 4:26.

**Checked against the sources:** the Greek of 4 Baruch 5–9 and the Ge'ez for names and numbers; Swete's Greek of Genesis 2:21, 7:11, 8:7; Ruth 1:20–21; Judges 7:4; 1 Samuel 7:12; Psalms 106:9 and 136:3–4 (Greek numbering); Isaiah 1:18; the Hebrew of Psalm 81:10 (English 81:9).

**The checker** found no errors; the two CHECKs are above.

**What these chapters are:**
- **5:** Abimelech sleeps sixty-six years under a tree, wakes with the figs still fresh, and learns from an old man that the people are in Babylon.
- **6:** an angel brings him to Baruch's tomb; Baruch reads the figs as a sign that the flesh will be kept, prays, and is told an eagle will carry a letter; the letter: whoever keeps Babylon will be shut out of both cities.
- **7:** the eagle flies to Babylon, raises a dead man before the people, and brings Jeremiah's answer back: the hangings, the god Zar, the songs of Zion.
- **8:** the return at the Jordan. Half of those with Babylonian wives refuse to part with them, are turned away from Jerusalem and from Babylon, and build Samaria.
- **9:** Jeremiah offers sacrifice, lies as dead three days, rises and speaks of the coming of Jesus the Messiah; the people stone him, first a stone that takes his likeness, then Jeremiah; Baruch and Abimelech bury him.

**Left standing on purpose:**
- 6:7, *in your virgin faith*: *parthenikos* is the word for a virgin; the English keeps the weight of it.
- 8:12, *your high place*: the sentence does not say whether a place on earth or in heaven is meant; the note gives the range.

**Choices for you:**
- **6:16, *ho genomenos en hymin xenos, aphoristhētō***
  - (a) **Let the stranger who has come among you be set apart** (now): the foreigner living among the exiles, the Babylonian husbands and wives whom 8:3 names.
  - (b) **Whoever among you has become a stranger, let him be set apart**: an Israelite who has become foreign, the way v24 says the one who does not listen *will become a stranger* (*xenos genēsetai*).
  - Recommend (a): at the Jordan (8:3) the line is drawn at the Babylonian spouses, and v17 speaks of being set apart *from Babylon*.

## 4 Baruch 1–4 — rendered 2026-10-20, 5:54 PM CDT

**Source:** the Greek (Online Critical Pseudepigrapha) is the base text, and verse numbers follow it. The other witness is the Ge'ez (Dillmann 1866), held only as uncorrected machine-read text, so it was used, as `SOURCES.md` says, for names, numbers and the shape of each passage, and is never quoted. No departures from the Greek. The first two runs of a new writer: style taken from the Greek, rules from the fixed models. Rendered in the same run as the Letter of Jeremiah (report in `PASTE/renders/38-letter-of-jeremiah.md`).

**Where the witnesses differ (checked in the Ge'ez):**
- 2:1: the Ge'ez begins at the temple, without Jeremiah's run to Baruch (the checker's length flags at 2:1, 2:10, 3:22 and 4:12 are the Ge'ez being shorter; none changes what happens).
- 3:1: the Ge'ez names *the sixth hour*; the Greek says *the hour*.
- 3:12, 21: the Ge'ez also has *Abimelech the Ethiopian*. 4:12: the Ge'ez also has a tomb.

**Form kept:**
- *And it came to pass* at 1:1, which opens the book, with its *that*. Every *idou* cut (3:2, 3:8, 4:1, 4:2).
- *Kyrios* is *the Lord*, *Kyrie pantokrator* *Lord Almighty* (1:6); *angelos* is *angel*, as in the other Greek books.
- God's command at 1:1 and 1:8, and *Speak, My chosen one Jeremiah* at 1:5 and 3:7, are word for word the same each time, as in the Greek.
- *Ta skeuē tēs leitourgias* is *the vessels of the service* each time (3:9, 11, 18).
- No sentence starts with *And* except 1:1.
- Names: *Abimelech*, the form this book and its Ge'ez use; the note gives Jeremiah's *Ebed-melech*.
- Matched to this project's English: Lamentations' Greek opening, *sat… and lamented this lament* (4:7, *sat down and wept this lament*).

**Echoes in the notes, checked against this project's English where quoted:**
- Jeremiah 1:1, 1:18, 9:1, 27:22, 36:18, 38:6, 38:7, 39:17, 52:19; Lamentations 4:12 and the Greek of 1:1; Isaiah 52:7; Ecclesiastes 4:2.
- Named but not quoted, not yet rendered or not in this canon: Joel 2:13; Acts 25:13; 2 Maccabees 2:4–8.

**Checked against the sources:**
- The Greek of 4 Baruch 1–4, and the Ge'ez for names and numbers; Swete's Greek Jeremiah 1:18 (no pillar), 45:6 (*borboros*, the mud), 46:16 (*Abdemelech the Ethiopian*); Greek Lamentations 1:1; Greek 2 Maccabees 2:4–7.

**The checker** found no errors and nothing to look at.

**What these chapters are:**
- **1:** the night before Jerusalem falls, God tells Jeremiah to leave with Baruch: his prayers are what hold the city. Jeremiah asks that God destroy it Himself rather than let Babylon boast.
- **2:** Jeremiah in dust and torn clothes in the temple; Baruch learns the news, and both wait at the altar weeping.
- **3:** at midnight angels with torches stand on the walls. The temple vessels are given to the earth, which swallows them; Abimelech is sent out of the city for figs, to be spared the sight.
- **4:** an angel opens the gate to the Chaldeans; Jeremiah throws the temple keys to the sun and is dragged off to Babylon; Baruch laments and stays in a tomb outside the city.

**Left standing on purpose:**
- 3:14, 21, *Agrippa*: a name of a much later age, kept as the Greek has it; the note says so.

**Choices for you:**
- **3:11, *heōs tēs syneleuseōs tou ēgapēmenou***
  - (a) **until the gathering of the beloved** (now): *the beloved* is the people, as in 4:7, *the beloved people*, and the gathering is their return to the city, promised twice in 3:14–15.
  - (b) **until the coming of the Beloved**: a person, and *syneleusis* his arrival.
  - Recommend (a): the book itself pairs *beloved* with *people* in the next chapter (4:7), and 2 Maccabees 2:7 tells the hiding of the temple's things with the same end in view: the place is to stay hidden until God gathers His people together.
