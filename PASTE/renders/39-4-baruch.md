## 4 Baruch 1–4 — rendered 2026-10-20, 5:54 PM CDT

**Source:** the Greek (Online Critical Pseudepigrapha) is the base text, and verse numbers follow it. The other witness is the Ge'ez (Dillmann 1866), held only as uncorrected machine-read text, so it was used, as `SOURCES.md` says, for names, numbers and the shape of each passage, and is never quoted. No departures from the Greek. The first two runs of a new writer: style taken from the Greek, rules from the fixed models. Rendered in the same run as the Letter of Jeremiah (report in `PASTE/renders/38-letter-of-jeremiah.md`).

**Where the witnesses differ (checked in the Ge'ez):**
- 2:1: the Ge'ez begins at the temple, without Jeremiah's run to Baruch (the checker's length flags at 2:1, 2:10, 3:22 and 4:12 are the Ge'ez being shorter; none changes what happens).
- 3:1: the Ge'ez names *the sixth hour*; the Greek says *the hour*.
- 3:12, 21: the Ge'ez also has *Abimelech the Ethiopian*. 4:12: the Ge'ez also has a tomb.

**Form kept:**
- *And it came to pass* at 1:1, which opens the book, with its *that*. Every *idou* cut (3:2, 3:8, 4:1, 4:2).
- *Kyrios* is *the Lord*, *Kyrie pantokrator* *Lord Almighty* (1:6); *angelos* is *angel*, as in the other Greek books.
- God's command at 1:1 and 1:8, and *Speak, My chosen one Jeremiah* at 1:5 and 3:7, are word for word the same each time, as in the Greek.
- *Ta skeuē tēs leitourgias* is *the vessels of the service* each time (3:9, 11, 18).
- No sentence starts with *And* except 1:1.
- Names: *Abimelech*, the form this book and its Ge'ez use; the note gives Jeremiah's *Ebed-melech*.
- Matched to this project's English: Lamentations' Greek opening, *sat… and lamented this lament* (4:7, *sat down and wept this lament*).

**Echoes in the notes, checked against this project's English where quoted:**
- Jeremiah 1:1, 1:18, 9:1, 27:22, 36:18, 38:6, 38:7, 39:17, 52:19; Lamentations 4:12 and the Greek of 1:1; Isaiah 52:7; Ecclesiastes 4:2.
- Named but not quoted, not yet rendered or not in this canon: Joel 2:13; Acts 25:13; 2 Maccabees 2:4–8.

**Checked against the sources:**
- The Greek of 4 Baruch 1–4, and the Ge'ez for names and numbers; Swete's Greek Jeremiah 1:18 (no pillar), 45:6 (*borboros*, the mud), 46:16 (*Abdemelech the Ethiopian*); Greek Lamentations 1:1; Greek 2 Maccabees 2:4–7.

**The checker** found no errors and nothing to look at.

**What these chapters are:**
- **1:** the night before Jerusalem falls, God tells Jeremiah to leave with Baruch: his prayers are what hold the city. Jeremiah asks that God destroy it Himself rather than let Babylon boast.
- **2:** Jeremiah in dust and torn clothes in the temple; Baruch learns the news, and both wait at the altar weeping.
- **3:** at midnight angels with torches stand on the walls. The temple vessels are given to the earth, which swallows them; Abimelech is sent out of the city for figs, to be spared the sight.
- **4:** an angel opens the gate to the Chaldeans; Jeremiah throws the temple keys to the sun and is dragged off to Babylon; Baruch laments and stays in a tomb outside the city.

**Left standing on purpose:**
- 3:14, 21, *Agrippa*: a name of a much later age, kept as the Greek has it; the note says so.

**Choices for you:**
- **3:11, *heōs tēs syneleuseōs tou ēgapēmenou***
  - (a) **until the gathering of the beloved** (now): *the beloved* is the people, as in 4:7, *the beloved people*, and the gathering is their return to the city, promised twice in 3:14–15.
  - (b) **until the coming of the Beloved**: a person, and *syneleusis* his arrival.
  - Recommend (a): the book itself pairs *beloved* with *people* in the next chapter (4:7), and 2 Maccabees 2:7 tells the hiding of the temple's things with the same end in view: the place is to stay hidden until God gathers His people together.
