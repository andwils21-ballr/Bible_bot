**What these chapters are:**
- **1:** Paul in chains thanks God for the Philippians' partnership in the gospel, and rejoices that Christ is preached even by rivals. *For to me, to live is Christ, and to die is gain*: he would rather depart and be with Christ, but will stay for their sake. Live as citizens worthy of the gospel.
- **2:** *Have this mind among yourselves*: Christ, in the form of God, emptied Himself, took the form of a slave, humbled Himself to death on a cross, and God exalted Him, *so that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow*. Work out your salvation, for God is at work in you; shine as lights in the world. Timothy will come; Epaphroditus, who nearly died, goes home to them.
- **3:** *Look out for the dogs.* Paul had every reason for confidence in the flesh, and counts it all as loss to gain Christ and the righteousness from God. He presses on toward the prize; *our citizenship is in heaven*, and Christ will transform our lowly body to be like His.
- **4:** *Rejoice in the Lord always.* Do not be anxious; the peace of God will guard you; think on whatever is true, honorable, just, pure. Paul thanks them for their gift: *I have learned to be content… I can do all things through Him who strengthens me.*

**Choices for you:**
- **2:5, *touto phroneite en hymin ho kai en Christō Iēsou***
  - (a) **Have this mind among yourselves, which was also in Christ Jesus** (now): the mind Christ had, shown in vv6–8.
  - (b) **Have this mind among yourselves, which is yours in Christ Jesus**: the mind that belongs to those who are in Him.
  - Recommend (a): the hymn that follows tells what Christ Jesus thought and did, and the *humility* asked in v3 is His *He humbled Himself* in v8; the note gives both.
- **2:6, *ouch harpagmon hēgēsato to einai isa theō***
  - (a) **did not count equality with God a thing to be seized** (now): the Greek's own picture, which holds both readings.
  - (b) **did not count equality with God something to be used for His own advantage**: it was His already, and He did not hold onto it.
  - Recommend (a): *harpagmos* is from *harpazō*, *seize*, and (a) keeps the word without choosing between the readings; the note gives both.
- **2:7, *morphēn doulou labōn***
  - (a) **taking the form of a slave** (now): the lowest place set against *the form of God*.
  - (b) **taking the form of a servant**: Isaiah's *servant*, and the King James wording.
  - Recommend (a): Paul's word is *doulos*, the word he uses for slaves in a household (Ephesians 6:5); the Greek of Isaiah 52:13 calls the servant *pais*, another word; the note gives the Isaiah echo.
