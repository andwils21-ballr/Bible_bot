**What these chapters are:**
- **3:** the LORD sends Jonah again, and this time he goes. He walks a day into the city and cries *Forty more days, and Nineveh is overturned!* The people believe God and fast, from the greatest to the least; the king leaves his throne for sackcloth and ashes and orders everyone, even the animals, to fast, cry to God and turn from violence: *Who knows? God may turn and relent.* God sees them turn, and relents of the evil He had said He would do.
- **4:** Jonah is furious: this is why he ran, he says, because he knew God is gracious and merciful; he would rather die. He sits east of the city to watch. God makes a plant grow to shade him, then sends a worm to kill it and a scorching east wind, and Jonah again asks to die. God answers: you pitied a plant you did not grow; should I not pity Nineveh, with its hundred and twenty thousand people who do not know their right hand from their left, and its many animals? The book ends on that question.

**Choices for you:**
- **3:3, *ir gedolah le-Elohim***
  - (a) **a great city to God** (now): word for word; it can carry both senses, very great, and great in God's eyes, which is where the book ends (4:11).
  - (b) **an exceedingly great city**: *of God* used to make a word as great as it can be, as in *a prince of God* (Genesis 23:6).
  - Recommend (a): this project keeps the same idiom word for word in Genesis 23:6 and Psalm 36:6, and 4:11 gives the second sense its weight; the note gives both.
- **4:4, 9, *ha-heitev charah lakh***
  - (a) **Is it right for you to be angry?… It is right for me to be angry, even to death** (now): *heitev*, **do well**, as in *if you do well* (Genesis 4:7).
  - (b) **Are you very angry?… I am very angry, even to death**: *heitev* as **greatly**, as the Greek takes it (*Are you very grieved?*).
  - Recommend (a): the LORD asks Jonah to judge his own anger, as He asked Cain, and Jonah's reply turns the question back; the note gives the Greek.

**What these chapters are:**
- **1:** the LORD sends Jonah to Nineveh, the great city of Assyria, to cry out against its evil. Jonah runs the other way, down to the port of Joppa and onto a ship bound for Tarshish, and goes to sleep below deck. The LORD hurls a storm at the sea. The pagan sailors pray and throw the cargo overboard; the lot falls on Jonah, who tells them he fears the LORD who made the sea, and asks them to throw him in. They try to row to shore, pray not to be blamed for his death, and throw him overboard. The sea goes calm, and the sailors fear the LORD and make vows. A great fish swallows Jonah.
- **2:** from inside the fish Jonah prays a psalm, made largely of the words of the Psalms: he went down to the bottom of the sea, the waters closed over him, and the LORD brought his life up from the pit. He promises sacrifice and vows: *Salvation belongs to the LORD!* The fish spits him out on dry ground.
