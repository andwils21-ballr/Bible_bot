## Job 6–9 — rendered 2026-10-07, 1:41 PM CDT

**Source:** Hebrew (Westminster Leningrad Codex).

**What these chapters are:** Job answers Eliphaz: his friends are like a wadi that runs dry when it is needed (ch. 6); man's life is hard service, "What is man, that You make so much of him?" (ch. 7). Bildad: God does not pervert justice; ask the fathers (ch. 8). Job: how can a man win his case with God? There is no arbiter between us (ch. 9).

**Written and read forms that differ in meaning (read form followed, with a note):**
- 6:21 written "not" (you have become nothing), read "to it" (you have become like that).
- 6:29 written "my turning back," read "turn back."
Spelling only, no note: 6:2, 7:1, 7:5, 9:13, 9:30.

**Noted, open to you:**
- 7:20 "a burden to myself": the scribes' tradition says the original was "to You," which the Greek has. Rendered as the Hebrew stands.
- 9:19 "who will summon me?": the Hebrew has "me"; the Greek reads it of God.
- 9:33 "There is no arbiter": the Greek has "Would that there were."
- 6:14 the sense of the line is uncertain; the other reading is in the note.

**Word choice:** 9:31 *ti'avuni* is "abhor," keeping "detestable" for *to'evah* and "loathe" for *sheqets* as the word table has them; 7:16 and 9:21 "loathe" are *ma'as*, the ordinary verb, and are not the table's word.

## Job 2–5 — rendered 2026-10-07, 1:37 PM CDT

**Source:** Hebrew (Westminster Leningrad Codex). Written and read forms differ at 2:7 (*ad* written twice) and 5:18; spelling only, read form followed, no note.

**What these chapters are:** the second test and Job's wife; the three friends sit in silence seven days (ch. 2). Job curses the day of his birth (ch. 3). Eliphaz's first speech: the innocent do not perish; a voice in the night says no one is pure before God (ch. 4); seek God, and do not reject the discipline of the Almighty (ch. 5).

**Form:** chapter 3 onward is poetry, set in lines with the trailing double space. The speeches are not put in quotation marks; a quotation inside a speech is (4:17–21).

**"And it came to pass" kept:** 2:1, *one day*, as at 1:6.

**Decisions made, open to you:**
- **2:9 "Curse God and die"** — Hebrew *barekh*, "bless," rendered by the sense as at 1:5 and 1:11.
- **3:10 "my mother's womb"** for Hebrew "my womb," with a note.
- **4:18 "His messengers"** for *mal'akhav*, heavenly beings here; the word table's rule (messenger, never angel) followed.
- **5:17 "the Almighty"** for *Shaddai*, first time in the book, with a note.
- **Eloah** (singular) is "God," noted at 3:4.

**Read with other vowels? Left as written, with a note:** 3:8 "day" (or "sea"); 3:22 "exulting" (or "the grave mound"); 4:17 "before God" (or "more than God"); 5:5 "the snare" (or "the thirsty," as the Greek has it).

## Job 1 — rendered 2026-10-07, 1:34 PM CDT

**Source:** Hebrew (Westminster Leningrad Codex). Written and read forms differ in 1:4, 1:10 and 1:21; all spelling only, read form followed, no note.

**What this chapter is:** Job's goodness and wealth; the accuser's question, "Is it for nothing that Job fears God?"; four messengers in one day; Job's answer, "The LORD gave, and the LORD has taken."

**"And it came to pass" kept:** 1:6 and 1:13, both *one day*, a new time (spec rule 3). 1:5 *va-yehi ki* is inside the running scene and is cut.

**Decisions made, open to you:**
- **"cursed God" (1:5, 1:11)** for Hebrew *berakh*, "blessed," the scribes' polite substitute. Rendered by the sense, with a note; 2:5 and 2:9 will do the same.
- **"Satan"** for *ha-satan*, "the accuser," with the article. Kept as the name readers know, with a note that it is a title here.
- **"the young people" (1:19)** for *ha-ne'arim*, which is "the young men" (servants) in 1:15–17.
- **v21** set as verse lines, as the one poem in the prose opening.
