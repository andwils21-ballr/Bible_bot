## John 1–2 — rendered 2026-11-03, 1:43 PM CST

**Source:** Greek (SBLGNT). The edition's readings are followed: its punctuation of 1:3–4, *monogenēs theos* (1:18) and *ho eklektos* (1:34), both marked as places where manuscripts differ, and the notes say so. This is the first chapter of John, so its style is set here, from Luke and the spec: John's Greek is plain and repeats its words on purpose (*was… came into being*, *witness*, *light*, *remain*, *believe*), and the English keeps them the same.

**Form kept:**
- The prologue (1:1–18) set as prose, as the verses run, with v15 kept in parentheses as the edition has it.
- No sentence starts with *And*: John's *kai* at the head of a sentence is cut, or made *yet*, *but* or *so*.
- *Ide*, **look**, kept where John points at Jesus (1:29, 36): *Look: the Lamb of God*; cutting it would lose the pointing. *Ide* in 1:47 is *Here truly is*.
- *Amēn amēn* as *Truly, truly* (1:51).
- Received names: *Cephas*, *Peter*, *Nathanael*, *Cana*, *Bethsaida*.
- John's own glosses kept: *Rabbi (which translated means Teacher)*, *Messiah (which translated means Christ)*, *Cephas (which is translated Peter)*.
- *Naos* is *sanctuary* (2:19–21), as Mark 14:58; *hieron* is *temple* (2:14–15).
- Fixed terms: *angels* (1:51), against *Messengers of God* in Genesis 28:12, which the note quotes; *the Holy Spirit* (1:33); *Messiah* for *christos* (1:20, 25).
- Capital pronouns for the Word from 1:2.

**What these chapters are:**
- **1:** *In the beginning was the Word*: the Word with God and God, through whom all things came to be, the light the darkness has not overcome. John comes as a witness; the Word becomes flesh and pitches His tent among us. John tells the priests he is not the Messiah, Elijah or the Prophet, and points to Jesus, *the Lamb of God*. Andrew, Simon (named Cephas), Philip and Nathanael follow Him, and He promises they will see heaven opened.
- **2:** at a wedding in Cana, Jesus turns six jars of water into wine, *the first of His signs*. At Passover He drives the traders out of the temple, *My Father's house*, and says *Destroy this sanctuary, and in three days I will raise it up*, speaking of His body.

**Choices for you:**
- **1:3–4, *ho gegonen***
  - (a) **All things came into being through Him, and without Him not one thing came into being. What has come into being in Him was life** (now): the edition's punctuation; what was made has its life in Him.
  - (b) **…and without Him not one thing came into being that has come into being. In Him was life**: the King James division; nothing that exists was made without Him.
  - Recommend (a): it is the edition's reading, and it keeps the verses' own chain, *came into being… came into being… has come into being*; the note gives (b).
- **1:5, *ou katelaben***
  - (a) **the darkness has not overcome it** (now): *katalambanō* as seize, overpower; the light keeps shining.
  - (b) **the darkness has not grasped it**: understood it, as the King James *comprehended*.
  - Recommend (a): this book uses the verb again of darkness overtaking a man (12:35), and the next verses tell of the world that would not receive the light; the note gives (b).
- **1:9, *erchomenon eis ton kosmon***
  - (a) **The true light, which gives light to everyone, was coming into the world** (now): the light is coming.
  - (b) **The true light, which gives light to everyone coming into the world, was there**: each person is coming into the world.
  - Recommend (a): v10–11 go on to say *He was in the world… He came to His own*; the note gives (b).
- **1:18, *monogenēs theos***
  - (a) **the only Son, who is God** (now): *monogenēs* as in v14, *the only Son*, and *theos* as what He is.
  - (b) **the only God, who is at the Father's side**: word for word.
  - Recommend (a): v14 has just used *monogenēs* of the Son *from the Father*, and (b) can read as if the Father were not God; the note gives the word for word.
