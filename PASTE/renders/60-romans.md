**What these chapters are:**
- **5:** justified by faith, we have *peace with God* and boast in hope, even in suffering, because *while we were still sinners, Christ died for us*. Then Adam and Christ: sin and death came through one man to all; grace and life come through one man to *the many*; *where sin increased, grace overflowed all the more*.
- **6:** shall we go on sinning? *By no means!* Baptized into Christ's death, buried and raised *with Him*, believers are to *reckon* themselves dead to sin. Everyone serves a master; *the wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life*.
- **7:** death releases a wife from her husband's law, and believers have died to the Law. The Law is holy, but sin seized its chance through the commandment and killed; the *I* does what it hates, and cries *Wretched man that I am!*
- **8:** *no condemnation* for those in Christ. The Spirit gives life, makes us children who cry *Abba! Father!*, groans with the groaning creation, and intercedes. Those God foreknew He appointed to the image of His Son, called, justified, glorified, and nothing *in all creation* can separate them *from the love of God in Christ Jesus our Lord*.

**Choices for you:**
- **5:12, *eph' hō pantes hēmarton***
  - (a) **because all sinned** (now): each person sins, and so each dies.
  - (b) **in whom all sinned**: all sinned in Adam, *the one man*.
  - Recommend (a): Paul's other uses of *eph' hō* are *because* or *for which* (2 Corinthians 5:4; Philippians 3:12; 4:10). The note gives both.
- **8:3, *peri hamartias***
  - (a) **as an offering for sin** (now): the Greek Law's term for the sin offering.
  - (b) **for sin** / **to deal with sin**: sin as what the sending dealt with, with no sacrifice named.
  - Recommend (a): it is the sin offering's name in Leviticus (5:7) and the servant's in the Greek of Isaiah 53:10, and *condemned sin in the flesh* is what an offering does.
- **8:20, *dia ton hypotaxanta***
  - (a) **because of Him who subjected it** (now): God, who cursed the ground *in hope* (Genesis 3:17).
  - (b) **because of him who subjected it**: Adam, *on your account*.
  - Recommend (a): *in hope* belongs to the one who did the subjecting, and only God subjects in hope.
- **8:28, *panta synergei eis agathon***
  - (a) **all things work together for good** (now): *all things* is the subject.
  - (b) **He works in all things for good**: the Spirit of vv26–27 (or God) is the subject.
  - Recommend (a): with no subject named, *panta* is the natural subject of *synergei*; (b) is good Greek and fits the Spirit of v27.
- **8:29–30, *proōrisen***
  - (a) **appointed beforehand** (now): *horizō* of 1:4 with *pro*, *before*, so the Son *appointed* and the many *appointed beforehand* to His image share one word.
  - (b) **predestined**: the word most English Bibles have.
  - Recommend (a): it keeps the link to 1:4, and Acts 4:28 in this project already has *decided beforehand* for the same verb, not *predestined*.

## Romans 1–4 — rendered 2026-11-07, 2:20 PM CST

**Landed:** Romans 1, 2, 3, 4. Progress 1,395 → **1,399 of 1,989 (70.3%)**. John 8 is still held (see the John report).

**Widespread decisions** (they reach Galatians, Philippians and every letter after; listed first so they are settled before they spread):

- **3:22, 26, *pistis Iēsou Christou*** (also Galatians 2:16, 20; 3:22; Philippians 3:9)
  - (a) **faith in Jesus Christ** (now): the believer's faith, with Christ as its object.
  - (b) **the faithfulness of Jesus Christ**: Christ's own faithfulness, through which God's righteousness comes *for all who believe*.
  - Recommend (a): in Galatians 2:16 Paul uses the phrase twice and puts *we believed in Christ Jesus* between them. (b) is good Greek, and *pistis* is *faithfulness* in 3:3; the 3:22, 26 note gives both.
- ***dikaioō*** (2:13; 3:4, 20, 24, 26, 28, 30; 4:2, 5; already *justified* in Luke 18:14 and Acts 13:38–39)
  - (a) **justify / justified** (now): the word of Luke and Acts; the notes show it is one word family with *righteous* (2:13) and that it is a court word (3:4).
  - (b) **declare righteous**: says outright what the court sense means.
  - (c) **make righteous**: righteousness given, not only declared.
  - Recommend (a): it keeps the word English readers know, and the Greek itself does not settle (b) against (c); 3:4, where the one *justified* is God, shows the court sense, and the notes carry it.

**Source:** Greek (SBLGNT). The edition's readings are followed. A new writer: the style is taken from Paul's Greek (long sentences that build clause on clause, questions answered *By no means!*, quotations strung together), and the rules from the fixed models.

**What these chapters are:**
- **1:** Paul greets the believers in Rome and names his gospel: *the righteousness of God is revealed from faith to faith*. Then the other revelation, the wrath of God: humankind knew God, did not glorify Him, and *exchanged the glory of the immortal God* for images; three times *God handed them over*.
- **2:** the one who judges them is *without excuse* too. God *will repay each one according to his deeds*, Jew and Greek alike. Gentiles who do what the Law requires show it *written on their hearts*; the one who teaches the Law and breaks it dishonors God; the true circumcision is *of the heart*.
- **3:** God stays faithful though Israel is unfaithful. The Psalms say *there is no one righteous*. *But now* the righteousness of God is made known apart from the Law, through faith, for all; God set Christ forth as *an atonement cover*, so that He is *righteous and the one who justifies* the one who has faith. Boasting is shut out, and *God is one*, God of Jews and Gentiles.
- **4:** Abraham *believed God, and it was reckoned to him as righteousness*, before he was circumcised, so he is father of all who believe. The promise rests on faith, so that it is grace. Against hope he believed the God *who gives life to the dead*, and it was written *for us also*.

**Choices for you:**
- **1:4, *horisthentos***
  - (a) **appointed Son of God in power** (now): the resurrection marks Him out as Son *in power*; He is already *His Son* in v3.
  - (b) **declared to be the Son of God with power**: the resurrection shows what He already was.
  - Recommend (a): *horizō* is *appoint* in Acts 10:42 and 17:31, the second also tied to the resurrection; *in power* keeps it from meaning He became Son only then.
- **1:17, *ho dikaios ek pisteōs zēsetai***
  - (a) **The righteous will live by faith** (now): faith is how the righteous one lives.
  - (b) **The one who is righteous by faith will live**: faith is how one becomes righteous, and life follows.
  - Recommend (a): Habakkuk's Hebrew and Greek both join *by faith* to *live*, and Paul sets it beside *shall live by them* in Galatians 3:11–12.
- **2:14, *physei***
  - (a) **Gentiles, who do not have the Law by birth, do what the Law requires** (now): Gentiles who keep the Law, as the believers of v29.
  - (b) **Gentiles, who do not have the Law, do by nature what the Law requires**: a law known to every conscience.
  - Recommend (a): Paul uses *physei* of birth in Galatians 2:15, and *ek physeōs* is *by birth* in v27 of this chapter.
- **3:25, *hilastērion***
  - (a) **an atonement cover** (now): the gold cover of the ark (Exodus 25:17), the word's use in the Greek Law and in Hebrews 9:5.
  - (b) **a propitiation**: a means of turning away wrath.
  - (c) **a sacrifice of atonement**: the offering itself.
  - Recommend (a): sixteen of the twenty-one verses that use it in the Greek Old Testament name that cover, and *by His blood* is the Day of Atonement (Leviticus 16:14–15).
- **4:17, *kalountos ta mē onta hōs onta***
  - (a) **calls into being what does not exist** (now): the Creator's call.
  - (b) **speaks of what does not exist as though it did**: God naming Abraham *father of many nations* before he had a son.
  - Recommend (a): both halves of the line are things God does by power, *gives life to the dead* and *calls into being*; (b) is the point of the next verse.
