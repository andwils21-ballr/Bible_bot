## Ezekiel 5–8 — rendered 2026-10-21, 12:49 PM CDT

**Source:** Hebrew (WLC), with one departure: **8:2**, *a man* for *fire*, from the Greek (*andros*; Hebrew *ish* for *esh*, the same letters), because the verse goes on to *His waist* and the figure is the one of 1:26–27; the note quotes the Hebrew (choice below). Swete's Greek checked and quoted where it differs: 5:7 (agrees, *not*); 6:9 (*I have sworn*); 6:14 (agrees, *Deblatha*); 7:2–9 (another order, no *doom*); 7:11; 8:14 (*Thammouz*); 8:17 (*like people sneering*). Verse counts match. Style follows Ezekiel 1–4.

**Form kept:**
- *And it came to pass* at 8:1 (a new date). *The word of the LORD came to me* (6:1; 7:1) plain.
- Every *hinneh* cut (7:5, 6, 10; 8:2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 10, 14, 16, 17). *Hineni alayikh gam ani* (5:8) is *I Myself am against you*; *hineni ani mevi* (6:3) *I Myself am about to bring*; *gam ani* with an imperfect is *I in turn* (5:11) and *I, too* (8:18).
- Chapter 7 set as verse lines: a poem of short cries (*An end! The end has come*).
- Repeated words kept: *you will know that I am the LORD* (6:7, 10, 13, 14; 7:4, 9, 27); *My eye will not spare, and I will have no pity* (5:11; 7:4, 9; 8:18); *I, the LORD, have spoken* (5:13, 15, 17); *detestable* (*to'evah*), *loathsome* (*shiqquts*, *sheqets*, 5:11; 7:20; 8:10), as fixed.
- *Zanah* of worship is *gave itself to others* / *gave themselves to* (6:9), as fixed. *Re'ach nichoach* (6:13) is *soothing aroma*.
- Read forms followed, no note: 6:3 *ve-la-ge'ayot*; 7:2 *arba*; 7:21 *ve-chillelhu*; 8:6 *mah hem*.
- No sentence starts with *And* except the formula.
- Matched to this project's English: Leviticus 26:22, 25, 26, 29, 30 (5:10, 16–17; 6:3–5); Numbers 17:8 *sprouted… put out blossoms* (7:10); Leviticus 25:13 (7:13); Lamentations 1:17 *an unclean thing* (7:19–20); Exodus 33:4 *ornaments* (7:20); Jeremiah 18:18 *the law will perish from the priest* (7:26).

- Swete's Greek: Ezekiel 5:1–8:18.

**The checker** found no errors and nothing to look at.

**What these chapters are:**
- **5:** Ezekiel shaves with a sword and divides his hair in thirds: Jerusalem burned, cut down, scattered, by the curses of Leviticus 26.
- **6:** against the mountains of Israel and their high places; a remnant will remember how God was broken by their faithlessness.
- **7:** a poem of the end: *An end! The end has come*; buying and selling stop, gold becomes unclean, the law perishes from the priest.
- **8:** taken by a lock of his hair to Jerusalem, he is shown four detestable things in the temple: the image of jealousy, seventy elders before wall carvings, women weeping for Tammuz, and men bowing to the sun.

**Left standing on purpose:**
- 6:14, *Diblah*: a place named only here; the Greek has the same name, and where it was is not known.
- 7:7, 10, *Your doom*: the sense of *tsefirah* is not settled; the note gives the range.
- 7:11: four short phrases with no verb; the English keeps them as short as the Hebrew.
- 8:17, *putting the branch to their nose*: what the act was is not known.

**Choices for you:**
- **8:2, *demut ke-mar'eh esh***
  - (a) **a likeness like the appearance of a man** (now): the Greek, *andros*; the same Hebrew letters read *ish*. The verse goes on to *His waist*, and the figure is the one of 1:26–27.
  - (b) **a likeness like the appearance of fire**: the Hebrew as pointed; fire is then the whole figure, before the verse divides it at the waist.
  - Recommend (a): the waist needs a body, and 1:26–27 describes this same sight as *a likeness like the appearance of a human*.

## Ezekiel 1–4 — rendered 2026-10-21, 7:50 AM CDT

**Source:** Hebrew (WLC) throughout, with no departures. Swete's Greek checked verse by verse and quoted in notes where it differs: 1:4, 27 (*ēlektron*); 1:14 (lacking); 1:24 (shorter); 3:12 (agrees, *Blessed*); 4:5, 9 (*one hundred and ninety days*). Verse counts match the Hebrew, and Hebrew and English number them alike. A new writer's first run: style taken from the source, rules from the fixed models. No Part 2 section for Ezekiel.

**Form kept:**
- *And it came to pass* at 1:1 (opens the book, with its *that*) and 3:16 (*at the end of seven days*, a new time). *Va-yehi* cut as *came* at 1:3 and 1:25.
- Every *hinneh* cut (1:4, 15; 2:9 twice; 3:8, 23, 25; 4:8, 14); 4:16 *hineni shover* is *I am about to break*.
- *Adonai YHWH* is *the Lord GOD*, as in Isaiah and Jeremiah; *koh amar* is *This is what the Lord GOD says* (2:4; 3:11, 27).
- *Ben adam* is *son of man*, lowercase, God's address to a human, as in Psalm 80:17. *Shaddai* (1:24) is *the Almighty*.
- Ezekiel's repeated words kept the same: *mar'eh* *appearance* and *demut* *likeness* through chapter 1 (the 1:28 note is built on them); *beit meri* *a rebellious house* (2:5, 6, 8; 3:9, 26, 27); *whether they hear or refuse* (2:5, 7; 3:11); *chazaq* *hard* (2:4; 3:7–9) and *strong* (3:14).
- *Nasa avon* is *carry the guilt* in chapter 4, as in Leviticus 7:18, 17:16 and Numbers 14:34; *avon* in the watchman speech (3:18–19) is *iniquity*.
- Doubled verbs: 1:3 *hayoh hayah* (*came*); 3:18 *mot tamut* (*You shall surely die*, as Numbers 26:65 has it); 3:21 *chayo yichyeh* (*he shall surely live*).
- Read forms followed, no note (same meaning): 1:8 *vide*; 3:15 *va-eshev*; 4:6 *ha-yemani*; 4:15 *tsefi'ei*.
- No sentence starts with *And* except the two formulas.
- Matched to this project's English: Genesis 1:6 *expanse*; Exodus 24:10 *sapphire*; Exodus 28:20 *beryl* (*tarshish*, 1:16); Genesis 9:13 *the bow… in the cloud*; Jeremiah 17:1 *diamond*; Isaiah 33:19 *speech too deep to understand*; Jeremiah 6:21 *lay stumbling blocks*; Leviticus 17:15 *an animal that died on its own or was torn by beasts*; Leviticus 7:18 *foul*; Leviticus 26:26 *staff of bread*, *by weight*; Leviticus 26:39 *rot away in their guilt*.

**Echoes in the notes, checked against this project's English where quoted:**
- Genesis 1:6, 9:13; Exodus 4:10, 24:10, 32:15; Leviticus 7:18, 17:15, 26:26, 26:39; Numbers 14:34; 2 Kings 24:12–15 (named); Job 2:13; Psalm 119:103; Isaiah 33:19; Jeremiah 1:6, 6:17, 6:21, 15:16, 17:1.
- Later in Ezekiel, named not quoted: 8:2; 10:14, 20; 24:27; 28:24; 33:7–9, 22.

**Checked against the sources:**
- Hebrew: *mar'eh* and *demut* in chapter 1 (26 times together); *chashmal* only in Ezekiel; *chazaq* in 3:7–9; *sarabim* only at 2:6; *nasa avon* in 4:4–6.
- Swete's Greek: Ezekiel 1:1–4:17.

**The checker** found no errors and nothing to look at (4:14 *come into my mouth* was changed to *entered*).

**What these chapters are:**
- **1:** by the river Chebar, in exile, Ezekiel sees a storm, four living creatures with wheels full of eyes, and above them a throne, and on it the appearance of the likeness of the glory of the LORD.
- **2:** he is called *son of man* and sent to a rebellious house, and handed a scroll of laments.
- **3:** he eats the scroll; he is made hard against a hard people, made a watchman, then bound and struck silent.
- **4:** he acts out the siege of Jerusalem on a brick, lies on his side 390 and 40 days for the guilt of Israel and Judah, and eats siege bread.

**Left standing on purpose:**
- 1:1, *in the thirtieth year*: the book does not say what the year counts from, and the English does not supply it.

**Choices for you:**
- **Ezekiel's *ruach* with no owner named (2:2; 3:12, 14, 24; and later 8:3, 11:1, 11:24, 43:5)**
  - (a) **the spirit** (now): lowercase, as the spec's fixed term has it for any *ruach* not called *of God* or *of the LORD*. It keeps the link the Hebrew makes with *the spirit* that moves the living creatures and the wheels (1:12, 20).
  - (b) **the Spirit**: capital, since it plainly comes from God; it enters the prophet, sets him on his feet and carries him.
  - Recommend (a): the spec draws the line at the words *of God* or *of the LORD*, which Ezekiel uses when he means it (11:5, 37:1), and the lowercase keeps the one word through chapters 1–3.
- **1:4, 27, *ke-ein ha-chashmal***
  - (a) **something like the gleam of amber** (now): the fossil resin, glowing gold-brown; the Greek *ēlektron* can mean this.
  - (b) **something like the gleam of glowing metal**: a shining alloy of gold and silver, which the same Greek word also means.
  - Recommend (a): it is the traditional English and the Greek allows it; the note gives the range, since the Hebrew word is found only in Ezekiel and what it names is not known.
