The text supports you, word for word:

Creation. The dry ground Israel walks on is yabbashah, the word of Genesis 1:9: let the waters be gathered, and let the dry ground appear. The wind that opens the sea is ruach, the same word as the Spirit over the waters in Genesis 1:2. The deeps of 15:5 and 15:8 are the tehom of Genesis 1:2. The cloud splitting light from darkness in 14:20 is the first day of creation replayed.
The flood. The sea is turned into charavah (14:21), the dry ground where everything died in the flood (Genesis 7:22). And split (14:16) is the flood's verb (Genesis 7:11).
The prophets saw it too. Isaiah 51:10: was it not You who dried up the sea, the waters of the great deep, who made the depths of the sea a road for the redeemed to cross? The great deep is exactly the flood's phrase in Genesis 7:11.
Judgment day. Paul: all were baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea (1 Corinthians 10:2). Revelation 15:3: the saved stand beside a sea and sing the song of Moses, which is this song. Revelation 21:1: a new heaven and a new earth, and the sea is no more.
So the pattern you saw is the one Scripture itself draws: waters split, a people walks through on dry ground, the waters close over those who stand against God, and in the end the sea itself is gone. The Genesis 1 / Genesis 7 word links are now a note in Exodus 14 (vv21–22). The rest stays here in chat.

The purpose of running this by you is to decide if it should go in the notes. After all, it is definitely interesting.
Creation is separating waters with a hard expanse. Then, the Passover exists as a demarcation event that sets God's people down a path towards his Kingdom for the rest of time, on earth, existing between the waters. 
Then, God's people are moving through this life where they are guided by the light, while those people led by evil walk in the dark. When it comes time to pass through the waters and arrive in God's heavenly dwelling, He provides the path. 
Finally, the enemy (Devil) of God is defeated by the hands of God and the people following the devil die and go to hell, while God leads His people out of the expanse to His land of Glory and heavenly dwelling.

Now, compare that to God bringing plagues upon the land of Egypt to free His people and send them into the wilderness, guided by the light. Meanwhile, the enemy travels through the wilderness in the dark. 
Sons of Israel follow the light to find God's path through the waters on dry, hard land, which divides said waters. The sons of Israel follow the path to pass through the waters to the end to find the reward of God's glorious dwelling place.
Meanwhile, the enemy, along with their leader, the Pharaoh, end up lost in the waters. They are driven by greed and anger through the wilderness and through the waters, only to be destroyed by God's crushing power, and thus can't escape the waters.
They die a terrible death and are left on the shore as they go to hell. 

This just feels like a microcosm of God's greater plan.
