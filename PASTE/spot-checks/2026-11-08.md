# Spot Checks

## Romans 13–16 (weekly, 3:10 PM Central, 2026-11-08)

Bible Main's routine cycle `828144b` (2:11 PM CST), re-rendered blind by a fresh helper agent (Opus) from the commit before it, then compared by Bible 2. It is the first spot check of the new Bible Main (session of 2026-11-07), and the first under the notes rule *less is more* (CLAUDE.md §3, 2026-11-07).

**Overall:** both sides pass `check_chapters.py` with no errors and nothing to look at. Of 94 verses, 22 are word for word the same, and most other differences are wording only. Both sides end the letter at 16:24 as the edition does, read *Junia* at 16:7, and keep *servant* for the ruler (13:4) and Christ (15:8). Bible Main raised 16:1 *diakonos*, 16:7 *episēmoi en tois apostolois* and the missing 16:25–27 as choices; the helper raised 16:1 and 16:7 as well. Every quotation in Bible Main's notes was checked against this project's English and against Swete's Isaiah 45:23 (*ὀμεῖται*, *shall swear*); all match.

**Rule breaks, Bible Main:**
- **14:6** *the one who does not eat does not eat for the Lord*: the English says the opposite of the Greek (`ὁ μὴ ἐσθίων κυρίῳ οὐκ ἐσθίει`, he abstains *for* the Lord). Spec, readability pass item 7, *read the English for what it says*. The helper: *abstains for the Lord*.
- **14:21** drops `ἢ σκανδαλίζεται ἢ ἀσθενεῖ`, *or is made to fall or is weakened*, which the edition prints (with its mark for a place where manuscripts differ), and the report does not say so. The helper renders it.

**Words kept the same, or not** (posted to the council):
- **14:23** `ὁ δὲ διακρινόμενος`: Bible Main *whoever has doubts*. It is the verb of Abraham, *No unbelief made him waver* (4:20), and v5 has 4:21's *fully convinced*: the faith of Abraham asked at the table. The helper: *the one who wavers*, with a note.
- **15:31** `τῶν ἀπειθούντων`: Bible Main *the unbelievers*. Romans has *disobedient* for this verb everywhere else in this project (2:8; 10:21; 11:30–31). The helper: *the disobedient*.
- **13:7** `τῷ τὸν φόβον τὸν φόβον`: Bible Main *respect to whom respect*, after *terror… no fear… be afraid* for the same root in vv3–4. The helper: *fear to whom fear*.

**A choice of meaning the cycle did not raise (the helper's):**
- **14:20 `τῷ διὰ προσκόμματος ἐσθίοντι`**: the harm may be to the brother who watches (*to make another stumble by what he eats*, both sides), or to the eater himself, who eats with a conscience that stumbles. Added to the Romans 13–16 choices in `PASTE/renders/60-romans.md`.

**Notes:** Bible Main's notes are fewer and keep to what matters (13: 3; 14: 3; 15: 5; 16: 5), as the new rule asks. The helper found connections Bible Main does not have:
- 13:11–14, *ēngiken* is Jesus' *the kingdom of God has come near* (Mark 1:15), and *put on Christ* is the baptism of Galatians 3:27.
- 14:5, 23, Abraham's two words (4:20–21), above.
- 15:16, *leitourgos* is Isaiah 61:6, *ministers of our God*, and the same word as the rulers' (13:6).
- 15:12, *anistamenos* is the verb of the resurrection.
- 16:19–20, the Greek of Genesis 3:15 has another verb (*tērēsei*), and Revelation 12:9 names the serpent Satan.

Bible Main has some the helper missed:
- 13:1–7, *apodote* is Jesus' *Give back to Caesar* (Mark 12:17).
- 13:8–10, Leviticus 19:18 opens with *You shall not take revenge*, so both halves of 12:19–13:10 stand in one verse.
- 13:12, *hopla* is the *weapons* of 6:13.
- 15:24–32, Acts tells how the plan went (Acts 21:33; 28:16).
- 16:13, Rufus of Mark 15:21.

**The helper's own mistakes:**
- **16:1** puts *a deacon* in the text, deciding a choice of meaning that is Andrew's; Bible Main keeps *servant* and raises it.
- **Its notes** run longer than the new rule allows: 13:1–2 (*tassō* four times) and 16:22 (Tertius the scribe) are word studies that open no main idea.
- **15:24** keeps Paul's broken sentence with dashes; Bible Main's smoother English carries the same meaning.
