**14:25 "broke":** applied as you gave it. The Hebrew says *removed*, the Greek *bound*; the note gives both.

**14:27, gentle vs. violent:** partly because of what you said, and that was a mistake. *Eitan* means constant, ever-flowing, enduring. It says the sea went back to what it always is, not how fast. "Steady flow" leaned gentle; "full force" leaned toward your crashing picture. "Full flow" says what the word says. The violence is in the rest of the verse: they fled into it and God shook them into it.

**"Pass over the land":** the word in 15:16 is *avar*, pass through. It is the verb of the Passover night itself: *I will pass through the land of Egypt* (12:12), *the LORD will pass through to strike Egypt* (12:23). The "pass over" that names the feast is a different word, *pasach* (12:13). So the text reads **passed through**, matching 12:12, and a note makes the link. "The land" isn't in the Hebrew, so it stays out.
