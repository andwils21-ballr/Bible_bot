## Jeremiah 46–49 — rendered 2026-10-19, 5:56 PM CDT

- ✅ Decided 2026-11-22 (settled by the council, item 110): (a), as the text has it. **46:15, *nischaf abbirekha***
  - (a) **Why are your mighty ones swept away? They did not stand, for the LORD thrust them down** (now): *abbirekha* as written, a plural, with the singular verbs after it read as each one of them.
  - (b) **Why has Apis fled? Your bull did not stand, for the LORD thrust him down**: the Greek, which reads *nischaf* as two words, *nas*, "has fled," and the name; *abbir* as "bull," as in *strong bulls of Bashan* (Psalm 22:12). The heifer (v20) and fattened calves (v21) go with it.
  - Recommend (a): it keeps the Hebrew words and their pointing; the note gives the Greek, and (b) is a strong second.
- ✅ Decided 2026-11-22 (settled by the council, item 110): (a), as the text has it. **48:9, *tenu tsits le-Mo'av***
  - (a) **Give wings to Moab, for she must fly away** (now): *tsits* as a wing, to fit *natso*, "fly."
  - (b) **Set up a marker for Moab, for she will go away**: the Greek, *Give signs to Moab*, a grave or boundary marker for a land left empty.
  - Recommend (a): the next words, *she must fly away*, ask for wings.
- ✅ Decided 2026-11-22 (settled by the council, item 110): (a), as the text has it. **49:1, 3, *malkam***
  - (a) **Milcom** (now): the god of Ammon, *Milcom the god of the sons of Ammon* (1 Kings 11:33); he has *priests* and goes into exile like Chemosh (48:7). The Greek has *Melchol*.
  - (b) **their king**: the Hebrew as pointed, as at 2 Samuel 12:30, *the crown of their king*.
  - Recommend (a): v3 gives him priests, and 48:7 says the same words of Moab's god; the letters are the same.

## Jeremiah 41–45 — rendered 2026-10-19, 12:59 PM CDT

- ✅ Decided 2026-11-22 (settled by the council, item 110): (a), as the text has it. **41:9, *be-yad Gedalyahu***
  - (a) **all the corpses of the men he had struck down because of Gedaliah** (now): *be-yad*, "by the hand of," read as "on account of": the pilgrims die for the murder that was still hidden (v4).
  - (b) **The cistern into which Ishmael threw all the corpses of the men he had struck down was a great cistern, the one King Asa had made**: the Greek, *it is a great cistern*, in place of *by the hand of Gedaliah*.
  - Recommend (a): it keeps the Hebrew, and the context does not need the Greek; the note gives both.
- ✅ Decided 2026-11-22 (settled by the council, item 110): (a), as the text has it. **42:20, *hit'eitem be-nafshoteikhem***
  - (a) **For you have gone astray at the cost of your lives** (now): *be-nafsho* as in Adonijah's *at the cost of his own life* (1 Kings 2:23) and *it will cost him his life* (Proverbs 7:23). Verse 22 then names the cost: *you will die by the sword*.
  - (b) **For you have deceived yourselves**: *nefesh* as the self; they deceived themselves when they swore to obey (vv5–6).
  - Recommend (a): this project reads the same phrase that way in 1 Kings and Proverbs, and the chapter ends on the price.
- ✅ Decided 2026-11-22 (settled by the council, item 110): (a), as the text has it. **43:12, *ve-atah et erets Mitsrayim***
  - (a) **He will wrap himself in the land of Egypt as a shepherd wraps himself in his garment** (now): *'atah*, "wrap," the conqueror putting on Egypt as easily as a shepherd his cloak.
  - (b) **He will pick the land of Egypt clean as a shepherd picks the lice from his garment**: the Greek, *φθειριεῖ*; Egypt stripped as thoroughly as a cloak picked clean.
  - Recommend (a): *wrap* is the sense of the verb in Leviticus 13:45, 1 Samuel 28:14 and Psalms 104:2 and 109:19 (*like a garment he wraps around himself*); (b) rests on the Greek alone.

## Jeremiah 37–40 — rendered 2026-10-19, 8:02 AM CDT

- ✅ Decided 2026-11-22 (settled by the council, item 110): (a), as the text has it. **38:23, *ve-et ha-ir ha-zot tisrof ba-esh***
  - (a) **and you will burn this city with fire** (now): the Hebrew as it is read. Jeremiah lays the city's burning on the king's refusal; v17 had said that if he went out, *this city will not be burned with fire*.
  - (b) **and this city will be burned with fire**: the same letters read as *tissaref*, "will be burned," as the Greek has it.
  - Recommend (a): it is the reading the Hebrew gives, the *et* before *this city* reads most naturally with a verb that has a subject acting on it, and the note gives the Greek.
- ✅ Decided 2026-11-22 (settled by the council, item 110): (a), as the text has it. **40:5, *ve-odennu lo yashuv***
  - (a) **While Jeremiah had not yet turned to go, he said, "Go back to Gedaliah…"** (now): the words are the storyteller's, with *he said* supplied. The captain speaks again before Jeremiah leaves.
  - (b) **"…But if not, go back to Gedaliah…"**: the Greek, which has *But if not, run off* in place of the Hebrew's phrase and keeps the captain speaking.
  - Recommend (a): it keeps the Hebrew's words and adds only *he said*; the note gives the Greek.

## Jeremiah 33–36 — rendered 2026-10-18, 10:53 PM CDT

- ✅ Decided 2026-11-22 (settled by the council, item 110): (a), as the text has it. **34:18, *ve-natatti et ha-anashim… ha-egel***
  - (a) **I will make the men who transgressed My covenant… like the calf that they cut in two** (now): *ve-natatti* takes two objects, *the men* and *the calf*, as in *father of a roar of nations I have made you* (Genesis 17:5). The men are made what the calf was made: cut in two.
  - (b) **The men who transgressed My covenant… the calf that they cut in two… I will give them into the hand of their enemies**: *the calf* names the covenant they made, and the sentence waits for v20, which takes up *I will give* again.
  - Recommend (a): it uses both of the Hebrew's verbs *natatti* (vv18, 20) and finishes v18 as a sentence; the note shows the grammar. (b) is also a fair reading of a long sentence.

## Jeremiah 29–32 — rendered 2026-10-18, 6:00 PM CDT

- ✅ Decided 2026-11-22 (settled by the council, item 110): (a), as the text has it. **29:8, *asher attem machlemim***
  - (a) **the dreams that you cause them to dream** (now): the causative form, found only here. The people call up the dreams they want to hear, as 27:9 counts *your dreams* among their own diviners.
  - (b) **the dreams that you dream**: the form read as the plain verb, as the Greek has it.
  - Recommend (a): it is the form the Hebrew uses, and it fits v15, where the people themselves boast of their prophets in Babylon.
- ✅ Decided 2026-11-22 (settled by the council, item 110): (a), as the text has it. **32:39, *lev echad ve-derekh echad***
  - (a) **one heart and one way** (now): the Hebrew. One mind and one road for the whole people; Ezekiel's promise has the same words, *one heart* (Ezekiel 11:19).
  - (b) **another heart and another way**: the Greek, reading *acher*, "another," for *echad*, "one"; the two words differ by one letter. A changed heart, as the new covenant of 31:33.
  - Recommend (a): the Hebrew makes sense as it stands, and Ezekiel's promise has the same words.

## Jeremiah 25–28 — rendered 2026-10-18, 12:56 PM CDT

  - (b) **you will fall like the choice rams**: the Greek, *hōsper hoi krioi hoi eklektoi*. The leaders of the flock go down at the slaughter like the best rams, and the picture of shepherds and flock runs unbroken through vv34–36.
  - Recommend (a): the Hebrew makes sense as it stands, and broken pots and jars run through the book (19:10–11; 22:28).
- ✅ Decided 2026-11-22 (settled by the council, item 110): (a), as the text has it. **25:38, *mippenei charon ha-yonah***
  - (a) **because of the oppressing sword** (now): the Greek reads *sword*, and *because of the oppressing sword* is the book's own phrase at 46:16 and 50:16.
  - (b) **because of the fierceness of the oppressor**: the Hebrew here. *Charon*, "fierceness," comes three times in two verses: *the fierce anger of the LORD* (v37), here, and *His fierce anger* in the next line, so the enemy's fury and the LORD's are one (the spot check of 2026-10-18).
  - Recommend (a): the Greek and the book's phrase in two other places agree, and the sword is what the LORD sends through the whole chapter (vv16, 27, 29, 31). (b) is a sound reading of the Hebrew as it stands, and the note gives it.
- ✅ Decided 2026-11-22 (settled by the council, item 110): (a), as the text has it. **28:13, *ve-asita tachteihen motot barzel***
  - (a) **but you have made in their place bars of iron** (now): the Hebrew. By breaking the wooden yoke, Hananiah has brought on an iron one.
  - (b) **but I will make in their place bars of iron**: the Greek. The LORD answers the broken bars with iron ones.
  - Recommend (a): the Hebrew makes sense as it stands, and v14 still says *I have put an iron yoke*, so the LORD's hand in it is not lost.

## Jeremiah 21–24 — rendered 2026-10-18, 8:04 AM CDT

- ✅ Decided 2026-10-18: (a), as the text has it. **22:23, *mah nechant***
  - (a) **how you will be pitied** (now): the Hebrew vowels, from *chanan*, "to show favor." The one nested among the cedars becomes someone to pity.
  - (b) **how you will groan**: the Greek. The sound of a woman in labor, which the line goes on to name.
  - Recommend (a): the Hebrew makes sense as it stands, and the Greek does not tell the event better; it only trades mockery for pain.
- ✅ Decided 2026-10-18: (a), as the text has it. **23:6, *asher yiqre'o YHWH tsidqenu***
  - (a) **this is the name by which he will be called: The LORD Is Our Righteousness** (now): the Hebrew accents join the LORD's name to the name, as in *The LORD Is My Banner* (Exodus 17:15).
  - (b) **this is the name by which the LORD will call him: Our Righteousness**: the same words, with the LORD as the one who names him, as the Greek takes them (the Greek's name is *Iosedek*).
  - Recommend (a): the name answers Zedekiah's, *Tsidqiyahu*, which needs both words, and 33:16 gives the same name to Jerusalem.
- ✅ Decided 2026-10-18: (a), as the text has it. **23:33, 39, *massa*: the burden**
  - (a) **You are the burden… I am about to lift you up and cast you off** (now): at v33 the Greek, *attem ha-massa*, the same letters divided otherwise; at v39 the letters of *nasa*, "to lift," as the Greek reads them (*I take*).
  - (b) **What burden?… I am about to forget you completely and cast you off**: the Hebrew vowels at both verses.
  - Recommend (a): the passage is built on *massa*, a load that is lifted, and (a) finishes the picture. (b) has its own thread: *you shall no longer mention* (v36), *I will forget you* (v39), *that will not be forgotten* (v40).

## Jeremiah 9–12 — rendered 2026-10-17, 12:51 PM CDT

- ✅ Decided 2026-11-22 (settled by the council, item 110): (a), as the text has it. **9:25, *kol mul be-orlah***
  - (a) **all who are circumcised in the flesh alone** (now): *be-orlah*, "in foreskin," as "only outwardly." The LORD will punish those who bear the sign in the body without the heart, and v26 says it of Judah with the nations: *uncircumcised in heart*.
  - (b) **the circumcised and the uncircumcised alike**: *be-orlah* as "with the foreskin," the circumcised together with the uncircumcised. The LORD will punish everyone, whatever the mark on the body.
  - Recommend (a): v26 ends with *uncircumcised in heart*, and the Greek also has *all circumcised in their foreskins*.
- ✅ Decided 2026-11-22 (settled by the council, item 110): (a), as the text has it. **10:5, *ke-tomer miqshah***
  - (a) **like a scarecrow in a cucumber field** (now): *miqshah* as the *cucumber field* of Isaiah 1:8, and *tomer* as a post standing in it. The idols look like guardians and do nothing, as the rest of the verse says: *Do not be afraid of them, for they can do no harm*. The Letter of Jeremiah has the same picture.
  - (b) **like a post of hammered work**: *miqshah* as *hammered work*, the hammered gold of the lampstand (Exodus 25:31). The idols are beaten metal on a post, as vv4 and 9 describe them; the Greek has *it is chased silver*.
  - Recommend (a): the verse turns on the idol that frightens and cannot act, and the Letter of Jeremiah, in this canon, reads it as a scarecrow.

## Jeremiah 5–8 — rendered 2026-10-17, 7:53 AM CDT

- ✅ Decided 2026-11-22 (settled by the council, item 110): (a), as the text has it. **6:2, *ha-navah ve-ha-me'unnagah damiti***
  - (a) **The lovely and delicate one, the daughter of Zion, I will destroy** (now): *navah*, "lovely," and *damiti*, "I will cut off." The LORD will destroy the city He has pampered, the *delicate* woman of Deuteronomy 28:56.
  - (b) **To a lovely pasture I liken the daughter of Zion**: the same letters read *naveh*, "pasture," and *damiti*, "I have likened." The city is a green pasture, and the shepherds and flocks of v3 come and graze it bare.
  - Recommend (a): *me'unnagah*, "delicate, pampered," is said of a woman, not of a pasture, and the line stands among the war cries of vv1 and 4–6. (b) runs smoothly into v3, and the note gives it.
- ✅ Decided 2026-11-22 (settled by the council, item 110): (b) **When I would gather them in, there are no grapes**, the harvest reading, with the Hebrew vowels in the note; applied (`PASTE/changes/35-jeremiah.md`). **8:13, *asof asifem***
  - (a) **I will gather them up and sweep them away** (now): the Hebrew vowels, which join *asaf*, "gather," to *suf*, "come to an end," as Zephaniah 1:2 does (*I will utterly sweep away everything*). The LORD's harvest is His judgment.
  - (b) **When I would gather them in, there are no grapes on the vine**: *asaf* alone, the harvest. The LORD comes to gather His crop and finds nothing; the Greek has *they will gather their produce*.
  - Recommend (b): the rest of the verse is all harvest (no grapes, no figs, the leaf withered), and the Greek reads it that way. (a) is the Hebrew as voweled, and the note gives it.

## Jeremiah 1–4 — rendered 2026-10-16, 10:54 PM CDT

- ✅ Decided 2026-11-22 (settled by the council, item 110): (b) **you broke your yoke… "I will not serve"**, the written form and the Greek; applied (`PASTE/changes/35-jeremiah.md`). **2:20, *shavarti ullekh… lo e'evor***
  - (a) **For long ago I broke your yoke and tore off your bonds, and you said, "I will not transgress"** (now): the Hebrew as read. The LORD freed Israel from Egypt (*I broke the bars of your yoke*, Leviticus 26:13); Israel promised to be faithful, and then lay down under every green tree.
  - (b) **For long ago you broke your yoke and tore off your bonds, and you said, "I will not serve"**: the written form *e'evod*, "serve," with the Greek, and *shavarti* read as "you broke," as Jeremiah's written *-ti* is read elsewhere (2:33; 3:4, 5; 4:19). Israel threw off the LORD's yoke and refused to serve Him.
  - Recommend (b): 5:5 says the same of the people in the same words (*they have broken the yoke, they have burst the bonds*), and v20 then gives the reason for v19, *the fear of Me is not in you*.
- ✅ Decided 2026-11-22 (settled by the council, item 110): (a), as the text has it. **3:1, *ve-shov elai***
  - (a) **and would you return to Me?** (now): a question, with the law's answer understood. Under Deuteronomy 24:1–4 a wife who was sent away and became another man's cannot go back to her first husband.
  - (b) **yet return to Me**: a call. The LORD invites back the wife the law says cannot return.
  - Recommend (a): the verse is a run of questions, and the Greek has a question. The call comes in vv12, 14 and 22, where it cannot be mistaken, and it is all the stronger after the law has been heard.
