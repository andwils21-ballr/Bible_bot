## Joel 3 — rendered 2026-10-27, 6:48 PM CDT

- ✅ Decided 2026-11-22 (settled by the council, item 111): (a), as the text has it. **3:14, *emeq he-charuts***

- ✅ Decided 2026-11-22 (settled by the council, item 111): (a), as the text has it. **3:21, *ve-niqqeiti damam lo niqqeiti***

  - (b) **I will avenge their blood, which I had not avenged**: as the Greek has it, *I will seek out their blood*; this takes the verb as *naqam*, **avenge**, which is not the word the Hebrew has.
  - Recommend (a): it is the Hebrew, and it answers *innocent blood* two verses before; the note gives the Greek.

## Joel 1–2 — rendered 2026-10-27, 1:09 PM CDT

- ✅ Decided 2026-11-22 (settled by the council, item 111): (b) **cutting locust, locust, young locust, consuming locust**, one English word for each Hebrew word, in every book; applied (`PASTE/changes/45-joel.md`). **The four locust names, Joel 1:4 and 2:25 (affects several books).** Joel names four insects side by side: *gazam*, *arbeh*, *yeleq*, *chasil*, so they need four different English words. This also settles the *chasil* question from the 2 Chronicles spot check. Right now the project has *locust* for *arbeh* everywhere; *young locusts* for *yeleq* (Psalm 105:34); *grasshopper* for *chasil* (1 Kings 8:37; 2 Chronicles 6:28; Psalm 78:46) but *caterpillar* in Isaiah 33:4; *grasshopper* for *chagav* too (Leviticus 11:22; 2 Chronicles 7:13); and *locust* for *gazam* in Amos 4:9, which clashes with *arbeh*.
  - (a) **cutting locust, locust, young locust, grasshopper** (now): *chasil* keeps *grasshopper*, as in 1 Kings 8:37, so *grasshopper* still stands for two Hebrew words (*chasil* and *chagav*). Isaiah 33:4 *caterpillar* becomes *grasshopper*.
  - (b) **cutting locust, locust, young locust, consuming locust**: *chasil* gets its own name, from its verb in *for the locust will consume it* (Deuteronomy 28:38), and *grasshopper* is left to *chagav* alone. 1 Kings 8:37, 2 Chronicles 6:28, Psalm 78:46 and Isaiah 33:4 change to *consuming locust*.
  - Either way, Amos 4:9 changes from *locust* to *cutting locust*, and Jeremiah 51:14 and 51:27 (*locusts*, *bristling locusts*) become *young locusts* and *bristling young locusts*, so each Hebrew word has one English word.
  - Recommend (b): one English word for each Hebrew word, as the spot check asked, and the name comes from a verb the reader can see in Deuteronomy.
- ✅ Decided 2026-11-22 (settled by the council, item 111): (a), as the text has it. **2:17, *li-mshol bam goyim***
  - (a) **for the nations to rule over them** (now): *mashal* with *bam*, **over them**, and the nations as the ones doing it; the Greek has *to rule over them*.
  - (b) **for the nations to make a proverb of them**: *mashal* as the *proverb* of *a horror, a proverb and a byword* (Deuteronomy 28:37), next to *scorn* in the same line.
  - Recommend (a): the wording, *bam*, **over them**, and the Greek; the note gives the other reading.
- ✅ Decided 2026-11-22 (settled by the council, item 111): (a), as the text has it. **2:23, *ha-moreh li-tsdaqah***
  - (a) **the early rain for righteousness** (now): the next line uses *moreh* for the early rain, and the verse is about rain.
  - (b) **the Teacher for righteousness**: *moreh* as **teacher**, as in *your eyes will see your Teacher* (Isaiah 30:20).
  - Recommend (a): the same word is the rain in the next line; the note gives *teacher*.
